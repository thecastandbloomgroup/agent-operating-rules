# Headline

Freeze all external statements now, convene one response team today, verify the facts under privilege, then reply through one spokesperson.

- Three unaligned channels are the largest immediate risk, because conflicting replies create legal admissions and fresh stories [judgment].
- A same-day, privileged fact check decides the path: correction if the claim is true, rebuttal if it is false.
- One spokesperson turns three message streams into a single decision, which limits further damage [2].

# Background

The CEO made a public statement yesterday. A journalist has since published that the statement was false. Legal, PR, and the board are messaging separately, and no position is agreed. The request is a plan for the next steps.

# Relevance

Each hour without coordination raises two risks: contradictory public statements and lost evidence. A fast single reply protects both the legal position and the company's credibility [judgment].

# Information

**Evidence**

- Preservation: a published falsity claim makes litigation reasonably anticipated, so the duty to preserve records applies now [1].
- Fit to facts: crisis research shows denial works only for a false claim, while corrective action fits a true one [2].
- Disclosure: if the company is listed, Regulation Fair Disclosure (Reg FD), the rule against selective release of market-moving news, can govern the reply [3].
- One voice: two crisis-communication sources advise a single spokesperson and channel; no reviewed source disagrees.

- Speed: stakeholders read a reply gap longer than one news cycle as confirmation [judgment].

**Assumption log**

A1. The truth of the journalist's claim is unverified. Why not measured: the records that settle it were not reviewed in this session. Validate by: the document comparison in Step 2. If wrong: skip Step 2 and apply the branch that matches the known facts.

A2. The requester can convene legal, PR, and a board contact. Why not measured: the material does not state the requester's role. Validate by: send the invite now; a refusal shows the limit. If wrong: Step 1 becomes a brief to the officer who can convene it.

A3. The company is listed on a public exchange. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: one question to counsel on the first call. If wrong: drop the Reg FD review in Step 3; the rest stands.

**Alternative considered**

The strongest alternative is an immediate public denial before the fact check, to stop the spread. It loses because a denial that later proves false turns one story into a lasting credibility and liability event [judgment]. Research also ties denial to false claims only [2]. Silence loses in reverse: audiences read it as confirmation [judgment].

# End

I recommend that we run five steps in this order. Chaotic goes first, Complicated follows by leverage, and the Complex probe runs capped and in parallel.

**Step 1 (Chaotic: no agreed cause and effect yet, so act first to stabilize).** Within one hour, start one call with legal, PR, the board contact, and the CEO's office. Announce a freeze: nobody makes external statements until the team approves one. Have counsel issue a preservation notice for all records tied to the statement. Blocker removed: uncoordinated output. Test first, deterministic:
```text
Given the freeze notice reached legal, PR, and the board,
When any of them receives a media or investor inquiry,
Then the inquiry goes to the single channel and gets no direct reply.
```

**Step 2 (Complicated: documents settle cause and effect, so deduction from records).** By end of day, counsel leads the fact check, so legal privilege can protect it. Collect the statement text, the records behind each claim, and the journalist's evidence. Deliverable: a one-page memo marking every claim true, false, or unverified, with its source. Blocker removed: unknown facts. Test first, deterministic:
```text
Given the statement text and the underlying records,
When counsel compares the statement line by line,
Then every factual claim carries a mark and a cited record.
```

**Step 3 (Complicated: the memo determines the right practice).** Step 3 MUST NOT start until the Step 2 memo exists. Choose the reply with a Kepner-Tregoe screen [5]. MUST, that is go/no-go: the reply is consistent with the memo. WANTs, weighted: legal risk reduction 40%, stakeholder trust 30%, speed 20%, reuse across audiences 10%. Options: correction if the memo says true; evidence-based rebuttal plus a correction request to the outlet if false; a holding statement with a date for the full answer if unverified. Pass threshold: the winner clears the MUST and scores at least 70 of 100. If A3 holds, securities counsel reviews the winner under Reg FD before release. Kind: evaluative; rubric written first.

**Step 4 (Clear: single-voice release is a known best practice [2]).** Step 4 MUST NOT start until Step 3 passes. Draft a statement under 150 words, obtain legal sign-off, and have one named spokesperson release it on one channel. Give every internal party the same Q&A sheet. Blocker removed: fragmented voice. Test first, deterministic:
```text
Given the approved statement and Q&A sheet,
When a journalist, investor, or employee asks about the story,
Then the answer matches the approved statement.
```

**Step 5 (Complex: public reaction shows cause and effect only afterward, so run a capped probe; leverage here is a guess).** Step 5 runs in parallel with Step 4's release. For 48 hours, one analyst checks follow-on coverage twice a day. Metric: the share of the 20 most-read follow-on stories that repeat the claim without the company's position. Baseline: that share in the 12 hours after release. Pass threshold: the share falls below baseline within 48 hours. Stop condition: two checks with no new pickups, or any legal move, which returns the matter to Step 1. Kind: stochastic; sample size 20 stories.

# Follow-up

Conditions that change the recommendation:

- If the memo finds the claim true, issue a correction with the accurate figures; do not defend the original statement [2].
- If the memo finds the claim false, send the outlet a formal correction request before public comment; a defamation suit stays a last resort [judgment].
- If A3 is true, no external reply goes out before securities counsel clears it [3].
