# Headline

Retrospectives fail when they produce discussion instead of committed, tracked experiments. The fix has three parts. Convert each retro outcome into one small owned action. Review past actions at the next retro's opening. Cut the meeting's scope if it no longer feeds decisions.

# Background

The team meets regularly and talks, but no change follows. This pattern, called "retro theater", appears when actions lack owners, deadlines, and follow-up.

# Relevance

Each retro that changes nothing trains the team that the meeting is optional. Attendance then decays, and real problems surface later and at higher cost.

That pattern matches the failure mode where retrospectives generate no committed actions or lost follow-through [judgment]. The observation of full attendance points toward the second cause, because people still invest time. High confidence is claimed here on one observation only.
