### Headline

Build an evaluation dataset of fifty support conversations to measure and fix summarisation errors.
First, classify the reported errors into omissions, fabrications, and formatting bugs.
Second, build an automated rubric to test summary quality before prompt changes.

### Background

Your support tool now includes a large language model summariser for customer tickets. Support agents report that the generated summaries are sometimes incorrect. The team currently has no quantitative baseline or error tracking system.

### Relevance

Inaccurate summaries cause agent errors, extend ticket resolution times, and reduce customer trust. Measuring error rates immediately prevents wasted engineering effort on blind prompt tweaks.

### Information

Customer support summarisation typically fails in three distinct ways [judgment]:
1. Factual fabrication: the summary invents facts not present in the customer transcript.
2. Key detail omission: the summary drops order numbers, error codes, or customer requests.
3. Polarity reversal: the summary flips customer sentiment or issue status.

A study on summarisation quality shows that structured extraction prompts reduce factual inconsistency by up to 30% [1]. However, teams cannot verify improvements without a reference dataset [2]. Therefore, you must establish an initial evaluation set before altering generation parameters.
