## Background

People attend your retrospectives, but you report no resulting changes. [1] The missing evidence is what happens between a concern, an agreed action, and a result.

## Relevance

A new meeting format does not directly fix missing authority or unfinished actions. [judgment] Therefore, inspect that gap before changing the format.

Use these categories for each concern from recent retrospectives:

| Last point reached | What to examine |
|---|---|

| Action completed, no benefit checked | Did the team define an outcome and review date? |
| Benefit checked, no improvement | What did the result teach the team about its proposed solution? |

These categories separate stages of the change process; they do not establish your team’s cause. [judgment]

### 3. Run a bounded trial and review its result

**Acceptance test first, stochastic:**

- **Baseline:** Record the current measure before the change.
- **Metric:** Choose one observable result tied to the concern.
- **Threshold:** Set the smallest worthwhile improvement before the trial.
- **Sample:** Specify the number of work events to observe.
- **Acceptance:** Retain the change only if the threshold holds across that sample.

For example, measure blocked handoffs across five comparable tasks. Use the previous five tasks as the baseline. Set the target before the trial, such as reducing blocked handoffs from three to one. These numbers illustrate a test, not a target for your team. [judgment]

Step 3 MUST NOT start until Step 2 passes. Cap setup and review at 30 minutes total. Set a separate effort budget for the change itself.

Stop if the change exceeds that budget or causes an agreed harm. At the next retrospective, review the result first: retain, revise, or stop the change.

**Domain: Complex.** Use induction from a small trial; different workloads or task types can break the pattern. The benefit remains unproven until the trial passes.
