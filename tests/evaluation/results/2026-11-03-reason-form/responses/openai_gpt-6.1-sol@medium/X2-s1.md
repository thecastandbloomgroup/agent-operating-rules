## Headline

Turn the next retrospective into a small change trial, rather than another discussion.

- First, find where past actions stop: selection, completion, or effect.
- Next, choose one change with an owner, time, and a visible success measure.
- Then, review the result before discussing new topics.

## Background

People attend your retrospectives, but you report no resulting change. [1] That establishes the symptom, but not where the process fails.

## Relevance

A new discussion format does not, by itself, address unfinished actions or missing authority. [judgment] Therefore, inspect what happens after the meeting before changing the meeting itself.

## Information

### Find the failure point

Use these three categories. Classify each past action at its earliest failure point.

| Failure point | Evidence to check | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Selection | No specific action leaves the meeting | Choose one observable change |
| Completion | An action exists, but nobody completes it | Provide an owner, time, and needed authority |
| Effect | People complete the action, but the problem remains | Test a different change against a baseline |

These categories are a diagnostic aid, not a finding about your team. [judgment]

The Scrum Guide describes retrospectives as a way to increase quality and effectiveness. It also calls for prompt attention to useful changes. [2] Therefore, treat the meeting as the start of work, not the output.

**Assumption log**

A1. The team controls at least one small change. Why not measured: The material does not state it. Validate by: Check authority for the chosen action. If wrong: Request a sponsor’s decision before another trial.

**Alternative considered:** Change the facilitator or meeting format first. This loses priority because your report identifies absent change, not absent participation. [judgment]

**Confidence:** Moderate confidence, based on your report and established retrospective guidance. Past action records would distinguish the causes.

## End

I recommend that we complete these three actions in order.

### 1. Inspect the last three retrospectives

**Test first, deterministic:**

```text
Given the available retrospective notes
When each agreed action is checked
Then each action has an evidence-backed status:
not selected, not completed, or completed without demonstrated effect
```

In one 20-minute session, inspect the notes with the facilitator and one team member. Record missing evidence rather than guessing. If no notes exist, record that gap.

This removes uncertainty about where action stops. [judgment]

**Domain: Confused.** Gather evidence first because the cause is not yet known. The resulting classification determines the next intervention.

### 2. Prepare one change that the team can actually try

**Test first, deterministic:**

```text
Given the failure point found in Step 1
When the team writes one change card
Then the card names the action, owner, time, authority,
baseline, success measure, and review date
```

After Step 1, spend 15 minutes creating that card. Choose a change small enough to try within one working session. Reserve time for it before closing the retrospective.

For example: “For the next handoff, use a five-item checklist; count requests returned for missing information.”

If the team lacks authority, request one specific sponsor decision instead. Do not assign an action that depends on authority nobody has confirmed.

This gives the change a practical route to completion. [judgment]

**Domain: Clear.** Deduction from the card’s fixed requirements determines whether it is ready.

### 3. Run a two-cycle trial, then decide

**Acceptance test first, stochastic:**

- **Metric:** The issue-specific measure on the change card.
- **Baseline:** Record the current value before the trial.
- **Sample:** Two retrospective cycles, with one observed trial per cycle.
- **Pass threshold:** Both trials occur, and at least one improves the measure against baseline.
- **Decision:** Keep, adjust, or stop the change using the recorded results.

Step 3 MUST NOT start until Step 2 passes. Limit each trial to one working session.

Start each retrospective with the previous action’s result. If an action remains incomplete, address its blocker before selecting another.

Stop a trial if it harms service, exceeds its time budget, or requires unapproved authority. If neither trial improves the measure, reject that change rather than repeat it without new evidence.

This tests whether the proposed change helps, rather than merely whether someone completes a task. [judgment]

**Domain: Complex.** Use induction from small trials; different workloads or cases can break the observed pattern. Expected benefit remains a judgment until measured.

## Follow-up

If Step 1 finds completed actions but no results, focus on change design. If it finds blocked actions, focus on authority and time.
