## Headline

Freeze all external messaging today, verify what the CEO actually said, and route every future statement through one appointed spokesperson. Three points: (1) conflicting messages from legal, PR, and board create a second crisis on top of the first [judgment]; (2) you cannot choose a response until you know whether the journalist is right [18]; (3) one accountable incident lead replaces three competing voices.

## Background

The CEO gave a public statement yesterday. A journalist published that it was false. Legal, PR, and board contacts are all reaching you with different advice, and no one agrees on a plan.

## Relevance

Decisions in the first hours shape the rest of the story [18]. Early errors are hard to reverse because a correction reaches fewer people than the original claim [judgment].

## Information

**Evidence.** Two facts drive the plan. First, the truth of the claim is unverified: no transcript, recording, or article text is in front of you [no source]. Second, no one has stated authority to decide, so messaging drifts until someone takes that role [18].

**Assumptions.**

- A1. The journalist's claim is unverified either way. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: collect the article, the recording, and the transcript of the statement. If wrong: if the statement is confirmed false, replace Step 4 with a correction and apology plan agreed with legal.
- A2. No named incident lead exists on your side. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: check the crisis plan for a named authority. If wrong: if a lead is named, skip the convening step and route to that person.
- A3. The false claim, if real, is materially risky to markets or regulators. Why not measured: measuring costs more than the decision is worth right now. Validate by: legal reviews disclosure duties on the Step 2 call. If wrong: if immaterial, handle the issue inside PR only.

**Uncertainty.** The recommendation below is conditional on A1. If the transcript shows the CEO was accurate, the plan changes to a demand for correction against the outlet. Confidence: high on the freeze-first step, because the domain analysis supports it [18]. Confidence: moderate, without a source, on the 48-hour holding window.

**Alternative considered.** Respond immediately to deny the claim. This loses because a denial issued before verification becomes a second false statement if A1 resolves against the CEO [18], [judgment].

## End

I recommend that we take the following steps. The names Clear, Complicated, Complex, and Chaotic follow the framework in [18].

1. **Freeze messaging (Chaotic: act first to stabilise).** Within 2 hours, send a written instruction to legal, PR, and board contacts: no media contact, no social posts, and no statements until the incident lead speaks. Reason: no cause-and-effect map exists yet, so stabilise first [18]. Test first (deterministic):
   - Given the freeze notice is sent to all three groups
   - When any member receives a media question
   - Then the member answers only "we are reviewing the statement and will respond by Friday"
2. **Appoint an incident lead (Clear: one known best action).** Hold a 30-minute call today with legal, PR, and one board member. Name one lead who owns all external words. This step MUST NOT start after any party speaks publicly. Test first (deterministic):
   - Given the call ends
   - When the internal channel posts a decision
   - Then one named lead appears, and the freeze from Step 1 transfers to that person
3. **Build the fact file (Complicated: analysis by experts).** Collect the recording, the transcript, and the article by end of day. Compare each sentence of the article against the transcript. Reason: the analysis, not a guess, picks the response [18]. Test first (deterministic):
   - Given the transcript and the article exist
   - When each journalist claim is matched to a transcript timestamp
   - Then each claim is labelled "supported", "contradicted", or "not covered", with the timestamp noted
4. **Issue one holding statement (Evaluative: Kepner-Tregoe [15]).** The lead releases a statement in the form: "We are reviewing the report and will respond by <date>." Score it against this rubric before release. Criteria and weights: accuracy 0.4, one commitment to respond 0.3, tone 0.2, length 0.1. Pass threshold: 4 of 5 points total, and zero unverified factual claims. Evidence needed: legal sign-off record and a word count under 80 words. Wait for Step 3 output only if the article can be answered factually the same day.
5. **Run two monitoring probes in parallel (Complex: safe-to-fail, see [18]).** First, count negative articles per day for 48 hours, with a stop condition to escalate to the board if the count doubles from the day-1 baseline. Second, watch for regulator or exchange contact, with an immediate escalation rule. Budget: 2 hours of PR analyst time per day. Do not commit beyond the budget. Leverage is a guess here [judgment].

Step 3 MUST be complete before any statement beyond Step 4 goes out.
