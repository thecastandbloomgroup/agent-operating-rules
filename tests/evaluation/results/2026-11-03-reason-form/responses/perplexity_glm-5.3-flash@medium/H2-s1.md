Freeze all external statements now, then build a verified fact record before anyone responds.

## Background

The CEO made a public statement yesterday. A journalist published today that the statement was false. Legal, PR, and the board are each messaging the user, and no shared line exists yet [judgment, from the request].

## Relevance

The first 24 to 48 hours of a public dispute set its narrative, and conflicting messages extend the story [18]. Wrong wording now can create legal exposure that later wording cannot remove.

## Information

**The bind.** The request names three parties with different duties. Legal limits admissions, PR manages tone, and the board owns accountability. Because no document in this session quotes the CEO or the article, the facts of the claim are unverified. Confidence in the steps below is high; confidence in how Step 3 classifies the claim is low until the record exists.

**Theme check.** Across crisis-management practice, two themes recur: control of the message channel first, and verification of facts before correction [18]. No observed source in this session disagrees. The second theme has thin support here, so it matters less for speed but more for the legal record.

**Assumptions.**

Validate by: ask the comms team for the verbatim text and its source. If wrong: the fact record starts from press coverage instead, which weakens Step 3.

A2. The journalist's article states a specific factual discrepancy, not only an opinion. Why not measured: the material does not state it. **Alternative considered.** Respond to the journalist at once with a correction. It loses because it commits wording before the fact record exists, and legal review after publication cannot retract it [judgment].
