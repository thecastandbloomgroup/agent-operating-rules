## Headline

Stop the parallel messaging first, then verify the facts, and say nothing about truth or falsity until the check is done.

- One owner and one channel replace three competing streams of advice.
- A fact check against primary records decides whether the statement was false, partly false, or true.
- A short holding statement protects the company while the check runs.
- The final response (correct, clarify, or contest) follows from the facts, not from who pushes hardest.

## Background

A journalist published on Nov 3-4, 2026 that the CEO's statement from yesterday was false. Legal, public relations (PR), and the board each send you separate guidance. The messages conflict, and no one owns the decision.

## Relevance

The first hours set the story. A wrong denial or a late correction costs more than a short, honest pause. [judgment]

## Information

**Evidence**

- Conflicting advice from three groups is a coordination failure, not a content problem. [judgment]
- The facts of the CEO's statement are unknown to this response. Every option depends on them. [judgment]
- A holding statement buys time without a claim that can later prove false. [judgment]
- The five actions use the Cynefin framework [1]. It sorts work by how cause and effect relate.

**Assumption log**

A1. The statement is not yet verified as false. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: compare the statement with the source records. If wrong: if it is clearly false, skip to a correction in Step 4.

A2. You can name a decision owner, or ask the CEO or chair to name one. Why not measured: the material does not state your role. Validate by: ask the CEO's office. If wrong: escalate the owner choice to the chair.

A3. The company may have disclosure duties to regulators or investors. Why not measured: the material does not state the company type. Validate by: ask Legal in one line. If wrong: drop the disclosure check in Step 3.

**Alternative considered**

The strongest alternative is an immediate public denial that backs the CEO. It loses because the facts are unchecked, and a denial that proves false adds a second story. [judgment]

## End

I recommend that we take these five actions in this order:

1. **Fix one owner and one channel (Chaotic: act first, because no cause and effect are visible yet).**
   - Increment: in 60 minutes, send one message to Legal, PR, and the board. It names one decision owner and one thread. Until the owner signs off, no one speaks externally.
   - Blocker removed: three uncoordinated streams of advice.
   - Test (deterministic):
     - Given the message is sent
     - When Legal, PR, and a board member reply
     - Then each reply confirms the owner and the thread, and no outside statement is issued.

2. **Verify the statement against primary records (Complicated: analysis and expertise find the answer).**
   - Step 2 MUST NOT start until Step 1 passes.
   - Increment: in one session, list each claim in the CEO's statement and the journalist's claim. Match each to a record, such as a contract, a filing, or an email.
   - Blocker removed: no one knows if the statement was false.
   - Test (deterministic):
     - Given a two-column list of claims
     - When each claim is checked against a named record
     - Then each claim is marked true, false, or unverifiable, with a record cited.

3. **Issue a holding statement (Complicated: legal and PR expertise set the wording).**
   - Step 3 MUST NOT start until Step 2 lists at least the disputed claims.
   - Increment: draft two sentences. They say the company is reviewing the report and will update by a stated time. Legal checks the disclosure duty from A3.
   - Blocker removed: silence lets the journalist's version stand.
   - Test (deterministic):
     - Given the draft statement
     - When Legal and PR review it
     - Then it contains no claim about truth or falsity and gives an update time.

4. **Choose the response (evaluative: a judgment between options, scored with a rubric).**
   - Step 4 MUST NOT start until Step 2 is complete.
   - Domain: Complicated, because the facts from Step 2 make the analysis possible.
   - Increment: score three options (correct, clarify, contest) before any group argues for one.
   - Rubric, written first. Pass threshold: an option scores at least 70 of 100, and no criterion scores zero.

   | Criterion | Weight | Evidence needed |
   |---|---|---|
   | Factual accuracy | 40 | Step 2 list |
   | Legal exposure | 25 | Legal written view |
   | Trust of staff, customers, investors | 20 | PR and board input |
   | Speed to publish | 15 | Drafting time |

5. **Run a coverage probe (Complex: spread of the story is visible only afterward).**
   - Run Step 5 in parallel with Steps 2 to 4.
   - Increment: one person counts outlets that repeat the claim, at four readings over 24 hours. Budget: two hours of effort in total.
   - Leverage is a guess. [judgment]
   - Test (stochastic):
     - Metric: outlets repeating the claim.
     - Baseline: the count at the first reading.
     - Threshold: the count doubles between readings.
     - Sample size: four readings.
   - Stop condition: the count falls for two readings in a row. If the threshold is met, bring the Step 4 decision forward.
