## Headline

Fix follow-through first: limit each retrospective to one owned, tracked action, and review it at the next session.

- Retrospectives change nothing when actions have no owner, date, or review. [judgment]
- The cause is unconfirmed, so a short diagnosis comes first.
- Small, tracked actions are cheap to start and easy to test.
- A changed format is a probe, not a commitment.

## Background

Your team holds retrospectives (regular meetings to review how the team works). People attend, but no changes follow. You ask what to do.

## Relevance

Each retrospective that ends with no change costs team time and lowers trust in the meeting. Attendance can then fall.

## Information

**Evidence**

1. The Scrum Guide says the team addresses the most impactful improvements as soon as possible. It adds that the team can place them in the next Sprint Backlog [1]. The guide is a primary source for this practice.
2. Derby and Larsen describe a retrospective as a sequence that ends with decisions on what to do next [2]. This source is secondary.
3. Most stalled retrospectives share one cause: actions have no owner and no review. [judgment] No data from your team supports this yet.
4. The Cynefin framework separates Clear, Complicated, Complex, Chaotic, and Confused work [3]. It is a primary source. Team behavior is mostly Complex, so format changes need probes.

**Assumption log**

A1. The team works in fixed cycles of one to four weeks. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask the team lead. If wrong: change the review point to the next regular meeting.

A2. The main cause is weak follow-through, not low morale. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: Step 1 below. If wrong: replace Steps 2 and 3 with actions that fit the cause found.

A3. Few or none of the actions from the last four retrospectives were completed. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: count the actions in past notes. If wrong: the baseline in Step 4 changes.

**Alternative considered**

Replace the facilitator or the format now. It loses because it treats a guess as the cause. It also changes many things at once, so you cannot tell what worked. [judgment]

## End

I recommend that we take these steps in order:

1. **Diagnose the cause (Confused: the cause is unknown, so gather facts to classify the work).** Increment: a 10-minute anonymous poll of the team, asking "Why did the last three retrospectives not change anything?" with four fixed choices and one free-text field. Blocker removed: the unconfirmed cause (A2). Kind: evaluative. Rubric, written first: criteria are response rate (weight 40%), one choice named by at least half of the answers (weight 40%), and free-text themes with at least two sources each (weight 20%). Pass threshold: 70% weighted score. Evidence: the poll results. Steps 2 to 4 MUST NOT change if the poll supports A2. If the poll refutes A2, replace Step 2 and Step 3 first.

2. **Limit each retrospective to one action with one owner and one date (Clear: a known practice exists [1]).** Increment: a rule written in the team's working agreement and one tracker entry template. Blocker removed: actions with no owner. Kind: deterministic. Test, written first:

   Given a retrospective has ended
   When the team lists its actions
   Then exactly one action exists, with one named owner and one due date, in the sprint backlog

3. **Open each retrospective with a 5-minute review of the last action (Clear: deduction from the known practice in Step 2).** Increment: one fixed agenda item. Blocker removed: no review of past actions. Kind: deterministic. Test, written first:

   Given a retrospective has started
   When the facilitator opens the agenda
   Then the first item shows the last action as done, not done, or dropped, with a reason

   Step 3 MUST NOT start until the test in Step 2 passes.

4. **Run a probe: change the format for the next four retrospectives (Complex: induction from trials; the pattern could break if the cause is not the format).** Increment: spend the whole session on one problem, chosen by team vote, and use the "5 whys" method (ask "why" five times to reach a root cause). Run this in parallel with Steps 2 and 3. Leverage is a guess. Kind: stochastic. Acceptance test, written first: the metric is the share of actions completed by the next retrospective. The baseline is the count from A3. The pass threshold is at least 3 of 4 actions completed. The sample size is four retrospectives. Budget: 4 sessions, 60 minutes each. Stop condition: stop after two retrospectives if the team rates the format below 3 of 5. Accept the format only when the threshold is met at that sample size.
