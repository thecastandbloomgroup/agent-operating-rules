Stop all outside comment now, name one decision owner, and verify the CEO's statement against records before anyone chooses a public response.

- Conflicting messages from Legal, PR, and the board cause more damage than the article does. [judgment]

## Background

A journalist published a report today (Nov 4, 2026) that says the CEO's statement from yesterday was false. Legal, PR, and the board each give you different advice. You need an ordered plan for the next hours.

## Relevance

Each hour without a single voice lets the story set the frame. Each unplanned statement adds new claims that others can test.

## Information

**Evidence**

- Cause and effect here are partly known and partly not. Document preservation is a known practice. Public reaction is not predictable. [judgment]

## End

I recommend that we take these four actions in this order.

1. **Stabilise the channel.** Hold all external comment and name one decision owner. Hold a 30-minute call with Legal, PR, and the board chair.
   - Increment: one call and one written owner, done in one session.
   - Blocker removed: four conflicting voices.
   - Kind: deterministic. Test, written first:
   ```text
   Given Legal, PR, and the board chair are on one call
   When the call ends
   Then one named owner and one outside-comment hold are recorded in writing
   ```
   - Domain: Chaotic. No cause and effect is clear, so act first and stabilise. [1]

2. **Preserve records and check duties.** Ask Legal to issue a document hold and list any disclosure deadlines.
   - Increment: one hold notice and one deadline list.
   - Blocker removed: loss of evidence and missed legal duties.
   - Kind: deterministic. Test, written first:
   ```text
   Given the article names the CEO's statement
   When Legal issues the hold
   Then every record behind the statement is covered and each deadline has a date
   ```
   - Domain: Clear. A known best practice exists, so apply it. [1]
   - Step 2 MAY run in parallel with Step 3.

3. **Build a fact sheet.** List each claim in the statement and each claim in the article. Mark each as supported, unsupported, or disputed, with the record behind it.
   - Increment: a one-page sheet.
   - Blocker removed: the lack of agreed facts.
   - Kind: evaluative. Rubric, written first: criteria are source quality (weight 50%), completeness of claims (30%), and independent review by Legal (20%). Pass threshold: 80%. Each criterion needs a named record as evidence.
   - Domain: Complicated. Experts can deduce the answer from records.
   - Step 4 MUST NOT start until Step 3 passes its rubric.

4. **Choose the response.** Score three options with a Pugh matrix: correct the statement, defend it, or hold comment until more facts arrive. Use the current plan of "no comment" as the baseline.
   - Increment: one scored matrix and one decision by the owner.
   - Blocker removed: disagreement on what to say.
   - Kind: evaluative. Criteria are accuracy (weight 40%), legal exposure (30%), board support (15%), and trust of staff and customers (15%). Pass threshold: the winning option scores above the baseline on accuracy and legal exposure.
   - Domain: Complicated. Analysis of the Step 3 facts picks the practice.

If A1 is false, because the statement proves accurate, replace Step 4 with a factual rebuttal that cites the Step 3 records.
