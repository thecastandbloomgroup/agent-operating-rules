## Headline

I recommend that we name one incident lead, verify the CEO's statement against records, and speak externally only after that check.

- **Control:** one owner and one channel stop the conflicting messages from legal, PR, and the board.
- **Facts:** a claim-by-claim check shows how much of the article is accurate.
- **Decision:** a scored comparison of response options replaces opinion.
- **Message:** a short, verified holding statement replaces improvised replies.

## Background

A journalist published today that the CEO's statement of November 3, 2026 was false. Legal, PR, and the board each send you separate guidance. No one owns the decision. The request is for the next steps.

## Relevance

Each hour without one owner raises the chance of a second, conflicting public statement. [judgment] A second conflict would add a new story to the first one.

## Information

The situation is not yet stable, so stabilise first and analyse second (Cynefin framework) [1]. Evidence is thin because the material does not state what the CEO said or what the article claims. All driving claims below are [judgment] unless a reference is shown.

Uncertainty: the statement is partly or wholly inaccurate with about as likely as not (33 to 66%) probability. [judgment] The basis is that a journalist published, but the article is unseen.

Assumption log:

A1. The CEO statement is partly or wholly inaccurate. Why not measured: the material does not state the claim. Validate by: compare each claim to dated records in Step 2. If wrong: Step 3 favors defending the statement, and the correction option drops.

A2. You hold authority to name an incident lead. Why not measured: the material does not state your role. Validate by: read the crisis policy, or ask the CEO's chief of staff. If wrong: send Step 1 to the person who holds that authority.

A3. The company has a legal duty to disclose material facts. Why not measured: the material does not state listing or regulator status. Validate by: ask counsel one yes or no question. If wrong: add a disclosure deadline before Step 4.

Alternative considered: issue a fast public denial or apology now. It loses because the facts are unchecked, and a wrong statement is costly to reverse. [judgment]

## End

I recommend that we take these five actions in order. Step 5 runs in parallel with Step 3.

**Step 1. Name one incident lead and one channel.**
- Kind: deterministic.
- Increment: a one-paragraph message, sent today, that names the lead and freezes external statements.
- Blocker removed: three groups give conflicting guidance.
- Cynefin: Chaotic. Act first, because the conflict happens now and no cause is visible yet.
- Test, written first (Gherkin [2]):

```text
Given an article disputes the CEO statement
When any team member drafts an external message
Then the incident lead approves it before release
```

**Step 2. Preserve records and build a fact table.**
- Kind: deterministic.
- Increment: counsel issues a records hold. Then the team lists each claim in the CEO statement beside a dated record.
- Blocker removed: nobody knows which claims hold.
- Cynefin: Complicated. Deduce from records, so the analysis is the compare step itself.
- Test, written first:

```text
Given the CEO statement and the article
When the team compares each claim to a dated record
Then each claim is marked supported, unsupported, or unverifiable, with a record name
```

**Step 3. Choose the response option with a rubric.**
- Step 3 MUST NOT start until the test in Step 2 passes.
- Kind: evaluative.
- Increment: score three options: correct now, defend, or hold and investigate.
- Blocker removed: the groups argue from opinion.
- Cynefin: Complicated. Deduce by analysis, using Kepner-Tregoe decision analysis [3].
- Rubric, written first. Pass threshold: 70 of 100 points, and no criterion at zero.

| Criterion | Weight | Evidence needed |
|---|---|---|
| Factual accuracy | 35 | Step 2 fact table |
| Legal exposure | 25 | Written view from counsel |
| Stakeholder trust | 25 | Input from PR and the board chair |
| Reversibility | 15 | Cost to undo, estimated by the lead |

**Step 4. Release a holding statement.**
- Step 4 MUST NOT start until an option scores at or above the threshold in Step 3.
- Kind: deterministic.
- Increment: one statement of 120 words or fewer, plus a board note.
- Blocker removed: the press and staff wait for a reply.
- Cynefin: Clear. A known best practice applies: state only verified facts. Deduce from that rule.
- Test, written first:

```text
Given the option chosen in Step 3
When counsel and the incident lead review the draft
Then it has 120 words or fewer, states only verified facts, and carries two sign-offs
```

**Step 5. Probe alignment of the three groups.**
- Step 5 MUST NOT start until Step 2 passes.
- Kind: stochastic.
- Increment: show the fact table to legal, PR, and the board chair, one group at a time.
- Blocker removed: no shared view of the facts.
- Cynefin: Complex. Induce from trials. Group politics could break the pattern, and the leverage here is a guess.
- Acceptance test, written first:
  - Metric: groups that name the same next action after reading the table.
  - Baseline: 0 of 3 today.
  - Threshold: 3 of 3.
  - Sample: 3 groups.
  - Budget: 1 hour.
  - Stop condition: any group forwards the table outside the company.
