## Headline

Track retro actions as real work, because the likely cause is that actions are agreed but never finished.

- The first step is to count what happened to past actions, since the cause is not yet measured.
- Next, cut the number of actions and tie each one to the team's normal work.
- Then test a format change as a small, capped probe.
- Last, hand off blockers that the team cannot fix.

## Background

The team holds retrospectives (regular meetings to review how the team works). People attend, but nothing changes. The message gives no data on past actions, team size, or format.

## Relevance

A retro that changes nothing costs team time each cycle and teaches people that speaking up has no effect. Each further empty retro makes the pattern harder to reverse [judgment].

## Information

**Evidence**

- The Scrum Guide treats the most impactful improvements as work for the next Sprint (a fixed work cycle) [1].
- This supports one rule: an improvement is work, so it needs a place in the backlog.

Derby and Larsen split a retro into five phases. The fourth phase is "decide what to do" [2]. Many teams stop after they gather data and skip this phase [judgment].

Cause and effect in team behavior show up only afterward. This is the Complex domain [3]. So format changes are probes, not fixes.

Confidence that the main cause is weak follow-through: likely (66 to 100%). Basis: one source and judgment, no team data yet [judgment].

**Assumption log**

A1. The team runs retros on a fixed cycle and records action items. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask for the last three retro notes. If wrong: Step 1 becomes "start recording actions".

A2. Actions are agreed but not finished, instead of never agreed. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: Step 1 audit. If wrong: replace Step 2 with a fix for idea generation.

A3. The team owns its own process and can change it. Why not measured: the user has not answered. Validate by: check whether any past action failed for lack of authority. If wrong: Step 4 becomes the first step.

**Alternative considered**

The strongest alternative is to pause retros for a month and then restart them. It loses because it removes the feedback channel and does not test the cause. The audit is cheaper and reversible [judgment].

## End

I recommend that we take these four steps in order.

1. **Audit the last three retros (Complicated: the cause is knowable with analysis).**
   - Increment: list every action item with its owner and outcome in one table, in one session.
   - Blocker removed: nobody knows why changes stop. The audit also gives the baseline for Step 3.
   - Task kind: deterministic.
   - Test:
     - Given the notes of the last three retros
     - When the team lists each action item with owner and outcome
     - Then the table shows a completion rate and a reason for each unfinished item

2. **Adopt a one-action rule (Clear: a known good practice exists [1]).**
   - Increment: at the next retro, pick one action with a named owner and a due date. Add it to the team's backlog.
   - Blocker removed: too many actions with no owner.
   - Task kind: deterministic.
   - Test:
     - Given a retro that ends with a chosen action
     - When the retro closes
     - Then the backlog holds exactly one new item with an owner and a due date
   - Step 2 MUST NOT start until the table from Step 1 is shared with the team.

3. **Probe a format change (Complex: team response shows only afterward).**
   - Increment: open each retro with a five-minute review of the last action. Change the main exercise once.
   - Blocker removed: the agenda no longer holds attention.
   - Task kind: stochastic.
   - Test:
     - Metric: share of retro actions finished by their due date.
     - Baseline: the rate from Step 1.
     - Pass threshold: at least 75%.
     - Sample size: four consecutive retros.
   - Budget: two hours of preparation in total. Stop condition: the rate falls below baseline after two retros. Leverage is a guess here.
   - Step 3 MUST NOT start until Step 1 gives the baseline. It MAY run in parallel with Step 2.

4. **Escalate blocked actions (Complicated: the owner of each blocker is knowable).**
   - Increment: from the Step 1 table, pick the unfinished items that need outside help. Send one to a manager with a clear request.
   - Blocker removed: the team cannot fix causes outside its control.
   - Task kind: deterministic.
   - Test:
     - Given an unfinished item that needs outside help
     - When the team sends the request to its manager
     - Then the manager replies with a decision or a named owner within one cycle

If A2 is false, replace Step 2 with a short session that builds ideas for change.
