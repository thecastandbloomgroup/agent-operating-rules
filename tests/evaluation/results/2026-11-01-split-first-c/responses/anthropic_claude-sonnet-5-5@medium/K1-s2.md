## Headline

Restore the cache TTL now, then resize the connection pool within the database limit; the pool cut in this release caused most of the slowdown.

- The pool change explains most of the delay: pool wait grew 687 ms of the 780 ms p95 rise.
- The cache change adds load: it raised the miss rate and so the number of database trips.
- The ORM change is not the cause: query times are flat.

## Background

The API p95 latency rose from 120 ms to 900 ms after release 2026.10.21. The release cut the pool size, cut the cache TTL, and added eager loading. The attached logs, traces, and diff are the evidence.

## Relevance

The error rate is now 0.40%, up from 0.02%. Each pool timeout returns a 503 to a customer after 5 s.

## Information

**Pool (primary cause).** The diff cuts `max_size` from 50 to 8 and `acquire_timeout_s` from 30 to 5.
- Source: release.diff, config/db.yaml.

Pool wait p95 rose from 3 ms to 690 ms. That is 88% of the 780 ms rise in total p95.
- Source: traces.txt, span table.

96% of errors are PoolTimeout. The error rate rose from 0.02% to 0.40%.
- Source: traces.txt, error line.

Pool exhaustion warnings went from none to 1,800 to 2,400 per hour.
- Source: logs.txt, note.

Peak demand was 31 connections before the release. The new cap allows 8 per pod, or 32 for 4 pods, so headroom is near zero [inference].

**Cache (secondary cause).** The TTL fell from 300 s to 60 s. The miss rate rose from 12% to 31%, or 2.6 times more database trips [inference].
- Source: traces.txt, cache miss row.

The cache.get span is unchanged (2 ms). The effect works through added pool demand [judgment].

**ORM (not a cause).** Eager loading adds a query for order lines. That span did not slow: p50 went from 11 ms to 9 ms.
- Source: traces.txt, db.query order_lines row.

Database CPU peaked at 41%, against 38% before. The database is not the bottleneck.
- Source: traces.txt, last line.

Confidence that the pool is the main cause: high, three independent measurements (span table, log warnings, config diff).

**Open point.** Logs show up to 14 waiters on a pod with 8 worker threads. Something else asks for connections [judgment]. Step 3 covers it.

**Assumptions**

A3. The TTL cut has no business reason. Why not measured: no ticket is attached. Validate by: ask the author of the commit. If wrong: keep 60 s and give Step 2 more connections.

**Alternative considered.** Roll back the whole release. It loses because the ORM change is not at fault (flat query times). A full rollback also restores 50 connections, which can break the new database limit.
