## Headline

Fix the link between the retrospective and the work, because retrospectives that end without owned, tracked actions produce no change.

- First, check the past action items to find where change stops.
- Next, shrink each retrospective to one or two actions with an owner and a date.
- Then, review those actions at the start of the next retrospective.
- Last, test one new format as a capped experiment.

## Background

Your team holds retrospectives, and people attend. Nothing changes afterward. The request gives no data on past action items, team size, or format.

## Relevance

Each retrospective without change costs the team an hour of attention per person. Repeated empty meetings also teach people that speaking up has no effect. Therefore attendance and honesty will fall further.

## Information

**Evidence**

- A standard retrospective has five phases: set the stage, gather data, generate insights, decide what to do, and close [1]. The "decide what to do" phase is where change begins. Teams that skip it or leave it vague end with talk only. This reading is [judgment].
- The Scrum Guide says the most impactful improvements are addressed as soon as possible, and can enter the next Sprint Backlog [2]. That is, an improvement needs a place in the real work plan.
- The cause is likely (66 to 100%) weak follow-through on actions rather than a lack of ideas. Confidence is low, because no team data exists yet. This is [judgment].
- Cynefin classes the work as Complicated overall: experts can find the cause by analysis, but the team's response to a change is Complex [3].

**Assumption log**

A1. The team has a recurring retrospective and a shared task board. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask one team member. If wrong: track actions in a shared document in Step 3.

A2. The main cause is weak follow-through, not a lack of ideas. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: the audit in Step 1. If wrong: replace Step 2 with a root-cause session on why ideas do not form.

A3. The team can act on its own items without outside approval. Why not measured: the user has not answered. Validate by: check whether past items waited on another group. If wrong: add an escalation step with a named sponsor.

**Alternative considered**

The strongest alternative is to replace the facilitator or the format now. It loses because it changes a variable before the cause is known. A format change also does not fix missing owners and dates [judgment].

## End

I recommend that we take these steps in order.

1. **Audit the last three to five retrospectives.** Increment: list every action item with its owner, due date, and status, in one working session. Blocker removed: we do not know where change stops. Domain: Clear, because counting past records has a known method. Test (deterministic):
   - Given the notes of the last retrospectives
   - When we list each action with owner, date, and status
   - Then we can state the share of actions done, and every action has a status

2. **Cap the next retrospective at one or two actions, each with an owner and a due date.** Increment: apply the cap at the next meeting. Step 2 MUST NOT start until Step 1 shows the completion share. Blocker removed: too many vague actions. Domain: Complicated, because several good practices exist and the audit picks among them [3]. Test (deterministic):
   - Given a retrospective that ends
   - When we close the "decide what to do" phase
   - Then at most two actions exist, and each has one named owner and one date

3. **Put each action on the task board and review it first in the next retrospective.** Increment: add a five-minute review item to the agenda and create the board cards. Blocker removed: actions live outside the real work plan [2]. Domain: Clear, because the practice is standard. Test (deterministic):
   - Given actions from the last retrospective
   - When the next retrospective opens
   - Then the team states done or not done for each action before any new topic

4. **Run one format change as a capped experiment, in parallel with Steps 2 and 3.** Increment: test a "one problem only" format for three retrospectives. Domain: Complex, because we see the team's response only afterward. Leverage is a guess. Budget: no more than three retrospectives, 60 minutes each. Stop condition: stop after any retrospective that ends with no action. Test (stochastic):
   - Metric: share of actions done within one iteration.
   - Baseline: the share from Step 1.
   - Pass threshold: at least 20 percentage points above baseline, and at least 70% done.
   - Sample size: all actions from three retrospectives, about 3 to 6 items. This sample is small, so treat a pass as a weak signal.

If A2 is false, replace Step 2 with a root-cause session on why ideas do not form.
