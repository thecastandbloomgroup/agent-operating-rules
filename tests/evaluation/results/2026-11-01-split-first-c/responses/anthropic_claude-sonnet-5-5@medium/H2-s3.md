## Headline

Freeze outside comment, verify the facts against records, then decide one response with one owner.

- One person must own the decision, because three groups now give conflicting direction.
- The facts come first, because every option depends on whether the statement was false.
- The board, legal, and PR then approve one response, scored against shared criteria.
- After release, track the effect and adjust.

## Background

A journalist published that the CEO's statement from yesterday was false. Legal, PR, and the board each send you separate guidance. No one has agreed on the facts or on a response.

## Relevance

Today is Sunday, so you have time before the work week starts. Uncorrected claims spread, and a split response from your own side adds to the damage [judgment].

## Information

Evidence:
- Crisis research finds that the best response depends on how much responsibility the public assigns to the organization [4].
- A response chosen before the facts are known risks a second error [judgment].
- The situation has no agreed cause and effect today, so Step 1 treats it as Chaotic (see Section End and [1]).

Assumption log:
- A1. The journalist's claim is true in part or in full. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: compare the statement with source records (Step 2). If wrong: skip the correction and send a rebuttal.
- A2. No legal duty to disclose applies, such as a stock market rule. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask counsel in Step 1. If wrong: legal timing overrides Step 4.
- A3. You hold authority to name a decision owner. Why not measured: the user has not answered. Validate by: confirm with the board chair in Step 1. If wrong: the chair names the owner.

Alternative considered: answer the journalist at once with a denial. It loses because a denial before verification is unsafe if A1 holds, and a second false statement costs more than a delay of hours [judgment].

## End

I recommend that we take these four steps in order.

1. **Freeze and assign an owner (within one hour).** This removes the blocker of conflicting direction. Kind: deterministic. Domain: Chaotic, because no agreed facts or authority exist yet. Test:
   - Given the CEO statement is disputed in public
   - When the board chair names one decision owner and issues a hold on outside comment
   - Then legal, PR, and the board confirm receipt in writing within 60 minutes

2. **Verify the claim against primary records (within four hours).** Step 2 MUST NOT start until the Step 1 test passes. This removes the blocker of unknown facts. Kind: evaluative. Domain: Complicated, because records and expert review can settle it. Rubric, with pass at 80 of 100:
   - Each disputed claim is checked against a primary record (40).
   - Legal reviews the findings (30).
   - One person outside the PR team reviews them (30).
   
   Each criterion needs a cited record.

3. **Choose the response (same day).** Step 3 MUST NOT start until Step 2 passes. This removes the blocker of disagreement. Kind: evaluative. Domain: Complicated, because options can be scored [2]. Options: correct, clarify, or defend. Musts: truthful and approved by counsel. Weighted wants, pass at 70 of 100:
   - Repairs trust (40)
   - Limits legal exposure (30)
   - Speed (30)
   
   Each score needs evidence from Step 2.

4. **Release the response and track it (first 24 hours).** This adds a feedback loop. Kind: stochastic. Domain: Complex, because public reaction shows itself only afterward. This is a capped probe. Budget: two staff hours. Stop condition: a new false claim appears, and then return to Step 3. Test: sample 50 press and social posts at hour 0 and at hour 24. Baseline is the hour 0 share that repeats the false claim. Pass when that share falls by at least 30%.

If A1 is false, replace Step 3 with a factual rebuttal reviewed by counsel.
