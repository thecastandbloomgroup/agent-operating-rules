## Headline

Freeze outward messages under one named owner, verify the facts claim by claim, then pick a response by scored criteria.

- Alignment: one owner and one channel end the conflict among Legal, PR, and the board.
- Facts: a claim-by-claim check shows what is true before anyone commits in public.
- Choice: a scored comparison of three responses replaces opinion.
- Spread: a capped probe tracks how far the story travels.

## Background

A journalist published that the CEO's statement from yesterday was false. Legal, PR, and the board each send you separate advice. No one has confirmed the facts, and no one owns the decision.

## Relevance

Each hour without one owner raises the chance of a second, conflicting statement. A conflicting statement turns one disputed claim into a credibility problem for the whole company [judgment].

## Information

Evidence:
- The facts are unverified. The claim is about as likely as not (33 to 66%) to be fully correct [judgment].
- Crisis response research says the response should match how much responsibility the public assigns to the organization.

  See [1].
- The Cynefin framework says to stabilise first when no cause and effect is visible.

  See [2].
- The three groups give conflicting advice. This is a symptom of missing ownership [inference].

Assumption log:

A1. You can convene Legal, PR, and the board chair today. Why not measured: the material does not state your role. Validate by: ask the board chair for a 30-minute call. If wrong: ask the chair to name a convener, and Step 1 stays the same.

A2. The truth of the CEO's statement is unknown. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: Step 2. If wrong: if the statement is proven true, replace Step 3 with a request for correction.

A3. The company has a disclosure duty to a regulator or exchange. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask Legal one yes or no question. If wrong: remove the disclosure check from Step 2.

Alternative considered: issue a full denial now. It loses because the facts are unverified. A denial later shown false would add a second falsehood to the first [judgment].

## End

I recommend that we take these four steps, in this order.

1. **Freeze and assign one owner** (Chaotic: no cause and effect is visible, and three groups act at once). Increment: within 60 minutes, hold one call with Legal, PR, and the board chair. Name one decision owner. Agree that no outside statement leaves without that owner's sign-off. Blocker removed: the conflicting advice. Kind: deterministic.

   ```text
   Given Legal, PR, and the board chair are on one call
   When the call ends
   Then one named owner is recorded in writing
   And every group confirms the sign-off rule in writing
   ```

2. **Verify the facts claim by claim** (Complicated: the facts are knowable by analysis of records). Step 2 MUST NOT start until the test in Step 1 passes. Increment: in one session, Legal and an internal subject expert list each claim in the article and in the CEO's statement. Each claim gets a document and a status. Include the disclosure check from A3. Blocker removed: no one knows what is true. Kind: deterministic.

   ```text
   Given the article and the CEO's statement are split into single claims
   When each claim is checked against records
   Then each claim has a status of supported, unsupported, or unknown
   And each status cites a named document
   ```

3. **Choose the response by score** (Complicated: the analysis in Step 2 picks the practice). Step 3 MUST NOT start until Step 2 is complete. Increment: score three options in one session: stand by the statement, clarify it, or retract it. Use a Pugh matrix [judgment]. Blocker removed: the choice rests on opinion. Kind: evaluative.
   - Criteria and weights: factual accuracy 40%, legal exposure 25%, stakeholder trust 20%, speed 15%.
   - Scale: 1 to 5 per criterion.
   - Pass threshold: a weighted score of at least 3.5, and no criterion below 2.
   - Evidence: the Step 2 table for accuracy, written advice from Legal for exposure, and a PR note for trust and speed.

4. **Run a capped probe of the spread** (Complex: public reaction is visible only afterward, so leverage is a guess). Run this step in parallel with Steps 2 and 3. Increment: send the journalist a written request for the evidence. Issue a one-line holding statement that says the company is reviewing the claim. Count outlets that repeat the claim. Budget: one PR person, 24 hours. Stop condition: stop at once if a new outlet adds a new allegation, and return to Step 1. Kind: stochastic.
   - Metric: new outlets that repeat the claim per 6-hour window.
   - Baseline: the count at the start of the probe.
   - Pass threshold: no rise above baseline in two consecutive windows.
   - Sample size: four windows.
