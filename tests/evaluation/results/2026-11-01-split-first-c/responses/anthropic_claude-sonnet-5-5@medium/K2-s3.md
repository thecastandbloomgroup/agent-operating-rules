## Headline

I recommend Northwind Analytics, the managed cloud warehouse, because it is the only option that is both within budget and covered by an SLA.

- Northwind costs 372,000, which is 28,000 under the 400,000 budget [1].
- Bluepeak costs 455,000 at current volume, which is 55,000 (13.75%) over budget, with no price cap [1].
- Cedar costs 286,000 but has no SLA, and one engineer would run it [1].
- Northwind has an exit path: Parquet export with no fee [1].

## Background

Three vendors proposed a data warehouse. The budget is 400,000 over three years. Current data is 14 TB, growing about 3 TB per year, and all vendor figures are self-reported [1].

## Relevance

The contract locks in cost and risk for three years. Two costs are missing from every proposal: staff time to run the system and the price after year 3 [1].

All values come from [1].

- Northwind data forecast: 14 TB grows to 17, 20, and 23 TB at year ends. The licence covers 20 TB. If billed on the year-3 peak, the overage is 3 TB × 9,000 = 27,000. The total is then 399,000, only 1,000 under budget [1].
- Bluepeak cost is uncapped. Storage growth adds to the 455,000 estimate. Egress on exit at 0.08 per GB adds about 1,120 for 14 TB (decimal units) [1].
- Cedar has 114,000 of headroom (400,000 minus 286,000). A second database engineer plausibly costs more than that over three years [judgment].
- Cedar hardware fits 30 TB, which covers the 23 TB forecast [1].
- All benchmarks ran on 10 TB samples, not on our 14 TB. Confidence in the query times is low: three vendor-run tests, none independent.
- Confidence that Northwind stays within budget: 70% likely [judgment]. The main risk is growth above 3 TB per year.

**Assumption log**

A1. Growth stays near 3 TB per year. Why not measured: the material gives only a team estimate. Validate by: read storage logs for the last 12 months. If wrong: Northwind overage rises, and each extra TB at year 3 costs 9,000.

A2. A 3-second median on the 12 reference queries is acceptable. Why not measured: the material does not state a latency need. Validate by: ask the main report users. If wrong: Bluepeak moves up despite its cost.

A3. Business-hours support is acceptable. Why not measured: the material does not state uptime needs. Validate by: ask which reports run overnight. If wrong: the 4-hour, business-hours support of Northwind fails, and the answer moves toward Bluepeak.

**Alternative considered**

Cedar is the strongest alternative. It is cheapest by 86,000 against Northwind. It loses because it has no SLA and unpriced staff cost, and one engineer is a single point of failure [judgment].
