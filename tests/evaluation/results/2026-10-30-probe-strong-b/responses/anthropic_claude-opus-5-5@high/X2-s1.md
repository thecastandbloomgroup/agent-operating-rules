## Headline

Find the stage where your retrospectives break, then make each action small, owned, and tracked in the team's work.

- First, audit the last six retrospectives to see whether the team fails to raise problems, decide actions, or do them.
- Next, limit each retrospective to one owned action in the backlog, and review it first at the next meeting.
- Send each action that is outside the team's authority to a named manager with a reply date.
- In parallel, test anonymous input for three meetings to see whether people raise new problems.

## Background

A retrospective is a regular team meeting where the team reviews how work went and picks improvements. Your team attends these meetings, but the meetings no longer produce changes. You asked what to do.

## Relevance

Under A1 and A4, the meeting costs about 156 person-hours per year with no return [inference]. Each meeting without change also teaches people that raising a problem has no effect [judgment].

## Information

This section covers the failure stages, the evidence, the assumptions, and the alternative.

**Failure stages.** A retrospective produces change only when three stages all work. The stages do not overlap, and together they cover the path from problem to change [judgment].

| Stage | What must happen | Failure sign |
|---|---|---|
| Raise | People name the actual problems. | The same safe topics repeat. |
| Decide | The team picks a small action that it has authority to do. | Actions are vague, large, or outside team control. |
| Do | A person completes the action before the next retrospective. | Actions stay in notes, and nobody reviews them. |

**Theme 1: a change needs a concrete action that enters the team's work.** Two sources support it. No source in this set disagrees.

- The Scrum Guide says the team can add top improvements to the next Sprint Backlog, the ordered list of planned work:
  [1]
- Derby and Larsen give the meeting a separate stage, "Decide What to Do":
  [2]

**Theme 2: people must feel safe to raise problems.** Psychological safety is a shared belief that the team will not punish a person for speaking up. Two sources support the theme. No source in this set disagrees.

- Edmondson found that psychological safety in teams links to learning behavior:
  [3]
- Kerth's Prime Directive asks the team to agree that everyone did their best, so that blame drops:
  [4]

All four sources come from practice authors or one study, so the absence of a disagreeing source is a gap, not proof [judgment].

**Analysis.** The symptom "people attend but nothing shifts" fits the Do stage best. Attendance shows that the meeting runs. However, the lack of change shows that actions do not reach the work. A Do stage failure is likely (66 to 100%) the main cause [judgment]. A Raise stage failure is about as likely as not (33 to 66%) a second cause [judgment]. The basis is low confidence: one sentence of description and no data. Therefore Step 1 replaces this estimate with counts.

**Assumption log.**

A1. The team holds a one-hour retrospective every two weeks. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: check the team calendar. If wrong: the cost in Relevance changes, and the steps stay the same.

A2. A written record of past action items exists. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: search the team board or wiki for the last six retrospectives. If wrong: replace Step 1 (see Follow-up).

A3. The team has authority over part of its own process. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask the manager which changes need approval. If wrong: Step 3 moves before Step 2.

A4. The team has about six members. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: count the attendee list. If wrong: only the cost in Relevance changes.

**Alternative considered.** Change the meeting format or the facilitator. This alternative loses because a new format acts only on the Raise stage. If the Do stage fails, new topics still produce no change [judgment].

## End

I recommend that we run four steps. A Cynefin domain describes how cause and effect relate in a piece of work:
[5]

A Gherkin test states a check in Given, When, Then form:
[6]

1. **Audit the last six retrospectives.**
   - Increment: one 60-minute session for the facilitator.
   - Blocker removed: nobody knows which stage fails.
   - Kind: evaluative. Write this rubric before the audit. Score each stage 0 (fails), 1 (partial), or 2 (works). The weights are equal, because no data favors one stage [judgment]. The thresholds are [judgment].

     | Criterion | Weight | Evidence | Score 2 | Score 1 | Score 0 |
     |---|---|---|---|---|---|
     | Raise | 1 | Topics that repeat in 3 or more of 6 meetings | None | One | Two or more |
     | Decide | 1 | Share of meetings that end with an owned, dated action inside team authority | 80% or more | 50 to 79% | Below 50% |
     | Do | 1 | Share of actions done by the next meeting | 80% or more | 50 to 79% | Below 50% |

     Pass threshold: each stage has a score from the listed evidence, and the audit names the lowest stage.
   - Domain: Confused. The cause is not yet known, so this step gathers what is needed to classify the rest.

2. **Limit the next retrospective to one owned action.** Step 2 MUST NOT start until Step 1 names the lowest stage.
   - Increment: the last 10 minutes of the next meeting.
   - Blocker removed: actions stay in notes that nobody reads.
   - Kind: deterministic. Put this test into the meeting template before the meeting:

     ```gherkin
     Given the retrospective has chosen one action
     When the meeting ends
     Then the backlog holds one item with one owner and one due date
     And the next retrospective agenda starts with a review of that item
     ```
   - Domain: Clear. Putting the action into the backlog is a known practice [1]. This step has the highest leverage of the Clear and Complicated steps [judgment].

3. **Send actions that are outside team authority to a named manager.** Step 3 MUST NOT start until Step 1 lists which open actions are outside team authority.
   - Increment: one 30-minute session for the team lead.
   - Blocker removed: the team picks actions that it cannot complete.
   - Kind: deterministic. Write this test first:

     ```gherkin
     Given an open action that needs approval outside the team
     When the team lead sends it to the manager
     Then the backlog item names the manager and a reply date
     And the next retrospective reviews the reply
     ```
   - Domain: Complicated. The Decide score from Step 1 is the analysis that picks this practice.

4. **Probe anonymous input for three meetings.** Step 4 MUST NOT start until Step 1 gives the baseline. Run it in parallel with Steps 2 and 3.
   - Increment: 10 minutes at the start of each of the next three meetings. Open with an anonymous 1 to 5 safety vote [2]. Then each person writes problems on anonymous cards before the discussion starts.
   - Capability added: the team sees whether people hold back problems.
   - Kind: stochastic. Write this acceptance test first. Metric: new topics per meeting, that is, topics absent from the Step 1 audit. Baseline: mean new topics per meeting in the last six meetings. Pass threshold: the mean rises by at least one topic. Sample size: three meetings.
   - Domain: Complex. This step is a safe-to-fail probe, that is, a small capped trial. Budget: 30 minutes in total. Stop condition: stop after three meetings, or stop at once if two or more members object. Its leverage is a guess.

## Follow-up

**Conditions that change the recommendation.**

- If A2 is false, replace Step 1 with a 15-minute team round. In the round, each person names the last change they remember from a retrospective.
- If Step 1 shows that Raise scores lowest, start Step 4 at the next meeting and keep Step 2.
- If A3 is false, move Step 3 before Step 2.
