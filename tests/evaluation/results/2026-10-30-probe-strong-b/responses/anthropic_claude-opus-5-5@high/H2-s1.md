Stabilize first: name one person who decides and one spokesperson, then check the facts before the company says more.

1. Within one hour, one call with Legal, public relations (PR), and the board chair names an incident lead and a spokesperson.

3. By the end of today, a reviewer outside the chief executive officer's (CEO's) office checks each claim against company records.

## Background

A journalist reports that a public statement by your CEO on Thursday 29 October 2026 was false. Legal, PR, and the board send you separate messages, and the messages conflict. You need the next steps.

## Relevance

Separate messages from three groups create a risk of a second public statement that conflicts with the first [judgment]. Today is Friday. One working day remains before the weekend and before Monday's market open (A3).

## Information

**Evidence**

The material contains no numbers, so no comparison with a baseline is possible yet. Step 5 creates the first baseline.

The situation is Chaotic in Cynefin terms. Cynefin is a model that sorts work by how cause and effect relate. The groups give conflicting direction, and nobody has confirmed the facts [judgment].

In a Chaotic domain, the leader acts first to set order, then senses, then responds [1].

Coombs links the best crisis response to how much responsibility the public gives the organization [2].

When that responsibility is high, Coombs recommends apology and corrective action, not denial [2].

In an experiment, an organization that disclosed its own crisis earned higher credibility than one exposed by a third party [3].

The journalist disclosed first here. Therefore the company gains most if it releases each new fact before others do [inference].

The reviewer in Step 3 must be outside the CEO's office. That office has a direct interest in the result, so its check would not convince the board or the journalist [judgment].

The table shows the main priority of each group. The material does not include their messages, so the table is judgment, not evidence (see A4).

| Group | Main priority [judgment] | Risk if the group acts alone [judgment] |
|---|---|---|
| Legal | Limit legal exposure | A reply too thin to answer the report |
| PR | Respond fast | A correction before the facts are known |

The board's view has only one source. However, it matters, because the board oversees the CEO whose statement is in question [judgment].

Confidence in Steps 1 and 2 is high. Basis: both steps hold whether the statement was true or false [judgment]. Confidence in the Step 4 choice is low now. Basis: the choice depends on Step 3 results.

## End

I recommend that we take these five actions in this order.

**Step 1. Name one incident lead and one spokesperson.**

- Increment: hold one 30-minute call within the next hour. Invite General Counsel, the PR lead, the board chair, and you.
- The call names one incident lead, one spokesperson, and the time of the next call.
- The incident lead is the one person who decides for the company until the issue closes.
- If the CEO's own conduct is in question, the board chair names the incident lead, not the CEO [judgment].
- Blocker removed: three groups that give conflicting direction.
- Kind: deterministic. Test, written before the call:

```gherkin
Given the Step 1 call has ended
When Legal, PR, or a director receives a question from the media
Then that person sends the question to the spokesperson within 15 minutes
And the written call record names the incident lead, the spokesperson, and the next call time
```

- Cynefin domain: Chaotic. It comes first because it sets order.

**Step 2. Release a holding statement.**

Step 2 MUST NOT start until Step 1 names the spokesperson.

- Increment: within two hours, PR writes a holding statement and a short note for employees. Legal approves both texts.
- A holding statement is a short public statement. It confirms that the company knows of the report and states when the company will say more.
- Blocker removed: silence that lets the report stand alone, and leaks from staff who do not know the agreed text.
- Kind: deterministic. Test, written before the draft:

```gherkin
Given a draft holding statement
When General Counsel and the PR lead review it
Then it makes no claim that the CEO's statement was true or false
And it states the time of the next update
And both reviewers approve it in writing before release
```

- Cynefin domain: Chaotic.

**Step 3. Check each claim in the CEO's statement.**

- Increment: one working session that ends today. A reviewer who does not report to the CEO splits the statement into separate claims. This reviewer is, for example, General Counsel or outside counsel who reports to the board.
- The reviewer scores each claim against company records. The reviewer also records what the CEO knew when the CEO spoke, because Step 4 needs it.
- Blocker removed: the company does not know if the report is correct (A2).
- Kind: evaluative. Rubric, written before the check. Score each claim 0, 1, or 2 on each criterion.

| Criterion | Weight | Evidence needed |
|---|---|---|
| Matches company records | 50% | A primary record, for example a contract, a data set, or a filing |
| Not misleading in context | 30% | A written comparison with the full facts |
| Evidence is independent of the CEO's office | 20% | Source of each record |

- Pass threshold: a claim stands only if its weighted score is at least 1.6 of 2. It also needs a score of 2 on "Matches company records".
- Cynefin domain: Complicated. This check is the analysis that picks the practice in Step 4.

**Step 4. Choose and release the public response.**

Step 4 MUST NOT start until Step 3 scores every claim.

- Increment: one session after Step 3. The incident lead compares three options: correct and apologize, clarify, or defend with evidence.
- The incident lead uses a Kepner-Tregoe decision analysis [4].
- If any claim fails Step 3, the default option is to correct the statement and apologize [2].
- Blocker removed: the company has no agreed public position.
- Kind: evaluative. Rubric, written before the comparison:

| Criterion | Type | Weight | Evidence needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Agrees with every Step 3 score | Must | Pass or fail | Step 3 rubric |
| General Counsel approves | Must | Pass or fail | Written approval |
| Board chair approves | Must | Pass or fail | Written approval |
| Meets any duty to disclose (A3) | Must | Pass or fail | Note from General Counsel |
| Restores trust of customers and investors | Want | 40% | PR assessment [judgment] |
| Ready by the time the holding statement gave | Want | 30% | Draft and approval times |
| Limits legal exposure | Want | 30% | Note from General Counsel |

- Pass threshold: the option passes every Must. It also has the highest weighted Want score, and that score is at least 7 of 10.
- Cynefin domain: Complicated.

**Step 5. Measure how the coverage changes.**

- Increment: start now, in parallel with Steps 3 and 4. One PR analyst keeps a log of new articles and posts about the report.
- Capability added: evidence of whether the response works, not a guess.
- Kind: stochastic. Acceptance test, written before the log starts:
  - Metric: share of new items that include any company statement.
  - Baseline: that share in the 24 hours before Step 4 releases the response.
  - Pass threshold: at least 50% of new items in the 48 hours after release [judgment].
  - Sample size: at least 30 new items.
  - Budget: two hours of one analyst per day, for three days.
  - Stop condition: the share is below 25% at 48 hours. Then the analyst stops the log. The incident lead calls the Step 1 group again.
- Cynefin domain: Complex. This step is a capped probe. Its leverage is a guess [judgment].
