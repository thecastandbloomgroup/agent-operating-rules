## Headline

Restore the database connection pool to the largest size the new connection limit allows, because the cut from 50 to 8 connections causes most of the slowdown.

- Pool wait explains 88% of the p95 rise: `db.pool.acquire` p95 went from 3 ms to 690 ms, in a total rise of 780 ms [1].
- Query work and load did not change: `db.query orders` p95 went from 52 ms to 58 ms.
- The cache TTL cut adds a smaller load on the pool: the miss rate rose from 12% to 31% [1].
- The ORM change shows no cost: `db.query order_lines` p95 fell from 30 ms to 26 ms [1].

## Background

Release 2026.10.21 changed the ORM layer, the cache config, and the connection pool. The ORM is the code layer that builds database queries. After the release, p95 latency (the time under which 95% of requests finish) rose from 120 ms to 900 ms. The request is to decide what to do.

## Relevance

The error rate rose from 0.02% to 0.40% [1]. Of those errors, 96% are pool timeouts, so users now see failed requests as well as slow ones.

## Information

Evidence, in three themes:

1. The pool is the bottleneck.
   - `release.diff` cut `max_size` from 50 to 8 and `acquire_timeout_s` from 30 to 5 [2].
   - Active connections from this service peaked at 31 before the release and at 8 after it [1].
   - Pool exhaustion warnings went from none to 1,800 to 2,400 per hour [3].
   - Median pool wait went from 1 ms to 140 ms, which is 86% of the 162 ms median rise [1].
2. The database and the app are not the bottleneck.
   - Database CPU peaked at 38% before and 41% after [1].
   - Peak load was 410 requests per second before and 405 after [1].
   - `cache.get` p95 was 4 ms before and 5 ms after [1].
3. The cache change adds pressure.
   - The TTL (time to live of a cache entry) fell from 300 s to 60 s [2].
   - More misses send more queries to a pool that is too small. The size of this effect is not measured.

Demand estimate [inference]: the median connection hold time is about 29 ms (20 ms plus 9 ms). At 405 requests per second, that is about 12 busy connections on average. The pool offers 8.

Disagreeing source: the commit message says the limit follows a database connection limit [2]. So the database side may cap the fix. No source disputes the pool as the cause.

Confidence that the pool is the main cause: high confidence, three independent measurements (trace spans, error mix, and connection counts).

Confidence in the cache effect: about as likely as not to be a major share. The traces give no split.

Scope gap: the logs show "8 of 8" for each pod [3]. The trace shows 8 active connections for the service [1]. The two figures differ in scope, and the fix is the same.

Assumption log:

A1. The limit in OPS-412 allows at least 32 connections for this service. Why not measured: the material does not state the limit. Validate by: read OPS-412. If wrong: replace Step 2 with a smaller pool plus shorter connection hold time, and ask for a higher quota.

A2. The 60 s TTL has no business reason, such as a data freshness rule. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask the author of the cache change. If wrong: drop Step 4.

Alternative considered: roll back the whole release. It loses because the old pool of 50 may break the new database limit, and the rollback also removes the ORM change, which shows no cost [judgment].
