**Check the last change first, then page the right owner while you stabilize.** Most checkout 500 storms trace to a deploy, config change, or a downstream dependency, in that order. Here is your ten-minute plan:

1. **Stabilize now (minutes 0-2):** If a deploy or config change went out near 09:12, roll it back. Rollback is faster than diagnosis and is almost always safe for stateless services.
2. **Check scope (minute 2-3):** Confirm whether it's checkout only or broader (health checks, other endpoints). Checkout-only points to your payment/checkout path or its dependencies.
3. **Check dependencies (minutes 3-6):** Look at errors in logs for the failing endpoint. Timeout/refused connections to a payment gateway, database connection-pool exhaustion, or a token service down are the usual culprits. Check the third-party's status page too.
4. **Get metrics on the exec call slide (minutes 6-8):** Error rate, which services are failing, and what you've rolled back or tried. Execs want: known scope, action taken, next checkpoint time.
5. **Escalate in parallel:** If rollback didn't help and the failing dependency isn't yours, page that vendor or owning team now rather than after the call.

On the exec call, lead with: scope, action taken, next update time (give a specific minute). If you found nothing conclusive by minutes 8-10, say so plainly and commit to a checkpoint; that's a stronger position than a guess.

Send your first update to stakeholders before dropping off to debug again. Good luck.
