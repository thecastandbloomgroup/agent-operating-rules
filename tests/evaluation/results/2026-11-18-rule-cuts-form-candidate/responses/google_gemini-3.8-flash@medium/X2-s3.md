## Headline

Limit each retrospective to one measurable experiment, place it on the delivery board, and review it first in the next session.
First, teams generate too many action items and complete none of them [judgment].
Second, unseen action items lose attention against customer delivery tasks [judgment].
Third, unverified experiments teach the team nothing about their process [judgment].

## Background

The team attends retrospectives regularly.
However, these meetings fail to create actual changes in workflow or behavior.
The user requests a plan to restore team improvement.

## Relevance

Stalled retrospectives waste team time and erode trust in the process.
Therefore, the team must fix accountability now before members disengage.

## Information

This section covers the causes of retrospective failure and the supporting evidence.
Retrospectives fail most often because teams create long lists of vague goals [1].
Derby and Larsen show that successful retrospectives produce one or two specific experiments that the team can test immediately [1].
Furthermore, teams forget actions when they store them outside their primary task tracker [2].
Schwaber and Sutherland require teams to place at least one high-priority process improvement item in the Sprint Backlog [2].
When teams review the previous experiment first, follow-through increases because accountability is explicit [judgment].
It is likely (66 to 100% probability) that the team suffers from unassigned, unmonitored action items rather than a lack of ideas.

Assumption: The team uses a visible task board for daily work. Why not measured: The material does not state it. Validate by: Inspect the team workspace. If wrong: Create a physical or digital board before adding process tasks.

Alternative considered: Cancel retrospectives for two months and run an anonymous survey. This option loses because it stops team communication without fixing follow-through [judgment].

## End

I recommend that we:

1. Step 1: Audit the past three retrospective logs to find incomplete action items.
   - Test (evaluative, Kepner-Tregoe analysis):
     - Criterion 1: Action items identified with an assignee and a deadline (weight: 0.6, evidence: meeting notes).
     - Criterion 2: Status verified as completed or abandoned (weight: 0.4, evidence: delivery records).
     - Pass threshold: 100% of past items scored against these two criteria.
   - Increment: One thirty-minute review of recent meeting notes.
   - Blocker removed: It removes ignorance about why earlier initiatives stopped.
   - Cynefin domain: Clear. Deduction from documented team records decides the status of each item.

2. Step 2: Establish a rule that restricts the team to exactly one improvement experiment per retrospective.
   - Dependency: Step 2 MUST NOT start until the audit in Step 1 finishes.
   - Test (deterministic, Gherkin):
     Given a completed retrospective session
     When the facilitator compiles the output
     Then the output contains exactly one improvement experiment with an owner and a metric.
   - Increment: One team agreement documented in the team charter.
   - Blocker removed: It removes focus fragmentation from competing action items.
   - Cynefin domain: Complicated. Analysis of team capacity shows that one item fits within current sprint work.

3. Step 3: Insert the single improvement experiment into the active delivery backlog as a top-priority task.
   - Dependency: Step 3 MUST NOT start until the team writes the experiment in Step 2.
   - Test (deterministic, Gherkin):
     Given an active delivery board at sprint start
     When the team reviews the sprint backlog
     Then the improvement experiment card sits in the top column position.
   - Increment: One ticket created and positioned on the delivery board.
   - Capability added: It gives process change equal visibility with customer work.
   - Cynefin domain: Clear. Deduction from the standard operating rule puts the card into the backlog.

4. Step 4: Run the experiment as a safe-to-fail trial during the next iteration.
   - Dependency: Step 4 MUST NOT start until Step 3 places the card on the board.
   - Test (stochastic):
     - Metric: Task completion rate for the experiment.
     - Baseline: 0% completion over the past three cycles.
     - Pass threshold: 100% of planned trial actions completed.
     - Sample size: 1 sprint cycle.
   - Increment: One sprint cycle of testing the selected process change.
   - Capability added: It tests whether the process change improves delivery.
   - Cynefin domain: Complex. Cause and effect appear only after trial; therefore, this probe has a budget of one cycle and a stop condition if work disrupts delivery.
