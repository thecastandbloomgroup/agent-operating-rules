### Headline

Limit retrospective output to one owner-assigned action item and track it on the team sprint board [judgment].
First, excessive action items dilute focus and cause execution failure [1].
Second, unassigned or untracked actions create accountability voids [judgment].
Third, public tracking of small process experiments restores team agency [2].

### Background

Your team attends regular retrospectives, but the discussions fail to produce changes in work practices.
You requested an action plan to restore the effectiveness of these sessions.

### Relevance

Unproductive meetings consume team hours and lower morale [judgment].
Fixing this execution gap prevents cynicism and improves team performance.

### Information

Retrospective failure often stems from generating too many action items without explicit follow-through mechanisms [1].
A study of agile teams found that teams completing one improvement item per iteration improve delivery speed over teams that track multiple open items [1].
Therefore, limiting work in process applies to process changes just as it applies to software tasks [3].
In addition, actions must sit on the primary delivery board rather than in separate meeting notes [judgment].
When improvement tasks share space with daily work, teams complete them more reliably [judgment].

Assumption: The team possesses the authority to change its internal working agreements. Why not measured: The material does not state it. Validate by: Ask the team manager during the next planning session. If wrong: Escalate blocked process changes to department leadership.

Alternative considered:
Hire an external agile coach to facilitate retrospectives.
This option loses because it incurs service costs before the team tests simple internal process controls [judgment].

1. Define a strict one-item policy for retrospective commitments.
