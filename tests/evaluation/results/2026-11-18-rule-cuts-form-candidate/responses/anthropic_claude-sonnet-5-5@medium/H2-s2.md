## Headline

Verify the facts first, hold all public statements until then, and choose one response path with one owner.

- Legal, PR, and the board disagree because nobody has agreed on what is true yet.
- A fast, joint fact check settles most of the disagreement.
- One decision owner and one channel stop the conflicting messages.
- The response (correct, clarify, or defend) follows the facts, not the loudest team.

## Background

A journalist published that the CEO's statement from yesterday (Tuesday, November 17, 2026) was false. Legal, PR, and the board each send you separate guidance. You need a sequence of next steps that aligns them.

## Relevance

Coverage grows with each hour that the company gives no answer. Conflicting messages from inside the company also raise the risk of a second, worse error.

## Information

**Evidence**

- Crisis research holds that the response should match the evidence of fault, and that a denial that later fails costs more trust than an early correction [1].
- The three teams use different goals: legal limits liability, PR protects reputation, the board protects the company. This is why they conflict [judgment].
- A claim-by-claim fact sheet is the cheapest shared base. It uses documents the company already holds [judgment].

**Assumption log**

- Assumption: The statement is only partly wrong, or the article is partly wrong. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: compare each claim with source records (Step 1). If wrong: if the statement is fully false, skip the "defend" option in Step 3. If the article is fully false, a legal demand for correction becomes the lead option.
- Assumption: No one has yet responded publicly to the article. Why not measured: the material does not say. Validate by: search company accounts and spokesperson logs. If wrong: add the existing response to the fact sheet in Step 1.
- Assumption: The company has no legal duty to disclose (for example, as a listed company). Why not measured: the material does not state the company type. Validate by: ask legal counsel for a yes or no. If wrong: disclosure counsel takes the lead in Step 3 and sets the timing.

**Alternative considered**

Defend the CEO's statement at once and demand a retraction from the journalist. It loses because it commits the company before the facts are checked. If the statement is wrong, the defense adds a second false statement [judgment].

## End

I recommend that we take these four actions, in this order:

1. **Confused: build a claim-by-claim fact sheet within two hours.**
   - Increment: one table that lists each claim in the CEO statement and in the article, with the source record and a verdict (true, false, or unverified).
   - Blocker removed: no shared view of the facts.
   - Domain: Confused, because we cannot yet tell if the statement was false. This step gathers what we need to classify the situation.
   - Kind: evaluative. Rubric first: criteria are source quality (weight 50%), claim coverage (30%), and reviewer agreement among legal, PR, and one executive (20%). Pass threshold: 80% weighted score, and every claim has a verdict. Evidence per criterion: a document, email, or data record for each claim.

2. **Clear: in parallel with Step 1, name one decision owner and freeze outside messages.**
   - Increment: a one-page note that names the owner, the single channel for all three teams, and a legal hold on related records.
   - Blocker removed: conflicting guidance and the risk of deleted records.
   - Domain: Clear, because a standard best practice applies (deduction from a known rule).
   - Kind: deterministic. Test:
     - Given the note is sent to legal, PR, and the board
     - When any team wants to issue a public statement
     - Then the decision owner approves it first and the log records it

3. **Complicated: pick the response with a Pugh matrix.**
   - Step 3 MUST NOT start until Step 1 passes its threshold.
   - Increment: one matrix that scores three options (correct, clarify, defend) against a baseline of "say nothing".
   - Blocker removed: the disagreement over what to say.
   - Domain: Complicated. Analysis of known facts picks the option (deduction from the fact sheet).
   - Kind: evaluative. Rubric first: criteria are factual accuracy (40%), legal exposure (30%), trust with readers (20%), and speed (10%). Pass threshold: the winning option beats the baseline on accuracy and scores at least 70%. Each score cites a row of the fact sheet or a legal opinion. Legal, PR, and the board each score separately, and the owner sets the final weights.

4. **Complex: run a 24-hour probe on the chosen response.**
   - Step 4 MUST NOT start until Step 3 selects an option and the decision owner approves it.
   - Increment: publish the response on one owned channel and send it to the journalist. Budget: two PR staff for one working day.
   - Blocker removed: no data on how the market reacts. Leverage is a guess.
   - Domain: Complex, because reaction is visible only afterward (induction from trials). The pattern can break if a larger outlet or a regulator joins the story.
   - Kind: stochastic. Acceptance test first: metric is the count of outlets that repeat the disputed claim. Baseline: the count at publication of the response. Threshold: no more than 2 times the baseline after 24 hours. Sample: the 20 outlets on the monitoring list. Stop condition: if the count passes the threshold, escalate to the board and return to Step 3.

If the company has a disclosure duty (see the assumption log), replace Step 4 with a counsel-led disclosure plan.
