## Headline

Change the retrospective from a talking session into a short cycle that ends in one or two owned, dated actions, and measure whether they get done.

- The most likely cause is that actions have no owner, no date, or no place in daily work [judgment].
- Nobody has checked the past actions, so the team has no baseline.
- A small, measured trial of a new format tests the fix at low cost.
- Some blockers sit outside the team, so those need a separate route.

## Background

The team holds retrospectives (regular meetings to review how the work went). People attend, but nothing changes afterward. The message gives no data on format, team size, or past actions.

## Relevance

Each retrospective that ends with no change costs the team meeting time and lowers trust in the meeting. Trust falls with each repeat, so the problem grows.

## Information

**Evidence**

- A standard retrospective has five phases: set the stage, gather data, generate insights, decide what to do, and close [1]. Teams that skip or rush "decide what to do" lose the link between talk and action [judgment].
- It is likely (66 to 100%) that the stall comes from weak follow-through rather than weak discussion [judgment]. The basis is that attendance is high and only change is missing.
- No tertiary source supports a driving claim here.

**Assumption log**

Assumption: The team runs retrospectives on a fixed cadence of two to four weeks. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: check the calendar. If wrong: change the trial length in Step 3.

Assumption: Past actions were written down somewhere. Why not measured: no tool can reach the team's notes. Validate by: Step 1. If wrong: Step 1 becomes "start a log" and the baseline is zero.

Assumption: The team can change its own process without outside approval. Why not measured: the user has not answered. Validate by: ask the team lead. If wrong: add the manager to Step 4.

**Alternative considered**

Replace the retrospective with a different meeting, such as a monthly review with a manager. It loses because it removes the one place where the team owns its own process, and the cause is not yet shown to be the format [judgment].

## End

I recommend that we take these four actions in order:

1. **Audit past actions.**
   - Increment: list every action from the last three retrospectives, with owner, date, and status. This takes one session.
   - Blocker removed: no baseline exists.
   - Kind: deterministic. Test, written first:
     - Given the notes of the last three retrospectives
     - When we list every action item
     - Then each item shows an owner, a due date, and a status, and we know the completed share
   - Cynefin: Clear. A known method applies, which is to count and record, by deduction.

2. **Set a "two actions" rule.**
   - Increment: end each retrospective with at most two actions. Each has one named owner and a due date. Put them in the team's normal work backlog.
   - Blocker removed: actions sit in meeting notes, away from daily work.
   - Kind: deterministic. Test, written first:
     - Given a retrospective has ended
     - When we open the work backlog
     - Then it holds at most two new actions, each with an owner and a due date
   - Cynefin: Complicated. Good practice exists, and the audit in Step 1 picks the limit by analysis [1].
   - Step 2 MUST NOT start until Step 1 is done.

3. **Run a three-retrospective trial.**
   - Increment: open each retrospective by reviewing the last actions for 10 minutes.
   - Blocker removed: no feedback loop shows whether change happens.
   - Kind: stochastic. Acceptance test, written first:
     - Metric: share of actions completed by the due date.
     - Baseline: the Step 1 result.
     - Threshold: at least 70%, and above the baseline.
     - Sample size: about six actions across three retrospectives. This is small, so treat a pass as a signal, not proof.
   - Budget: 10 minutes per meeting. Stop condition: if the share stays at or below the baseline after two retrospectives, move to Step 4 early.
   - Cynefin: Complex. Team behavior shows cause only afterward, so this is a safe-to-fail probe by induction. The pattern breaks if the team's workload changes sharply. Leverage here is a guess.
   - Step 3 MUST NOT start until the test in Step 2 passes.

4. **Review blockers outside the team.**
   - Increment: sort every action that failed in Step 3 into "team can fix" or "needs someone else". Send the second group to the team lead.
   - Blocker removed: some actions cannot finish without outside help.
   - Kind: evaluative. Rubric, written first:
     - Criteria: team control (weight 50%), effort (30%), impact (20%).
     - Evidence: the failed action, its stated reason, and an effort estimate in hours.
     - Pass threshold: an item scoring below 60% goes to the team lead.
   - Cynefin: Complicated. Analysis of each failure picks the route, by deduction.
   - Step 4 MUST NOT start until Step 3 ends or its stop condition fires.

If the audit in Step 1 shows that actions were done but the problems stayed, replace Step 2 with a review of how the team picks actions.
