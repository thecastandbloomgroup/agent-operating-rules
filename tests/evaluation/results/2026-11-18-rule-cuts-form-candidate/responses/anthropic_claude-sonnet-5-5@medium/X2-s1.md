## Headline

I recommend that we treat this as a follow-through failure first, and test that before we change the meeting format.

- Retrospectives (recurring meetings where a team reviews its work and picks changes) only matter if the changes happen [1].
- The cause is unknown, so a short audit of past action items comes first.
- A limit of one owned change per retrospective is cheap and fixes the most common failure.
- A format change is a trial, not a commitment, because its effect is not predictable.

## Background

The team holds retrospectives and people attend. The meetings produce no visible change. The request is for a way to fix this.

## Relevance

Each retrospective that ends without a change costs the team an hour per person. It also teaches people that speaking up has no effect. Attendance then falls.

## Information

Evidence:

- The Agile principles state that a team reflects at regular intervals, then adjusts its behavior [1]. A retrospective with no adjustment breaks that loop.
- Practitioner guidance says a retrospective must end with a small set of actions that people own [2]. This is a book by practitioners, so it is secondary evidence.
- The main cause is lack of follow-through, not poor topics. This is 60% likely [judgment].
- Other causes are no authority to act, no time to act, and low trust. Together they are 40% likely [judgment].

## End

I recommend that we take these four actions in order.

1. **Audit the last three retrospectives.**
   - Increment: one 60-minute session. Kind: deterministic.
   - Blocker removed: we do not know if actions were made, or made and not done.
   - Cynefin: Complicated. We deduce the answer from existing records.
   - Test:
     - Given the notes from the last three retrospectives
     - When we list each action item with owner, due date, and status
     - Then a table shows the item count and the share completed
   - Step 4 MUST NOT start until this table exists.

2. **Adopt a rule of one change per retrospective.** Each change gets one named owner and one due date on the team board.
   - Increment: one retrospective, with the first 5 minutes of the next one used to review the change. Kind: deterministic.
   - Blocker removed: vague actions that nobody owns.
   - Cynefin: Clear. The practice is well known [2], so we apply it.
   - Test:
     - Given a retrospective has ended
     - When we open the team board
     - Then it shows exactly one change with an owner and a due date

3. **Poll the team to find the main cause.** Use an anonymous poll with three questions: why do actions fail, what topic matters most, and what blocks you.
   - Increment: one 10-minute poll, one 20-minute read-out. Kind: evaluative.
   - Blocker removed: we guess at the cause.
   - Cynefin: Complicated. Analysis of the answers picks the cause.
   - Rubric, written before the poll:
     - Criteria and weights: how many people name the cause (50), whether the team can fix it alone (30), support from the Step 1 audit (20).
     - Score: 0 to 3 for each criterion.
     - Pass threshold: a weighted score of 2.0 or more makes it the main cause.
     - Evidence: poll counts, and the Step 1 table.

4. **Run one format trial: a retrospective on a single problem with a root-cause step.**
   - Increment: the next retrospective, with 30 extra minutes. Kind: stochastic.
   - Blocker removed: the format may not suit the team's real problems.
   - Cynefin: Complex. We learn the effect only after we try it. The leverage is a guess. Induction from trials could fail if the team changes size or if a deadline crunch hits.
   - Test, written first:
     - Metric: share of retrospective changes done by the due date.
     - Baseline: the Step 1 completion rate.
     - Threshold: 70% or more, and above baseline.
     - Sample size: three retrospectives.
   - Stop condition: stop after two retrospectives if the rate is at or below baseline. Budget: 90 extra minutes in total.
   - Step 4 can run in parallel with Step 3.

Condition that changes this recommendation: if Step 3 names "no authority to act" as the main cause, replace Step 4 with a meeting between the team lead and the person who holds the authority.
