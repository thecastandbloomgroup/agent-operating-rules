## Background

Your team holds retrospectives (regular meetings to review how the team works). People attend, but no change follows. The request is for a way to make retros produce change. The material does not say team size, cadence, or how action items are tracked.

## Relevance

A retro that produces no change teaches the team that speaking up has no effect. Attendance then drops or turns into silence. A fix now is cheaper than a rebuild later.

## End

I recommend that we take these four actions in order.

1. **Audit the last three retros.** Increment: one hour to list every action item with its owner, due date, and status.
   - Blocker removed: we do not know why nothing shifts.
   - Kind: deterministic.
   - Test:
     - Given the notes of the last three retros
     - When we list each action item with owner, date, and status
     - Then every item has a status and the completion rate is known
   - Domain: Complicated. Analysis of records, by deduction, shows which cause applies.

2. **Adopt a one-action rule.** Increment: at the next retro, the team picks one action, names one owner, sets one date, and adds it to the backlog. Step 2 MUST NOT start until Step 1 shows the completion rate.
   - Blocker removed: too many unowned items.
   - Kind: deterministic.
   - Test:
     - Given a retro has ended
     - When we check the backlog
     - Then it holds one action with an owner and a date
   - Domain: Clear. Owned, dated actions are a known good practice.

3. **Run a two-retro format trial.** Increment: in the next two retros, spend the first 15 minutes reviewing the last action, then use a new format for data gathering. Run this alongside Step 2.
   - Capability added: evidence on whether the format matters.
   - Kind: stochastic.
   - Test: the metric is the share of actions done by the next retro, plus a 1 to 5 vote on "this retro was worth my time". The baseline comes from Step 1 and a vote at the start. Pass: the action is done in both retros and the median vote rises by at least 1 point. The sample is two retros, so confidence stays low.
   - Stop condition: revert after one retro if the median vote falls. Budget: two retros, 15 extra minutes each.
   - Domain: Complex. This is a safe-to-fail probe by induction from trials. Leverage is a guess. Team turnover or a deadline crunch could break the pattern.

4. **Score the result at day 30.** Increment: one 30-minute review using a rubric written before the trial.
   - Blocker removed: no agreed way to judge success.
   - Kind: evaluative.
   - Rubric: pass threshold is 3.5 of 5.

   |---|---|---|
   | Actions completed | 50% | Backlog status |
   | Team vote on value | 30% | Vote results |
   | Blockers escalated | 20% | Items sent to management with a reply |

   - Domain: Complicated. The rubric applies analysis to known data.

If Step 1 shows that most actions were done, replace Steps 2 and 3 with a review of whether the actions target the right problems.
