## Headline

Replace open-ended retrospectives with a short cycle: identify the barrier, choose one feasible change, and test it.

- First, check where past actions stopped: agreement, ownership, authority, or execution.
- Next, give one change an owner, time, and a clear test.
- Finally, use the next retrospective to review evidence, not repeat the discussion.

## Background

Your team attends retrospectives, but you report that nothing changes afterward. [1] The immediate task is to find where discussion stops becoming action. [judgment]

## Relevance

A new meeting format does not address missing time, authority, or follow-through. [judgment] Therefore, examine those barriers before you change the agenda. [judgment]

## Information

**Evidence.** Attendance continues while change does not, so attendance alone is not the success measure. [1]

The Scrum Guide says retrospectives should identify helpful changes and address the most useful improvements promptly. [2] This principle applies here without requiring your team to adopt Scrum. [judgment]

**Working diagnosis.** Treat this as a failure to turn discussion into action, rather than a lack of topics. [judgment] Check four distinct points:

| Point | Evidence to seek |
|---|---|
| Agreement | Did the team choose a specific change? |
| Ownership | Did one person accept responsibility? |
| Authority | Could that person make the change? |
| Execution | Did anyone reserve time and review the result? |

These checks distinguish a meeting problem from a work-priority or authority problem. [judgment]

**Assumption:** Past retrospective notes are available. **Why not measured:** The material does not state it. **Validate by:** Open the latest notes. **If wrong:** Reconstruct one example together.

**Assumption:** The team can try one small, reversible change. **Why not measured:** The material does not state it. **Validate by:** Confirm authority before selection. **If wrong:** Seek approval instead.

**Alternative considered:** Change the format or facilitator first. This loses because it does not directly address action ownership or execution. [judgment]

## End

I recommend that we take these three steps, each limited to one working session.

### 1. Audit the last three retrospectives in 20 minutes

**Kind:** Deterministic, meaning the output has fixed acceptance criteria.

**Test first:**
```gherkin
Given the available notes from the last three retrospectives
When the team reviews every proposed action
Then each action has a status and an identified stopping point
```

Create a short list: action, owner, status, evidence, and stopping point. Use “not recorded” rather than guessing.

This removes the barrier of not knowing where follow-through fails. [judgment]

**Domain: Complicated.** Analysis of the action history identifies the failure point; deduction then guides the response.

### 2. Choose one change and authorize it in 15 minutes

Step 2 MUST NOT start until Step 1 passes.

**Kind:** Deterministic.

**Test first:**
```gherkin
Given the action audit
When the team chooses one reversible change
Then its action card names an owner, authority, time allowance, review date, and observable result
```

Write one action card:

> For the next work item, [owner] will try [change], using at most [time], with approval from [person].  
> Success means [observable result]. Review the evidence on [date].

Choose something within the team’s control. If nothing qualifies, make obtaining a specific approval the action.

This removes unclear responsibility and hidden approval barriers. [judgment]

**Domain: Clear.** Deduction from the acceptance rule determines whether the action card is complete.

### 3. Run one small trial, then record a decision

Step 3 MUST NOT start until Step 2 passes.

**Kind:** Stochastic, meaning results can vary between trials.

**Acceptance test first:**
- **Metric:** Number of completed trials with evidence and a recorded decision.
- **Baseline:** Zero completed trials of this newly selected change.
- **Pass threshold:** One completed trial, with a decision to keep, adjust, or stop.
- **Sample size:** One work case; this tests feasibility, not lasting benefit.

Cap the trial at one work session and 60 minutes of extra effort. Stop if it exceeds that cap or creates customer risk.

Record what happened against the action card’s success measure. Start the next retrospective with that evidence.

This adds a direct link between the meeting and actual work. [judgment]

**Domain: Complex.** Induction from one trial supports only a provisional decision; different work or team conditions can break the pattern.
