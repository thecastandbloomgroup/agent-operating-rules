## Headline

Replace the current retrospective routine with one small change, one owner, and a visible check of the result.

- First, find where past actions stopped: choice, ownership, time, authority, or review.
- Next, choose one change the team can test during normal work.
- Finally, review what happened before creating more actions.

## Background

Your team attends retrospectives, but you report no resulting change. [1]  
That report does not establish whether the cause sits inside the meeting or in the work that follows. [judgment]

## Relevance

A new meeting format alone does not address missing time, authority, or ownership. [judgment]  
Therefore, start with the path from discussion to action, rather than another discussion exercise. [judgment]

## Information

The Scrum Guide describes retrospectives as a way to plan improvements in quality and effectiveness. It says teams should address the most useful improvements soon. [2]

Use these checks to find the break:

| Check | Evidence to inspect | Response if absent |
|---|---|---|
| Concrete change | An action describes different work, not “communicate better” | Rewrite it as an observable action |
| Ownership | One person agrees to coordinate it | Name a willing owner |
| Capacity | The team reserves time or removes other work | Make the trade-off explicit |
| Authority | The team controls the change | Request a specific decision from someone who does |
| Review | A later meeting checks the result | Reserve the first five minutes |

These checks form a practical diagnostic, not a finding about your team. [judgment]

**Assumption:** The team can change at least one work practice. **Why not measured:** The material does not state it. **Validate by:** Check authority during Step 1. **If wrong:** Seek an external decision instead of assigning another team action.

**Alternative considered:** Change the facilitator or meeting format first. This loses because the current evidence does not identify facilitation as the cause. [judgment]

## End

I recommend that we take these three actions in order.

### 1. Trace the last three retrospectives, in 20 minutes

**Kind:** Deterministic, with fixed completion criteria.

**Test first:**
```gherkin
Given the available records from the last three retrospectives
When the team traces each recorded action
Then every action has a status, owner, and known blocker
```

Inspect the records together. Mark missing information as “not recorded”; do not treat missing records as proof that nobody acted.

**Output:** A checked action list showing where progress stops. This removes the need to guess about the process. [judgment]

**Domain:** Clear, by deduction from the stated checklist.

### 2. Write one change card, in 15 minutes

Step 2 MUST NOT start until Step 1 passes.

**Kind:** Deterministic, with fixed completion criteria.

**Test first:**
```gherkin
Given one unfinished action within the team's authority
When the team writes its change card
Then the card names a change, owner, work slot, measure, and review date
```

Take the oldest unfinished action that the team still supports and controls. Reduce it until one work session can test it.

Use this form:

> For the next five relevant cases, [owner] will try [change].  
> We will record [observable result].  
> We will review the result on [date].

If no action qualifies, use the card to request one external decision instead.

**Output:** One complete card, not another list of intentions. This adds a clear commitment and a place in the work. [judgment]

**Domain:** Clear, by deduction from the card’s required fields.

### 3. Run one bounded trial, then review the evidence

Step 3 MUST NOT start until Step 2 passes.

**Kind:** Stochastic, because work results vary.

**Acceptance test first:**

- **Metric:** Cases that meet the card’s observable success condition.
- **Baseline:** Record the result for the last five comparable cases.
- **Sample:** Try the change on five new cases.
- **Pass threshold:** At least four successes, exceeding the baseline by at least one.
- **Guardrail:** Stop if the change causes a missed customer commitment.

Treat this threshold as a small trial rule, not statistical proof. [judgment]

Start with one work session. Cap extra effort at 60 minutes across the trial and stop after two weeks.

At the review, record **keep, revise, or stop**, with the observed result. Do not add another trial until this decision exists.

**Output:** A completed trial record. This tests whether the change helps, rather than whether people agree with it. [judgment]

**Domain:** Complex, through induction from trials; different work or time pressure can break the pattern. Leverage remains a guess.

## Follow-up

If people report fear of consequences, pause public attribution and provide a private route to raise concerns. [judgment]
