## Background

Your team attends retrospectives, but you report no resulting change. [1] The available information does not show whether actions lack ownership, face blockers, or fail after implementation.

## Relevance

A new meeting format does not directly address those different problems. Therefore, inspect the path from discussion to action first. [judgment]

## Information

Treat these as possible failure points, not established causes:

- **Selection:** the team discusses problems but does not choose an action.
- **Execution:** someone accepts an action but lacks time, authority, or support.
- **Review:** the team completes an action but never checks its effect.

This distinction helps match the response to the failure point. [judgment]

**Assumption:** The team can authorize one small, reversible change. **Why not measured:** The material does not state it. **Validate by:** Confirm authority before selecting the change. **If wrong:** Seek a decision from someone with authority instead.

**Alternative considered:** Change the retrospective format or facilitator now. This loses because it changes the discussion before establishing where action stops. [judgment]

## End

I recommend that we take these three steps, in order.

### 1. Trace the last three retrospective actions

**Kind:** Deterministic, meaning the output has fixed acceptance criteria.

**Test first:**
```gherkin
Given the available retrospective records
When the team traces up to three recent actions
Then each action has a status and a named blocker
```

Spend 15 minutes producing a short action record. Use these statuses: not selected, not started, blocked, completed without review, or reviewed.

If no actions exist, record that result. Do not reconstruct missing details as facts.

**Blocker removed:** Uncertainty about where follow-through stops. [judgment]

**Domain:** Clear, through deduction from a fixed checklist.

### 2. Create one change card

Step 2 MUST NOT start until Step 1 passes.

**Kind:** Deterministic.

**Test first:**
```gherkin
Given the action record
When the team creates one change card
Then it states the change, owner, authority, deadline, measure, and review date
```

Spend 20 minutes choosing one small change within the team’s authority. First, compare the recorded blockers to find the earliest repeated failure. Use that analysis to select the change.

Give the owner time to act, not just responsibility. Choose a deadline within one week.

For example: “Before work starts, the requester and developer confirm acceptance criteria.”

**Blocker removed:** An action without the means or agreement to complete it. [judgment]

**Domain:** Complicated, through analysis of the action record and the team’s authority.

### 3. Run a capped trial and review its result

Step 3 MUST NOT start until Step 2 passes.

**Kind:** Stochastic, meaning results vary between cases.

**Acceptance test first:**

- **Metric:** One observable problem measure, such as minutes lost to clarification.
- **Baseline:** The five most recent comparable cases.
- **Sample:** The next five comparable cases.
- **Pass threshold:** At least 20% improvement, with no added customer delay.
- **Evidence:** Record the measure for each case.

The sample and threshold are practical trial limits, not proof of a lasting effect. [judgment]

Launch the trial in one session. Cap extra team effort at 60 minutes. Stop if it delays customer work or exceeds that budget.

At the review date, keep, adjust, or stop the change based on the recorded results. Do not count completion alone as success.

**Capability added:** A check that connects action to results. [judgment]

**Domain:** Complex, through induction from a small trial. Different work or unusual cases can break the pattern, so leverage remains a guess.
