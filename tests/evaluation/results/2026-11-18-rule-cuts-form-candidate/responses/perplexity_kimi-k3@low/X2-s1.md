## Headline

Retrospectives fail when they produce discussion but no binding decisions, so the fix is to make every retro end with owned, tested actions.

- Retros without action items, owners, and follow-through decay into ritual [judgment].
- The quickest lever is to reduce each retro to one committed experiment, not many wishes.
- A one-cycle pilot costs little and shows within one sprint whether behavior changes.

## Background

Your team meets regularly, talks, but nothing changes afterward. The request is what to do about retrospectives that stopped producing changes.

## Relevance

Retros that produce no change train the team to disengage, so attendance without outcomes costs more than it saves. Fixing the action loop recovers the meeting's value within one or two sprints.

## Information

Retros fail for three recurring causes: no owner per action, actions too large to finish in a sprint, and no check on the last retro's outcomes [judgment].

Assumption: Your team runs Scrum-style sprints of one to three weeks. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask in the next retro. If wrong: adjust the cycle length, the fix still applies.

Assumption: Actions from past retros were not tracked visibly. Why not measured: no tool can reach it. Validate by: inspect the last three retro notes. If wrong: the problem shifts to action quality, not tracking.

Alternative considered: replace retros with a different format such as lean coffee. It loses because the failure is follow-through, not discussion format [judgment].

## End

I recommend that we:

1. Run one retro that ends with exactly one action item. Blocker removed: overload of unpicked improvements. Domain: Clear, a known best practice applies, leverage estimate reliable.
   Given a retro with at least three improvement ideas,
   When the team votes on one,
   Then the retro record shows one action with a named owner and a done-date inside the sprint.
2. Open the next retro by reviewing the prior action's result. Blocker removed: follow-through decay. Domain: Clear. Depends on Step 1.
   Given the prior retro's action,
   When the retro starts,
   Then the first agenda item records it as done, partial, or dropped with a reason.
3. Convert improvements into safe-to-fail experiments when the cause is unclear. Example: "try pairing on reviews for two weeks." Blocker removed: guessing which fix works. Domain: Complex, cause and effect visible only after trial, so this is a probe with a measure and a stop condition. Metric: cycle time of code reviews, threshold: 20% faster over at least ten reviews, stop after two sprints.
4. Timebox discussion to 30 minutes and reserve 15 for the decision. Blocker removed: talk without a decision. Domain: Clear.
   Given a 45-minute retro,
   When 30 minutes pass,
   Then the facilitator moves to the vote regardless of open topics.

Step 3 may run in parallel with Steps 1 and 2, capped at two sprints. If Step 1 shows actions were already tracked and done, the problem is action quality, so replace Steps 1 and 2 with a root-cause session on why the changes had no effect.
