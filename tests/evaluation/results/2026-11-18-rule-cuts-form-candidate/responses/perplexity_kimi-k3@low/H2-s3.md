# Headline

Run the next 24 hours as one controlled loop: centralise decisions, fact check, then respond in two timed stages.

- Parallel replies from legal, PR, and the board create more risk than the article, so one decision cell comes first.
- A denial issued before the fact check collapses if any part of the article is true, so verification precedes any rebuttal.
- A litigation hold has the shortest closing window, because routine auto-deletion can destroy records within days.
- The public response branches on the fact-check result: accountability if the statement was false, documented rebuttal if accurate.

## Background

On 17 November 2026 the CEO made a public statement. A journalist has published that the statement was false. Counsel, PR, and board members are messaging separately, and no shared position exists.

## Relevance

The first public move is hard to retract. A denial that later collapses, or a slow correction when the company is wrong, raises both legal exposure and reputational loss.

## Information

Crisis experiments find that accountability responses protect reputation better than denial when the organisation is responsible [1].

US courts impose a duty to preserve once litigation is reasonably expected [2].

Sanctions can follow where electronically stored information is lost after that duty arises [3].

For a listed company, an inaccurate public statement can trigger securities disclosure duties [4]. This applies only if the statement concerned financial information [judgment].

**Assumption log**

Assumption: You can convene legal, PR, and the board, that is, you hold an executive or chief-of-staff role. Why not measured: the material does not state your role. Validate by: confirm authority in your reply. If wrong: step 1 becomes a request that the CEO or general counsel convene the group.

Assumption: The truth status of the CEO statement is unknown at start. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: the step 4 fact sheet. If wrong: step 5 follows the matching branch with no other change.

Assumption: Litigation risk is live, so the preservation duty may already apply. Why not measured: the material does not state whether litigation has been threatened. If wrong: the hold costs little and the plan is unchanged.

**Alternative considered**

Issue an immediate, detailed rebuttal now. It loses because the facts are unverified: a rebuttal that overlaps one true point must be withdrawn, and withdrawal compounds the damage [judgment]. It also adds defamation exposure if the article is accurate [inference].

## End

I recommend that we:

1. **Stabilise decision rights.** Within 2 hours, convene a 30-minute call: general counsel, head of PR, one board member. Name one spokesperson. Order all staff to route every inquiry to that person. This removes the blocker of three conflicting channels. Test, written before the call, deterministic:
   Given the decision cell exists and the spokesperson is named.
   When any media, investor, or internal inquiry arrives in the next 24 hours.
   Then only the spokesperson answers, from approved lines, and the log records every contact.
   Domain: Chaotic. Acting first, because no cause and effect is visible while three parties act on separate information.

2. **Publish a holding statement.** Draft, approve, and publish within 60 minutes of step 1. This removes the perception vacuum that silence creates [judgment]. Test, deterministic:
   Given the cell has approved the holding text.
   When the text is published.
   Then it states only that a review is under way and names the update time, with zero unverified facts.
   Domain: Clear. Deduction from established crisis practice.

3. **Issue a litigation hold.** (A litigation hold is an internal order to stop deletion and preserve records.) Counsel sends hold notices to named custodians today. This removes the blocker that auto-deletion destroys records the fact check needs. Test, deterministic:
   Given the custodian list from step 1.
   When notices are sent.
   Then 100 percent of custodians acknowledge within 24 hours, and auto-deletion is suspended.
   Domain: Clear. Deduction from the preservation duty described above.

4. **Complete the fact sheet.** One working session: the data owner and counsel review the statement claim by claim against primary records and the journalist's evidence. This adds the capability no step so far has: a known truth baseline. Test, deterministic:
   Given the statement, the article, and preserved records.
   When the review ends.
   Then every claim is marked confirmed, refuted, or unverified, each with a named source.
   Domain: Complicated. Analysis by experts selects the response. Step 4 may run in parallel with steps 2 and 3. Step 5 MUST NOT start until this test passes.

5. **Publish the full response as a capped probe.** Within 24 hours of the fact sheet: if any claim was refuted, publish a correction with verified figures and an apology; if all claims stand accurate, publish an evidence-backed rebuttal and request a formal correction from the outlet. Test, stochastic, written first: metric is the share of new coverage stating the verified facts. Baseline: share in the 20 most recent items at release. Threshold: at least 60 percent [judgment] within 72 hours, across at least 20 news items [judgment]. Stop condition: below threshold at 48 hours triggers direct journalist briefings. Domain: Complex. Reaction is visible only after release, so leverage here is a guess; do not pre-commit further waves.

## Follow-up

Conditions that change the recommendation:

1. If the company is listed and the statement concerned financial information, securities counsel reviews disclosure duties before any correction [4].
2. If the statement concerned safety or health, regulator notification deadlines can override the two-stage timeline [inference].
