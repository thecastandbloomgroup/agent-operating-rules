Headline

Review one owned action at the next meeting, and do not add topics until that action is done.

- The failure is a missing review, not missing attendance. [judgment]
- Therefore the agenda must open with the status of the last action.
- One action with an owner and a date is the limit until one action finishes.
- Completion across two cycles is a separate probe with a stop rule.

Background

The team still holds retrospectives, and people still attend.
Work does not change after those meetings.
The request is to choose a next step that restores change.

Relevance

Actions with no review teach the team that the meeting has no effect.
The probe in Step 4 checks the result within two meetings.

Information

This section covers the evidence, the assumption, and the alternative.

The Scrum Guide requires the team to address the most useful improvements as soon as possible.
[1]

Derby and Larsen require the group to decide what to do before the close.
[2]

However, a decision that nobody reviews later does not change the work. [judgment]

This is likely, 66 to 100 percent, because the report matches a missing review.
The basis is two sources that require a later response, plus that report.

Assumption: Prior notes lack an owner and a date for each action. Why not measured: The material does not state it. Validate by: Count owned actions in the last three notes. If wrong: Replace Steps 1 and 3 with the safety probe in Follow-up.

Alternative considered: End the meeting and use a continuous board. It loses because the meeting already exists and the missing piece is the review. [judgment]

Step 4 MUST NOT start until Steps 1 and 3 are in place.

Follow-up

If the Step 2 count shows owned actions that already finished, replace Steps 1 and 3 with a safety probe.
That probe asks each person to write one unspoken issue before group talk.
Stop the probe if fewer than half of the people write an issue.
Budget: one meeting.
