### Headline

Limit each retrospective to one action item with an assigned owner and track it on the team sprint board.
First, teams abandon action items when they select too many items at once.
Second, visible tracking connects discussion directly to daily work.
Third, reviewing the past action item at the start of the next meeting restores accountability.

### Background

Your team attends regular retrospectives, but the meetings do not cause changes in team practice [judgment].
You want to know what changes will make these meetings effective again.

### Relevance

Unproductive meetings consume team time without improvement.
Fixing the process now prevents cynicism and disengagement.

### Information

Retrospective failure usually stems from weak commitment tracking and excessive work in progress [1].
Research shows that teams complete action items at a higher rate when they limit work in progress to one or two items [2].
Therefore, focusing on single actionable experiments creates measurable progress [judgment].
This section covers evidence, assumptions, and alternatives.

See A1 and A2 in the assumption log below.

#### Assumptions

A1. The team has authority to change its internal work practices. Why not measured: The material does not state it. Validate by: Ask the team manager in one email. If wrong: Escalate blockers to leadership instead of internal agreements.

A2. Meeting attendance remains regular. Why not measured: The user states people attend. Validate by: Check meeting attendance logs. If wrong: Address attendance before fixing action tracking.

#### Alternative Considered

The team could pause retrospectives for two months to reset expectations.
This option loses because pausing the meetings removes the only shared forum for process improvement [judgment].

### End

I recommend that we take these four actions:

1. Step 1: Establish a single-item rule for the next retrospective.
   - Domain: Clear, because team rules operate on direct shared consent.
   - Blocker removed: Stops the team from generating unmanageable lists of actions.
   - Increment: Agree on one retrospective improvement item during a 30-minute team meeting.
   - Test (Evaluative):
     - Criterion 1: Action count equals one (Weight: 50%, Threshold: 1).
     - Criterion 2: Action has one named owner (Weight: 50%, Threshold: 1).
     - Pass threshold: 100% score based on meeting minutes.

2. Step 2: Place the action item on the team sprint board.
   Step 2 MUST NOT start until the test in Step 1 passes.
   - Domain: Clear, because the board accepts standard work cards.
   - Capability added: Makes the retrospective agreement visible in daily standups.
   - Increment: Create one ticket on the active sprint board within 15 minutes.
   - Test (Deterministic):
     Given a selected retrospective action item,
     When the owner creates a board ticket,
     Then the ticket appears in the active sprint column.

3. Step 3: Run a safe-to-fail experiment with daily tracking.
   Step 3 MUST NOT start until the ticket in Step 2 appears on the board.
   - Domain: Complex, because team habit shifts show emergent behavior.
   - Capability added: Gathers real feedback on whether the team follows the new agreement.
   - Increment: Run the single action item for one two-week sprint with a budget of two hours of effort.
   - Test (Stochastic):
     - Metric: Daily standup mentions of the action item ticket.
     - Baseline: Zero mentions per sprint.
     - Pass threshold: At least five mentions across ten working days.
     - Sample size: Ten standups in one sprint.

4. Step 4: Review completion at the start of the following retrospective.
   Step 4 MUST NOT start until the sprint in Step 3 concludes.
   - Domain: Complicated, because assessing process impact requires qualitative analysis of sprint metrics.
   - Blocker removed: Prevents moving to new topics before closing old commitments.
   - Increment: Spend the first ten minutes of the next retrospective checking the result of the action item.
   - Test (Evaluative):
     - Criterion 1: Verification of ticket completion status (Weight: 40%, Threshold: pass).
     - Criterion 2: Team consensus on whether practice changed (Weight: 60%, Threshold: 70% approval).
     - Pass threshold: 80% total weighted score.

### Follow-up

If A1 is false, replace Step 1 with a meeting with the team manager to define the boundary of team authority.
