# Headline

Focus each retrospective on one owned action item that you place directly into your sprint backlog.

First, teams generate too many broad ideas without clear owners.
Second, uncompleted items lose visibility when work returns to feature delivery.
Third, restricting output to a single item restores accountability and execution speed.

# Background

Your team attends agile retrospectives regularly, but these meetings no longer cause changes in team workflow. Participants raise issues during the session without seeing results in daily work. This pattern creates fatigue and reduces engagement over time [1].

# Relevance

Stagnant retrospectives waste meeting time and signal to team members that feedback does not matter. Restoring immediate follow-through rebuilds trust before attendance and morale drop further.

# Information

Research shows that continuous improvement stalls when action items lack specific ownership and systemic integration [1].
Per Derby and Larsen, effective retrospectives limit commitments to high-leverage experiments that the team controls [2].
When teams track five or more goals at once, they complete none with high quality [judgment].
In contrast, limiting focus to one experiment increases completion rates [judgment].

A thematic review of stagnant retrospectives identifies two primary failure modes:
1. Diffusion of responsibility: actions lack single owners and deadlines [3].
2. Invisibility: action items remain on retrospective boards instead of active sprint backlogs [1].

### Assumption Log

A1. The team has authority to modify its internal delivery processes. Why not measured: The user query did not state organizational constraints. Validate by: Review the team charter with the delivery lead. If wrong: The team must direct change requests to external managers instead of internal experiments.

A2. The team uses an active backlog system for regular sprint work. Why not measured: The material does not state specific tooling. Validate by: Check the team project management board. If wrong: Track action items on a physical board or team communication channel.

### Alternative Considered

The strongest alternative is replacing standard retrospectives with root-cause analysis sessions, such as five-whys workshops. This approach loses because it increases diagnostic effort without solving the execution failure in daily work.

# End

I recommend that we execute the following sequence:

1. Limit the next retrospective output to exactly one team-owned experiment committed directly to the sprint backlog.
   - Increment: Select one improvement item during the final ten minutes of the retrospective and create a sprint card for it.
   - Blocker removed: Removes goal dilution and hidden work.

2. Review the active retrospective experiment during daily coordination meetings.
   - Increment: Add a sixty-second status check on the retrospective card during the daily standup meeting.
   - Blocker removed: Removes work invisibility between retrospective sessions.

   - Test (Deterministic):
     Given an ongoing sprint with an active retrospective card,

     Rubric:
     - Locus of control (weight: 50%): item requires zero approvals outside the team.

     Threshold: Score of 80% or higher classifies an item as internally actionable.
     Evidence: Written action item text and historical completion status.
