## Headline

Replace open-ended retrospectives with a short cycle: choose one change, protect time for it, and check the result.

- First, find where earlier decisions stop: agreement, ownership, capacity, or follow-through. [judgment]
- Then, test one small change rather than collect another long action list. [judgment]
- Finally, judge the retrospective by what changes afterward, not by attendance. [judgment]

## Background

Your team attends retrospectives, but you report no resulting changes. [1] The missing evidence is what happens between discussion and the next retrospective.

## Relevance

Another discussion format does not directly address that gap. [judgment] Therefore, start with the path from a decision to an observed result.

## Information

**The goal is improvement, not action-item volume.** The Scrum Guide says teams should address their most useful improvements promptly. [2]

Use these checks to locate the failure:

| Where progress stops | Evidence to check | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Agreement | Notes contain concerns but no specific change | Define one observable change |
| Ownership | A change exists, but nobody owns the next step | Name one owner |
| Capacity | An owner exists, but regular work displaces the change | Reserve time or remove competing work |
| Follow-through | Work happens, but nobody checks its effect | Schedule a result review |

These are diagnostic categories, not findings about your team. [judgment] An owner coordinates the change; that person does not inherit blame for the underlying problem. [judgment]

## End

I recommend that we take these three steps:

### 1. Trace the last three retrospective decisions

**Test first, deterministic**

Given the available notes from the last three retrospectives,  
When the team checks each agreed change,  
Then each has a status, an owner if recorded, and evidence of its result.

Spend **20 minutes** on this check. Mark missing information as “not recorded,” rather than assume nothing happened.

This removes the diagnostic gap. **Clear:** the task checks records against fixed fields.

### 2. Launch one small, protected experiment

**Acceptance test first, stochastic**

- **Metric:** Number of changes that meet their agreed outcome test.
- **Baseline:** The count from Step 1, reported per retrospective cycle.
- **Sample:** Two consecutive retrospective cycles.
- **Pass threshold:** At least one change meets its outcome test within those two cycles.
- **Status:** Future trial; no results are available yet.

After Step 1, spend **15 minutes** choosing one reversible change. Record:

- The problem and the evidence that it occurs.
- One owner and any required decision-maker.
- One observable outcome, with a baseline and pass threshold.
- A review date and protected work time.

For example: “Replace one status meeting with a written update; pass if every required decision still occurs before its deadline.”

Cap the experiment at **two person-hours**, unless the team explicitly approves more. Stop if it exceeds that cap or causes harm.

This tests whether a smaller commitment can produce change. **Complex:** the effect depends on team behavior, so use a bounded probe; its leverage remains a guess. [judgment]

### 3. Review the result before discussing new problems

**Test first, deterministic**

Given the experiment’s outcome test and review date,  
When the team reviews the evidence,  
Then it records one decision: keep, adapt, or stop.

Reserve the **first 10 minutes** of the review meeting for this decision. Do not start this step until Step 2 produces evidence or reaches its review date.

If the team never attempted the experiment, identify the blocked decision or displaced work. Do not automatically create another action.

This closes the decision loop. **Clear:** the task applies a fixed review rule, even when the experiment fails.

## Follow-up

If people fear blame or retaliation, replace the group diagnosis with a safer, confidential route. [judgment] If the team lacks authority, involve the person who controls the blocked decision before launching the experiment.
