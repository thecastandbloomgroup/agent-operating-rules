## Headline

Fix the follow-through loop at the next retro and audit the root cause within two weeks.

- Execution fails most often when nothing shifts [judgment], so the plan reviews prior actions and caps new ones first.
- A one-session audit of six past retros identifies the weakest stage, so later effort hits the real blocker.
- A capped probe raises candor if the audit shows low safety, because safety effects are visible only afterward.
- An escalation path removes out-of-scope items that recycle and erode trust.

## Background

Your team holds retrospectives (retros). People attend. Nothing changes afterward. This note gives a repair plan with tests and a sequence.

## Relevance

Each retro that changes nothing lowers trust in the ritual and in the team's ability to improve. The repair costs one half-day session plus about ten minutes per retro.

## Information

A retro converts talk into change in three stages: input, conversion, execution. The stages do not overlap, and they cover the whole path from discussion to result. Input fails when people withhold real problems; one cause is low psychological safety, that is, fear that speaking up brings punishment [1]. Conversion fails when actions lack an owner, a done-criterion (an observable condition that proves the action is finished), or a finishable size. Execution fails when nobody reviews prior actions. Practitioner literature recommends few concrete actions per retro, each with an owner, plus a review at the next retro [2]. No source in scope disagrees. Steps below use Cynefin, a framework that sorts work by how cause and effect relate [3].

Baseline: a healthy loop finishes most of its actions between retros. The threshold used here is 70 percent of actions done [judgment]. Team data is absent, so confidence in any failure-stage ranking is low until Step 3 completes. Confidence that Steps 1 and 2 help is high, two practitioner sources and standard practice, because they apply in all three failure stages.

Assumption log:

A1. The team holds a retro every one to three weeks. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: check the calendar for the last three dates. If wrong: set a fixed cadence before Step 1.

A2. Retros already produce written action items. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: read the notes of the last three retros. If wrong: expand Step 3 to fix output capture before Step 1.

A3. One person facilitates each retro and can apply the new rules. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask at the next retro. If wrong: assign a facilitator before Step 1.

Alternative considered: replace the retro format or hire an external facilitator. This loses because a new format changes the discussion, not the follow-through, and attendance without change points to the execution stage [judgment]. External facilitation also costs more and keeps ownership outside the team. If Step 3 names facilitation quality as the dominant cause, revisit this alternative.

## End

I recommend that we run these five steps in order. Each test uses a Given, When, Then format and appears before its step.

Step 1. Review prior actions at every retro. Kind: deterministic. Cynefin domain: Clear, because a documented best practice exists [2].

- Test first:
- Given the previous retro recorded action items,
- When the next retro starts,
- Then the facilitator marks each prior action done, in progress, or dropped, in the first ten minutes.
- Increment: add this agenda item and a tracking board at the next retro.
- Blocker removed: actions vanish between retros, so nothing shifts.

Step 2. Cap and sharpen new actions. Kind: deterministic. Cynefin domain: Clear, because the practice is standard [2].

- Test first:
- Given the retro surfaces candidate improvements,
- When the team selects actions,
- Then it selects at most two, each with one named owner, one done-criterion, and a due date before the next retro.
- Increment: apply the rule at the same retro as Step 1.
- Blocker removed: vague, ownerless actions that nobody can finish.

Step 3. Audit the last six retros. Kind: evaluative. Cynefin domain: Complicated, because the cause is knowable through analysis of records and interviews. Write the rubric first as a weighted scoring method (Kepner-Tregoe decision analysis).

- Criteria and weights: rate of actions done, 40 percent; actions with owner and due date, 20 percent; raised items within team control, 20 percent; candor signals (distinct problems per retro, safety-check average), 20 percent.
- Pass threshold: 70 percent of actions done, and no criterion at zero.
- Evidence per criterion: notes and boards from the last six retros, plus five interviews of 15 minutes each.
- Increment: one half-day session by the facilitator and one volunteer.
- Blocker removed: the unknown root cause. Step 4 MUST NOT start until this step reports the candor score.

Step 4. Probe candor with anonymous input. Kind: stochastic. Cynefin domain: Complex, because safety effects appear only afterward, so this is a safe-to-fail probe with a cap.

- Acceptance test first. Metric: distinct problems raised per retro. Baseline: from Step 3. Pass threshold: 50 percent rise, sustained. Sample size: three retros.
- Stop condition: safety-check average below 3 of 5 at two consecutive retros; then escalate to the manager.
- Budget: ten minutes per retro for three retros.
- Probe content: collect topics anonymously before each retro and run a safety check, that is, an anonymous 1 to 5 vote on how safe people feel to speak [1].
- Increment: ten minutes per retro, three retros.
- Blocker removed: low candor hides the real problems.

Step 5. Route items outside team control. Kind: deterministic. Cynefin domain: Clear, because routing to the authority level is standard practice. Condition: run this step only if Step 3 finds that scope failures dominate.

- Test first:
- Given an improvement needs budget or authority outside the team,
- When the retro raises it,
- Then the facilitator logs it on an escalation list, and the manager reviews that list within seven days.
- Increment: create the list and book a recurring 15-minute review.
- Blocker removed: out-of-scope items recycle each retro.

Sequencing: Steps 1 and 2 start at the next retro. Step 3 runs in parallel and finishes within two weeks. Step 4 waits on Step 3. Step 5 is conditional on Step 3.

## Follow-up

Conditions that change the recommendation:

- If A2 is false, fix output capture inside Step 3 before Steps 1 and 2.
- If Step 3 shows that management ignores escalations, replace Steps 4 and 5 with a written improvement agreement with the sponsor.
- If the Step 4 probe misses its threshold after three retros, stop the probe and address safety outside the retro, for example in one-to-ones.
