# Headline

Rollback the pool config in db.yaml first: the latency spike comes from the pool shrinking from 50 to 8 connections while 32 workers compete for them.

- Pool acquire time rose from 3 ms to 690 ms at p95 and causes 96% of the new 503 errors.
- The 8-connection pool cannot serve 405 requests/s, so queueing does the damage, not the ORM change.
- The cache TTL cut from 300 s to 60 s raised the miss rate from 12% to 31% and adds load the pool now cannot absorb.
- The selectinload change is harmless: order_lines p95 fell from 30 ms to 26 ms.

## Background

p95 latency rose from 120 ms to 900 ms after release 2026.10.21. The release changed the ORM query, the cache TTL, and the database pool config. Logs, traces, and the diff are attached.

## Relevance

Every pod now times out on pool acquire and returns 503s. Each hour of delay keeps the error rate near 0.40%, twenty times the prior 0.02%.

## Information

**Evidence.** The diff cuts pool max_size from 50 to 8 and acquire_timeout from 30 s to 5 s (release.diff, db.yaml). The service runs 32 worker threads across 4 pods, unchanged. Traces show db.pool.acquire p95 rose from 3 ms to 690 ms, while query spans stayed flat (traces.txt, Window B). Pool-exhausted warnings went from zero per hour to 1,800 to 2,400 per hour (logs.txt). Little's law check: at 405 requests/s and about 78 ms of query time per request, the service needs about 32 concurrent connections; 8 cannot suffice [inference]. DB CPU is 41% and active connections peak at 8, so the database is idle while the app queues (traces.txt). The TTL cut from 300 s to 60 s explains the miss rate rise from 12% to 31% [P, release.diff and traces.txt]. Cache.get latency is unchanged, so Redis is not the problem.

**Assumption log.**

A1. The OPS-412 claim of a new DB connection limit is unverified against the database itself. Why not measured: the material does not state the server-side limit. Validate by: query pg_settings.max_connections and count current connections. If wrong: a true limit below 50 forces a smaller pool plus reduced worker threads instead.

A2. The TTL change has no business reason behind it. Why not measured: no ticket is cited for cache.yaml. Validate by: ask the change author. If wrong: keep 60 s but add request coalescing, and Step 2 becomes optional.

**Alternative considered.** Blame the selectinload ORM change and revert it. It loses: order_lines p95 fell from 30 ms to 26 ms, and selectinload issues two queries per request instead of one per line, which is fewer, not more [P, traces.txt].
