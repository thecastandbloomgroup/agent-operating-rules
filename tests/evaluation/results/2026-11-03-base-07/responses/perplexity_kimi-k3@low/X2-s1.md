## Headline

Diagnose the cause this week, then repair three loops in parallel: follow-through, escalation, and safety.

- The most common defect is failed follow-through: improvements leave the meeting with no owner and no place in the sprint plan [judgment].
- The other two candidate causes are unanswered escalation and low safety: no reply from outside the team, or silence inside it.
- The fix is therefore a short diagnosis first, the follow-through repair as the default, and a capped probe for the rest.

## Background

Your team still holds retrospectives, but they no longer produce change. People attend, speak, and leave, and the next sprint looks like the last. You ask what to do about it.

## Relevance

The Scrum Guide timeboxes this meeting at up to three hours per month [1]. A meeting that produces nothing wastes that time, and repeated silence teaches people that input has no effect [judgment].

## Information

This section holds the evidence, the assumptions, and the alternative.

**Evidence by theme.** Theme 1, follow-through: two sources support it, none disagree.

- The Scrum Guide says the retrospective exists to plan improvements, and the most impactful ones should start as soon as possible [1].
- Derby and Larsen advise choosing few improvements per meeting and closing each one before adding more [2].

Theme 2, safety (how safe people feel to raise problems): one source supports it, none disagree.

- Edmondson's field studies show that teams report problems and errors more where interpersonal risk feels low [3].

Theme 3, escalation: no formal source in this set; the claim rests on practitioner experience [judgment].

- When items that need outside authority get no answer, teams stop raising them.

A one-source theme still matters here: safety failures hide by design, because silent people leave no data.

**Number anchor.** The diagnosis uses one number: the completion rate of past improvement items. Below 50% marks follow-through as a driver [judgment].

**Confidence.** High confidence that one of the three causes applies: one primary standard and two secondary sources agree. Which cause dominates in this team is unknown; Step 1 measures it. Follow-through is the expected primary cause, about 70% likely [judgment].

**Assumptions.**

A1. The team keeps notes and improvement items from past retrospectives. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: open the notes of the last three retrospectives. If wrong: Step 1 first creates the record, and Step 4 takes its baseline from the next three retrospectives.

A2. A manager or sponsor exists who can answer escalated items. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask the team lead once. If wrong: replace Step 3 with a request to appoint a sponsor.

A3. The team controls at least part of its own process. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: tag each past improvement as inside or outside team authority. If wrong: Step 3 carries the main weight.

A4. Retrospectives run on a regular cadence, one per sprint or per month. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: check the calendar. If wrong: spread Step 4's three sessions across the real cadence.

**Alternative considered.** Strongest alternative: change the format or the facilitator, or cancel the meeting. It loses because format does not create owners, answers, or safety; the same silence returns after the novelty fades [judgment]. Derby and Larsen support varied activities for engagement [2]. Format is therefore a later tactic, not the first move.

## End

I recommend that we repair the loop in four steps, with the diagnosis first.

**Step 1: Diagnose the primary cause.** Domain: Confused, because the cause is unknown; this step gathers the facts needed to classify it. Kind: evaluative; write the rubric before you score. Increment: about two hours of facilitator and lead time. Blocker removed: the team stops treating a guessed cause. Rubric, that is, a weighted scoring of options against criteria [4]:

- Options: no follow-through, no authority, low safety, weak format.
- Criteria and weights: past-item completion rate (3), survey answers (2), who speaks in the room (1).
- Evidence per criterion: notes of the last six retrospectives; a five-question anonymous survey; the facilitator's count of who speaks.
- Threshold: the top option MUST reach 60% of the maximum weighted score, or collect one more data round.

**Step 2: Cap and track improvements.** Domain: Clear, because a best practice exists in the Scrum Guide [1]; apply it. Kind: deterministic. Increment: one standing rule on the retrospective agenda. Blocker removed: improvements die after the meeting. Test, written before the rule starts:

Given the team selects improvements at the end of a retrospective
When the sprint plan is updated
Then at most two improvements stay open
And each improvement has one named owner
And each improvement appears as a task in the sprint plan
And the next retrospective opens with a status check on each open improvement

**Step 3: Open an escalation path.** Domain: Clear, because the intake-and-answer pattern is a standard practice [judgment]. Kind: deterministic. Increment: one visible list and one named sponsor. Blocker removed: items beyond team authority get no answer, so people stop raising them. Test, written first:

Given an improvement needs authority the team does not have
When the facilitator adds it to the escalation list
Then the sponsor answers accept, decline, or question within five working days
And the answer and its date are visible to the team

**Step 4: Probe safety with anonymous input.** Domain: Complex, because cause and effect show only afterward; run it as a capped probe. Kind: stochastic; write the acceptance test before the first session. Increment: three consecutive retrospectives with anonymous written input, one extra facilitator hour each. Blocker removed: the team cannot tell whether silence means fear. Budget: three sessions. Acceptance test:

- Metric: new issues raised per retrospective.
- Baseline: mean count over the last three recorded retrospectives (uses A1).
- Pass threshold: at least twice the baseline.
- Sample size: three retrospectives.
- Stop condition: if the threshold fails after three sessions, stop; safety is not the blocker at this dose.

**Sequence.** Step 1 runs this week. Steps 2 and 3 start at the next retrospective. Step 4 runs in parallel with them and MUST NOT exceed three sessions. Step 2's rule MUST NOT become permanent until Step 1's rubric passes. If Step 1 names a different primary cause (about 30% likely [judgment]), replace Step 2's rule with the matching fix. If Step 1 names low safety as primary, move Step 4 ahead of Step 2.

## Follow-up

Conditions that change the recommendation:

- If A1 fails, Step 1 first creates the record, and Step 4's baseline shifts by three sessions.
- If A2 fails, appoint a sponsor before Step 3.
- If the Step 4 probe passes, keep the anonymous input channel permanently.

Open questions: none; the assumption log carries the unknowns.
