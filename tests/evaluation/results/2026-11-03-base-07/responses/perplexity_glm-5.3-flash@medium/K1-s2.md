## Headline

The connection pool change (max_size 50 to 8, acquire timeout 30 s to 5 s) is the primary cause of the 900 ms p95; raise the pool capacity, restore the timeout and cache TTL, and keep the ORM change.

1. Pool pressure matches the timing and the errors: pool exhausted warnings went from 0 per hour to 1,800 to 2,400 per hour, and 96% of new errors are PoolTimeout after 5 s [1][2][3].
2. The acquire wait carries most of the latency gap: db.pool.acquire p95 rose from 3 ms to 690 ms, while every other span stayed within a few ms of its baseline [2].
3. The cache TTL cut added DB load: the miss rate rose from 12% to 31%, a 2.6 times increase, but the cache span itself stays at 2 to 5 ms [2].
4. The ORM change is measured as safe: the order_lines query span fell from 30 ms to 26 ms at p95 [2].

## Background

Release 2026.10.21 changed three areas: the pool size and acquire timeout, the cache TTL, and an ORM eager load on order lines [1]. After the release, p95 rose from 120 ms to 900 ms, and the error rate rose from 0.02% to 0.40% at a flat request rate of about 405 rps [2].

## Relevance

Pool timeout errors are live now and hit about 1 in 250 requests at peak [2]. Because the release notes make this config pattern repeatable, a capacity guard is needed before the next release.

## Information

**Evidence, compared with the window A baseline:**

- Acquisition: pool acquire p50 1 to 140 ms, p95 3 to 690 ms [2]. All other spans are flat [2]. Therefore the pool queue, not query time or cache time, drives the p95; it accounts for 690 of the 900 ms, 77% [2][judgment].
- Errors: PoolTimeout at 5 s matches the new acquire_timeout_s of 5 [1][3]. Window B errors: 96% PoolTimeout [2].
- Pool saturation: warning rate 0 per hour to 1,800 to 2,400 per hour [3]. The old timeout of 30 s absorbed waiting without failing requests [1][inference from the absent pool errors in window A].
- Database health: CPU peak 38% to 41%, active connections peak 31 to 8 [2]. The DB is not saturated, so the bottleneck sits in the client-side pool [judgment].
- Cache: miss rate 12% to 31% [2]. Misses cause extra DB reads, so the TTL cut raises demand on the small pool; the cache span stays flat [2].
- ORM: order lines span p50 11 to 9 ms, p95 30 to 26 ms [2]. No regression there.

One discrepancy needs care: window A peaked at 31 connections, and window B peaked at 8, equal to the new max_size [1][2]. Because 4 pods each report "8 of 8 in use" [3], a simple sum of 4 × 8 rests expected at exhaustion, not 8. See A2.

**Assumption log:**

A1. The DB per-service connection limit in OPS-412 allows at least 36 connections. Why not measured: the ticket is not in the material. Validate by: read OPS-412 and the DB limit setting. If wrong: keep the pool at or below that limit and rely on Steps 3 and 4 plus shorter connection hold time.

A2. The max_size value applies per process or per pod, not service-wide. Why not measured: the material does not state the pool scope, and the peak of 8 connections conflicts with 4 pods at 8 each. Validate by: read the pool init code and check DB-side connections across pods at peak. If wrong: Step 2 computes size as the limit divided over the process count.

Demand estimate [judgment]: at 405 rps with p50 total 210 ms, holding a connection for whole requests rests at about 85 concurrent; holding only during DB spans rests at about 12. This spread is why Step 1 fixes the limit value before Step 2 sets size.

**Alternative considered:** the ORM selectinload added one query per customer (an N+1 pattern) as the cause. It loses: the order_lines span fell, not rose [2]. Selectinload batches lines into one extra query per customer, not per row [judgment].

## End

2. **Fix the pool config.** Set acquire_timeout_s to 30 and set max_size to fill the limit from Step 1 across pods, with headroom over the pre-release peak of 31 connections. This removes the 690 ms acquire wait and the PoolTimeout failures. Test, written first:

   When the config loader validates the file

   Domain: Clear, because the practice (size the pool above measured concurrency) is known [18].

3. **Restore the cache TTL.** Set default_ttl_s to 300 in config/cache.yaml. This cuts the miss rate from 31% toward the 12% baseline, so DB read demand falls about 2.6 times. Test, written first:
   ```
   Given cache.yaml with default_ttl_s 300, deployed

   ```
   Domain: Clear, because it reverses one measured setting to its known-good value [18].

4. **Run the acceptance test at peak.** Measure 10,000 sampled requests per trace window over 1 h of peak traffic. Accept only when every threshold holds at that sample size:

   - Pool exhausted warnings: 0 (baseline 0 per hour [3]; window B was 1,800 to 2,400 [3]).
   - Cache miss rate ≤ 15% (baseline 12% [2]).
   Domain: Clear, because the measures and thresholds are known and the decision rule is fixed [18].

5. **Keep the ORM change and add a capacity guard.** Add an alert on db.pool.acquire p95 above 50 ms and on pool exhausted warnings above 10 per hour. This adds the guard that the 2026.10.21 release was missing. Test, written first:
   ```

   When acquire p95 stays above 50 ms for 10 min
   Then the on-call channel receives one alert
   ```
   Domain: Clear, because alerting on a measured regression is an established practice [18].
