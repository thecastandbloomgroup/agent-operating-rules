## Headline

Make every retrospective produce exactly one owned change, and review that change at the start of the next retrospective. Supporting points:

1. Retrospectives that produce no change have a broken follow-through loop, so the fix is ownership and review, not better discussion [1].
2. First, audit the last three retrospectives to confirm where the actions die.
3. Then, add an ownership loop and run the discussion formats as a capped, safe-to-fail probe.

## Background

The team attends retrospectives, holds open discussion, and leaves with no changes in place. The request asks for a way to make the meetings produce a result.

## Relevance

Unproductive retrospectives waste team hours and erode belief that the team can improve itself. Fixing the loop recovers that time within one or two meeting cycles.

## Information

**Evidence.** A retrospective that works ends with a few action items, each with an owner, and reviews those items at the next retrospective [1]. Team effectiveness research identifies psychological safety as the leading factor in whether a team can face its own issues [3]. The common failure is not a lack of ideas but a lack of tracked follow-through [1], [judgment].

**Assumption log.**

A1. The last three retrospectives produced written actions with no owner or no review. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: read the notes from the last three meetings. If wrong: the actions have owners, and the cause is likely team safety [3], so run Step 3 first.
A2. Attendance is not the problem; execution is. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: check attendance against the invited count for the last three meetings. If wrong: address scheduling or senior leader attendance before any format change [3].

**Alternative considered.** Replace retrospectives with continuous stop-the-line feedback during work. It loses because the team still needs a scheduled forum to compare notes across tasks [1], and adding ownership to the existing forum costs less than a process replacement [judgment].

## End

Step 1. Audit the last three retrospectives. Increment: one labeled table of past actions. Blocker removed: the team cannot see which part of the loop fails. Cynefin domain: Confused, because the output is needed to classify the failure before deeper work. Kind: deterministic. Test (write first):

Step 2. Install the ownership loop. Increment: from the next meeting on, record one action per retrospective with an owner, a due date, and a tracker entry, and review it first at the next meeting. Blocker removed: actions stop dying between meetings, per the pattern of few, owned, reviewed items [1]. Cynefin domain: Clear, because a known best practice exists. Kind: deterministic. Test (write first):


Step 3. Run a root cause session on the audit data. Dependency: Step 3 MUST NOT start until the test in Step 1 passes. Increment: one meeting that ranks the causes from the Step 1 counts. Blocker removed: the team learns why actions die, and the probe get a target. Cynefin domain: Complicated, because cause is knowable with analysis of the audit counts. Kind: evaluative. Rubric (write first): criteria are frequency (weight 3), owner behavior (weight 2), scope outside team control (weight 2); each scored 1 to 5 with the audit counts as evidence; pass threshold is a ranked list where the top cause has at least 60 percent of the weighted score of the sum.

Step 4. Probe three retrospective formats. Dependency: runs in parallel with Step 3, capped at three meetings, and reverts if the stop condition fires. Increment: rotate the facilitator and the format once per meeting for three meetings. Cynefin domain: Complex, because the effect of a format is visible only after it runs. Kind: stochastic. Acceptance test (write first): metric is percent of actions shipped within two weeks; baseline is the Step 1 completion rate; pass threshold is 15 percentage points above baseline; sample size is three meetings, each with at least one action. Stop condition: revert to the old format if shipped percent falls below baseline. Budget: three meetings, zero new tools.
