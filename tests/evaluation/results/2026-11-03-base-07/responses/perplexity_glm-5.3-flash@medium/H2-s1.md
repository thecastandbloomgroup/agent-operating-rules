## Headline

Stabilize the messaging today, verify the journalist's claim before any public reply, choose the reply posture from the verified fact base, and publish first through a small monitored statement. The supporting points, each one part of the problem, are:

1. The first blocker is not the article; the blocker is three groups that each plan to speak as the company.
2. The reply posture depends on a claim-by-claim verification, so that step decides the rest.
3. The first public output should be a small statement, because large statements in a moving story invite a second news cycle [judgment].

## Background

A journalist published an article stating that the CEO's statement from yesterday is false. Legal, PR, and the board are each messaging the user in parallel, with no agreed owner of the company's voice. The request asks for the next steps.

## Relevance

Uncoordinated replies from Legal, PR, and board members can contradict each other. Each contradiction then becomes a follow-up story that is more expensive to correct than the first article [judgment].

## Information

Sequence the material into four non-overlapping parts. Every addressable part fits one of them:

1. Control: who speaks for the company.
2. Facts: whether the published claim is false, disputed, or set in dropped context.
3. Posture: correct, clarify, or refute.
4. Publication: what ships, and what the reaction shows.

The chance that the article is accurate on its core claim is about as likely as not (33 to 66%). Reason: the request states one side, and articles that pass an editor usually rest on a document [judgment]. So the verification step carries the decision weight in this plan.

The task is partly Confused. The material does not state which sentence the journalist marked as false, so the first pass cannot yet classify the incident.

**Assumption log**

A1. The article and the CEO's statement are retrievable today. Why not measured: the material does not state where they live. Validate by: obtain both documents before the 30-minute call. If wrong: verification slips one day, and only the holding statement ships this cycle.

A2. The CEO's statement was not part of a regulatory filing or disclosure. Why not measured: the material does not state the statement's context. Validate by: Legal screens the context in the 30-minute call. If wrong: disclosure counsel owns the timeline, and Steps 3 and 4 defer to their review.

A3. No further public or mass-internal statements are queued for today. Why not measured: the material does not state the outbound schedule. Validate by: one comms check in the 30-minute call. If wrong: pull those items first, because each one creates a new record for Step 2.

**Alternative considered**

Rebut the article today without waiting for verification. It loses because an unverified rebuttal risks a second false statement, and a company correction carries more weight than an article and so doubles the exposure [judgment].

## End

I recommend that we execute the following four steps in order.

1. **Stabilize the company's voice** (Chaotic, because no shared control exists, so stabilizing comes first; see [1]). Increment: a 30-minute call today. It confirms one owner (Legal approves, PR executes, the board receives status only) and one approved holding line. Blocker it removes: three voices speaking for one company. Test first, kind: deterministic, appendix [2]:

```gherkin
Given the alignment call has ended
When any of Legal, PR, or the board receives a press request
Then each of them replies with the approved holding line
And each of them logs the request in the shared channel
```

2. **Verify the claim claim-by-claim** (Confused, because the incident cannot yet be classified, and this output decides the rest; see [1]). Increment: one analyst builds a table in one session. Each row: the article's sentence, the CEO's statement, the source document, and a verdict of verified false, verified true, disputed, or unverifiable. Blocker it removes: the unknown fact base. Test first, kind: deterministic. This step MUST NOT start until Step 1 ends:

```gherkin
Given the article, the CEO statement, and the source documents
When the analyst compares each row against the source document
Then the table carries a verdict for every row
And Legal signs the signed verdicts
```

Wait, "signed" is not the completion criterion; the Then line reads: "Then the table carries a verdict for every row, and Legal signs off on the table."

3. **Choose the reply posture** (Complicated, because analysis across the three options picks the practice; see [1]). Increment: pick one of correct, clarify, or refute. Blocker it removes: the gap between the fact base and any public wording. Test first, kind: evaluative, Kepner-Tregoe layout [3]. Rubric: legal exposure, weight 0.4, evidence: the Step 2 table. Credibility repair, weight 0.4, evidence: the Step 2 table plus the article's reach. Speed to the next cycle, weight 0.2, evidence: the publication schedule of the outlet. Pass threshold: the posture with the highest total, with no criterion scoring below 2 of 5. This step MUST NOT start until the Step 2 table exists.

4. **Publish the holding line now; the full response is a probe** (Complex, because reader reaction is visible only after publication, so leverage claims are a guess; see A1 and [1]). Increment: the holding line plays at Step 1's end. It buys time. The full response moves only when Step 3 lands. Measure, kind: stochastic. Acceptance test: metric, share of sampled articles that carry the company's version within 48 hours; baseline, 0 of 10, because no company version is published now; pass threshold, 6 of 10 articles; sample size, 10 articles per the next co-ordinated articles. Also run it in parallel with Step 2. Stop condition: a second false claim appears, or Legal flags the wording, then pull the full response and return to the holding line.

Condition that changes this plan: if A2 is false and the statement touched a filing or disclosure, disclosure counsel owns the timeline, and Steps 3 and 4 wait for their written review.

**Follow-up**

1. Which sentence did the journalist mark as false, and what source does the article cite? Use: it sets the depth and tools for Step 2 (see A1).
2. Did the statement touch financial results or a filing? Use: it tests A2.
3. Was the statement live, recorded, or in writing? Use: it decides what Step 2 can compare against.
