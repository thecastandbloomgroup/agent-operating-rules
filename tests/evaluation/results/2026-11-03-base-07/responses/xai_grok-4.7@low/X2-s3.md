Headline
The team should finish one owned change before it adds another.
A retrospective that does not check an action does not change the work.
Follow-through, weak decisions, and low safety are separate breaks and need separate fixes.
The last four meetings show which break is active, so the format should wait.

Background
The team still holds retrospectives, and people attend. After the meeting, the work does not shift. The request is a next step that links the meeting to a change.

Relevance
A meeting with no checked change teaches the team to ignore the next one. Therefore the next meeting should prove one change, not collect more ideas.

Information
Derby and Larsen name five stages, and stage four is decide what to do [1].
The Scrum Guide says the team picks the most helpful changes to raise effectiveness [2].
Kerth asks the group to assume people did the best they could with what they knew [3].

Three breaks fit this report, and they do not overlap [judgment]. First, the team records no owned action. Next, the team records actions but never checks them. Last, few people speak, so the data is thin. Together these cover attend-but-no-shift.

A1. The team has notes from recent retrospectives. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: open the last four notes. If wrong: use recall from the last two meetings.

A2. Nothing shifts means the work outcome, not only attendance. Why not measured: the material does not define the metric. Validate by: name one work metric the last actions targeted. If wrong: measure action completion first.

The strongest alternative is to stop retrospectives and use a standing improvement board. It loses because the team already meets, so one checked change is the cheaper test [judgment].

End
I recommend that we take four steps.

1. Test first, evaluative. Rubric: owner on each action (weight 40), status of the prior action (weight 40), share of attendees who spoke or wrote (weight 20). Each criterion scores 0, 1, or 2. Pass threshold: 4 or more means follow-through. Below 4 means decision quality or safety. Evidence: the meeting notes. Then score the last four retrospectives before any format change. This removes the unknown break. The domain is Confused, because the cause is not yet known. This step gathers what classifies the rest.

2. Test first, deterministic.
Given the meeting is in the decide stage
When the team closes
Then one change has one owner, one date, and one check line
Then keep one change at the close of the next retrospective. This removes a pile of open items. Step 2 MUST NOT start until Step 1 scores 4 or more, or the notes show more than one open action. Domain: Clear, because one owner and one date is a known practice.

3. Test first, deterministic.
Given a change from the prior meeting
When the retrospective starts
Then the owner states done, not done, or blocked before new topics
Then open the next retrospective with that change. This removes actions that disappear between meetings. Step 3 MUST NOT start until Step 2 passes. Domain: Clear, because the check is a fixed step.

4. Test first, stochastic. Metric: people who add at least one note. Baseline: the contributor count from Step 1. Pass threshold: a higher count than the baseline. Sample size: one meeting. Then use one silent-write gather if the safety score is 0. This probes whether silence blocks insight. Leverage is a guess. Domain: Complex, because cause shows only after the probe. Budget: one meeting. Stop if the contributor count does not rise. Do not change other stages in that meeting. Run Step 4 in parallel with Steps 2 and 3 when the safety score is 0.

Follow-up
If A1 is false, replace Step 1 with a 20-minute recall from the facilitator and two attendees. Use the same rubric.
If two checks show done actions but the work metric does not move, replace the change. Do not add a second change.
If the safety probe does not raise the contributor count, change the facilitator before the next format trial.
