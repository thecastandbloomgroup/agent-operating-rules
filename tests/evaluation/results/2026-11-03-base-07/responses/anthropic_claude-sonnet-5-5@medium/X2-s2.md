## Headline

Track retrospective actions as backlog work with an owner and a date, because the likely failure is follow-through, not discussion.

- Actions fail when they have no owner, no date, and no place in the backlog.
- Actions fail when the team has no spare capacity to do them.
- Actions fail when the issue sits outside the team's control.
- Format fatigue is a fourth cause, and a new format alone does not fix the other three.

## Background

The team holds retrospectives (regular meetings that review how the team works). People attend, but no change follows. The request gives no data on past actions, owners, or team size.

## Relevance

Each empty retrospective costs the team its meeting time with no return. Repeated empty meetings also teach people that speaking up has no effect [judgment].

## Information

**Evidence**

E1. The Scrum Guide states that the retrospective exists to plan ways to increase quality and effectiveness.

[1]

E2. The same guide says the most impactful improvements are addressed as soon as possible. It allows the team to add them to the next Sprint Backlog.

[1]

E3. Derby and Larsen split a retrospective into five phases. The last phases are to decide what to do and to close. They favor a small number of chosen items.

[2]

E4. Team behavior is a Complex system, so cause and effect show only afterward. Small probes fit this domain better than a full redesign.

[3]

E5. No team interviews exist yet, so no qualitative themes are available. Step 1 below collects the first data.

E6. It is likely (66 to 100%) that at least one of the first three causes applies [judgment]. Confidence is low, because no team data was seen.

**Assumption log**

A1. The team records actions but completes few. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: count the actions in the last five retrospective notes. If wrong: if no actions are recorded, Step 2 becomes the main fix.

A2. The team has no reserved capacity for improvement work. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: check whether the last sprint plan held time for action items. If wrong: drop the capacity reserve in Step 3.

A3. Notes from past retrospectives exist. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: search the team wiki and chat history. If wrong: rebuild the list from memory in a 15-minute team session.

**Alternative considered**

The strongest alternative is a new retrospective format, such as a new facilitator or new activities. It loses because it addresses only engagement, the least supported of the four causes [judgment]. The three follow-through causes stay unchanged.

## End

I recommend that we do the following, in this order:

1. **Audit the last five retrospectives.**
   - Increment: one 60-minute session that lists every action with its owner, date, and status.
   - Blocker removed: the cause of the stalled change is unknown.
   - Kind: deterministic. Test:
     - Given the notes of the last five retrospectives
     - When one person counts actions, owners, dates, and done status
     - Then a table shows the completion rate in percent and the count of actions with no owner or date
   - Domain: Confused, because the cause is unknown. This step gathers the data to classify it.

2. **Change how every retrospective starts and ends.**
   - Increment: open with a 5-minute review of open actions. Close by entering each new action in the backlog with one owner and one due date.
   - Blocker removed: actions have no home and no owner.
   - Kind: deterministic. Test:
     - Given a retrospective has ended with one chosen action
     - When the facilitator checks the backlog
     - Then the action appears there with one owner and a due date within 14 days
   - Domain: Clear, because this is a known good practice [1].
   - Step 2 can run at the same time as Step 1.

3. **Run a three-retrospective probe.**
   - Increment: cap each retrospective at two actions. Reserve 10% of sprint capacity for them. Send each issue outside the team's control to a named sponsor.
   - Blocker removed: no capacity and no authority to act.
   - Kind: stochastic. Test, written first:
     - Metric: share of actions done by the due date.
     - Baseline: the completion rate from Step 1.
     - Pass threshold: 5 of 6 actions done (83%), and above the baseline.
     - Sample size: 3 retrospectives, 6 actions. The sample is small, so treat a pass as a signal, not proof.
   - Domain: Complex, because team response is visible only afterward. Leverage is a guess.
   - Budget: 10% of sprint capacity for 3 retrospectives. Stop early if the team reports that the reserve harms delivery.
   - Step 3 MUST NOT start until Step 1 gives the baseline.

- If Step 1 shows completion above 80%, follow-through is not the cause. Replace Step 3 with a probe of a new format.
- If A2 is false, remove the capacity reserve from Step 3.
