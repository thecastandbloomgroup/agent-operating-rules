## Headline

Find out why action items do not turn into change, then fix follow-through before you change the meeting format.

- Attendance is intact, so the likely gap is between the meeting and the work that follows it.
- The cause is unproven, so a short audit and a team diagnostic come first.
- A fixed review step and a one-action limit are low-cost fixes that match known practice.
- Each fix has a test, so you can see within three retrospectives whether it works.

## Background

A retrospective is a recurring team meeting. The team reviews how work went and picks improvements. Your team attends these meetings, but no changes result. The request is a plan to restore the link between discussion and change.

## Relevance

Each retrospective that produces nothing costs team time and lowers trust in the meeting. Trust loss makes the next meeting less useful.

## Information

**Evidence**

- A standard retrospective format has five stages: set the stage, gather data, generate insights, decide what to do, and close [1]. Your meetings may stop at the insight stage. [judgment]
- Teams that pick many action items finish few of them. [judgment]
- It is likely (66 to 100%) that the cause is weak follow-through, not the meeting format. Basis: [judgment], no team data yet.

**Assumption log**

A1. The team produces action items, but most are not finished. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: count the items from the last three retrospectives and their status. If wrong: if no items exist, replace Step 4 with a change to the "decide what to do" stage.

A2. The team can change its own process without outside approval. Why not measured: the user has not answered. Validate by: ask the team lead whether any past action item stalled for lack of approval. If wrong: add an escalation step before Step 4.

A3. The team has 5 to 10 people and meets every two weeks. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: check the calendar. If wrong: adjust the sample size in Step 4.

**Alternative considered**

Replace the format with new games or a new facilitator. It loses because attendance is already present and format does not fix follow-through. [judgment] It also costs a session with no baseline to judge it.

## End

I recommend that we take these four actions:

1. **Audit the last three retrospectives.**
   - Increment: one table of every action item, its owner, its due date, and its status. This takes one hour.
   - Blocker removed: the cause is unknown.
   - Kind: deterministic.
   - Cynefin domain: Confused [2], because the cause could be missing owners, missing dates, or lost items.
   - Test, written first [3]:
     - Given the notes of the last three retrospectives
     - When the table is complete
     - Then every action item has an owner, a due date, and a status, and the completion rate is stated

2. **Open every retrospective with a five-minute review of the last action items.**
   - Increment: one agenda line and one board column called "Retro actions". This takes one session.
   - Blocker removed: nobody checks past items.
   - Kind: deterministic.
   - Cynefin domain: Clear, because reviewing past actions is a known practice.
   - Test, written first:
     - Given a retrospective with open action items
     - When the meeting starts
     - Then the facilitator reads each item and its status before any new topic
   - Step 2 MUST NOT start until the test in Step 1 passes. Step 2 runs in parallel with Step 4.

3. **Run a 30-minute written diagnostic.**
   - Increment: each person answers "What stops our retrospective actions from happening?" in writing. Then the team groups the answers into themes and counts the sources per theme [4].
   - Blocker removed: unknown causes beyond follow-through.
   - Kind: evaluative.
   - Cynefin domain: Complicated, because thematic analysis can find the cause.
   - Rubric, written first: criteria are theme support (weight 50%), a disagreeing source named (20%), and a cause the team controls (30%). The pass threshold is 70%. Evidence is the answer sheets.
   - Step 3 MUST NOT start until Step 1 is done, so that the audit can be compared with the themes.

4. **Probe a one-action rule for three retrospectives.**
   - Increment: the team picks one action item, gives it one owner and one date, and puts it in the sprint backlog.
   - Blocker removed: too many items and no place in the work plan.
   - Kind: stochastic.
   - Cynefin domain: Complex, because team response is visible only afterward. Leverage is a guess.
   - Acceptance test, written first: the metric is the share of chosen actions finished by their due date. The baseline is the rate from Step 1. The pass threshold is 100% in at least 2 of 3 retrospectives. The sample size is 3 retrospectives. The budget is 3 retrospective slots. The stop condition is a completion rate below the baseline after 2 retrospectives. Then replace this probe with the top theme from Step 3.
