## Headline

**Name one decision lead now, freeze new public statements, then verify the facts before you choose a response.**

- Three groups give conflicting advice, so one owner must decide.
- The journalist's claim is unverified inside the company, so facts must come before any reply.
- A fast, accurate response limits harm more than a fast denial.
- The response must go out once, with legal, PR, and board sign-off.

## Background

A journalist published that the CEO's statement from yesterday (Nov 3, 2026) was false. Legal, PR, and the board each give you different advice. No one owns the decision.

## Relevance

The story is moving now. Every unaligned message adds a second error to the first.

## Information

**Evidence**

- Cynefin separates work by how clearly cause and effect link [1]. A live crisis with conflicting orders has no clear link, so stabilise first.
- No internal record has been reviewed. Confidence that the claim is true or false: low confidence, no records reviewed.
- Public denial before verification raises exposure if the claim holds. [judgment]
- A single approver shortens review time compared with three parallel reviews. [judgment]

**Assumption log**

A1. You hold authority to convene legal, PR, and the board. Why not measured: the material does not state your role. Validate by: ask the CEO's office to confirm in one message. If wrong: Step 1 moves to the CEO or chief of staff.

A2. The CEO has made no further statement since yesterday. Why not measured: no tool can reach internal channels. Validate by: check the CEO's calendar and social accounts. If wrong: Step 1 also covers withdrawing or aligning that statement.

A3. The company may have legal disclosure duties (for example, if it is publicly listed). Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask legal in Step 3. If wrong: drop the disclosure criterion in Step 3.

**Alternative considered**

The strongest alternative is to deny the article at once and defend the CEO. It loses because no facts are verified. A denial that proves wrong adds a second false statement. [judgment]

## End

I recommend that we take these five actions, in this order:

1. **Stabilise: name one incident lead and freeze outbound statements.**
   - Increment: in the next hour, the CEO or chief of staff names one lead in writing. Only a holding line ("we are reviewing the article") may go out.
   - Blocker removed: three groups issue conflicting orders.
   - Kind: deterministic. Domain: Chaotic, because the story moves hourly and no group's advice is trusted.
   - Test, written first:
     - Given the CEO statement is disputed in a published article
     - When the CEO names an incident lead in writing
     - Then every outbound statement needs that lead's approval

2. **Verify: build a claim table.**
   - Increment: in one session, list each disputed claim next to the CEO's words and the internal record that proves or refutes it. Legal directs the work.
   - Blocker removed: no one knows if the article is right.
   - Kind: evaluative. Domain: Complicated, because records and experts can settle the answer.
   - Rubric, written first (pass at 80 of 100):
     - Every claim is covered: 40 points. Evidence: one row per claim.
     - Each row cites a primary record: 40 points. Evidence: document name and date.
     - Legal reviewed the table: 20 points. Evidence: a recorded sign-off.

3. **Align: decide the response option in one 30-minute call.**
   - Step 3 MUST NOT start until Step 2 scores 80, or two hours pass, whichever comes first.
   - Increment: legal, PR, and the board score three options (correct, clarify, stand by) on a one-page Pugh matrix [3]. The baseline is "stand by".
   - Blocker removed: no shared basis for the decision.
   - Kind: evaluative. Domain: Complicated, because more than one good practice exists and analysis picks one.
   - Rubric, written first: criteria are factual accuracy (weight 40), legal exposure (30), trust of customers and staff (20), board support (10). The option wins if its weighted score beats the baseline and it has no negative on factual accuracy.

4. **Respond: publish one approved statement.**
   - Step 4 MUST NOT start until Step 3 selects an option.
   - Increment: the lead releases one statement and one internal note to staff.
   - Blocker removed: the public record stays wrong or unanswered.
   - Kind: deterministic. Domain: Complicated, because the option chosen in Step 3 sets the wording.
   - Test, written first [2]:
     - Given Step 3 selected an option and the lead approved the text
     - When the statement is released
     - Then it addresses each disputed claim, and legal and PR sign-offs are on record

5. **Probe: track the reaction for 48 hours.** This step runs in parallel from Step 1.
   - Increment: one person logs each new article and post that repeats the disputed claim, every two hours.
   - Blocker removed: no data shows if the response works.
   - Kind: stochastic. Domain: Complex, because the press and public reaction is visible only afterward. Leverage is a guess.
   - Budget: one person, two hours a day. Stop condition: stop at 48 hours, or when no new item appears for 12 hours.
   - Acceptance test, written first: metric is the count of new items repeating the claim per 12 hours. Baseline is the count in the 12 hours before Step 4. Threshold is a 50% drop. Sample size is at least 20 items.

## Follow-up

**Conditions that change the recommendation**

- If A1 is false, replace the incident lead in Step 1 with the person the CEO's office names.
- If Step 2 shows the CEO statement was accurate, replace the "correct" option in Step 3 with "clarify with evidence".
- If A3 is true, add a disclosure deadline check to Step 3 before the call ends.
