## Headline

Find out why action items die, then limit each retrospective to one owned change that the next meeting checks.

- Diagnosis: the team has no data yet on whether action items are not made, not owned, or not finished.
- Mechanism: a small number of owned changes with a review step is a standard fix [1].
- Format: a changed meeting format is a safe experiment, but only after the diagnosis.

## Background

A retrospective is a team meeting that reviews past work to plan improvements. Your team attends, but nothing changes afterward. The material does not say how many action items exist or what happens to them.

## Relevance

Each retrospective without change costs the team meeting time and lowers trust in the next one. Trust is hard to rebuild once people stop expecting results [judgment].

## Information

**Evidence**

- The Scrum Guide says the retrospective exists to plan improvements, and it says the most impactful ones should be addressed soon [2].
- Derby and Larsen end each retrospective by choosing a small set of actions to carry forward [1].
- The cause in your team is unmeasured. Follow-through is the main cause: about as likely as not (33 to 66%) [judgment].
- Cause and effect in team behavior show only afterward, so format changes are Complex work [3].
- Source count: two sources support the follow-through theme. No source disagrees. Neither source describes your team, so confidence is low.

**Assumption log**

A1. The team meets every one to two weeks and has 5 to 10 people. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: check the calendar. If wrong: change the sample size in Step 4.

A2. The team can act on its own action items without outside approval. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask the team in Step 2. If wrong: add an action to escalate blocked items.

A3. The team writes action items but does not finish them. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: Step 1 audit. If wrong: replace Step 3 with a fix for idea generation.

**Alternative considered**

Stop retrospectives for a month and reset. This loses because it removes the only forum for change and does not fix follow-through [judgment].

## End

I recommend that we take these four actions, in this order:

1. **Audit the last three retrospectives.**
   - Increment: list every action item with its owner, due date, and status. One session.
   - Blocker removed: no data on why nothing shifts.
   - Kind: deterministic. Domain: Clear, because counting items has one right method.
   - Test:
     - Given the notes of the last three retrospectives
     - When the team lists every action item
     - Then each item shows an owner, a due date, and a done or not done status, and the completion rate is computed.

2. **Collect the reasons with a short anonymous survey.**
   - Step 2 MUST NOT start until Step 1 is done, so the questions can use the audit results.
   - Increment: three questions to every team member, grouped into themes. One session.
   - Blocker removed: the cause of the stalled changes.
   - Kind: evaluative. Domain: Complicated, because analysis of the answers picks the fix.
   - Rubric: criteria are a theme count (weight 50%), a disagreeing source check (30%), and a link to the audit (20%). The pass threshold is a score of 70%. Each criterion needs the survey answers as evidence.

3. **Run the next retrospective with one change only.**
   - Step 3 MUST NOT start until Step 2 is scored.
   - Increment: the team picks one action item, one owner, and one due date. The next meeting opens by checking it. One session.
   - Blocker removed: unowned and unchecked action items.
   - Kind: deterministic. Domain: Complicated, because good practice exists [1] and the survey picks the variant.
   - Test:
     - Given a retrospective that ends
     - When the team closes the meeting
     - Then exactly one action item has a named owner and a due date, and the next agenda lists it first.

4. **Run a capped probe of a new meeting format.**
   - Run Step 4 in parallel with Step 3. Leverage is a guess.
   - Increment: change the format for three retrospectives, for example by adding a silent writing phase. Budget: 45 extra minutes in total.
   - Blocker removed: stale meeting habits.
   - Kind: stochastic. Domain: Complex, because the effect on behavior shows only afterward.
   - Acceptance test: the metric is the share of action items done by the due date. The baseline is the Step 1 rate. The pass threshold is the baseline plus 25 percentage points. The sample size is three retrospectives. Stop the probe early if the rate falls below the baseline after two retrospectives. The small sample gives low confidence, so repeat before you adopt the format.

If A3 is false, replace Step 3 with a fix for idea generation.
