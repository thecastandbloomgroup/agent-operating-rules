## Relevance

`print` writes plain text with no level, time, or source name. The `logging` module adds all three and lets you filter by level. A wrong setup can hide every message, so the first step needs care.

**Setup block** (place at the entry point):

```python
import logging
import sys

logging.basicConfig(
    level=logging.INFO,
    stream=sys.stdout,
    format="%(asctime)s %(levelname)s %(name)s: %(message)s",
)
logger = logging.getLogger(__name__)
```

**Conversion rules**

- `print("Started")` becomes `logger.info("Started")`.
- `print("Port", port)` becomes `logger.info("Port %s", port)`.
- `print(f"User {uid} in")` becomes `logger.info("User %s in", uid)`.
- A call with `sep=`, `end=`, or `file=` needs a manual edit.

**Alternative considered**

The strongest alternative is one regex that swaps `print(` for `logger.info(`. It loses because multi-argument calls and keyword arguments change meaning under logging (see the format-string fact above).
