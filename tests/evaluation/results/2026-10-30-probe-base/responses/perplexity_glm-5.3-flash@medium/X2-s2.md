Headline

Retros stopped producing change because the actions from them have no owner, no date, and no review in the next session. Fix the follow-through loop before you change the retro format. The plan has four parts: measure what happened to past actions, commit to at most one action per retro, open each retro with that action's result, and escalate blockers that exceed team authority.

Background

Your team holds retrospectives, people attend, and no work practice changes afterward. The request is a method to make retros produce real changes.

Relevance

Retrospective time is wasted when it ends as discussion only. A team that fixes follow-through gets value from time it already spends.

Information

A retrospective needs to end with a plan the team owns, not only a list of observations [1]. The reported symptom, "nothing shifts", points at the close-out and review steps, not at idea generation [judgment].

Assumption log:

A1. Retro attendance is stable and meetings run on a schedule. Why not measured: the query does not state the schedule. Validate by: check the calendar for the last three sessions. If wrong: treat attendance as the first problem, not follow-through.
A2. The team holds authority to change some of its own work practices. Why not measured: the query does not state team autonomy. Validate by: ask the lead for one change the team made without outside approval. If wrong: move Step 4 forward and get management sponsorship first.
A3. Someone writes notes in each retro. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: look for notes from the last session. If wrong: add a note-taker to Step 1.

Alternative considered: rotate retro formats, for example new games or prompts. That choice loses because the symptom is a follow-through failure, so new formats would raise idea count and still produce zero closed actions [judgment].

End

I recommend that we fix the follow-through loop in this order.

Step 1 (Confused). Log the outcome of each action from the last three retros: raised, assigned, done, or dropped. Increment: one status table, built in one session. Blocker it removes: no shared record of what happens to actions, so the team cannot classify the failure. Test (deterministic): Given the notes from a past retro, When I fill the table, Then each action carries exactly one status. Domain: Confused, so this step only gathers data and cannot claim leverage.

Step 2 (Clear). Adopt the one-action rule: close each retro with at most one committed change, with one owner and one date. Increment: apply the rule in the next retro. Capability it adds: every session ends in a closed commitment. Test (deterministic): Given a retro is ending, When the record is written, Then it contains at most one action with one owner and one date. Domain: Clear, because a best practice applies directly [1].

Step 3 (Clear). Open the next retro with a 10 minute review of the previous action, then raise new topics. Increment: one standing agenda item. Capability it adds: closed actions become visible, so skipping them is not free. Test (deterministic): Given a retro starts, When the agenda runs, Then the review item appears before new topics. Domain: Clear. Step 3 MUST NOT start until the test in Step 2 passes.

Step 4 (Complicated, stochastic test). After four retro cycles, compute the share of actions completed. Baseline: the measured rate from Step 1. Threshold: 50 percent at a sample of four cycles. If the rate is below the threshold, list the blockers that exceed team authority and take that list to management. Increment: one review meeting after four cycles. Blocker it removes: silently exceeding the team's authority, which no amount of retro method can fix. Test (stochastic): metric is the completion percentage, baseline from Step 1, pass threshold 50 percent, sample size four cycles. Domain: Complicated, because analysis of the recorded rate picks the next practice.

Steps 2 and 3 run in parallel with Step 4. Step 4 needs four cycles after Step 3 starts.

Follow-up

Conditions that change the recommendation: if assumption A2 is false, insert a management sponsorship step before Step 2 and delay Step 4 no longer. If completion stays above 50 percent, keep the loop as is and stop adding process.
