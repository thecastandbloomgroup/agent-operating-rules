## Headline

Stabilize who speaks first, establish the true record second, then decide the correction with legal and publish one tested message.

- One spokesperson and one statement end the parallel messaging today.
- A verifiable record decides whether the CEO statement was inaccurate or only disputed.
- Legal and PR then weigh correction options against legal exposure.
- You cannot draft the public response before the record is verifiable.

## Background

A journalist published that a statement from your CEO yesterday was false. Legal, PR, and the board now send messages with no alignment. This is a Chaotic situation in Cynefin terms, since cause and effect and the reaction of the press are not yet visible [18]. Stabilization comes before analysis.

## Relevance

The next hours fix the cost of the correction. A wrong early statement doubles the harm; a held statement lets the record control the story [judgment].

## Information

**Evidence.** The material contains no article, no transcript, and no message from legal or PR. Every factual claim below is therefore an assumption or judgment. The probability that the statement was materially inaccurate, and not only disputed, is about as likely as not (33 to 66%). The article names specific falsehoods, so the risk of silence is high [no source].

**Assumption log.**

- A1. The CEO statement was materially inaccurate. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: compare each claim in the article with the transcript line by line. If wrong: the Step 4 message becomes a rebuttal with evidence.
- A2. No regulator or litigant has set a response deadline. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask legal today for all deadlines and disclosure duties. If wrong: the legal step moves first and its output constrains Step 3.
- A3. Staff will follow an internal directive to forward press requests. Why not measured: I cannot see internal channels. Validate by: one email, then ask comms to log forwarded requests. If wrong: the board issues the directive before any step runs.

**Alternative considered.** Respond immediately with a public rebuttal without the verified record. It loses because a rebuttal that later turns out wrong is reported as a second falsehood, and that pattern is safer to avoid [judgment]. Therefore the record comes before public response.

## End

I recommend that we run the following steps in this order. Steps 1 and 2 are safe for today. Step 3 MUST NOT start before the record in Step 2 is verified. Start Step 4 in parallel with Step 3, capped by its budget.

1. **Stabilize the message (Chaotic, deterministic).** Increment: in the next 3 hours, name one spokesperson, freeze all other channels, and send staff one line to forward press requests. Blocker removed: the parallel messages from legal, PR, and the board, which you cannot debug while they run. Test first: Given a press request, when any employee other than the named spokesperson receives it, then that employee forwards it to the spokesperson and gives no comment.

2. **Establish the true record (Confused, deterministic; gathers what Step 3 needs).** Increment: one working session; assemble the full transcript of the statement, the article, and every source document behind both. Blocker removed: the decision between correction and rebuttal, which waits on facts. Test first: Given the article, when each factual claim in it is matched to a source document, then each claim is labeled false, true, or unverifiable, with the document named.

3. **Weigh correction options (Complicated, evaluative).** Increment: one working session with legal and PR; score the options: full correction plus error acknowledgment, correction plus defense, or rebuttal with evidence. Blocker removed: the unmade response choice. Test first: a Kepner-Tregoe rubric with weighted criteria: legal exposure 40%, credibility with press 30%, board alignment 20%, speed 10%; pass threshold: 70 of 100; evidence per criterion: the record from Step 2 and the deadlines from A2 [15].

4. **Publish and monitor (Complex, stochastic, run as a capped probe) [18].** Increment: publish one message and hold it for 48 hours. Blocker removed: the news cycle uncertainty, which only observation can settle. Test first: metric, count of follow-up stories that repeat or drop the false-claim framing; baseline, the current framing; pass threshold, at least half of sampled stories drop the framing; sample size, 20 stories over 48 hours; stop condition, a legal finding in A2 that requires removal, then pause publication. Budget: no new statements during the window.

Step 4 depends on the choice in Step 3. If Step 3 selects a rebuttal, replace the published message and keep the monitor unchanged.

## Follow-up

Open questions (answers unlock the steps above):

1. Which claims does the article show as false, and do you hold the transcript? This decides Step 2 effort and A1.
2. What duties and deadlines does legal state under A2? This can reorder the steps.
3. Does the board require approval before the public statement in Step 3? This sets the pass threshold for credibility.

Conditions that change this recommendation: if A1 is false, replace the Step 3 options with rebuttal plus evidence. If A2 shows a deadline inside 24 hours, run legal before step 1.
