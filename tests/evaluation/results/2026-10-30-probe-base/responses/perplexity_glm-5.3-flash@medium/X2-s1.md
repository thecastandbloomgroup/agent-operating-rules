## Headline

Decide to run the retros as a closed loop: end each retro with one owned action and score it at the start of the next one.

1. Retros stop producing change when they end with several vague actions and no owner, so nothing happens between meetings [1].
2. The one-action rule makes completion visible, because the team can count done actions against actions written.
3. A completion rate that stays above 80% while problems repeat means the causes sit outside the team, so escalate them with data [3].

## Background

Your retros meet, people attend, and no change follows. The request is a plan to make retros produce action again.

## Relevance

A retro that changes nothing costs the team two hours per meeting [judgment] and teaches people that raising problems is useless. That lesson lowers the quality of what gets said next time.

## Information

A reviewer would first ask: why do retros fail to produce change? The causes split into four parts that do not overlap and cover the whole case:

1. **Output**: actions are vague, unowned, or too many.
2. **Follow-through**: nothing tracks actions between meetings.
3. **Input**: people do not name the real problems [2].
4. **Scope**: the causes sit outside the team's authority.

The written retro cycle ends with a small set of actions and a plan to check them at the start of the next retro [1]. Teams skip that check, so the list becomes the deliverable instead of the change [1]. Theme: no owner plus no follow-through kills actions. Two sources support it [1] and the pattern matches your report [judgment].

Theme: what gets said depends on psychological safety, the shared belief that a team is safe for interpersonal risk [2]. One source supports it [2]. It matters anyway: in a study of 180 Google teams, psychological safety was the leading difference between high and low performing teams [3]. So if your completion rate rises and the same problems still appear, treat input or scope as the cause, and do not abandon rulos.

Measured claims you can test: the count of owned, dated, done actions from your last four retros. That count replaces the vague report "nothing shifts".

Assumption log:

- A1. Past retro actions are written down somewhere. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: read the notes from the last four retros. If wrong: add a note-taking step before everything else.
- A2. The team controls part of its process, for example its tools or workflow. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask the team in the next retro. If wrong: the escalation path in step 4 dominates the plan.

Alternative considered: change the format or the facilitator, for example a new retro template. It loses because it changes the conversation and not the loop between meetings, and the loop is where actions die [judgment].

## End

I recommend that we take four actions in this order.

1. **Audit (Clear, deterministic).** In one session, read the notes from the last four retros. Count actions written, actions with an owner, actions with a due date, and actions marked done. Blocker removed: the vague report becomes a measured cause. Test, written first: Given the notes from the last four retros, when we count each category, Then we hold four numbers on one page. This step MUST complete before step 2 starts.
2. **One-action rule (Clear, deterministic).** At the end of each retro, write exactly one action with one owner and a due date before the next retro. Blocker removed: the follow-through loop now exists. Test: Given a retro, when it ends, Then the record shows one action, one owner, and one due date.
3. **Completion probe (Complex, stochastic).** Track the percent of past actions marked done for four retros. Metric: completion rate. Baseline: the audit number from step 1. Pass threshold: 80% per retro [judgment]. Sample: all actions, four retros. Stop condition: below 50% after two retros, stop and diagnose psychological safety instead, because the cause is input or scope. Budget: four retros, about two hours total. The leverage of this step is a guess [judgment]. Do not judge the plan before four retros complete.
4. **Escalation (Complicated, evaluative).** If problems repeat at an 80% completion rate, send each repeat problem to management with its recursion count. Blocker removed: out-of-scope causes reach people who own them. Rubric, written first: criteria are recursion count (weight 3), outside team authority (weight 3), and data attached (weight 1); pass threshold is a score of 5 or more of 7. Score each named problem against the retro notes.

Condition that changes the recommendation: if step 1 finds zero recorded actions, insert a note-taker step before step 2, because the audit shows the loop never had a record.
