## Headline

Fix the follow-through loop first, because retrospectives that end without owned, checked actions stop producing change.

- The cause is unknown, so measure what happened to past actions before you change the format.
- Fewer actions with one named owner and one check date are more likely to get done than a long list [judgment].
- Review the last actions at the start of each retro, so the retro earns trust.
- Escalate blockers the team cannot fix, because repeat themes often sit outside the team's control [judgment].

## Background

Your team holds retrospectives (regular meetings to review how the team works). People attend, but nothing changes. You ask what to do.

The material gives no data on past actions, team size, or meeting format.

## Relevance

Each retro that ends with no change teaches the team that the meeting has no value. Attendance then falls, and the signal you need to improve disappears.

## Information

Evidence:

1. Retrospective practice guides advise a small number of actions and a review of earlier actions. See Derby and Larsen [1]. This is a secondary source for the practice, not a measurement of your team.
2. Cynefin (a model that sorts problems by how clear cause and effect are) classifies team behavior as Complex when cause is unclear [2]. So the plan uses a capped probe, not a full redesign.
3. Confidence that follow-through is the main cause: low confidence, no team data and one secondary source.
4. Disagreeing view: the cause may be low trust or low team authority, not weak follow-through. The audit in Step 1 tests this.

Assumption log:

A1. The team creates actions in retros but does not finish them. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: count actions from the last three retros and their status. If wrong: skip Step 3 and focus on the cause the audit finds.

A2. The team can change at least some of what it raises. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: tag each past theme as inside or outside team control. If wrong: Step 4 becomes the top action.

A3. The team has about six to ten people and meets every one to two weeks. Why not measured: the user has not answered. Validate by: check the calendar. If wrong: change the sample size in Step 2.

Alternative considered: change the retro format (new games, new facilitator). It loses because a new format does not fix missing owners or checks. That is a [judgment] until the audit shows the cause.

## End

I recommend that we take these steps in order:

1. **Audit past actions.** Increment: list the actions from the last three retros with owner, date, and status, in one session. Blocker removed: we do not know why nothing shifts. Cynefin: Confused, so this gathers what we need to classify the rest. Kind: deterministic. Test: Given the last three retro notes, When we count actions, Then each action has a status and a control tag (inside or outside the team). Step 2 and Step 4 MUST NOT start until this test passes.

2. **Run a capped probe.** Increment: in the next retro, commit to at most two actions. Each has one owner, one due date, and one check. Blocker removed: no one owns the outcome. Cynefin: Complex, so this is a safe-to-fail probe with a budget of three retros. Leverage is a guess. Kind: stochastic. Test: metric is the share of committed actions done by the due date. Baseline is the rate from Step 1. Pass threshold is 80%, or the baseline plus 30 points if higher. Sample size is three retros, which is small, so treat a pass as weak evidence. Stop condition: stop after two retros if fewer than half of the actions are done.

3. **Review old actions first.** Increment: add a five-minute review of last retro's actions to the agenda, starting at the next retro. Blocker removed: no visible link between retros. Cynefin: Clear, since the practice is known [1]. Kind: deterministic. Test: Given a retro starts, When the facilitator opens the agenda, Then each prior action shows done, open, or dropped before new topics begin. Run this in parallel with Step 2.

4. **Escalate outside blockers.** Increment: take the themes tagged outside team control in Step 1 to the person who can act, with one written request. Blocker removed: the team keeps raising issues it cannot fix. Cynefin: Complicated, because the choice of who to ask needs analysis of who holds the authority. Kind: evaluative. Rubric: criteria are authority to act (weight 50%), cost to the team of the delay (30%), and speed of reply (20%). Pass threshold is a weighted score of 3 out of 5. Each criterion needs one piece of evidence, such as an org chart or a past decision. Step 4 MUST NOT start until Step 1 passes.

## Follow-up

Conditions that change the recommendation:

- If A1 is false, replace Step 2 with a short anonymous survey on why people stay quiet.
- If most themes are outside team control (A2), move Step 4 ahead of Step 2.
