## Headline

Find out why actions die, then limit each retrospective to one owned, dated action that the team reviews first at the next meeting.

- Retrospectives that produce no change usually fail at follow-through, not at discussion.
- A count of past action items shows the real failure point within one session.
- A small, visible commitment rule fixes the most likely cause at low cost.
- A format change is a test, not a fix, so cap it.

## Background

Your team holds retrospectives (regular meetings where a team reviews its own work). People attend, but no change follows. You asked what to do.

The material gives no data on team size, meeting format, or action tracking.

## Relevance

Each empty retrospective costs the team meeting time and lowers trust in the next one. Trust falls with each repeat, so the problem grows.

## Information

**Evidence**

- The Agile principles say a team reflects at regular intervals, then tunes and adjusts its behavior. Reflection without adjustment misses the purpose. [1]
- Derby and Larsen describe a retrospective as a cycle that ends with action and follow-up, not with discussion. [2]
- Your team's cause is likely lack of follow-through (66 to 100%). Basis: medium confidence, one book, no data from your team. [judgment]
- Three other causes exist: wrong topics, low safety to speak, and actions too large. These do not overlap with follow-through, and the audit in Step 1 separates them. [judgment]

**Assumption log**

A1. The team records action items somewhere. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask for the last three retro notes. If wrong: Step 1 becomes a recall exercise with the team.

A2. The team can change its own process without outside approval. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask the team lead. If wrong: add an escalation step for actions that need approval.

A3. The retro is 45 to 90 minutes. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: check the calendar. If wrong: adjust the 10-minute review slot in Step 3.

**Alternative considered**

Replace the facilitator or the format first. It loses because a new format does not fix missing owners or dates. Format change is also hard to measure with few meetings. [judgment]

## End

I recommend that we take these four actions in order.

1. **Audit past actions.** Increment: count the action items from the last three retrospectives and mark each as done, open, or dropped. Blocker removed: we do not know where actions die. Kind: deterministic. Domain: Complicated, because the count needs analysis to pick the fix.
   - Test: Given the notes of the last three retrospectives, When we count the items, Then each item has one status and the totals match the item list.

2. **Set a one-action rule.** Increment: at the next retrospective, the team picks one action with one owner and one due date, and puts it in the team backlog. Blocker removed: shared ownership, which means no ownership. Kind: deterministic. Domain: Clear.
   - Test: Given a finished retrospective, When we read the backlog, Then it holds one new item with an owner and a date.
   - Step 2 MUST NOT start until Step 1 shows whether actions were dropped or never recorded.

3. **Review first.** Increment: the next retrospective opens with a 10-minute check of the previous action. Blocker removed: no visible consequence for dropped actions. Kind: deterministic. Domain: Clear.
   - Test: Given the agenda, When the retrospective starts, Then the first item is the status of the last action.
   - Step 3 MUST NOT start until the test in Step 2 passes.

4. **Probe a format change.** Run in parallel with Steps 2 and 3. Increment: change the format for two retrospectives, for example focus on one problem instead of a broad review. Kind: stochastic. Domain: Complex, so this is a safe-to-fail probe. Leverage is a guess.
   - Budget: two retrospectives.
   - Stop condition: stop at once if attendance or speaking drops.
   - Acceptance test: metric is the share of actions done by the next retrospective. Baseline comes from Step 1. Threshold is a higher share than baseline in both runs. Sample size is two, so treat a pass as weak evidence.

If A1 is false, replace Step 1 with a 15-minute team recall session and rebuild the list from memory.
