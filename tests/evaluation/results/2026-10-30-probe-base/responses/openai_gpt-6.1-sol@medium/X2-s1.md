## Background

Your team attends retrospectives, but you report no resulting changes. [1] That describes the outcome, but it does not identify the cause.

## Relevance

Another discussion format does not directly address the gap between agreement and action. [judgment] Therefore, test that gap before redesigning the meeting.

## Information

### What to check

Use these four stages to locate the first failure. They form a practical diagnostic framework. [judgment]

| Stage | Evidence to seek | Failure signal |
|---|---|---|
| Agreement | One specific change | Broad wishes, such as “communicate better” |
| Commitment | An owner and reserved time | Everyone agrees, but nobody commits |
| Execution | Completed work or a recorded blocker | The change repeatedly loses priority |
| Verification | A result compared with a baseline | The team declares completion without checking the effect |

The Scrum Guide identifies useful changes as the purpose of a retrospective and calls for prompt action on impactful improvements. [2]

Also check whether people can describe the real problem without personal risk. Edmondson’s team study links psychological safety with team learning behavior. [3] If people fear consequences, a public request for more candor is the wrong first intervention. [judgment]

**Confidence:** Moderate confidence in this approach because it tests observable commitments; low confidence about your specific cause without past action records.

## End

I recommend that we use the next two retrospectives to test one complete path from agreement to verified change.

### 1. Audit the last three retrospectives

**Test first:** Given the available records, when the audit ends, then each proposed change has a status and an identified failure stage.

**Action:** Spend 20 minutes recording each change, owner, reserved time, status, and result. Mark absent evidence as “not recorded.”

This removes uncertainty about where action stops. If no records exist, reconstruct the latest example together.

**Kind:** Deterministic, meaning fixed acceptance criteria.  
**Domain:** Clear, meaning a known procedure applies.

### 2. Create one action card

**Test first:** Given one stalled change, when its card is complete, then it names an owner, capacity, deadline, baseline, and success measure.

**Action:** After Step 1 passes, spend 15 minutes completing this card for the most recent change within the team’s control:

> By **[date]**, **[owner]** will **[specific change]**, using **[reserved time]**.  
> The current result is **[baseline]**. Success means **[observable result]**.  
> If blocked, **[named person]** will resolve or escalate the blocker.

This replaces shared agreement with a checkable commitment. Keep only one active retrospective change during this trial. [judgment]

**Kind:** Deterministic.  
**Domain:** Clear.

### 3. Run a limited trial and inspect the result

**Acceptance test first:**
- **Metric:** Share of commitments completed and checked against their success measure.
- **Baseline:** The share found in Step 1; if unavailable, record “baseline unavailable.”
- **Sample:** Two consecutive retrospective cycles, with one commitment per cycle.
- **Pass threshold:** Both commitments receive a result check by their deadlines.

**Action:** After Step 2 passes, run the trial. Begin each retrospective with a five-minute review of the previous commitment.

This tests whether the team can complete the action process, not whether every attempted change succeeds. [judgment]

**Kind:** Stochastic, meaning outcomes vary.  
**Domain:** Complex, meaning the trial reveals cause and effect.

Cap administration at 30 minutes per cycle, excluding the agreed change itself. Stop if the change causes harm or exceeds its agreed capacity. Leverage remains a guess until results exist.
