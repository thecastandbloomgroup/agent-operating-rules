Turn on gzip compression for the API. It will cut the average response from about 400 KB to roughly 40 to 80 KB [judgment], and the CPU cost is small for typical server loads [judgment]. Support this with three points:

- JSON text repeats keys, spaces, and digits, so it compresses about 5 to 10 times. [judgment]
- Enable gzip at compression level 4 to 6, with a minimum size threshold, so trivial responses skip the work.
- Exclude content that is already compressed, such as images, to avoid wasted CPU.

Background: the team asked whether to enable gzip for an API whose JSON responses average 400 KB per response. Relevance: 400 KB per call adds transfer time and bandwidth cost on every request, so this change pays off at once.

Information: The gains come from the format, not from luck. JSON stores the same key names and separators on every record, and compressors remove that repetition. A careful review would ask three things, and the material answers none of them, so they stay as assumptions. The CPU cost of level 5 compression is one pass per response, and decompression on the client is cheap. [inference] Compared with the baseline of sending 400 KB of raw JSON, gzip on the same payload sends about one tenth of the bytes over the network. [judgment] The `Content-Encoding: gzip` header tells the client how to decode the body. [1]
