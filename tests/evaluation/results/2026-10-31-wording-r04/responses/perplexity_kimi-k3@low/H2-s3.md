# Headline

Stabilise the response with one spokesperson and one verified fact base before anyone else speaks. First, freeze all external statements, because conflicting messages compound the damage. Second, assemble one small response team with one decision maker. Third, verify whether the journalist's claim about the CEO's statement is true, since the truth determines everything after.

# Background

A journalist published a claim that your CEO's statement yesterday was false. Legal, PR, and the board are messaging you without alignment, so there is no single response owner or fact base.

# Relevance

In the first hours of a public dispute, conflicting statements from inside the company create a second story. That story is usually worse than the first [judgment]. Speed matters, but accuracy matters more.

# Information

The situation is unverified on three facts. The truth of the claim, the exact wording of the CEO's statement, and the evidence the journalist holds are all unknown.

Assumption log:

`A1. The CEO's statement and the article exist and can be retrieved within one hour. Why not measured: the material does not contain them. Validate by: pull both documents now. If wrong: scope widens to figure out what was said.`

`A2. No regulator or court is involved yet. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask legal counsel in the first call. If wrong: add a Chaotic legal hold as Step 1, before any public response.`

`A3. You have authority to convene legal, PR, and the board. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: confirm with the CEO or board chair today. If wrong: escalate to whoever has that authority.`

Evidence: crisis communication research finds that a single, consistent voice reduces reputational damage more than speed alone [1]. One source supports this theme, no source disagrees. Confidence: medium, one secondary source.

Alternative considered: respond publicly at once to correct the record. It loses because responding before verification risks a second false statement, which is the highest-damage outcome [judgment].

# End

I recommend that we:

1. Freeze all external statements for four hours. Tell legal, PR, and the board that only you speak externally until the fact base exists. This removes the alignment blocker. Test (deterministic): Given the freeze message is sent, When any inquiry arrives, Then one designated person answers with one holding line. Cynefin domain: Chaotic. This step comes first.
2. Convene one 30 minute call with one decision maker, one legal counsel, and one PR lead. Pull the CEO's exact statement and the article. This creates the fact base. Test (deterministic): Given both documents, When the call ends, Then three outputs exist: what was said, what is alleged, what is verified. Domain: Chaotic. Step 2 MUST NOT start until Step 1 is sent.
3. Classify the gap. If the journalist is wrong and provable, prepare a sourced correction. If the CEO's statement was false, prepare a correction that states the fact without speculation about intent. If unverifiable today, state only what is confirmed. Test (evaluative): rubric with three criteria, factual accuracy (50%), legal risk (30%), consistency with the documented record (20%). Pass threshold: sign-off from legal and the decision maker. Domain: Complicated. This step MUST NOT start until Step 2 passes.
4. Run one Complex probe in parallel, capped: have PR sample reactions from two channels for 24 hours. Stop condition: any new factual claim appears, which triggers Step 3 again. Budget: two hours of staff time. Leverage here is a guess, so cap it. Domain: Complex.

If A2 is wrong and a regulator is involved, replace Steps 3 and 4 with legal-led litigation hold steps and no public statement.

# Follow-up

Conditions that change the recommendation: regulator involvement (see A2), discovery that the CEO's statement was knowingly false, or the journalist publishing more evidence.
