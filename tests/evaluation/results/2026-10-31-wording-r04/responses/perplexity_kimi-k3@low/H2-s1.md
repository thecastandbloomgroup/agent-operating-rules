# Headline

Treat the next 24 hours as a fact-verification crisis, not a messaging crisis. First, freeze all public statements. Second, build one verified timeline. Third, align Legal, PR, and the board on that single timeline before anyone speaks again.

# Background

Yesterday (October 31, 2026) the CEO made a public statement. Today a journalist published that it was false. Legal, PR, and the board are contacting you with no shared position.

# Relevance

Unaligned responses from leadership create more damage than the original article [judgment]. Regulators and courts treat fast retractions differently from slow corrections, so the next hours matter.

# Information

Assumptions made, because the material does not answer them:

A1. The article's claim is unverified in either direction. Why not measured: no tool can reach the underlying facts. Validate by: pull the source data behind the CEO's statement. If wrong: the tone changes, not the sequence.

A2. The CEO's statement is on the record and attributed. Why not measured: material does not state it. Validate by: read the original text. If wrong: the response scope shrinks to an internal correction.

A3. You can convene the three groups today. Why not measured: material does not state it. Validate by: send one meeting invite now. If wrong: appoint one decision owner instead.

Evidence: crisis communication research finds that a fast, factual acknowledgment reduces reputational loss compared with defensiveness or silence [judgment, no verified source in session].

Alternative considered: issue a rebuttal immediately. It loses because a rebuttal based on unverified facts risks a second false statement, which compounds liability [judgment].

# End

I recommend that we:

1. **Freeze all external statements now.** Scope: one instruction to Legal, PR, and the board within the hour. It removes the blocker of contradictory replies. Test (deterministic): Given the freeze notice is sent, When any group receives a press inquiry, Then they hold and refer to you. Domain: **Chaotic**, stabilise first.

2. **Build one verified timeline in a single working session.** Pull the source data behind the CEO's statement and the journalist's claim. Compare them line by line. Test (deterministic): Given both sources, When each factual claim is checked, Then it has a status of verified, disputed, or unknown, with a reference. Domain: **Confused**, its output decides everything else. Step 3 MUST NOT start until this step finishes.

3. **Decide the posture with a weighted rubric.** Options: confirm, correct, or contest. Rubric: legal exposure (40%), factual support (30%), stakeholder impact (30%). Test (evaluative): score each option against the timeline from Step 2. Threshold: the option must lead by 10 points. Domain: **Complicated**.

4. **Issue one aligned statement, only after Step 3 passes.** One spokesperson, one message. Test (deterministic): Given the statement, When Legal and the board review it, Then both approve in writing before release. Domain: **Clear**.

5. **Run a capped sentiment probe in parallel.** Monitor press and social reaction for 48 hours with a budget of 2 analyst-hours per day. Stop condition: new verifiable facts appear, then return to Step 2. Domain: **Complex**, leverage is a guess.

# Follow-up

If A1 shows the statement was accurate, replace Step 4 with a documented rebuttal and a correction request to the outlet. One open question: what, specifically, did the CEO claim and on what evidence? The answer determines whether Step 3 selects correct or contest.
