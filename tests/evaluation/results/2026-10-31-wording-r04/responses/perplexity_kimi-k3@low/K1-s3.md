# Headline

Revert the connection pool settings in `config/db.yaml`; that single change explains the regression, and the cache TTL cut is a secondary amplifier.

- Pool acquire p95 rose from 3 ms to 690 ms and now dominates total latency, while query time stayed flat [3].
- Pool max size fell from 50 to 8, but the service still runs 32 workers, so threads queue for connections [2].
- The acquire timeout fell from 30 s to 5 s, which turns the queue into the 503 errors [2], [3].
- Cache misses rose from 12% to 31% because the TTL fell from 300 s to 60 s, which sends more load to the starved pool [2], [3].

## Background

Release 2026.10.21 changed three things: the ORM layer, the cache config, and the pool config. After it, p95 latency rose from 120 ms to 900 ms, and pool warnings rose from zero per hour to 1,800-2,400 per hour [1], [2].

## Relevance

96% of the new errors are PoolTimeout failures, so customers see 503s under load. Traffic is flat, so the cause is internal, not demand [3].

## Information

Evidence, per change:

1. **Pool config (primary cause).** Active DB connections fell from a peak of 31 to 8. p50 acquire rose from 1 ms to 140 ms [3]. With 8 connections and 32 workers, queuing theory predicts long waits even at load well below capacity [judgment].
2. **Cache TTL (amplifier).** TTL 300 s to 60 s raised the miss rate from 12% to 31%. At 405 requests per second peak, that is roughly 77 extra DB hits per second arriving at a pool with 8 connections [2], [3].
3. **ORM change (not the cause).** `selectinload` adds one `order_lines` query, but that query runs at 9 ms p50. Total query time in B is about 29 ms, near A's 29 ms. DB server CPU is flat at 38-41% [3].

Assumption log:

`A1. The DB connection limit in ticket OPS-412 permits more than 8 connections per service. Why not measured: the material does not state the limit. Validate by: check the ticket or ask ops. If wrong: keep max_size low and instead raise it per below or cut worker threads to 2 per pod (8 total).`

Alternative considered: raise TTL only. Faster to deploy, but it cuts the miss rate, not the 8-connection wall. Traces show even cache hits queue behind acquire, so latency stays high [3].

## End

1. **Revert `pool.max_size` to 50 and `acquire_timeout_s` to 30 in `config/db.yaml`, then redeploy.** Removes the queue, the largest blocker. Cynefin: Clear.
