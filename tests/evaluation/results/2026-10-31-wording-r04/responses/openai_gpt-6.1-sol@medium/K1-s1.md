## Headline

Treat connection acquisition as the main latency bottleneck, but verify the database limit before changing pool capacity.

- Pool-acquire p95 rose from 3 ms to 690 ms while request p95 rose from 120 ms to 900 ms. [1]
- Pool exhaustion now causes failures, not just slower responses. [2]
- The release reduced pool capacity and cache lifetime, but the new database limit makes a full rollback unsafe without checks. [3]

## Background

The release changed three paths: database connections, cache lifetime, and order-line retrieval. [3]
The supplied traces compare 10,000 requests before release with 10,000 requests afterward. [1]

## Relevance

Errors rose from 0.02% to 0.40%, and pool timeouts account for 96% of post-release errors. [1]
Therefore, address connection pressure before tuning query execution. [judgment]

**Do not subtract span percentiles.** The difference between two p95 values does not measure their contribution within the same request. [judgment]

### The unresolved safety constraint

Four pods each configured for eight connections suggest 32 potential connections, yet observed service usage peaks at eight. [1]  
Check whether the limit applies across the service, through a proxy, or within each pool before choosing a new size. [judgment]
