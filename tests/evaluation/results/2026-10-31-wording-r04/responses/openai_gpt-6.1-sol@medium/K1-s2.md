## Headline

Treat connection acquisition as the main bottleneck, but do not restore the old pool size without confirming the database connection limit.

- Pool-acquire p95 rises from 3 ms to 690 ms, while total request p95 rises from 120 ms to 900 ms. [1]
- The cache miss rate rises from 12% to 31%, increasing the share of requests that reach the database. [1]
- The release explicitly reduces the pool for a new connection limit, so a full rollback risks exceeding that limit. [2]

## Background

The release changes the connection pool, cache lifetime, and ORM query behavior together. The supplied traces compare 10,000 requests before and after the release. [1]

## Relevance

The error rate rises from 0.02% to 0.40%, and pool timeouts account for 96% of post-release errors. [1]

Therefore, reduce connection demand first rather than increase connection capacity without a verified budget. [judgment]

## Information

### What the evidence supports

**High confidence: connection acquisition is the immediate bottleneck.** Traces show increased acquisition time, and logs independently show exhausted pools and five-second timeouts. [3]

The request p95 increases by 780 ms; the acquisition p95 increases by 687 ms. These are separate percentiles, so do not subtract them to assign an exact share of latency. [1]

**Moderate confidence: the shorter cache lifetime amplifies the bottleneck.** The configured lifetime falls from 300 seconds to 60 seconds. [2]

The miss rate becomes 2.58 times its baseline, despite similar peak traffic: 405 versus 410 requests per second. [1]

This supports the explanation, but the supplied comparison does not isolate the cache change from the other release changes. [judgment]

**The ORM change is not the first rollback target.** Orders-query p95 moves from 52 ms to 58 ms; order-lines-query p95 falls from 30 ms to 26 ms. [1]

However, these summaries omit query counts and connection-hold duration, so they do not rule out added ORM demand. [judgment]

**Resolve the pool scope before changing it.** Four pods each have eight worker threads, and the configured pool maximum is eight. [2]

Yet reported peak service connections fall from 31 to eight, not to 32. [1]

That discrepancy requires inspection of pool ownership, worker processes, and any database proxy before capacity changes. [judgment]
