## Background

## Relevance

## Information

### Define the failure before changing the prompt

Use these non-overlapping error classes. Apply them to separate claims or missing details, rather than whole summaries. [judgment]

| Error class | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Unsupported claim | The source does not support the claim | “The customer received a refund” without evidence |
| Contradiction | The claim conflicts with the source | “Issue resolved” when the customer says otherwise |
| Material omission | The summary excludes a detail needed for the next support action | An unresolved refund request disappears |
| Presentation defect | Correct content requires needless interpretation | The summary mixes customer statements with confirmed facts |

Define a **severe error** as one that changes the required support action, exposes sensitive information, or misstates a consequential commitment. [judgment]

Track these measures separately:

- **Severe-error rate:** summaries with at least one severe error divided by summaries reviewed.
- **Other factual-error rate:** summaries with unsupported claims or contradictions, excluding summaries already counted as severe.
- **Material-omission rate:** summaries missing at least one required detail.
- **Correction time:** seconds agents spend checking and correcting each summary.

Do not combine these measures into one score for release decisions. Otherwise, faster edits could conceal worse factual accuracy. [judgment]
