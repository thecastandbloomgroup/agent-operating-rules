Restart retrospectives with one small change, one owner, and a check at the next meeting, rather than a new meeting format. [judgment]

## Background

People attend your retrospectives, but you report no resulting changes. [1] The record does not establish whether actions lack owners, remain unfinished, or fail to improve work.

## Relevance

Attendance alone does not show whether a retrospective works. [judgment] Therefore, make the next two meetings accountable for a small, visible experiment, not a longer action list. [judgment]

## Information

### Evidence and diagnosis

The *Scrum Guide* says retrospectives should identify helpful changes and address the most impactful improvements promptly. [2]

Your report establishes the symptom, but not its cause. [1] Use these three checkpoints to locate the break:

| Checkpoint | Evidence to inspect | Response if absent |
|---|---|---|
| Selection | A specific change and an observable result | Define one small experiment |
| Execution | An owner with time and authority | Reserve time or seek the needed decision |
| Review | A result checked against the intended outcome | Review the result before selecting another action |

These checkpoints are a diagnostic proposal, not findings about your team. [judgment]

**Confidence:** Moderate confidence in this first intervention because it creates observable evidence; low confidence about the cause without action records. [judgment]

## End

I recommend that we take these three steps:

1. **Trace the last two retrospectives.**  
   **Kind:** Deterministic. **Domain:** Clear.  
   **Test first:** Given two retrospective records, when each agreed action is traced, then its owner, result, and review status are recorded.  
   Spend at most 20 minutes on this audit. Mark missing records as unavailable, not as failed actions.  
   This removes uncertainty about where actions stop. Record the baseline: how many meetings produced at least one completed, reviewed change.

2. **Agree on one experiment, not a list.**  
   **Kind:** Deterministic. **Domain:** Clear.  
   **Test first:** Given one proposed change, when the team accepts it, then its owner, time allowance, expected result, and review date exist.  
   After Step 1 passes, reserve 15 minutes to write that agreement. Cap the experiment’s total work at 60 minutes.  
   For example: “For one week, add acceptance examples before work starts; count tasks returned for clarification.”  
   This gives the action an owner and a test. If nobody has authority or time, record the required management decision instead.

3. **Run a two-meeting trial of the new process.**  
   **Kind:** Stochastic, because team behavior varies. **Domain:** Complex.  
   **Acceptance test first:** Measure meetings with at least one completed, reviewed experiment. Use Step 1’s baseline; pass at two of two meetings.  
   Start only after Step 2 passes. Run the trial alongside normal work, with no extra meeting.  
   At each meeting, review the previous experiment before accepting another. Compare its result with its stated target.  
   Stop an experiment if it harms delivery or exceeds its budget. An unsuccessful experiment still qualifies if the team checks and records its result.  
   This tests follow-through, not guaranteed improvement. The benefit remains uncertain until the trial ends. [judgment]

## Follow-up

If actions finish but work does not improve, change the experiments rather than increase their number. [judgment]

If people report fear of blame, use private input and address the reported behavior before requiring public ownership. [judgment]

If authority blocks the change, request one specific decision from the person who holds that authority. [judgment]
