# Headline

Focus each retrospective on one measurable improvement and place it directly into the sprint backlog.
A single improvement target prevents split focus and reduces task abandonment.
Treating retrospective actions as sprint work items guarantees engineering capacity during the sprint.
Reviewing the prior action at the start of each meeting establishes public accountability.

# Background

Your team attends retrospective meetings, but the discussions do not result in working practice changes.
The current process creates discussions that do not lead to completed actions.
This pattern indicates a breakdown between action agreement and sprint execution.

# Relevance

Stagnant retrospectives waste team hours and reduce morale over time.
Addressing this issue now restores continuous improvement before team members disengage completely.

# Information

Teams often identify too many problems at once, which causes execution failure [1].
Research shows that limiting work in progress increases completion rates across knowledge work [judgment].
When teams track improvement actions in separate documents outside the sprint backlog, completion rates drop [1].
Therefore, integrating process actions into the primary work backlog increases visibility.
