# Headline

Limit each retrospective to one actionable change and place it directly into the sprint backlog.
Action items fail when teams create too many unassigned goals [judgment].
Adding items to the sprint backlog makes improvement work visible alongside product delivery.
Reviewing the chosen action at the next meeting creates accountability.

# Background

Your team attends retrospective meetings regularly.
However, these meetings fail to generate actual changes in work habits or results.
You want an operating model that converts retrospective discussion into completed improvements.

# Relevance

Stagnant retrospectives waste team time and lower morale.
Therefore, fixing this feedback loop is necessary before the team disengages from the process entirely.

# Information

Research shows that retrospective failure usually stems from three causes [19]:
First, teams select too many action items at once.
Second, teams do not track retrospective actions within their primary work tracking system [20].
Third, teams do not define clear completion criteria for team habit changes [judgment].
Because the team already attends the meetings, attendance is not the root blocker.
Instead, execution discipline after the meeting is the missing element [judgment].
I assess this root cause as likely based on software team research [20].

A2. Team members have autonomy to alter their internal practices. Why not measured: the material does not state team authority limits. Validate by: ask the engineering manager during weekly sync. If wrong: limit candidate actions to practices inside local team control.

## Alternative Considered

Run an open root-cause analysis workshop on why prior retrospectives failed.
This alternative loses because it uses the exact meeting format that the team currently discounts [judgment].
Starting with concrete delivery of a single small action builds momentum faster.
