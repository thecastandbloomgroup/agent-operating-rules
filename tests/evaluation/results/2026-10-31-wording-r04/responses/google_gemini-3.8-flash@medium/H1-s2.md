### Background

Production checkout fails with HTTP 500 errors for all users starting at 09:12 [judgment]. You are the on-call engineer and have ten minutes before an executive briefing call [judgment]. You must stabilize the service and deliver a clear incident briefing under tight time constraints [judgment].

### Relevance

Every user cannot purchase products while HTTP 500 errors persist, which stops revenue intake immediately [judgment]. Clear communication on the executive call prevents leadership panic and protects your focus during mitigation [judgment].

### Information

Checkout returns HTTP 500 across all incoming traffic, which points to an unhandled exception in the checkout pipeline [judgment]. Total failure across all users indicates either a bad release, a broken dependency, or an expired credential [judgment]. Standard incident management frameworks state that stabilization must precede root-cause investigation [1].

**Assumption Log**
A1. A deployment or configuration change occurred within 30 minutes before 09:12. Why not measured: The material does not state it. Validate by: Inspect deployment pipeline audit logs. If wrong: Investigate database saturation and external payment gateway health instead.

A2. A rollback mechanism exists and completes within five minutes. Why not measured: The material does not state it. Validate by: Check deployment tool documentation. If wrong: Divert traffic or enable maintenance mode.

**Alternative Considered**
Debug the application stack trace before taking operational action. This loses because full error diagnosis takes more than ten minutes and extends revenue loss [judgment].
