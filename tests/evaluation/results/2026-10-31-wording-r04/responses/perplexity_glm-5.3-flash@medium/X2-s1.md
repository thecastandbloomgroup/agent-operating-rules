# Headline

Team retrospectives stall at follow-through, so fix the loop between meeting and action:
- Audit what happened to past action items before you change the meeting.
- Cut each retro to one owner-assigned item, checked at the start of the next retro.
- Widen input with a written form ahead of the meeting.

# Background

The team holds retrospectives on a fixed cycle. Attendance is stable, yet the meetings no longer change how the team works. You ask how to restore their effect.

# Relevance

A retro with no output costs the team its meeting hours and lowers trust in the practice. The fix lies in structure and cadence, not in a new meeting agenda.

# Information

Evidence, by theme:
- Breaks in follow-through: items pile up, no one owns them, and no one checks them later. Practitioners of the retrospective format recommend few action items, each with a named owner and a check at the next meeting [19].
- Narrow input: in a study of team effectiveness at Google, psychological safety ranked first among factors, and members spoke in roughly equal share on the effective teams [20]. People who stay silent do not raise their friction points.
- No source found in this session disagrees with either theme. The themes matter because they map to the reported symptom, a meeting that ends with no change [judgment].

Confidence: medium. The basis is practice literature only, with no direct data from your team.

Assumption log:
- A1. Retro meetings produce action items, and the items do not get done. Why not measured: the conversation does not state what happens to items. Validate by: reading the notes from the last ten retros. If wrong: the fault is item creation, not follow-through, so the plan changes.
- A2. Some members hold back concerns in the meeting. Why not measured: no tool reached your team in this session. Validate by: counting who speaks or who submits items over four retros. If wrong: skip Step 4.

Alternative considered: cancel the retros and track improvements in a shared log instead. It loses because the Step 1 audit shows within one session whether the meeting or its follow-through is at fault, and the meeting also serves team alignment [judgment].

# End

1. **Audit the last ten retros.** Increment: mark each past item as owned or not, done or not, and tag each gap with a cause. Blocker removed: you cannot see where the loop breaks. 2. **Pick the two break points that matter most.** Increment: score the cause tags from Step 1 by frequency and severity. Blocker removed: effort goes to the real fault, not to every fault. Test (write first): score with a Kepner-Tregoe sheet, weights are frequency at 0.5 and severity at 0.5, the pass threshold is agreement by the team lead. Domain: Complicated. Step 2 MUST NOT start until Step 1 is complete.
3. **Change the retro structure.** Increment: end each retro with one action, one named owner, and a due date; open the next retro by checking that action. Blocker removed: the loop from decision to done action now exists. Test (write first): Given a retro ends, When its action list is read, Then it holds one owned item and the next retro opens with a done or not done statement for it. Domain: Clear. 4. **Probe written input.** Increment: collect one friction point from each member in a form before the meeting. Blocker: silent members do not contribute (see Step 1 check). Test (write first): metric is distinct contributors per retro, baseline comes from the Step 1 notes, threshold is 80% of attendees submitting in 3 of 4 retros, sample size is 4 retros. Budget: 15 minutes per member per cycle. Stop condition: no rise after 4 retros, then close the form. Steps 3 and 4 start after Step 2 and run in parallel, each capped by its own budget.

Condition that changes the plan: if the Step 1 audit shows that items were owned and done, the loop works and the fault is item selection (see A1). Replace Steps 3 and 4 with a review of how items are chosen.
