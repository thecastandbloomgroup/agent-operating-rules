- The trace shows the pool acquire span is 690 ms of the 900 ms p95, up from 3 ms before the release [1].

## Background

API p95 latency rose from 120 ms to 900 ms after release 2026.10.21. The release changed the connection pool, the cache TTL, and the ORM query, and the logs, traces, and diff are attached.

## Relevance

The error rate is 0.40%, and 96% of the errors are customer-facing timeouts after 5 s [1]. So the fix has direct cost today, and the change is small and fast to ship.

## Information

**Evidence.** All numbers come from the three attached files.

- db.pool.acquire p95 rose from 3 ms to 690 ms, which is 77% of the 900 ms total p95 [1].
- "pool exhausted" warnings went from none per hour on 2026-10-20 to 1,800 to 2,400 per hour on 2026-10-22 [2].
- The diff cut max_size from 50 to 8 and acquire_timeout_s from 30 s to 5 s [3]. The service runs 4 pods with 8 worker threads each, so 32 workers share the pool [3].
- Peak active DB connections fell from 31 to 8, which equals the new max_size [1].
- Cache miss rate rose from 12% to 31%, about 2.6 times the baseline, because the release cut default_ttl_s from 300 s to 60 s [1][3]. However, cache.get p95 stayed at 5 ms [1]. Therefore the TTL adds DB load, not direct latency, so it is a secondary cause.
- The ORM change added `selectinload(Order.lines)`, but db.query orders p95 stayed near baseline (52 to 58 ms) and db.query order_lines p95 fell from 30 ms to 26 ms [1]. DB CPU peak stayed near 40% in both windows [1]. So the ORM change is ruled out.
