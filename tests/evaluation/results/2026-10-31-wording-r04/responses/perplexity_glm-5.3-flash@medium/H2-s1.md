## Headline

1. Stamp out the message chaos first, verify the facts in one review, then correct or defend the record with one voice.
- Freeze all external statements until one spokesperson owns them.
- Verify the article against the statement's source records within one day.
- Publish a short holding statement that adds no new facts.
- Pick the final path, correction, apology, or refutation, on the review verdict.

## Background

The CEO gave a public statement yesterday. A journalist reports that the statement is false. Legal, PR, and the board each send their own messages, with no agreed line between them.

## Relevance

The first hours set the story, because every new message becomes part of the coverage [judgment]. A freeze costs one message per party and stops new contradictions while the facts are checked.

## Information

**Evidence**

In a chaotic state, the first action must stabilise the situation before any analysis [1]. In a complex state, cause and effect show only in hindsight, so any probe needs a measure, a stop condition, and a budget [1]. A wrong public refutation doubles the story, because reporters then cover both the error and the failed rebuttal [judgment].

The fact review closes within one day: about as likely as not [no source]. The verdict itself decides which final path is available, so the review output is the pivot for everything after it.

**Assumption log**

- A1. The journalist's report contradicts a checkable fact, not only an opinion. Why not measured: no article text appears in the material. Validate by: read the article and the statement transcript side by side. If wrong: replace Steps 3 and 5 with an evidence-backed reply to the journalist.
- A2. No single spokesperson or channel exists today. Why not measured: the material does not state one. Validate by: ask the three parties in the freeze notice who owns external lines. If wrong: Step 1 shrinks to a confirmation of the existing owner.
- A3. The statement was public, for example a press event, an interview, or a filing. Why not measured: the material does not state the venue. Validate by: locate the transcript or recording within one hour. If wrong: treat it as internal-only, and Step 4 shrinks to employee channels.

**Alternative considered**

Respond to the article at once with a full refutation, before any review. It loses because a refutation without checked evidence risks a second, larger story [judgment].

## End

I recommend that we run the five steps below. Step 1 starts now. Step 2 MUST NOT start before Step 1 sends the notice. Step 3 drafting MAY run in parallel with Step 2, but the release MUST NOT go out before the Step 2 memo exists. Step 5 MUST NOT start until Step 2 closes and Step 4 has at least two samples.

**Step 1, freeze the channels (Chaotic, deterministic).** Send one freeze notice to legal, PR, and the board. The notice names one incident channel and one spokesperson role. Increment: one message plus a phone confirmation with each party, within one hour. Blocker removed: three unaligned message sources that create new contradictions by the hour. Test, written first:

```gherkin
Given the freeze notice reached all three parties
When a party prepares any external statement
Then the party routes the draft to the incident channel before release
```

**Step 2, fact review (Confused, evaluative).** Collect the statement text or recording, the article, and the underlying records. Produce a memo with a verdict per contested claim. Blocker removed: the unknown basis for a correction or a defense. Rubric, written before the work: score each criterion from 0 to 1. Criteria: (a) match between the statement wording and the records, weight 0.4; (b) quality and independence of the sources behind the article, weight 0.3; (c) context the article gives, weight 0.3. A claim counts as false when the weighted total reaches 0.8 or higher. Each criterion needs its concrete evidence: records, journalist sources, and the full article text.

**Step 3, holding statement (Complicated, deterministic).** Draft two to three sentences. The text names the review, gives a date, and adds no new facts. Legal approves. Blocker removed: an information vacuum in which others fill the story. Test, written first:

```gherkin
Given a draft holding statement
When legal and PR read it
Then it contains zero new facts, one confirmed date, and the incident channel
```

**Step 4, coverage probe (Complex, stochastic).** Count new outlets that report the claim, sampled twice per day for 48 hours. Baseline: one outlet, the current article [judgment]. Stop condition: no new outlet across two consecutive samples, or the 48-hour cap. Budget: two hours per day, one person. Leverage here is a guess; the probe only informs Step 5.

**Step 5, response path (Complicated, evaluative).** Choose the final public response with a decision table [2]. Options: data-backed correction, apology plus correction, or evidence-backed refutation to the journalist. Weights: legal exposure 0.4, audience reach 0.3, speed of correction 0.3. Pass threshold: the selected option ranks first on the weighted total. Evidence needed: the Step 2 memo and the Step 4 counts.

## Follow-up

Conditions that change the recommendation: if A1 is wrong, send an evidence-backed reply through the same single channel instead of Steps 3 and 5; if A3 is wrong, run Step 4 on internal channels only; if a regulator or the exchange requires a disclosure, insert its deadline above all steps.
