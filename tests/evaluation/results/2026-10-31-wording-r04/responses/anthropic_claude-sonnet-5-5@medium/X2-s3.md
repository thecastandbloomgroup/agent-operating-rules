## Headline

I recommend we find where action items die, then run one visible, owned change per retrospective.

- The cause is unknown, so the first move is to count what happened to past action items.
- A small commitment (one change, one owner, one due date) removes the most likely blocker.
- A short survey checks whether people lost trust in the meeting.
- A capped trial of a new format tests a fix without a full rollout.

## Background

A retrospective is a regular meeting where a team reviews its work and picks changes. Your team still attends, but the meetings no longer lead to change. The request is for a plan to fix this.

## Relevance

Each stalled retrospective uses team hours and teaches people that reflection has no effect. That lowers attendance quality and trust over time [judgment].

## Information

**Evidence**

- Derby and Larsen describe five phases: set the stage, gather data, generate insights, decide what to do, and close. Stalled retrospectives often skip or rush the "decide" phase [judgment].

[1]

- A change can fail at four points: no action is chosen, no one owns it, the owner does not finish it, or the finished action has no effect. These stages do not overlap and they cover the whole path.
- The owner or follow-through gap is the most likely cause: 60% likely [judgment].
- Group survey answers into themes, and count the people behind each theme:

**Assumption log**

A1. The team keeps notes from past retrospectives. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask the facilitator for the last five sets of notes. If wrong: Step 1 uses a 15-minute recall session instead.

A2. The team has 5 to 12 people and meets every one to two weeks. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: check the calendar. If wrong: change the trial length in Step 4.

A3. The failure is in follow-through, not in the topics. Why not measured: no data exists yet. Validate by: Step 1 results. If wrong: replace Step 2 with a change of topic selection.

**Alternative considered**

The strongest alternative is to change the meeting format at once or stop the meetings. It loses because the cause is unknown, so a new format could repeat the same failure [judgment].

## End

I recommend that we take these four steps in order:

1. **Audit past action items.** This is a Confused action: it gathers the data that classifies the problem. The increment: list the action items from the last five retrospectives and mark each as done, not done, or never owned. The blocker removed: we do not know where changes die. Kind: deterministic. Write the test first:

```gherkin
Feature: Action item audit
  Scenario: Every past action item has a status
    Given the notes of the last 5 retrospectives
    When the facilitator lists each action item
    Then each item has an owner field, a status, and a completion rate is reported
```

   The result also sets the baseline for Step 4.

2. **Adopt a one-action rule.** This is a Clear action. The increment: at the next retrospective, end with one action that has a named owner, a due date, and a place on the team board. The blocker removed: unowned actions. Kind: deterministic. Step 2 MUST NOT start until Step 1 shows which stage fails most often. The test:

```gherkin
Feature: One owned action
  Scenario: The retrospective ends with a tracked action
    Given the retrospective is in its closing phase
    When the team agrees on a change
    Then the board shows one action with an owner and a due date
```

3. **Run a three-question anonymous survey.** This is a Complicated action: analysis picks the fix. The increment: a 10-minute survey asking why changes stall, what topics are missing, and what would raise trust. The blocker removed: the hidden reasons behind silence. Kind: evaluative. The rubric, written first: criterion "number of respondents naming the theme" has weight 50%. Criterion "theme confirmed by Step 1 data" has weight 50%. The pass threshold: a theme passes when at least 3 respondents name it and Step 1 data supports it. Score each theme against both pieces of evidence.

4. **Trial a new format as a capped probe.** This is a Complex action: the outcome is visible only afterward. The increment: change one thing (for example, rotate the facilitator or limit the topic to one pain point) for three retrospectives. The capability added: a tested format fix. Kind: stochastic. Step 4 MUST NOT start until Step 1 gives the baseline. It MAY run in parallel with Step 2. The acceptance test, written first:
   - Metric: share of action items finished by their due date.
   - Baseline: the rate from Step 1.
   - Pass threshold: at least 5 of 6 actions done (up to two actions per retrospective).
   - Sample size: 6 actions. The sample is small, so confidence in a pass is low.
   - Budget: three retrospectives and 15 extra minutes each.
   - Stop condition: end the probe if the team drops two actions at the first retrospective.

   Leverage here is a guess [judgment].

If A3 is false, replace Step 2 with a change in how the team picks topics.
