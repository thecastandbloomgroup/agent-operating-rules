## Headline

**I choose Northwind Analytics (Proposal 1), on condition that four checks pass before signing.**

- Northwind is the only managed option that fits the 400,000 budget at the stated growth.
- Bluepeak costs more than the budget and has no price cap.
- Cedar is the cheapest, but it has no availability guarantee and relies on one engineer.
- The Northwind margin is thin, so growth and overage billing decide the result.

## Background

We have three data warehouse proposals and a budget of 400,000 USD over three years. The data is 14 TB today (TB means terabyte) and grows about 3 TB per year. The request is to recommend one vendor.

## Relevance

The proposals expire in practice once the vendors reprice, and a wrong pick locks in three years of cost. The Northwind margin is 28,000, so one wrong input can break the budget.

The figures come from the vendors' own statements in proposals.txt, Proposals 1 to 3 [1]. They are primary evidence of what each vendor offers. They are not independent measurements.

Northwind data size reaches 17 TB, 20 TB, and 23 TB at the ends of years 1 to 3 (14 TB plus 3 TB per year). The licence covers 20 TB, so 3 extra TB cost 27,000 in year 3. The total is then 399,000, which is 1,000 under budget. Growth above about 3 TB per year breaks the budget [inference].

Bluepeak exceeds the budget even at current query volume, and the price has no cap [1]. Egress fees (charges to move data out) are 0.08 per GB, so exit costs add more.

Cedar leaves 114,000 of headroom, about 38,000 per year. The proposals do not state the staff cost to run it [1]. One engineer runs databases today, and Cedar has no SLA (service level agreement, a written uptime promise). A second engineer would likely cost more than the headroom [judgment]. Cedar is also 62% slower than Northwind (3.4 s against 2.1 s).

The vendor benchmarks use 10 TB. Real data is 14 to 23 TB, so the speed figures may not hold [inference].

Confidence in the Northwind choice: medium, because all evidence is vendor-stated and no figure is independently measured.

A3. Northwind speed at 14 to 23 TB is close to its 10 TB result. Why not measured: no tool can reach vendor systems without a trial. Validate by: run the 12 reference queries on a trial copy of our data. If wrong: Bluepeak speed gains more weight, or Cedar is reconsidered.

A4. Running Cedar costs more than 114,000 in staff time over three years. Why not measured: no vendor states it. Validate by: ask our operations lead for a staffing estimate. If wrong: Cedar becomes the cheapest fit, and the Pugh comparison [4] decides.

Cedar is the strongest alternative because it costs 86,000 less than Northwind. It loses because it has no SLA, depends on one engineer, and is the slowest [judgment]. It wins only if A4 is false.
