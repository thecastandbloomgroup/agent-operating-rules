## Headline

**Measure why actions stall, then limit each retrospective to one owned action with a due date.**

- Retrospectives fail when ideas do not become finished work, so count the gap first.
- A single action with one owner and one date in the team backlog fixes follow-through.
- Problems outside the team's control need a person who can act on them.
- A short, capped trial of a new format tests whether the format is the cause.

## Background

Your team holds retrospectives (regular meetings where a team reviews its work and agrees on improvements). People attend, but no change follows. The material does not say the team size, the meeting rhythm, or what happens to action items.

## Relevance

Each retrospective without a result costs the team's time and lowers the odds that people engage in the next one [judgment]. The problem grows with each empty meeting.

## Information

**Evidence**

- The Scrum Guide says the retrospective plans ways to raise quality and effectiveness. It also says the most useful improvements should be addressed soon, even added to the next Sprint Backlog.

  [1]

- Derby and Larsen describe five phases: set the stage, gather data, generate insights, decide what to do, and close. The "decide what to do" phase is where your meetings likely break.

  [2]

- The cause is likely one of three: no follow-through, no authority over the problem, or a stale format [judgment]. These three do not overlap and cover the main paths from idea to change.
- Follow-through failure is the likeliest single cause [judgment, likely].

**Assumption log**

A1. The team has 4 to 9 people and meets every 1 to 2 weeks. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask the facilitator. If wrong: the sample size in Step 4 changes.

A2. Retrospectives produce action items, and most stay unfinished. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: count items in the last three retrospective notes (Step 1). If wrong: skip Step 2 and put more effort into Step 4.

A3. Team members feel safe to name real problems. Why not measured: no tool can reach it. Validate by: run the anonymous poll in Step 1. If wrong: replace Step 4 with trust-building work.

**Alternative considered**

The strongest alternative is to hire an outside facilitator now. It loses because it treats the format, and the data so far points to follow-through first [judgment]. It also costs more than a one-session count.

## End

I recommend that we take these four actions in this order:

1. **Measure the gap.** Domain: Confused. Kind: deterministic.
   - Increment: In one session, mark each action item from the last three retrospectives as done, open, or dropped. Add a three-question anonymous poll: "We raise real problems", "Actions get done", "Leaders support our actions". Use a 1 to 5 scale.
   - Blocker removed: the cause is unknown, so no fix can be chosen.
   - Test: Given the notes of the last three retrospectives, When we count the items, Then every item has a status and the completion rate is recorded as the baseline.

2. **Set a one-action rule.** Domain: Clear. Kind: deterministic.
   - Increment: At the next retrospective, end with one action. Give it one owner and one due date. Enter it in the team backlog. Open the following retrospective by reviewing it.
   - Blocker removed: vague actions with no owner.
   - Test: Given a retrospective ends, When the facilitator closes it, Then the backlog holds exactly one new item with an owner and a date.

3. **Escalate what the team cannot fix.** Domain: Complicated. Kind: evaluative.
   - Increment: Sort the open and dropped items from Step 1 by who controls them. Send items outside the team to a manager with a request and a reply date.
   - Blocker removed: items that stall because the team lacks authority.
   - Test: Score each item with this rubric. Criteria: team control (weight 40), expected effect (weight 40), effort (weight 20). Pass threshold: 70 of 100. Evidence: a named owner, the count of tasks affected, and an hour estimate.
   - Step 3 MUST NOT start until Step 1 is complete.

4. **Run a capped format trial.** Domain: Complex. Kind: stochastic. Leverage is a guess.
   - Increment: For two retrospectives, pick one problem and use the "5 Whys" exercise (ask "why" five times to find the root cause). Budget: two sessions of 60 minutes.
   - Blocker removed: a stale format that produces shallow topics.
   - Test: Metric: share of actions done by their due date, plus the poll question "this retrospective was worth my time". Baseline: the Step 1 values. Pass threshold: every action done on time and a poll mean at least 1 point above baseline. Sample size: two retrospectives, 4 to 9 voters each. Confidence in the result is low at this size. Extend to six retrospectives if the result is unclear.
   - Stop condition: stop after the first session if the poll mean falls below baseline.
   - Step 4 MUST NOT start until Step 1 records the baseline. Run it in parallel with Steps 2 and 3.
