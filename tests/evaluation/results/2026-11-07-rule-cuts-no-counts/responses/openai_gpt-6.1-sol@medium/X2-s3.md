## Headline

Replace open-ended retrospectives with a short cycle: choose one change, test it, and review the result.

- First, find where past agreements stop: choice, action, or review.
- Next, give one change an owner, protected time, and a success measure.
- Finally, review the result before collecting more ideas.

## Background

Your team attends retrospectives, but you report no resulting change. [1] The report does not show whether the blocker is unclear agreements, limited authority, competing work, or reluctance to speak.

## Relevance

Another discussion format will not address a blocked action process. [judgment] Therefore, inspect what happens after the meeting before changing the meeting itself. [judgment]

## Information

**Treat this as a process problem first, not an effort problem.** Attendance alone does not reveal why change stops. [judgment]

Look for the first broken link:

| Link | Evidence to inspect | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Choice | No specific change leaves the meeting | Choose one observable change |
| Action | A change exists, but nobody starts it | Assign an owner, time, and authority |
| Review | Someone acts, but nobody checks the result | Review evidence at the next retrospective |

These are diagnostic categories, not findings about your team. [judgment]

The Scrum Guide describes retrospectives as a way to plan improvements in quality and effectiveness. It also says teams address the most useful improvements promptly. [2] This supports a focus on completed changes rather than the volume of discussion.

**Assumption:** The team can test one small process change. **Why not measured:** The material does not state it. **Validate by:** Confirm authority during Step 1. **If wrong:** Seek a named decision-maker before starting the test.

**Alternative considered:** Introduce anonymous feedback or a new facilitator first. That becomes the stronger choice if people report withheld concerns or fear of consequences. Otherwise, it leaves the action process untested. [judgment]

**Confidence:** Moderate confidence, based on your report and the retrospective’s stated purpose, but no action records.

## End

I recommend that we take these three steps, using the tests below before starting each step.

### 1. Trace the last three retrospectives

**Test, deterministic:**

```gherkin
Given the available records from the last three retrospectives
When the team traces each agreed change
Then each change has an owner, status, and review result, or an explicit “missing” entry
```

Spend **20 minutes** creating that list. If no records exist, record that fact rather than reconstructing precise commitments from memory.

Then mark where each change first stopped: choice, action, or review. Also record whether the team controls the required decision.

**Blocker removed:** No shared account of what happens after the meeting.  
**Domain:** Clear, because deduction from a fixed checklist identifies missing fields.  
**Output:** One completed action list checked against the test.

### 2. Replace the next retrospective’s output with one change agreement

Step 2 MUST NOT start until Step 1’s test passes.

**Test, deterministic:**

```gherkin
Given one proposed change within the team’s authority
When the team writes its change agreement
Then it names an owner, first action, protected time, measure, review date, and stop condition
```

Use **15 minutes** to complete this template:

> For the next work cycle, we will change **[behavior]**.  
> **[Owner]** will take **[first action]** during **[reserved time]**.  
> Our current measure is **[baseline]**.  
> Success means **[target]**, checked on **[date]**.  
> We stop if **[harm or cost limit]** occurs.

Choose a reversible change that does not need outside approval. Do not make “communicate better” the change; name an observable behavior.

**Blocker removed:** An agreement without practical means to act.  
**Domain:** Clear, because deduction from the template checks whether the agreement is complete.  
**Output:** One agreement that passes the test.

### 3. Run one small trial and review it first

Step 3 MUST NOT start until Step 2’s test passes.

**Acceptance test, stochastic, meaning results can vary:**

- **Metric:** The behavior or outcome named in the agreement.
- **Baseline:** The prior comparable work cycle, measured before the trial.
- **Pass threshold:** The target agreed before the trial, with no stop-condition breach.
- **Sample:** Two consecutive work cycles; this is a local decision aid, not proof of causation.

Cap extra effort at **30 minutes per person per cycle**. Stop immediately if the agreed harm or cost limit occurs. This cap is a proposed constraint, not an evidence-based optimum. [judgment]

Start each retrospective with: **What happened to our change?** Record “continue,” “adapt,” or “stop,” supported by the measure.

**Capability added:** A visible link between discussion, action, and evidence.  
**Domain:** Complex, because induction from trials reveals local effects. Workload or staff changes can break the pattern; leverage remains a guess.  
**Output:** A trial record and an explicit decision, not another untracked promise.

## Follow-up

If Step 1 shows external blockers, replace the local trial with a specific decision request to the responsible leader.

If people report fear of consequences, use a private feedback route before asking for public explanations. [judgment]
