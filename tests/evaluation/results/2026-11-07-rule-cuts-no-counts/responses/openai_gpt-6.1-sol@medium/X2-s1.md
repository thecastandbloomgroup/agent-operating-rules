**What remains uncertain:** Attendance alone does not reveal whether the barrier is unclear commitments, lack of capacity, blocked authority, or unsafe discussion. [judgment] Confidence in any specific cause is low because the available evidence describes the result, not the process.

### 2. Create one funded action card

**Test first, deterministic:**

```gherkin
Given the barrier found in Step 1
When the team selects one change
Then its card names an owner, capacity, authority, expected result, and review date
```

After Step 1 passes, spend 15 minutes creating the card. Choose work that fits within one working session.

For example:

> **Change:** Add an acceptance checklist to the next three work items.  
> **Owner:** One named team member.  
> **Capacity:** Reserve 45 minutes; remove another task of equal size.  
> **Expected result:** Fewer questions about whether those items are complete.  
> **Review:** Compare questions and rework at the next retrospective.

Choose your own relevant change; the example is not a diagnosis. This step adds a concrete commitment and protects time to complete it. [judgment]

**Domain: Clear.** Check the card against explicit requirements; this uses deduction from a known checklist.

### 3. Run a capped, two-cycle trial

**Acceptance test first, stochastic:** Human follow-through varies, so assess a small trial rather than promise a result.

- **Baseline:** Use the completion record from Step 1; your reported baseline is no resulting change. [1]
- **Sample:** Two consecutive retrospective cycles, with one action opportunity per cycle.
- **Metric:** Completed experiments with evidence, plus explicit decisions to keep, change, or stop them.
- **Pass threshold:** Complete at least one experiment and record a decision in both cycles. This is a proposed local threshold. [judgment]

After Step 2 passes, start each retrospective by reviewing the previous action before choosing another.

Cap each experiment at one working session. Stop it if it creates harm, exceeds that budget, or requires authority the team lacks. This adds a review cycle without creating another large initiative. [judgment]

**Domain: Complex.** Use induction from small trials; workload changes or outside dependencies can break the pattern. Leverage remains a guess.

Two cycles provide a local signal, not proof of lasting improvement. If neither cycle produces action, replace another discussion with a sponsor decision about capacity or authority. [judgment]
