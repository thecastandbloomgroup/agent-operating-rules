## Headline

Measure what happens to retrospective actions, then fix the weakest link between a discussion and a finished change.

- First, find where change stops: no action chosen, action not done, action ineffective, or cause outside the team.
- Next, make each action small, owned, dated, and placed in the work backlog.
- Then, review last retro's actions at the start of the next retro.
- Last, test a changed format in a capped trial, because the cause is not yet known.

## Background

The team holds retrospectives (regular meetings to review how the team works). People attend, but nothing changes. The request is for a course of action.

## Relevance

Each retrospective without change costs meeting time and lowers trust. Attendance without effect trains people to stop raising problems.

## Information

The material gives no numbers about the team. These causes split the problem into four parts that do not overlap and cover every case:

1. No action is chosen at the end of the meeting.
2. Actions are chosen but not done.
3. Actions are done but the problem stays.
4. The cause sits outside the team's control.

Derby and Larsen describe a "decide what to do" phase as a required part of a retrospective. Few, specific actions follow from it. [1]

This is a secondary source. It supports the design of the actions.

Cause 2 is the most common failure in team retrospectives [judgment]. It is likely (66 to 100%) that the team has at least one of causes 1 and 2, because "nothing shifts" fits both [inference].

Assumption log:

- Assumption: the team runs a regular cadence and records its action items. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask one team member for the last five retro notes. If wrong: Step 1 becomes "start recording actions" and the rest holds.
- Assumption: no manager blocks changes on purpose. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask in Step 1 whether any action was refused. If wrong: add an escalation action for cause 4.

Alternative considered: replace the facilitator or the format now. It loses because the cause is unmeasured, and a new format cannot fix cause 2 or cause 4 [judgment].

## End

I recommend that we take these four actions, in this order:

1. **Audit the last five retrospectives.**
   - Increment: one session to count actions chosen, owned, finished, and effective.
   - Blocker removed: we do not know which of the four causes applies.
   - Kind: deterministic.
   - Cynefin: Complicated. Analysis of our own records picks the practice by deduction. [2]
   - Test:
     - Given the notes of the last five retrospectives
     - When we count the actions by status
     - Then we have a table of chosen, owned, finished, and effective actions, and a completion rate

2. **Limit each retrospective to one or two actions, each with an owner and a date, placed in the sprint backlog.**
   - Increment: one retrospective, with the actions entered as backlog items before the meeting ends.
   - Blocker removed: actions live in meeting notes, not in the work plan.
   - Kind: deterministic.
   - Cynefin: Clear. A known good practice applies by rule. [1]
   - Test:
     - Given a retrospective has ended
     - When we open the sprint backlog
     - Then it holds one or two retro items, each with an owner and a due date

   Step 2 MUST NOT start until Step 1 shows whether actions were chosen at all. If cause 1 applies, run Step 2 as written. If cause 4 applies, add an action to raise the issue with the person who holds authority.

3. **Open the next retrospective by reviewing the previous actions.**
   - Increment: a 10-minute agenda item at the start of the next meeting.
   - Blocker removed: no one is accountable for follow-through.
   - Kind: deterministic.
   - Cynefin: Clear. The rule is a known agenda change.
   - Test:
     - Given the previous actions are in the backlog
     - When the retrospective starts
     - Then the team states each action as done, not done, or dropped, with a reason

   Step 3 MUST NOT start until the test in Step 2 passes.

4. **Run a capped trial of a changed format to test whether the format affects completion.**
   - Increment: change one element for four retrospectives, for example choose the topic by vote on data instead of open discussion.
   - Blocker removed: low engagement that the audit may show.
   - Kind: stochastic.
   - Cynefin: Complex. We learn the effect only after trials. Leverage is a guess. The pattern could break if team members change or the sprint load rises.
   - Test:
     - Metric: share of chosen actions finished within one sprint.
     - Baseline: the completion rate from Step 1.
     - Pass threshold: 20 percentage points above baseline.
     - Sample size: four retrospectives.
   - Budget: 30 extra minutes of preparation per retrospective. Stop condition: two retrospectives in a row below baseline. Step 4 MUST NOT start until Step 1 gives the baseline. It can run in parallel with Steps 2 and 3.

Conditions that change the recommendation:

- If the audit shows that most actions are finished but problems stay (cause 3), replace Step 4 with a review of how we pick actions.
- If most refused actions need outside approval (cause 4), replace Step 4 with a request to the sponsor for decision rights.
