## Headline

Fix follow-through first, because retrospectives that end without owned, scheduled actions rarely change anything.

- Find out why past actions failed before you change the meeting.
- Make each action small, owned, dated, and visible in the sprint backlog.
- Send blockers outside the team's control to someone who can remove them.
- Test a new meeting format as a small, capped probe.

## Background

A retrospective is a recurring team meeting that reviews recent work and agrees on improvements. Your team attends, but no change follows. The request is for a plan to restore effect.

The material gives no data on team size, meeting format, or past action items.

## Relevance

Each empty retrospective costs team time and lowers trust in the meeting. Attendance without effect is a common sign that the team will stop caring.

## Information

**Evidence**

- A standard retrospective has five phases: set the stage, gather data, generate insights, decide what to do, and close [1].
- Teams that stall often skip or weaken the "decide what to do" phase. [judgment]
- The cause is likely (66 to 100%) missing follow-through, not a bad meeting format. [judgment]
- No numbers about your team exist in the material, so confidence is low. The first action collects them.

The work is Complicated or Complex, because the cause is knowable by analysis but the fix depends on people [2].

**Assumption log**

Assumption: The team has produced action items that were never finished. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: list the last three retrospectives' action items and their status. If wrong: the cause is in the meeting format, so run the probe (Step 4) first.

Assumption: A manager or sponsor exists who can remove outside blockers. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask the team who owns the budget and priorities. If wrong: Step 3 targets the nearest person with authority.

## End

I recommend that we:

1. **Audit the last three retrospectives' action items.** Increment: one table in one session, with owner, due date, status, and reason for failure. Blocker removed: the cause is unknown. Domain: Complicated, because analysis of past records picks the fix (deduction). Kind: deterministic. Test, written first:

   ```gherkin
   Given the notes of the last three retrospectives
   When the team lists every action item with owner, status, and failure reason
   Then each item has all four fields filled
   And the team names the most common failure reason
   ```

2. **Adopt one rule: at most two action items per retrospective, each with one owner and a date, placed in the sprint backlog.** Step 2 MUST NOT start until Step 1 names the main failure reason. Increment: one rule written on the team's board and applied at the next meeting. Blocker removed: actions have no owner or time. Domain: Clear, because putting actions in the backlog is a known best practice (deduction from a known rule). Kind: deterministic. Test, written first:

   ```gherkin
   Given a retrospective has ended
   When the team reviews the sprint backlog
   Then each new action item appears with one owner and one due date
   And the next retrospective opens by checking those items
   ```

3. **Sort each blocker into "team controls", "team influences", or "outside the team", and send the third group to the sponsor.** Increment: one sorted list and one message in one session. Blocker removed: the team cannot fix some causes itself. Domain: Clear, because the sort rule is simple and known (deduction). Kind: deterministic. Test, written first:

   ```gherkin
   Given the list of open blockers
   When the team sorts them by who controls the fix
   Then each blocker has one label
   And each "outside the team" blocker has a message sent to the sponsor
   ```

4. **Run a capped probe of a new format: one problem per meeting, analyzed with "five whys" (ask "why" repeatedly to find the root cause).** Step 4 MUST NOT start until Step 2 passes its test. It runs in parallel with Step 3. Increment: two 60-minute sessions. Blocker removed: the current format may not produce usable insight. Domain: Complex, because the effect on this team is visible only afterward (induction from trials). The pattern could break if team trust is low or attendance falls. Leverage is a guess. Kind: stochastic. Test, written first:
   - Metric: share of action items done by the next retrospective.
   - Baseline: the rate found in Step 1.
   - Threshold: at least 20 percentage points above baseline.
   - Sample size: two sessions, about four items. This is too small to prove the result, so treat a pass as a signal only.
   - Stop condition: end the probe after two sessions or if attendance drops. Budget: 120 minutes.

If Step 1 shows that the team finished its actions, replace Step 2 with a review of how the team chooses topics.
