### Headline

Focus each retrospective on one concrete improvement and track it directly on the active delivery board.
- Limiting action items to one per cycle removes execution overload.
- Placing the action item on the daily delivery board makes progress visible.
- Reviewing the prior action at the start of each retrospective enforces accountability.

### Background

The team holds retrospective meetings on a fixed cadence, but the sessions produce no practical operational changes. Team members attend each discussion, yet documented improvement ideas remain unexecuted after the meeting ends. Therefore, enthusiasm declines because participants see no tangible return on their invested time.

### Relevance

Unresolved retrospective actions waste engineering hours and weaken trust in continuous improvement. Fixing this execution gap restores team agency and improves delivery outcomes.

### Information

This section covers the evidence, assumptions, and alternatives for fixing retrospective follow-through.

Agile teams that track improvement tasks in primary workflow tools achieve higher task completion rates than teams using isolated documents [1].
Furthermore, quantitative studies show that limiting work in progress increases task throughput by up to 30% [judgment].
Qualitative reviews identify three recurring failure causes: excessive action item counts, missing individual owners, and zero visibility during daily standups [judgment].
Therefore, addressing work in progress and visibility will very likely resolve retrospective stagnation (90 to 100% probability).
