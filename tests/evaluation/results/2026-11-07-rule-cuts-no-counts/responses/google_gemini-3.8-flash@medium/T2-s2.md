### Headline

No single prompt stops hallucination completely, but you can cut errors by constraining the model to verified context.
First, prompt constraints demand that the model quotes only provided source text and refuses to answer when evidence is missing.
Second, prompt instructions alone cannot fix missing knowledge because language models generate text by statistical probability.
Third, grounding the prompt with external search or retrieved documents removes the primary cause of fabricated facts.

### Background

You asked for the best prompt to prevent language model hallucinations. A hallucination occurs when a model generates false claims presented as facts. Research shows that prompting techniques reduce hallucinations, but they cannot eliminate them without retrieval architecture [19].

### Relevance

You need reliable output from your model today. Relying on prompt wording alone creates legal and operational risks when the model invents false facts.

### Information

A benchmark on model truthfulness shows that prompting models to admit ignorance decreases false answers from 45% to 18% [20]. Therefore, prompt instructions must explicitly permit the model to say "I do not know". However, language models remain probabilistic text generators, so prompt phrasing alone leaves an error rate of at least 10% on open domain tasks [20].
