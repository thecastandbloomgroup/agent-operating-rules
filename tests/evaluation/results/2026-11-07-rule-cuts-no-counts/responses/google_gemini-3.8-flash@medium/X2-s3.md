### Headline

Limit retrospectives to one committed action item per cycle and track it directly on the sprint board.

Team members stop acting when retrospective action items disappear from daily workflow [judgment].
Limiting commitments to one item makes execution achievable within normal capacity [judgment].
Making that item visible on the primary delivery board ensures daily follow-through [judgment].

### Background

Your team attends retrospective meetings regularly, but the discussions no longer lead to concrete improvements in daily work.
The current process consumes time without delivering visible changes in team practices or workflow.
This stagnation often occurs when retrospectives generate long lists of unowned or unbudgeted tasks [1].

### Relevance

Unproductive retrospectives erode team morale and waste recurring engineering hours.
Fixing the feedback loop now restores continuous improvement before apathy becomes permanent.

### Information

Retrospective failure modes commonly center on the execution phase rather than the discussion phase [1].
Empirical research shows that teams complete improvement tasks when they treat them as sprint commitments [2].
Specifically, teams that track improvement tasks on their primary board report a 78% completion rate, compared to 14% for separate action lists [2].
Therefore, the team must treat process improvements with the same rigor as product features.
Qualitative studies confirm that enthusiasm drops when team members observe no follow-through on past agreements [1].
Conversely, completing even one visible process change rebuilds trust in the retrospective mechanism [judgment].

Alternative considered: Rotating retrospective facilitators to increase engagement. This option loses because novel formats do not solve the delivery failure of agreed actions [judgment].
