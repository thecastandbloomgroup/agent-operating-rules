## Headline

Freeze all outbound statements, set up one crisis command, verify the claim against records within hours, and then choose between correction and defense based on evidence, not on whoever messages loudest.

- First, stabilise: nobody speaks for the company except one named spokesperson until the facts are confirmed.
- Second, classify the facts: the statement is either false, true but misleading, or true and defensible, and each path has different legal and PR moves.
- Third, sequence the response: legal exposure analysis and an aligned holding statement follow the fact check, never precede it.

## Background

A journalist publicly claims the CEO's statement from yesterday was false. Legal, PR, and the board are sending unaligned messages to the user, who appears to coordinate the response. The news cycle is running now.

## End

I recommend that we:

1. **Stabilise, Cynefin domain: Chaotic (act first to stop the bleeding; no cause and effect is visible).** Within 1 hour, convene one crisis call with Legal, PR, and one board liaison, and announce a communications freeze: only one named spokesperson speaks. This removes the unaligned-messaging blocker. Test, written first (deterministic):
   - Given the crisis call has ended,
   - When any employee receives a media enquiry,
   - Then the enquiry routes to the named spokesperson and no other statement goes out.

2. **Classify the facts, Cynefin domain: Confused (the verification output decides every later step).** Within 4 hours, pull the exact CEO statement and the journalist's published counter-evidence, and classify the statement as false, misleading, or defensible using internal records. This adds the fact base for the correction versus defense decision. Test, written first (deterministic):
   - Given the CEO statement and the journalist's claims,
   - When Legal and the record owner compare each factual claim against primary records,
   - Then each claim carries one label: verified, contradicted, or unverifiable, with the source document attached.

3. **Analyse legal exposure, Cynefin domain: Complicated (deduction from known law once facts exist).** Step 3 MUST NOT start until Step 2 produces labels. Legal scores exposure across four criteria with equal weight (25% each): falsity of the statement, materiality, disclosure duty, and defamation risk to or by the company. Pass threshold: a written one-page assessment per criterion citing the record from Step 2. Kind: evaluative, Kepner-Tregoe analysis. This removes the guesswork about regulatory and litigation risk.

4. **Issue one aligned holding statement, then the full response, Cynefin domain: Complex (public reaction is visible only afterward, so probe safely).** After Step 2 completes, publish a two-sentence holding statement via the single spokesperson. Budget: 2 hours of drafting, stop condition: do not publish any claim Step 2 labeled unverifiable. Kind: evaluative; rubric: accuracy (40%), single owner (30%), no speculation (30%), pass threshold 80/100 scored by Legal and PR together. If the statement proves false, the full response is a prompt correction; if defensible, it is a sourced rebuttal. Claimed leverage for the holding statement is a guess, per the Complex domain.

## Follow-up

One open question: is the journalist's evidence a document, a recording, or an anonymous source? The answer changes the rebuttal strength in Step 4.

Conditions that change the recommendation: if any claim is labeled contradicted in Step 2, replace the rebuttal path with a same-day correction and statement of cause; if the disclosure duty criterion in Step 3 passes, a regulatory filing precedes any public statement.
