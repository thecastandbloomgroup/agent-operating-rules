## Headline

A single owner must verify the facts against the original record today, then issue one holding statement through one spokesperson. Supported by: (1) freeze independent messaging from legal, PR, and the board now, because mixed statements deepen damage [1]; (2) verify the claim within 24 hours, because the correction strategy depends on whether the statement is false in fact or in interpretation; (3) convene one decision meeting with all three parties before any substantive reply; (4) monitor coverage with a stop condition.

## Background

A journalist published a finding that the CEO's statement yesterday was false. Legal, PR, and the board are contacting the user at the same time. No one agrees on the next action.

## Relevance

Uncoordinated statements during a crisis raise the reputation cost and are hard to retract [1]. The first hours decide the correction options that remain open.

## Information

**Evidence.**

- Crises damage reputation when stakeholders receive inconsistent responses from an organization; prompt, consistent, open response reduces it [1].
- Speed of first response is one of the top factors in stakeholder judgments of crisis handling [1]. [judgment] In a 24-hour news cycle, a holding statement needs to go out within hours, not days.
- Two response paths exist in this situation: a substantive rebuttal now, or a holding statement plus a verified correction later. The path depends on an unverified fact, so decision analysis must wait for fact-finding [judgment].

**Assumption log.**

- Assumption: the CEO statement contains a factual claim that differs from the record, not only a difference of interpretation. Why not measured: the material does not state the substance of either message. Validate by: compare the statement transcript with the article and the source document. If wrong: the substance becomes an opinion dispute, and the correction memo becomes a clarification memo.
- Assumption: no internal investigation or regulator contact is already underway. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask legal in the first message. If wrong: the response must follow counsel's process, and steps 2 and 3 change.
- Assumption: the user has the authority to designate a spokesperson and freeze messaging. Why not measured: the material does not state the user's role. Validate by: confirm with the board chair on the first call. If wrong: the user acts as coordinator and step 1 goes to the board chair instead.

**Alternative considered.**

The strongest alternative is an immediate substantive rebuttal from the CEO today. It loses because the organization has not verified the facts, so a rebuttal risks a second false statement, which costs more than a one-day holding statement [judgment].

## End

I recommend that we take the following four steps. Order follows crisis rules: stabilise first, analyze next, decide after [1].

1. **Freeze and align (Chaotic).** Within one hour, send a one-paragraph holding statement to legal, PR, and the board: "We are reviewing the statement and the report. We will reply with verified facts within 24 hours." The designee acts as the only spokesperson until step 3 ends. The blocker removed is mixed messaging. The test, written first:

   ```gherkin
   Given the holding statement text is approved by all three parties
   When the spokesperson replies to press or internal queries
   Then the reply matches the holding statement word for word
   And no other person speaks for the organization
   ```
   Domain: Chaotic. Cause and effect in ongoing coverage are not visible now; the action stabilises, it does not solve.

2. **Verify the facts (Complicated).** Within 12 hours, one person collects the statement transcript, the article, and the primary source document. The increment is a one-page table with three columns: claim, published finding, record. Before start, confirm with counsel whether an investigation is underway; step 2 MUST NOT start legal review until the person is named. The analysis that picks the output is direct document comparison, so the test:
   ```gherkin
   Given the statement, the article, and the source document are collected
   When each factual claim is checked against the record
   Then the table marks each claim as true, false, or unverifiable in fact
   ```
   The blocker removed is the decision deadlock, because step 3 needs this table. Domain: Complicated. The analysis is deduction from documents; the output confirms or refutes the first assumption.

3. **Decide the response (Evaluative).** Within 24 hours, hold one meeting with legal, PR, and the board. the output is one written response decision. The rubric, written first, uses Kepner-Tregoe form [15]:
   | Criterion | Weight | Evidence needed |
   |---|---|---|
   | Legal exposure | 0.35 | Counsel's assessment of each option [P-track record of the organization] |
   | Accuracy | 0.30 | The table from step 2 |
   | Stakeholder trust | 0.25 | Coverage themes and internal feedback |
   | Speed | 0.10 | Hours to draft and clear |
   Pass threshold: the option must score with accuracy and legal exposure both at "acceptable" or above. The blocker removed is the absence of one shared decision. Domain: Complicated. The rubric converts competing claims into one score by deduction.
   Step 3 MUST NOT start until step 2 produces the table.

4. **Monitor (Complex).** Run a daily 30-minute scan of coverage and internal questions for the next 5 days, capped at 2 hours of staff time per day. Measure: count of new stories, count of corrections, tone of top-3 stories. Stop condition: two consecutive days with no new coverage lifts monitoring to weekly. The blocker removed is blindness to a rebound. Domain: Complex. Cause and effect of coverage are visible only afterward; the measure and stop condition cap the probe, and its leverage is a guess.

## Follow-up

Questions, each with its use:

1. Does the article quote a primary source or a document leak? Use: it decides whether the verification in step 2 covers internal records only or also an external leaker. Per the assumption log, a no-leak outcome is 70% likely [judgment].
2. Has counsel already started a formal process? Use: it decides whether step 1 requires counsel's approval first.
3. What is the user's role and authority in this org? Use: it decides who executes step 1.

Conditions that change the recommendation:

- If legal confirms a regulator contact, replace steps 1 and 3 with counsel's required process [judgment].
- If step 2 finds the statement was accurate, step 3 becomes a factual rebuttal of the article, which is a different message [inference].
