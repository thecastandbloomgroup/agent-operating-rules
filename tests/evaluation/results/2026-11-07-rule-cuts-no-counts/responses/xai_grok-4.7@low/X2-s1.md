Headline

Finish one owned change before you add another topic.

- People already attend, so more meetings will not fix the stall.
- The stall sits in follow-through: an owner, a date, and a reviewed result.
- A change must fit in one cycle, or the next meeting starts from zero.
- A new format helps only after the last change has a result.

Background

The team holds a retrospective, a meeting that looks back at the last work cycle. People attend, but the work does not change. The request asks for the next step that will produce a shift.

Relevance

Each cycle without a finished change trains the team to ignore the meeting. Therefore the next meeting should prove one change, not collect more ideas.

Information

This section covers the evidence, the assumption, and the alternative.

The request states that people attend and that nothing shifts.
[1]

The Scrum Guide says the team identifies the most helpful changes. It also says the team addresses those changes as soon as possible.
[2]

Derby and Larsen end the retrospective in a decision about what to do, then a close.
[3]

Therefore a meeting that ends in topics, with no owned result, misses the step those sources require.
[judgment]

Medium confidence, two practice sources and no team notes.

Assumption: follow-through is the gap, not fear of speaking. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: score the last three notes for owner, date, and review. If wrong: replace the experiment with a safety check.

The strongest alternative is a new format or an outside facilitator. It loses because a new format still fails if no one reviews the last change.
[judgment]
