## Headline

Fix the follow-through loop around the retrospective, not the meeting itself: actions decay without an owner, a check date, and a review.

- First, measure the completion rate of past retro actions, because the rate separates a follow-through gap from a content gap.
- Second, close the loop: cap each retro at two actions, each with one named owner and one check date.
- Third, put those actions on the team board, so daily work cannot push them out of sight.
- Fourth, if actions finish but outcomes stay flat, probe for input that people withhold in the room.

## Background

The team holds retrospectives on a regular cadence, and attendance is fine. The actions from these meetings do not lead to change, so nothing shifts from one retro to the next. The request is how to make the retro produce change again.

## Relevance

A retro that changes nothing still costs the team one to two hours per cycle. It also teaches people that raising a problem has no effect, so honest input decays next.

## Information

**Evidence**

A stalled retro has two common causes. Cause one: actions leave the room without an owner or a check, and then decay [1].

Cause two: people withhold the real problems. Low psychological safety suppresses what teams raise [3].

Psychological safety means the shared belief that speaking up is safe. The Scrum framework tells teams to address the most useful improvements at once, and allows adding them to the Sprint Backlog [2].

The Sprint Backlog is the team's visible work list for the current cycle. Between the two causes, follow-through is cheaper to test, because the Step 1 audit takes under one hour. On patterns from stalled retros, a follow-through gap explains the stall in about 7 of 10 teams [judgment].

**Assumption log**

Assumption: the team runs periodic retros that end with action items. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: read the notes of the last retro. If wrong: keep the same steps, adapt the wording to the actual format.

Assumption: past retro actions were rarely finished, that is, the gap is follow-through. Why not measured: the material does not state the completion rate. Validate by: the Step 1 audit, under one hour. If wrong: a high rate with flat outcomes means the gap is problem quality or safety, so Step 4 takes priority over Step 2.

Assumption: the team has the authority to change its own process. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask the manager once. If wrong: add an escalation action for blocked items.

**Alternative considered**

The strongest alternative is to change the retro itself: a new format, a new facilitator, or cancelling it. This loses because ownerless actions decay under any format, so the symptom returns [judgment]. The safety cause stays live, so Steps 1 and 4 test it rather than assume it.

## End

I recommend that we run these four steps in order, with Step 4 held as a conditional probe. Each step names its Cynefin domain, that is, how cause and effect behave in that work.

**Step 1. Audit the last three retros.**

- Increment: in one working session, list every action item from the last three retros. Mark each one done or not done against the team's definition of done.
- Blocker removed: the cause is unknown today. The audit replaces guessing with a rate.
- Test (deterministic, written before the work):
  - Given the notes of the last three retros,
  - When each action item is marked done or not done,
  - Then the completion rate is reported to the team as a percentage.
- Domain: Complicated. The cause is knowable through analysis, and this audit is the analysis that picks the practice. The inference is deduction from the team's own records.

**Step 2. Reset the retro format.** Step 2 MUST NOT start until Step 1 reports its rate, because the rate sets the baseline for the fix.

- Increment: update the retro agenda in one session. Open with a five-minute review of the previous actions. Cap new actions at two. Give each action one named owner and one check date.
- Blocker removed: actions leave the room with no owner, so they decay.
- Test (deterministic, written before the work):
  - Given the updated retro agenda,
  - When the next retro ends,
  - Then the record shows at most two actions, each with one owner and one date.
- Domain: Clear. A known best practice exists for owners, small action counts, and review, so the inference is deduction from a known rule [1].

**Step 3. Put retro actions on the team board.** Step 3 MUST NOT start until the test in Step 2 passes, because it needs owner-assigned actions as input.

- Increment: add the one or two actions to the Sprint Backlog with owner and status, in the same session as the retro.
- Blocker removed: actions are invisible between meetings, so daily work pushes them out.
- Test (deterministic, written before the work):
  - Given a retro action with an owner,
  - When any team member opens the board,
  - Then the action shows with its owner and its current status.
- Domain: Clear. The Scrum Guide names this practice, so the inference is deduction from a known rule [2].

**Step 4. Anonymous input round, a capped probe.** Run it only if the Step 1 rate is 80% or higher and outcomes stayed flat, or if problem lists look thin. The 80% threshold is a judgment, not a standard [judgment]. Budget: one retro, 15 minutes.

- Increment: in one retro, collect problems anonymously (cards or a digital form) before open discussion.
- Capability added: shows whether people withhold problems in the open round.
- Test (stochastic, written before the work): metric is the count of new material problem themes from the anonymous round. Baseline is zero new themes in the last open retro. Pass threshold is one or more new themes, which starts safety work. Sample size is one retro with the full team.
- Stop condition: if the anonymous round adds no new theme, stop the probe. Safety is not the gap, so the gap is problem selection instead.
- Domain: Complex. Whether people withhold problems is visible only after the probe, so the inference is acting first within the budget. Leverage here is a guess.

## Follow-up

Conditions that change the recommendation:

- If the Step 1 rate is 80% or higher and outcomes are flat, skip the Step 2 expansion and start Step 4. The gap is content or safety, not follow-through.
- If Step 4 finds withheld input, the next actions target safety, for example an anonymous safety check each cycle. That work is outside this plan's scope.
