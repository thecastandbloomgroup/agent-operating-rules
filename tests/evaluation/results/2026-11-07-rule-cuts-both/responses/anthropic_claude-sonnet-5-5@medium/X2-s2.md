## Headline

**Reduce each retrospective (a regular team meeting to review how the work went) to one owned action, and find out why past actions died.**

- Past actions likely lack owners, time, or a place in the work plan [judgment].
- Fewer actions with a clear owner finish more often than long lists [judgment].

## Background

The team holds retrospectives and people attend. The meetings do not lead to change. The request is a plan to fix this.

## Relevance

A retrospective that changes nothing costs team time and lowers trust in the process. The Agile principle on reflection requires the team to "tune and adjust its behavior" after it reflects [1]. Without that step, the meeting has no purpose.

- Derby and Larsen treat the retrospective as a sequence that ends with a decision on what to do next [2]. Their book is practitioner advice, not a controlled study.
