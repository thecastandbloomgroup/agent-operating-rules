## Headline

Fix follow-through first: measure what happens to past action items, then limit each retro to one owned, scheduled change.

- Retros fail when they end without a change that someone owns. [judgment]
- The team has not yet shown which cause applies, so a short audit comes first.
- Some blockers sit outside the team, so those need a different route.
- Format fatigue is possible, so test one format change on a small budget.

## Background

Your team holds retrospectives (regular meetings where a team reviews its work and picks improvements). People attend, but no changes follow. You ask what to do.

## Relevance

A retro that changes nothing costs team time and lowers trust in the process. The longer it continues, the harder it is to restart.

## Information

**Evidence**

- The Scrum Guide says the retro plans ways to raise quality and effectiveness. It also says the most impactful improvements should enter the next sprint's backlog [1].
- Derby and Larsen describe a retro in five phases. The last phase is to decide what to do and close [2].
- The two sources agree: a retro needs a concrete, scheduled output. Neither source gives a failure rate, so the cause in your team is [no source].
- Teams that raise many items and finish few lose focus. [judgment]

**Assumption log**

- Assumption: the team works in sprints and records action items. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask one team member. If wrong: Step 1 uses whatever notes exist.
- Assumption: the cause is follow-through, not attendance or trust. Why not measured: no tool can reach the team's records. Validate by: Step 1 audit. If wrong: replace Step 2 with a trust-building probe.
- Assumption: the team can change its own process. Why not measured: the user has not answered. Validate by: Step 3 rubric. If wrong: Step 3 becomes the main action.

**Alternative considered**

Replace the facilitator or drop retros for a quarter. This loses because it removes the habit before the cause is known. [judgment]

## End

I recommend that we:

1. **Audit the last three retros.** Count the action items raised, assigned, and finished.
   - Increment: one 45-minute session reading the notes.
   - Blocker removed: the cause is unknown.
   - Kind: deterministic. Domain: Confused, because the cause is unclear. This step gathers data to classify the rest.
   - Test:
     - Given the notes of the last three retros
     - When the team counts items by status
     - Then a table shows raised, owned, dated, and done counts for each retro
2. **Limit each retro to one action item with an owner and a date.** Put it in the sprint backlog. Step 2 MUST NOT start until Step 1 ends.
   - Increment: one retro run with this rule.
   - Blocker removed: items have no owner or schedule.
   - Kind: deterministic. Domain: Clear, because the practice is known and applied by rule (deduction) [1].
   - Test:
     - Given a retro has ended
     - When the sprint backlog is opened
     - Then it holds exactly one retro item with a named owner and a due date
3. **List blockers the team cannot fix and take them to the person who can.**
   - Increment: one list, scored in one session.
   - Blocker removed: items stall outside the team's control.
   - Kind: evaluative. Domain: Complicated, because analysis of each item picks the route (deduction).
   - Rubric: control by the team (weight 40%), cost of delay (30%), effort to fix (30%). Pass threshold: 70 of 100. Evidence: Step 1 counts and the team's own estimates. Score each item and escalate those below the threshold of control.
4. **Run a two-retro probe of a new format.** Spend the first 20 minutes on one problem, and review last item status first. Step 4 MUST NOT start until Step 1 gives a baseline. Run it in parallel with Steps 2 and 3.
   - Increment: two retros.
   - Capability added: a tested format.
   - Kind: stochastic. Domain: Complex, because results show only afterward (induction). The pattern breaks if the cause is trust or workload. Leverage is a guess.
   - Acceptance test: metric is the share of retro items finished within one sprint. Baseline is the Step 1 rate. Threshold is the baseline plus 25 points. Sample size is two retros, which is small, so treat a pass as a signal only.
   - Stop condition: end the probe after two retros or 40 total team minutes beyond normal retro time.

If Step 1 shows that items were finished but had no effect, replace Step 2 with a review of how the team picks items.
