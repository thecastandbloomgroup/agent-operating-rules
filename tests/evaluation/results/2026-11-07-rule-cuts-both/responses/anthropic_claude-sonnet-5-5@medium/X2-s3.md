## Headline

Make each retrospective end in one owned, dated change, and measure whether it ships.

- The cause is unknown, so first count how many past action items were finished.
- A single change per retrospective is easier to finish than a long list.
- Blockers outside the team need a different route than team habits.

## Background

The team holds retrospectives (regular meetings to review how the team works). People attend, but nothing changes afterward. The material gives no data on past action items, team size, or meeting format.

## Relevance

Each retrospective without a change costs the team time and lowers trust in the meeting. Attendance without results is likely (66 to 100%) to turn into disengagement [judgment].

## Information

Evidence:

- The Agile Manifesto states that the team reflects at regular intervals, then adjusts its behavior. Reflection without adjustment fails that principle [1].

- Derby and Larsen describe five phases: set the stage, gather data, generate insights, decide what to do, and close. Your problem is likely in the "decide what to do" phase or after it [2].

- The most likely cause is a gap in follow-through, not a gap in ideas. This is likely (66 to 100%) [judgment, no team data].

Assumption log:

- Assumption: the team records action items somewhere. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask who keeps the notes. If wrong: Step 1 starts from memory and the baseline is weaker.
- Assumption: the team has fewer than 12 people and runs regular sprints. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: check the team calendar. If wrong: Step 2 needs a larger sample or a longer cycle.

Alternative considered: replace the format with new activities, such as new games or boards. It loses because format changes do not fix missing owners or dates [judgment]. Step 1 tests this claim.

## End

I recommend that we take these steps in order:

1. **Audit past action items.** This is a Confused action: the cause of the stall is not yet classifiable, so this step gathers the data. Inference: deduction from records.
   - Increment: list the action items from the last three retrospectives and mark each done or not done. This takes under one hour.
   - Blocker removed: no one knows the real completion rate.
   - Kind: deterministic. Test, written first:
     - Given the notes from the last three retrospectives
     - When the team marks each item done or not done
     - Then the completion rate is recorded as the baseline

2. **Run a one-change retrospective.** This is a Complex probe: the response of the team is visible only afterward. Inference: induction from trials. A new pattern can break if the owner is overloaded or if the change needs outside approval. Leverage is a guess [judgment].
   - Step 2 MUST NOT start until the test in Step 1 passes.
   - Increment: the next retrospective ends with exactly one change, one owner, and one date inside the next sprint. The owner adds it to the sprint board.
   - Blocker removed: long lists with no owner.
   - Kind: stochastic. Test, written first:
     - Metric: share of chosen changes finished by their date.
     - Baseline: the rate from Step 1.
     - Pass threshold: at least 2 of 3 changes finished, and above the baseline.
     - Sample size: 3 retrospectives.
   - Stop condition and budget: stop after 3 retrospectives or after 2 misses in a row. Extra cost is about 15 minutes per meeting.

3. **Sort stalled items by who controls them.** This is a Complicated action: analysis picks the route. Inference: deduction from the Step 1 list. It runs in parallel with Step 2 once Step 1 ends.
   - Increment: label each undone item from Step 1 as team-controlled or outside-controlled. Send outside items to the manager who can decide on them.
   - Blocker removed: items that the team cannot finish alone.
   - Kind: evaluative. Rubric, written first:

   | Criterion | Weight | Evidence needed |
   |---|---|---|
   | Team can act without outside approval | 50% | Named approver or none |
   | Cost to fix fits one sprint | 30% | Owner estimate in days |
   | Effect on team work is visible | 20% | One example from the last sprint |

   - Pass threshold: a weighted score of at least 60% means the team owns the item. A lower score means escalate it.

## Follow-up

Conditions that change the recommendation:

- If Step 1 shows most items were done, the problem is not follow-through. Replace Step 2 with a probe on meeting content, for example whether the team raises real issues.
- If the team does not record action items, Step 1 reaches no baseline. Use the Step 2 result alone and set the threshold at 2 of 3 changes finished.
