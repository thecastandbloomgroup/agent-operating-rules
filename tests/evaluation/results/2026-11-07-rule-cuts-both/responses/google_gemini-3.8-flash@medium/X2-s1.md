### Headline

Focus each retrospective on one measurable team problem and assign a single owner to test a solution during the next sprint. 
First, broad action lists create diffusion of responsibility across the team. 
Second, unmeasured action items prevent the team from verifying operational improvements. 
Third, placing action items into the sprint backlog establishes execution accountability.

### Background

Your team attends regular retrospectives, but the meetings no longer yield operational changes [judgment]. The current format consumes team time without shifting behaviors or team outcomes [judgment]. You requested an actionable approach to restore meaningful change from these sessions.

### Relevance

Unproductive retrospectives erode team morale and waste continuous improvement time [judgment]. Therefore, restoring accountability now prevents resignation and disengagement across the delivery cycle.

### Information

Retrospective failure modes often stem from vague action items, absent ownership, and a lack of verification [1]. Derby and Larsen demonstrate that teams make progress when they limit action items to one vital experiment per cycle [1]. Furthermore, research on goal setting shows that specific commitments with clear owners achieve higher execution rates than general team resolutions [2].
