### Headline

Focus on one root cause and one verified action item per cycle to restore momentum. 
Teams stagnate when action items lack owners and explicit verification [1]. 
Limiting changes to one high-leverage process fix creates immediate progress [judgment]. 
Reviewing the previous action item at the start of every session enforces accountability [1].

### Background

Your software team holds regular retrospective meetings, but the meetings no longer yield process changes. Team members attend each session, yet daily workflows remain unchanged after the discussions conclude. You asked for actionable steps to resolve this lack of execution.

### Relevance

Unproductive meetings consume valuable engineering hours and erode team trust. Therefore, fixing the follow-through mechanism now will prevent cynicism and restore continuous improvement.

### Information

Retrospective stagnation usually stems from three factors: vague action items, too many goals, and absent follow-up [1]. When teams identify five or six issues, they dilute their focus [judgment]. Consequently, nobody completes the tasks before the next sprint. 

Derby and Larsen show that successful teams limit their improvements to one experiment per cycle [1]. Furthermore, teams must assign an explicit owner and measurable acceptance criteria to each experiment [1]. 

#### Assumption Log

Assumption: The team operates in one-week or two-week development sprints. Why not measured: The material does not state it. Validate by: Inspecting the team's sprint board. If wrong: Adjust the action cadence to match your release cycle.

Assumption: Team members feel safe enough to speak honestly during sessions. Why not measured: Measuring costs more than the decision is worth. Validate by: Checking whether negative issues appear in retrospective notes. If wrong: Run an anonymous safety check before changing the action process.

#### Alternative Considered

The strongest alternative is replacing the facilitator with an external agile coach. However, external facilitation fails when the team lacks a mechanism to track action items within its daily work [judgment].

### End

I recommend that we:

1. **Step 1: Set a single-action limit policy**
   - **Test** (Deterministic):
     Given a list of retrospective action items,
     When the facilitator closes the retrospective session,
     Then the list contains exactly one committed action item with one designated owner.
   - **Increment**: Write a one-page operating rule that restricts retrospective output to one item and publish it to the team wiki.
   - **Blocker removed**: Removes cognitive overload and diffusion of responsibility across multiple vague tasks.
   - **Domain**: Clear. A single-owner rule applies a verified best practice [1].

2. **Step 2: Add the action item to the sprint backlog**
   - **Test** (Deterministic):
     Given the agreed retrospective action item,
     When the team plans the next sprint,
     Then the action item appears on the active sprint board with assigned story points.
   - **Increment**: Create the ticket in the sprint tracking tool and link it to the sprint goal. Step 2 MUST NOT start until the policy in Step 1 exists.
   - **Blocker removed**: Removes the separation between regular product work and internal process improvement.
   - **Domain**: Clear. Modern agile delivery requires tracking process work alongside product tasks by deduction [1].

3. **Step 3: Begin every retrospective with an inspection of the previous item**
   - **Test** (Deterministic):
     Given the start of a retrospective meeting,
     When the facilitator presents the agenda,
     Then the first agenda item evaluates the completion and impact of the prior cycle's action item.
   - **Increment**: Update the retrospective meeting template with a mandatory five-minute opening review block. Step 3 MUST NOT start until Step 2 is complete.
   - **Blocker removed**: Removes the habit of ignoring past commitments.
   - **Domain**: Clear. Reviewing prior commitments follows standard meeting hygiene by deduction [1].

4. **Step 4: Run a two-sprint probe on action completion rates**
   - **Test** (Stochastic):
     Metric: percentage of retrospective action items completed during the sprint. Baseline: 0%. Pass threshold: 100% completion across two consecutive cycles. Sample size: 2 retrospective cycles.
   - **Increment**: Execute the revised workflow for two consecutive sprint retrospectives. Step 4 MUST NOT start until the team runs Step 3.
   - **Capability added**: Validates whether the new accountability cycle produces measurable workflow changes.
   - **Domain**: Complex. Human compliance varies; therefore, this probe tests whether the team adheres to commitments before wider process expansion. Leverage is a guess.
