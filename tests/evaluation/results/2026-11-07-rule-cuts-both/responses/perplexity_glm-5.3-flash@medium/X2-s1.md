## Headline

Stop using the retrospective to list problems, and turn it into the meeting that plans one improvement per cycle.

- Cap the output of each retrospective at one action with one owner and one date.
- Open the next retrospective by checking that action: done or not done.
- Measure the completion rate of retrospective actions over six cycles.
- Change the format only if the completion rate stays below the threshold.

## Background

The team holds retrospectives and attends them, but no work practice changes afterward. The request asks what to do. This pattern means the meeting feeds a backlog that nobody pulls from, so the meeting no longer drives work.

## Relevance

Every retrospective that produces no change costs about one person-hour per attendee and returns none of it. Because the loop is already broken, small fixes to the format will not help until the output becomes a committed piece of work.

## Information

The standard retrospective loop expects the team to decide on one or two improvements and carry them into the next cycle with owners [1]. When a team lists ten issues and assigns none, the loop breaks at the "decide what to do" step [1]. This answer treats that step as the failure point [judgment].

Assumption: The retrospectives run monthly or every two weeks, with a stable team of five to nine people. Why not measured: the material does not state the format or the team size. Validate by: one question to the team lead. If wrong: the step sizes below change, but the sequence does not.

Assumption: Past actions had owners, but the owners never finished them because normal delivery work won every time. Why not measured: the material does not state why past actions failed. Validate by: review the last three retrospective outputs with the team. If wrong: if no actions had owners at all, start with Step 1 only, because assignment is the missing piece.

Alternative considered: replace the retrospective with a permanent improvement backlog that anyone can pull from at any time. It loses because it removes the group reflection, and the review of why work did not happen is the part that keeps the loop honest [inference].

## End

I recommend that we run the following four steps. The steps keep the usual retrospective slot, so the change costs no extra calendar time.

1. **Cap the output at one action.** Increment: at the next retrospective, the team leaves with exactly one action, one owner, and one date, and the action fits in one working week. Blocker removed: diffuse responsibility, where an owner for everyone is an owner for no one. Test, written first:
   - Given a retrospective that people have closed
   - When the team records its output
   - Then the output holds exactly one action, one owner, and one date, and the action fits in one week
   Deterministic, Cynefin Clear: the rule is known to everyone, and the action is one step.

2. **Open the next retrospective with the check.** Increment: the first ten minutes of each retrospective review the last action, and the team marks it done or not done. This step depends on Step 1: it MUST NOT start until the team has produced one owned action. Blocker removed: silent failure, where an unfinished action never surfaces again. Test:
   - Given a retrospective that people have opened
   - When the team reads its previous output
   - Then the team has marked the last action done or not done before any new topic
   Deterministic, Cynefin Clear: same reasoning as Step 1.

3. **Measure the completion rate.** Increment: for six retrospective cycles, record the share of actions finished by their dates. Blocker removed: opinion-based debate about whether the meeting works. Acceptance test, written first: the metric is the completion rate per cycle; the baseline is unknown, so the first two cycles set the baseline; the pass threshold is 70 percent or higher over the last four cycles; the sample size is six cycles [judgment]. Stochastic, Cynefin Complicated: the cause-analysis needs measured data, because the trend picks the next step.

4. **Convert one retrospective into a working session.** Increment: once Step 3 shows a rate below the threshold for two consecutive cycles, replace one retrospective with a session that does the action instead of planning it. Budget: one session per cycle. Stop condition: a session that ships no change ends this probe. Measure: the count of changes shipped per session. Complex, because cause and effect appear only after the trial: the probe tests whether protected time, and not prioritisation, is the root cause. What could break the pattern: the team treats the session as ordinary delivery and plans no improvement at all.

## Follow-up

Conditions that change this recommendation:

- If the team is newer than three months, run the format review for the team first, because format problems and team formation problems look the same at this stage.
- If people stay silent because they fear blame, prioritise a safety fix before Step 1, because an owned action without safety becomes an owned fault.

Open questions, and what each answer changes:

- How often do the retrospectives run? The answer sets the cycle length in Steps 1 to 3.
- Do past actions have owners today? If not, Step 1 is the only step needed for now.
