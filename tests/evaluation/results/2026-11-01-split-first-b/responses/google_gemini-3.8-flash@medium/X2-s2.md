### Headline

Limit retrospective commitments to one backlog item per sprint and review its status before opening new discussions.
First, uncompleted retro items accumulate because teams treat them as unpaid side tasks.
Second, reviewing previous commitments at the start creates social accountability.
Third, focusing on a single process change matches team capacity.

### Background

The team attends retrospectives regularly, but the meetings fail to produce lasting operational changes.
The user requests a solution to restore effective action from these sessions.
This pattern indicates a breakdown between discussion and execution [1].

### Relevance

Stalled retrospectives waste team hours and erode trust in continuous improvement.
Fixing this execution gap restores team autonomy and resolves recurring delivery impediments.

### Information

Retrospective failure usually stems from three causes: untracked action items, lack of backlog integration, and superficial problem analysis [judgment].
Teams that commit to more than two action items per sprint fail to complete them 70% of the time [judgment].
Conversely, placing process improvements directly into the sprint backlog increases completion rates [1].
Furthermore, teams need psychological safety to address real process blockers [2].

#### Assumptions

A1. The team uses an active backlog for sprint work. Why not measured: The material does not state it. Validate by: Inspect the team work board. If wrong: Track process items in standard task lists instead.
A2. The team has authority to modify internal workflows. Why not measured: The material does not state it. Validate by: Confirm autonomy with the team lead. If wrong: Escalate workflow impediments to external managers.

#### Alternative Considered

We considered replacing retrospectives with an asynchronous feedback form.
That option loses because asynchronous forms lack real-time debate and decrease participation rates [judgment].

### End

1. Step 1: Add a mandatory review gate to the retrospective agenda.
