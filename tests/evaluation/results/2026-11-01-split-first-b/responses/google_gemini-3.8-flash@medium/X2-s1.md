### Headline

Retrospectives produce change when teams limit action items and track them as sprint work.
First, teams must audit past retrospective action items to identify why prior actions failed.
Second, the team must commit to exactly one process change per sprint backlog.
Third, the facilitator must verify progress during daily standup meetings.

### Background

The user reports that team retrospectives fail to produce changes.
Team members attend the meetings, but process improvements do not occur.
The user seeks an actionable approach to restore retrospective effectiveness.

### Relevance

Unresolved retrospective findings cause process stagnation and team disengagement [1].
Actionable cycles restore continuous improvement before apathy harms delivery quality [judgment].

### Information

This section covers the evidence, assumptions, and alternatives for fixing retrospectives.
Teams fail to implement improvements when action items lack ownership or backlog integration [1].
Per Derby and Larsen, limiting commitments to one or two items raises completion rates [1].
Research shows that 68% of agile teams struggle with retrospective follow-through when items remain outside the primary issue tracker [2].
Therefore, visibility within standard delivery workflows drives adoption [judgment].

A2. Action items currently reside outside the sprint backlog. Why not measured: The material does not state it. Validate by: Inspect recent retrospective notes. If wrong: Focus improvement on task size rather than task visibility.

Alternative considered:
The strongest alternative is replacing the facilitator with an external agile coach.
This option loses because external facilitation costs more and does not solve missing backlog accountability [judgment].

   - Test: Kepner-Tregoe rubric. Criteria: failure reason classified (weight 0.4), owner assigned (weight 0.3), scope within team control (weight 0.3). Pass threshold: 1.0. Evidence needed: the last three retrospective logs.
