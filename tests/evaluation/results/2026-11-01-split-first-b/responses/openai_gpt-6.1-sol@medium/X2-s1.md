## Headline

Replace open-ended retrospective discussions with one small change, protected time, and a check at the next meeting. [judgment]

- First, find where previous actions stopped: selection, ownership, execution, or review. [judgment]
- Then, test one change rather than create another list of intentions. [judgment]

## Background

Your team attends retrospectives, but you report no resulting changes. [1] That establishes an outcome problem, but not its cause.

## Relevance

Another discussion format does not directly address a failure to act. [judgment] Therefore, focus the next retrospective on what happens between meetings. [judgment]

## Information

**Check the path from discussion to change.** Use these four stages to locate the first failure:

| Stage | Evidence to inspect | Response if absent |
|---|---|---|
| Selection | One specific change | Reduce the list to one change |
| Ownership | One person accepts responsibility | Name an owner with authority |
| Execution | Time and resources exist | Reserve time or seek a sponsor |
| Review | The team checks the result | Start the next retrospective with that check |

These are diagnostic checks, not confirmed causes in your team. [judgment]

The Scrum Guide identifies improved quality and effectiveness as the purpose of a retrospective. [2] Attendance alone does not establish either outcome. [judgment]

**Avoid treating every problem as a team responsibility.** If the change requires outside authority, request a named decision from that authority. [judgment] Repeatedly assigning an impossible action to the team does not remove that dependency. [judgment]

**Confidence:** Low confidence about the cause, because the available evidence describes attendance and outcomes, not previous actions.

**Assumption log**

A1. The team controls at least one small process change. Why not measured: The material does not state it. Validate by: Check authority before selecting the change. If wrong: Seek a sponsor instead.

**Alternative considered:** Change the facilitator or meeting format. This loses as the first move because it does not directly test execution or review. [judgment]

## End

I recommend that we take these three steps, each small enough for one working session.

### 1. Inspect the last three retrospectives, or all available records

**Test first, deterministic:**
```text
Given the available retrospective records
When the team checks each agreed action
Then each action has a recorded owner, status, and review result
And absent details appear as “not recorded”
```

Spend **15 minutes** on this check. Identify the first stage in the table that lacks evidence.

- **Capability added:** A visible account of where actions stop.
- **Domain:** **Clear**, because this step extracts records rather than guesses motives.
- **Output:** A checked action list, including gaps rather than invented answers.

### 2. Create one action card and reserve its time

**Test first, deterministic:**
```text
Given one issue within the team’s control
When the team completes its action card
Then it names one owner, one change, and reserved time
And it states a baseline, success target, and review date
```

After Step 1 passes, spend **10 minutes** completing this card:

> By **[date]**, **[owner]** will test **[change]**, using **[reserved time]**.  
> Current result: **[baseline]**. Desired result: **[target]**.  
> Review: **[next retrospective date]**.

- **Blocker removed:** An intention without ownership, capacity, or a test.
- **Domain:** **Clear**, because completeness follows explicit fields, not a prediction of team behavior.
- **Output:** One complete card, not several competing actions.

If the owner lacks time, remove another commitment before accepting the action. [judgment]

### 3. Run one capped experiment

**Acceptance test first, stochastic:** The result can vary with team behavior.

- **Metric:** The local result named on the action card.
- **Baseline:** The current result recorded in Step 2.
- **Pass threshold:** The target recorded before the experiment.
- **Sample size:** One small trial.
- **Interpretation:** A pass supports another trial, not a proven lasting improvement. [judgment]

After Step 2 passes, execute a change that takes **at most 60 minutes**.

- **Capability added:** Evidence from an actual change.
- **Domain:** **Complex**, because the effect on team behavior becomes visible after the trial.
- **Budget:** One change and 60 minutes; the benefit remains an estimate.
- **Stop condition:** Stop if the trial exceeds that budget or requires authority the owner lacks.

Record the result, including a failed target. Do not present an unrun trial as progress.

## Follow-up

Start the next retrospective with: **“What happened to the change?”**

Review the result before collecting new topics. [judgment] If execution succeeds but results do not improve, change the experiment rather than add more actions. [judgment]

If people fear consequences for speaking, prioritize a confidential route to raise concerns instead of demanding more public detail. [judgment]
