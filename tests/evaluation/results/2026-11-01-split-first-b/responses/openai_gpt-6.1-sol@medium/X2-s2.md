## Headline

Replace open-ended retrospectives with a short cycle: identify the barrier, commit to one change, and check its effect. [judgment]

- First, find where past actions stop: agreement, ownership, authority, execution, or review. [judgment]
- Then, test one small change instead of producing another list. [judgment]
- If the team lacks authority, seek a decision from someone who has it. [judgment]

## Background

People attend your retrospectives, but you report that nothing changes. [1] That identifies the symptom, not its cause.

## Relevance

A new meeting format does not address a lack of authority or time. [judgment] Therefore, check what happens after the discussion before changing the discussion itself. [judgment]

## Information

**Treat this first as a failure to turn discussion into action, not a lack of participation.** [judgment]

Use these categories to locate the first barrier for each past action:

| First barrier | What to check |
|---|---|
| Agreement | Did the team choose a specific change? |
| Ownership | Did one person accept responsibility for the next step? |
| Authority | Could the team make the change without outside approval? |
| Execution | Did the team reserve time and complete the step? |
| Review | Did anyone check whether the change helped? |

Assign each action to its **first unmet condition**, so the categories do not overlap. [judgment]

The Scrum Guide calls for teams to address their most useful improvements promptly. [2] However, that guidance does not establish which barrier affects your team.

**Confidence:** low confidence about the cause, because the available evidence describes attendance and outcomes, not past commitments.

**Alternative considered:** Change the facilitator or meeting format first. This loses because it leaves barriers after the meeting untouched. [judgment]

## End

I recommend that we take these three steps.

### 1. Audit the last two retrospectives in 20 minutes

**Test first, deterministic:**
```text
Given notes or participant accounts from two retrospectives
When each proposed action receives a status
Then each action shows completion or its first unmet condition
```

Review the notes with one teammate. If notes do not exist, reconstruct the actions together.

Produce a short record: action, owner, status, and first barrier. Include “no action chosen” where appropriate.

- **Removes:** uncertainty about where progress stops.
- **Domain:** Clear, because this step records observable facts rather than diagnosing motives.
- **Output:** a checked record covering both retrospectives.

### 2. Use the next retrospective to write one complete commitment

**Test first, deterministic:**
```text
Given the barrier record from Step 1
When the team writes its next commitment
Then it names one change, one owner, reserved time, a deadline, and a success measure
```

Step 2 MUST NOT start until Step 1 passes its test.

Choose one change the team can complete before the following retrospective. Reserve time for it instead of treating it as extra work.

Use this template:

> Before [date], [owner] will make [change], using [reserved time].  
> Success means [observable result], compared with [current result].

For example, test a daily review slot if work waits too long for review.

If the team lacks authority, make the commitment a specific request to a named decision-maker. Include a response date.

- **Adds:** an executable commitment rather than an improvement wish.
- **Domain:** Clear, because the test checks whether the commitment contains the required fields.
- **Output:** one commitment that passes the test.

### 3. Test this approach across two retrospective cycles

**Acceptance test first, stochastic:** Results vary because people and work conditions vary.

- **Metric:** commitments completed and reviewed by their due dates.
- **Baseline:** completion and review counts from Step 1.
- **Sample:** two cycles, with one commitment per cycle.
- **Pass threshold:** two of two commitments completed and reviewed.
- **Effect check:** compare each change against its own success measure.

Step 3 MUST NOT start until Step 2 passes its test.

Begin each retrospective with the previous commitment, before introducing new topics.

Ask: “Did we do it, and did it help?” Record those as separate answers.

- **Adds:** evidence that the team can act and learn.
- **Domain:** Complex, because the effect on team behavior becomes visible only after the trial.
- **Budget:** two cycles, at most ten retrospective minutes per cycle, plus the agreed action time.
- **Stop condition:** stop an individual change if it harms delivery or exceeds its agreed time.
- **Leverage:** uncertain; this is a limited trial, not a proven cure.

These thresholds are proposed decision rules, not research-derived guarantees. [judgment]

## Follow-up

If commitments remain incomplete, address the recorded barrier rather than adding more actions. [judgment]

If commitments finish but do not help, change the experiment rather than blaming participation. [judgment]

If leaders repeatedly refuse necessary changes, address that authority problem instead of asking the team to repeat the discussion. [judgment]
