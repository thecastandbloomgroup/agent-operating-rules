**Headline**

No prompt can stop hallucination; you can lower its rate, and the best prompt does three things. First, it forces every claim to trace to a source you supply. Second, it orders the model to say "I do not know" when the sources lack the answer. Third, it adds a check step: the model lists its claims and deletes the ones it cannot trace.

```text
You answer from the material in SOURCES. Treat your memory as unverified.
Rules:
1. Support each claim with a quote or line ID from SOURCES.
2. If SOURCES do not answer, write: "I do not know from the sources."
3. Do not add names, numbers, or dates from memory.
4. First draft the answer. Then list each claim and mark it supported or unsupported.
5. Delete unsupported claims. Then output the answer.
```

**Information**

Prompt engineering reduces hallucination but does not remove it. Hallucination is plausible but false output, and it appears even with good prompts [1]. The root cause sits in training and decoding, so a system prompt on its own cannot reach zero errors [2]. Verification passes help: asking the model to check each claim against the source reduces unsupported statements in tests [3]. Therefore the prompt above adds grounding, refusal, and verification in one block.
