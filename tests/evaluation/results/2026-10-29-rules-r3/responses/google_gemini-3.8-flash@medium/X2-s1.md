### Headline

Tie retrospective actions directly to sprint planning and limit the team to one change per cycle.
First, teams take on too many items and drop them during sprint delivery.
Second, uncompleted items erode trust in the retrospective process.
Third, reviewing past actions at the start of each meeting restores accountability.

### Background

Your team attends retrospective meetings on schedule.
However, these sessions no longer produce tangible adjustments in workflow or behavior.
The team observes passive attendance without subsequent action.

### Relevance

Unresolved process issues waste team time and lower delivery speed.
Therefore, restoring action from retrospectives protects team morale and sprint outcomes.

### Information

Retrospective stagnation occurs when action items lack integration with standard backlog work [1].
Industry guidance indicates that teams complete more improvements when they limit work in progress to one item [2].
In addition, accountability drops when a meeting does not inspect the previous agreement [1].
I rate this pattern as very likely to be the primary cause [judgment].

#### Evidence
- Teams that pull process improvements into their sprint backlog report higher completion rates [1].
- Limiting work in progress increases task completion across team workflows [2].

#### Assumption log
A1. The team has control over its internal engineering practices. Why not measured: The material does not state it. Validate by: Ask the team during the next standup meeting. If wrong: Reframe action items toward organizational escalation instead.

A2. The team uses a visible backlog tool such as Jira or a physical board. Why not measured: The material does not state it. Validate by: Check the team workspace URL. If wrong: Track the improvement item on a shared document header.

#### Alternative considered
An alternative is to pause retrospectives for one month to reset team focus.
This option loses because skipping inspection cycles hides delivery defects and lowers psychological safety [judgment].

### End

I recommend that we take these three steps:

1. **Step 1: Cap retrospective output at one backlog item**
   - **Test (Deterministic)**:
     - Given a completed retrospective session,
     - When the facilitator records action items,
     - Then exactly one action item exists with a named owner and an estimate on the active sprint board.
   - **Increment**: Convert the top retrospective insight into a single story card during the retrospective closing.
   - **Blocker removed**: This action stops team overload and integrates improvement work into normal sprint tasks.
   - **Cynefin domain**: Clear.

2. **Step 2: Inspect previous action items at the start of the agenda**
   - Step 2 MUST NOT start until Step 1 finishes.
   - **Test (Deterministic)**:
     - Given the start of a retrospective meeting,
     - When the facilitator opens the agenda,
     - Then the team reviews the single action item from the prior cycle before collecting new topics.
   - **Increment**: Add a permanent five-minute review block to the retrospective agenda template.
   - **Blocker removed**: This action removes lack of follow-through and reveals recurring blockers early.
   - **Cynefin domain**: Clear.

3. **Step 3: Evaluate friction on stalled actions with a structured rubric**
   - Step 3 MUST run only if the item inspected in Step 2 was not done.
   - **Test (Evaluative)**:
     - Score stalled work against three criteria: clarity of scope (weight: 0.4), availability of tools (weight: 0.3), and external dependencies (weight: 0.3).
     - Pass threshold: score of 3.0 out of 5.0 based on team evidence.
   - **Increment**: Run a fifteen-minute analysis on the failed item to select an internal fix or an external escalation.
   - **Blocker removed**: This analysis removes hidden organizational impediments outside the direct control of the team.
   - **Cynefin domain**: Complicated.

### Follow-up

If A1 is false, replace Step 1 with an escalation item to management.
