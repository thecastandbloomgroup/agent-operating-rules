## Headline

Migrate to JWT (JSON Web Token, a signed token that carries user claims) and improve sign-up conversion as two separate changes, not one release.

- The two goals do not depend on each other, because JWT changes how the service proves identity, not how users sign up.
- A combined release hides the cause of any conversion change.
- A dual-mode rollout, with sessions and JWT valid together, keeps the migration reversible.
- A baseline measurement must come first, because no conversion number exists yet.

## Background

You ask for two things: move the auth service from server-side sessions to JWT, and raise sign-up conversion. The request gives no stack, client list, or current conversion rate. I assume the details in the log below and proceed.

## Relevance

Both changes touch the sign-up and login path. If you ship them together, a drop in conversion has two possible causes, and you cannot tell which one.

## Information

**Evidence**

1. A JWT carries claims such as expiry (`exp`) and audience (`aud`) [1].

2. Safe use needs a fixed signing algorithm, a check of `exp` and `aud`, and a check of the issuer [2].

3. A stateless JWT stays valid until it expires, so the service cannot revoke it early without a deny list [inference].

4. A JWT migration alone is unlikely (0 to 33%) to raise conversion. The reason: users do not see the token [judgment].

5. The current conversion rate is unknown [no source].
