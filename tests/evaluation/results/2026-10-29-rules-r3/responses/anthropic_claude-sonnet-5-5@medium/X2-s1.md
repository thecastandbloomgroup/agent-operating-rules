## Headline

Find out why past retrospective actions failed, then fix follow-through with one owned action per retrospective.

- The cause is unknown, so the first step is a short diagnosis of past action items.
- Most stalled retrospectives lack follow-through, so tie each action to one owner and one date.
- Changing the format is a probe, not a fix, so cap it and measure it.

## Background

Your team holds retrospectives (regular meetings to review how the team works). People attend, but no changes follow. You ask what to do. The material gives no data on past action items, team size, or format.

## Relevance

Each retrospective without a result costs the team an hour of attention. Trust in the meeting also drops with each repeat, so the problem grows.

## Information

Evidence:

- Retrospective practice ends with a step that picks a few actions to carry out. Without that step, the meeting only produces talk.
  [Derby and Larsen, ref 1]
- It is likely (66 to 100%) that missing follow-through, not missing ideas, is the main cause. Basis: [judgment], from the symptom "attend but nothing shifts".
- Team behavior links cause and effect only afterward. Format changes therefore belong to the Complex domain and need small probes.
  [Snowden and Boone, ref 2]
- The sources above do not measure your team. Confidence in the cause is low until Step 1 gives data.

Assumption log:

A1. The team records action items somewhere. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: open the last three retrospective notes. If wrong: add a step to start recording.

A2. The team can change its own work habits. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: count past actions that needed outside approval. If wrong: add a step to escalate blockers to management.

A3. The team has fewer than 12 people. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask the team lead. If wrong: split the retrospective into smaller groups.

Alternative considered: hire an outside facilitator now. It loses because it costs more than a one-session diagnosis, and it does not fix missing ownership. [judgment]

## End

I recommend that we take these steps in order:

1. **Diagnose past actions.**
   - Increment: in one session, list every action from the last three retrospectives and mark each as done, partial, or not started.
   - Blocker removed: we do not know why nothing shifts.
   - Kind: evaluative. Cynefin domain: Confused. This step gathers the facts that classify the rest.
   - Test, written first: a rubric with three criteria. Completion rate (weight 40%) needs the status of each item. Blocker type (weight 40%) needs a stated cause per unfinished item. Ownership (weight 20%) needs a named owner per item. Pass threshold: at least 80% of items scored with evidence. The completion rate becomes the baseline for Step 4.

2. **Limit each retrospective to one action with an owner and a date.**
   - Increment: add one closing step to the next retrospective.
   - Blocker removed: too many actions with no owner.
   - Kind: deterministic. Cynefin domain: Clear.
   - Test, written first (Gherkin [ref 3]):
     - Given a retrospective has ended
     - When the notes are published
     - Then they list exactly one action, one named owner, and one due date.

3. **Open each retrospective by reviewing the last action.**
   - Increment: add a five-minute first agenda item.
   - Blocker removed: nobody checks results, so actions fade.
   - Kind: deterministic. Cynefin domain: Clear.
   - Test, written first:
     - Given the previous retrospective set an action
     - When the next retrospective starts
     - Then the first item reports the status of that action.

4. **Probe a format change.** Step 4 MUST NOT start until Step 1 gives the baseline completion rate.
   - Increment: run a changed format for two retrospectives. Choose the change from the causes found in Step 1. Examples: a shorter meeting, or a theme per meeting.
   - Blocker removed: the old format may not surface useful actions.
   - Kind: stochastic. Cynefin domain: Complex, so this is a safe-to-fail probe. Leverage is a guess.
   - Test, written first: the metric is the share of actions done by their due date. The baseline comes from Step 1. The pass threshold is the baseline plus 25 points. The sample is two retrospectives, so power is low. Budget: two meetings. Stop condition: the team rates a meeting as a waste of time. Accept only if the threshold is met. Otherwise revert.

Steps 2 and 3 can run now. Step 4 runs in parallel with them once Step 1 ends. If A2 is false, replace Step 4 with a step that escalates the blocking items to management.

- If A3 is false, split the group before Step 2.
