Headline
Stop new retrospective topics until one prior action is done and its effect is checked.

Unfinished actions are the usual reason the meeting changes nothing.
One small owned change per cycle beats a long list.
Review that change at the start of the next retrospective.
If completed actions still leave results flat, change what you inspect.

Background
The team meets for retrospectives, but behavior and delivery do not change. People attend, and then the same problems return. The request is what to do so the meeting produces change.

Relevance
A cycle with no finished change teaches the team that the meeting has no effect. Attendance then becomes a ritual, and the next retrospective starts with less trust.

Information
The Scrum Guide says the team picks the most helpful changes and addresses the most impactful ones as soon as possible [1]. Derby and Larsen place the change in the step "decide what to do", not in the discussion alone [2]. A long list with no owner is the strongest local explanation when completion data are absent [judgment].

A1. The stall is unfinished actions, not a missing meeting. Why not measured: the material does not state completion data. Validate by: score the last four retrospective notes. If wrong: skip new action limits and inspect safety and topic choice first.

A2. The team can change its own working agreements. Why not measured: the material does not state authority. Validate by: name one change that needs no manager approval. If wrong: send that blocker to the sponsor before the next retrospective.

The strongest alternative is a new retrospective format or an outside facilitator. It loses because a new format does not finish an old action [judgment]. Use a format change only after the completion score is known.

End
I recommend that we take these steps in order.

1. Test: score each of the last four notes on four criteria, each 0 to 25: a concrete action, a named owner, a done or dropped mark, and a stated effect. Pass threshold: mean score at least 70, with owner plus done at least 35. Evidence is the notes. Increment: one working session to score four notes. Blocker: the stall type is unknown, so later steps wait on this class. Domain: Confused. This step gathers the facts that classify the rest.

2. Step 2 MUST NOT start until Step 1 shows owner plus done below 35. Test: Given one open action from the last retrospective, When the next retrospective starts, Then the first 10 minutes record done, not done, or dropped, and no new action is added while it is open. Increment: change one agenda and run it once. Blocker: new topics crowd out follow-through. Domain: Clear.

3. Step 3 MUST NOT start until Step 2 is on the agenda. Test: score up to three candidate actions on impact, effort, and reversibility, each 1 to 5. Pick the one with the highest impact that one person can finish inside the next cycle. Write owner, due date, and the signal you will see. Increment: one action on the team board. Blocker: vague wishes that nobody owns. Domain: Complicated. The scores are the analysis that picks the practice.

4. Step 4 MUST NOT start until Step 1 shows mean score at least 70 and the team still sees no shift. Test: metric is one outcome the team names, such as cycle time or escaped defects. Baseline is the prior two cycles. Pass means the direction is recorded after one cycle. Sample size is one cycle. Budget is one hour. Stop if two people will not speak. Increment: one small process probe, not a full process rewrite. Blocker: the team fixes the wrong thing. Domain: Complex. Leverage here is a guess.

Follow-up
If A1 is false, skip Steps 2 and 3 and run Step 4. If A2 is false, replace Step 2 with one written request to the sponsor for the decision the team cannot make.
