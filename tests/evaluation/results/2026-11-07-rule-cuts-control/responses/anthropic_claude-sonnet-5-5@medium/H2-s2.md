## Headline

Freeze outside statements now, verify the facts, then pick one response position through a single owner.

- **Alignment:** one decision owner and one channel stop the conflicting messages.
- **Facts:** a line-by-line check against records shows whether the claim is true.
- **Position:** a weighted comparison of response options selects the message.
- **Reaction:** a small, capped first release shows how the public responds.

## Background

A journalist published that the CEO's statement from Friday, November 6, 2026 was false. Legal, PR, and the board each send you separate advice. You need an order of steps.

## Relevance

Each hour without a shared position raises the chance of a second, contradicting statement [judgment]. A second contradiction is harder to repair than the first.

## Information

**Evidence**

- The journalist's claim is not yet checked. The claim is about as likely as not to be fully correct [judgment].
- Legal, PR, and the board pull in different directions. That is, three groups give advice and none has authority to decide [judgment].
- The Cynefin framework separates work by how cause and effect relate [1]. Here, fact checking is Complicated. The public reaction is Complex.

**Assumption log**

- Assumption: you coordinate the response and can name an owner. Why not measured: the material does not state your role. Validate by: ask the CEO or board chair to confirm in one message. If wrong: Step 1 moves to the person who holds that authority.
- Assumption: no regulator deadline falls in the next 24 hours. Why not measured: the material does not state the company's listing status. Validate by: ask legal for the deadline list. If wrong: Step 3 gains a fixed due time.
- Assumption: company records can confirm or refute the claim. Why not measured: no tool can reach them. Validate by: ask the records owner to find the source of each CEO claim. If wrong: Step 2 ends with "unverifiable", and Step 3 drops the retract option.

**Alternative considered**

Defend the CEO now with a firm rebuttal. It loses because the facts are unchecked. A rebuttal that proves false doubles the damage [judgment].

## End

I recommend that we:

1. **Name one decision owner and freeze outside statements for two hours.**
   - Increment: one message to legal, PR, and the board that names the owner and the freeze.
   - Blocker removed: conflicting advice with no authority to decide.
   - Kind: deterministic. Test, written first:
     - Given the owner and the freeze are named in one message
     - When legal, PR, and the board read it
     - Then each group confirms in writing and no outside statement is sent
   - Domain: Clear. Deduction from a known rule: one owner for crisis communication.

2. **Build a fact table that compares each CEO claim with company records.**
   - Increment: one table, one row per claim, each marked supported, refuted, or unverifiable.
   - Blocker removed: no shared view of what is true.
   - Kind: deterministic. Test, written first:
     - Given the CEO's statement is split into single claims
     - When legal and the records owner check each claim against a record
     - Then every row names its record and carries one of the three marks
   - Domain: Complicated. Deduction from records, checked by experts.
   - Step 3 MUST NOT start until this test passes.

3. **Score the response options and choose one position.**
   - Increment: one weighted score table for four options: correct, clarify, stand by, no comment.
   - Blocker removed: the dispute between legal, PR, and the board.
   - Kind: evaluative. Rubric, written first:
     - Factual accuracy, weight 35, evidence: the fact table.
     - Legal exposure, weight 25, evidence: counsel's written view.
     - Stakeholder trust, weight 20, evidence: board and PR input.
     - Speed, weight 10, evidence: time to release.
     - Reversibility, weight 10, evidence: cost to change the message later.
     - Pass threshold: 70 of 100, and any option that fails accuracy is rejected.
   - Domain: Complicated. Analysis picks among several good practices.

4. **Release the chosen statement to one channel as a capped probe.**
   - Increment: one statement, sent to the journalist's outlet and the company site only.
   - Blocker removed: no evidence of how the public responds. Leverage is a guess [judgment].
   - Kind: stochastic. Acceptance test, written first:
     - Metric: share of new coverage that repeats the false claim.
     - Baseline: the current coverage, counted before release.
     - Threshold: the share falls over 24 hours.
     - Sample size: all coverage that names the CEO in that period, at least 10 items.
   - Budget: one statement and 24 hours. Stop condition: a second outlet contradicts the statement. Then return to Step 2.
   - Domain: Complex. Induction from a trial. The pattern breaks if the journalist publishes new facts or a platform amplifies the story.
   - Step 4 MUST NOT start until Step 3 passes its threshold.

- If the fact table shows the CEO's claim is refuted, drop "stand by" and "no comment" from Step 3.
