## Headline

Find out why actions die, then run a small, capped test that makes one action per retrospective happen.

- Evidence on what blocks change is missing, so first count what happened to past actions.
- Follow-through is the most likely cause, and a fix needs an owner, a date, and a review.
- The fix runs as a limited trial, because team behavior cannot be predicted in advance.

## Background

The team holds retrospectives (regular meetings to review how the team works). People attend, but nothing changes. The request asks what to do. No data on past actions came with the request.

## Relevance

A retrospective that changes nothing costs team time and lowers trust in the next one. Each further meeting without change makes the habit harder to repair.

## Information

Evidence:
- A standard retrospective has five phases: set the stage, gather data, generate insights, decide what to do, close [1]. Teams that skip "decide what to do" or never review it produce talk without change. This is a [judgment] applied to the source.
- Missing follow-through is the likely cause of "nothing shifts" (likely, 66 to 100%). Basis: [judgment], no team data. Low confidence in the exact cause.
- Other causes exist: topics outside the team's control, low safety to speak, or too many actions. The poll in Step 2 tests these.

Assumption log:

- Assumption: the team runs retrospectives every two weeks. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: check the calendar. If wrong: change the trial length in Step 3 to four retrospectives.

Alternative considered: change the format or the facilitator. It loses because a new format does not fix missing owners and dates [judgment]. Ending retrospectives loses because the team loses its main feedback channel [judgment].
