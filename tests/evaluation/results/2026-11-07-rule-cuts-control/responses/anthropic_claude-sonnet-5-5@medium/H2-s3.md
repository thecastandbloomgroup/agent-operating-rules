## Headline

Name one decision owner, verify the facts, issue a neutral holding statement, and then choose the response from scored options.

- Alignment fails because no one owns the decision, so ownership comes first.
- The facts decide whether to correct or defend, so verify before any denial.
- A holding statement buys time without confirming or denying the claim.
- A scored comparison of options settles the legal, PR, and board views.

## Background

A journalist published a report that the CEO's statement from yesterday was false. Legal, PR, and the board each give you advice, and the advice conflicts. No one has yet checked the claim against company records.

## Relevance

The first 24 hours set the public frame of the story. [judgment] A wrong first move, such as a denial that later fails, is costly to reverse.

## Information

**Evidence**

- Legal guards against liability, PR guards reputation, and the board guards governance. Each view is valid, so the conflict comes from missing ownership. [judgment]
- The claim is false in at least one material respect: about as likely as not (33 to 66%). Basis: no documents seen, so confidence is low. [judgment]
- Conflicting advice is the main cause of delay: likely (66 to 100%). [judgment]

**Assumption log**

Assumption: the facts are unverified. Why not measured: the material does not state them. Validate by: compare each sentence of the CEO statement with primary records. If wrong: if the statement is true, skip correction and request a rebuttal through counsel.

Assumption: general counsel and a communications lead can act today. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: send both a message and ask for a reply within 30 minutes. If wrong: hire outside counsel and an outside communications adviser first.

Assumption: you have the authority to convene the CEO, the board chair, legal, and PR. Why not measured: the material does not state your role. Validate by: ask the CEO or board chair to confirm in writing. If wrong: ask the person with authority to run Step 1.

**Alternative considered**

The strongest alternative is a fast, firm public denial from PR. It loses because a denial that later fails raises the damage, and the facts are unchecked. [judgment]

## End

I recommend that we take these five steps in this order. No action is Chaotic or Confused: there is time to act, and Step 2 closes the main factual gap.

1. **Name one incident lead and one channel.**
   - Increment: a one-page charter, written in 60 minutes.
   - Blocker removed: four voices and no owner.
   - Kind: deterministic. Write this test before the charter.
   ```text
   Given a charter that names one incident lead and one channel
   When the CEO, board chair, legal, and PR read it
   Then each of the four confirms in writing within 60 minutes
   ```
   - Cynefin: Clear [1]. Reason: deduction from a known rule, since incident command roles are standard practice.
   - Steps 2 and 3 MUST NOT start until this test passes.

2. **Verify the facts.**
   - Increment: a table that rates each journalist claim as true, false, or unverified, built in three hours.
   - Blocker removed: no shared fact base.
   - Kind: evaluative. Rubric written first. Criteria and weights: accuracy against a primary record (50%), record completeness (20%), independent confirmation (30%). Pass threshold: 80 of 100, and every claim rated with at least one record. Evidence: emails, contracts, data exports, and meeting notes.
   - Cynefin: Complicated. Reason: deduction from expert analysis by legal and finance staff.

3. **Issue a holding statement and a document-preservation notice.**
   - Increment: a statement of under 100 words, plus a notice to relevant staff.
   - Blocker removed: silence while the story spreads.
   - Kind: deterministic. Write this test before the draft.
   ```text
   Given a draft holding statement
   When legal and the CEO review it
   Then it makes no claim on whether the earlier statement was true
   And it names the time of the next update
   ```
   - Cynefin: Clear. Reason: deduction from standard crisis practice.
   - Step 3 MAY run in parallel with Step 2. The statement MUST NOT go out until legal approves it.

4. **Choose the response.**
   - Increment: one scored comparison of four options: correct, clarify, defend, or make no comment.
   - Blocker removed: the legal, PR, and board disagreement.
   - Kind: evaluative. Rubric first, in Kepner-Tregoe form [2]. Criteria and weights: factual accuracy (35%), legal exposure (25%), trust of staff, customers, and board (25%), reversibility (15%). Pass threshold: the top-scoring option, signed by the incident lead, legal, PR, and the board chair.
   - Cynefin: Complicated. Reason: deduction from the Step 2 table.
   - Step 4 MUST NOT start until the Step 2 test passes.

5. **Run a capped stakeholder probe.**
   - Increment: brief 10 senior staff, customers, or investors with the holding statement.
   - Blocker removed: unknown reaction of key groups.
   - Kind: stochastic. Acceptance test first. Metric: distinct questions the statement leaves unanswered. Baseline: the count after the first three briefings. Pass threshold: fewer than three distinct unanswered questions. Sample size: 10 briefings.
   - Cynefin: Complex. Reason: induction from trials. A leak or a second article could break the pattern. Leverage is a guess.
   - Budget: two hours. Stop condition: any briefed person repeats the claim publicly, so escalate to the incident lead. Run in parallel with Steps 2 and 3.

## Follow-up

Open questions: none.

Conditions that change the recommendation:

- If Step 2 shows the statement is true, replace Step 4 with a rebuttal request to the journalist, sent through counsel.
- If Step 2 shows the statement is false, add a same-day correction from the CEO.
- If a regulator or stock exchange is involved, counsel leads disclosure and sets the timing, ahead of PR.
