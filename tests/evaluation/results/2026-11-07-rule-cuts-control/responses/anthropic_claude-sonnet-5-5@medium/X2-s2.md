## Headline

Run a short audit of past retrospective actions, then fix how actions are chosen, owned, and tracked.

- Retrospectives that produce no change usually fail at follow-through, not at discussion [judgment].
- The cause is not yet known, so the first step is to measure it.
- A small, owned, tracked action beats a long list of ideas.
- A format change is a test, not a fix, so cap its cost.

## Background

Your team holds retrospectives and people attend. No visible change follows. The material gives no data on past actions, team size, or sprint length.

## Relevance

A retrospective that changes nothing costs team time every cycle. It also teaches people that speaking up has no result, so attendance may fall next.

## Information

**Evidence**

- Retrospective guidance ends each session with a few chosen actions, not a long list [1].
- The cause of your stall has three parts: no action was chosen, no one owns it, or no one checks it [judgment].
- It is likely (66 to 100%) that at least one of these three parts is missing in your process [judgment].
- Only two or three retrospective cycles can be tested at a time, so confidence in any result is low [judgment].
- The Cynefin framework separates work with known causes from work where causes appear only afterward [2].

**Assumption log**

Assumption: The team has a backlog or board where work is tracked. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask one team member. If wrong: create a simple action list first.

Assumption: The retrospective happens every one to two weeks. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: check the calendar. If wrong: scale the trial length in the probe step.

Assumption: The stall is not caused by management blocking changes. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: the audit in Step 1 shows which actions were blocked. If wrong: escalate blocked items to the person who can remove them.

**Alternative considered**

Replace the retrospective format at once (new games, new facilitator). It loses because the cause is unknown, so a new format could repeat the same gap in follow-through [judgment].

## End

I recommend that we do the following, in this order:

1. **Audit the last three retrospectives.** List every action item, its owner, its due date, and its status. This takes one session. It removes the blocker of an unknown cause.
   - Kind: evaluative, with the test written first.
   - Rubric: criteria are owner named (weight 40%), due date set (30%), status recorded (30%). The pass threshold is that every action is scored. Evidence is the old notes or board.
   - Cynefin: Confused. The cause is unknown, so this step gathers data to classify. It comes first.

2. **Limit each retrospective to one or two actions, each with an owner and a due date, placed in the team backlog.** This step MUST NOT start until Step 1 is complete. It removes the gap between talk and work.
   - Kind: deterministic.
   - Given a retrospective ends
   - When the team lists its actions
   - Then at most two actions exist, each with an owner, a due date, and a backlog entry
   - Cynefin: Clear. Deduction from a known rule: owned, dated, tracked actions get done [1].

3. **Open each retrospective by reviewing the previous actions.** This step may run in parallel with Step 2. It adds a check that makes follow-through visible.
   - Kind: deterministic.
   - Given a retrospective starts
   - When the facilitator opens the session
   - Then each previous action shows as done, not done, or dropped, with a reason
   - Cynefin: Clear. Deduction from a known rule: a review makes status visible.

4. **Run a two-sprint probe on the format.** Ask the team to name the one change that would most help action completion. Try it for two retrospectives. This step adds learning about the true cause. It runs in parallel with Steps 2 and 3.
   - Kind: stochastic.
   - Metric: share of actions done by their due date.
   - Baseline: the rate found in Step 1.
   - Pass threshold: at least 70% and at least 20 percentage points above baseline [judgment].
   - Sample size: two retrospectives, which is small, so treat a pass as weak evidence.
   - Stop condition: if the rate falls below baseline after the first retrospective, revert the change.
   - Budget: one extra hour per retrospective.
   - Cynefin: Complex. Induction from trials. Team turnover or a release deadline could break the pattern. The leverage of this step is a guess.

If Step 1 shows that managers blocked most actions, replace Step 4 with an escalation of the blocked items.

## Follow-up

Conditions that change the recommendation:
- Step 1 shows that actions were done but had no effect. Then the problem is action quality, so pick actions tied to a measured team outcome.
- Attendance has already dropped. Then add a short private survey to Step 4.
