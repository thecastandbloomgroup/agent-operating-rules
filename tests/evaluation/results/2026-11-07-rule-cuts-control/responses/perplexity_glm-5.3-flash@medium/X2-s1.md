## Headline

Rebuild the follow-through loop around the retrospective, not the meeting itself.

- Actions fail after the meeting when they get no owner, no due date, and no ticket [2].
- Small, measured experiments produce visible change where long problem lists do not [3].
- Opening each retrospective with a review of past actions creates the missing feedback loop.

## Background

The team holds retrospectives on a schedule. Attendance holds, yet no work practice changes. The request asks for a way to turn the meetings into change.

## Relevance

Each failed retrospective spends one hour per attendee and returns nothing. A working loop converts that hour into measured improvement each cycle.

## End

I recommend that we rebuild the loop from ticket to review in one cycle, then add measured experiments.

**Step 1.** Convert every retro output into one ticket with one owner, one due date, and one metric name.
Blocker removed: actions evaporate after the meeting [2]. Kind: deterministic. Test (write it first):

**Step 2.** Open the next two retros with a 10 minute review of open tickets and their metric numbers.
Blocker removed: no follow-up loop existed. Kind: deterministic. Test (write it first):
Given the retro starts,

Then the first agenda item reviews each open ticket and reads its metric number aloud.
Domain: Clear. Deduction: the review step closes the loop. Step 2 MUST NOT start until Step 1 produces tickets.

**Step 3.** Cap each retro at two experiments. Each experiment changes one work practice for one cycle. Log the metric value before and after.
Blocker removed: broad action lists produce no test, so no learning [3]. Kind: stochastic. Acceptance test (write it first): metric = completed practice changes per cycle; baseline = 0 per cycle [1]; threshold = 1 change in at least 2 of 3 cycles; sample = 3 cycles.

This is a safe-to-fail probe; the stop condition is no metric movement after one cycle, then pause and reframe. Step 3 starts in the same cycle as Step 2.

**Step 4.** Rotate the format every third cycle. Before each meeting, collect three anonymous answers: one thing that works, one to change, one metric idea.
Blocker removed: falling contribution from repeated formats [judgment]. Kind: stochastic. Acceptance test (write it first): metric = attendees who gave at least one input; baseline = the count measured in the Step 2 review; threshold = a rise of at least one person; sample = 2 cycles.
Domain: Complex. Induction from trials: the effect of a format change is visible only afterward. Budget: 2 cycles. Stop condition: no rise for 2 cycles, then return to the prior format. Step 4 runs in parallel with Steps 2 and 3.
