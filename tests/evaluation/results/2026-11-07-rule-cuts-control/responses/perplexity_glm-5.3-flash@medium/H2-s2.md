## Headline

Freeze public replies now, verify the quoted record, hold one decision meeting with a written rubric, then ship one approved statement and monitor with a stop condition.

- A freeze ends the three-way divergence at near-zero cost and buys time for the fact work.
- The quote check is a text comparison, so the fact question and the response question stay separate.
- One meeting with a rubric resolves the legal, PR, and board conflict and names one decision owner.
- One message in one channel, plus a capped monitor, closes the loop without a second drift.

## Background

The CEO made a statement yesterday. A journalist published that the statement was false. Legal, PR, and the board have each messaged separate replies, so no shared fact base or owner exists.

## Relevance

Each mismatched reply becomes part of the story, so the fast and cheap step is the freeze [judgment]. A corrected message issued late costs more trust than one issued after a short review [inference].

## Information

**Analysis.** The three teams disagree because they optimize different outcomes: legal optimizes liability, PR optimizes public trust, the board optimizes fiduciary duty [inference]. Alignment therefore needs two inputs: one fact base and one decision owner. Split the problem into two parts. The fact question is deterministic: compare the article's quotes to the record. The response question is evaluative, and it depends on the fact verdict [judgment].

**Assumption log.**

- Assumption: the CEO statement exists in a fixed record (transcript, recording, or post). Why not measured: the request does not state the topic or medium. Validate by: pull the record in Step 2 tonight. If wrong: add one hour to reconstruct what was said.
- Assumption: no regulated-disclosure rule is already triggered. Why not measured: the topic is unknown. Validate by: legal reads the statement against listing rules in Step 3. If wrong: disclosure counsel leads, and Step 4 becomes a filing first.
- Assumption: the journalist expects a comment today. Why not measured: the request does not say. Validate by: check the article and the reply mailbox. If wrong: the freeze still holds; the contact step drops.

**Confidence.** High confidence for the freeze-then-verify order, because both steps depend only on facts in hand, not on the unknown topic [judgment].

**Alternative considered.** Wait for legal's complete review before any public action. It loses because the reply vacuum grows while three teams answer separately, and the freeze plus holding line costs minutes [judgment].

## End

I recommend that we run these four steps tonight, in this order.

**Step 1. Freeze and appoint. Domain: Chaotic.** Reason: stabilization requires acting before analysis, so order precedes leverage [18].
- Test (deterministic, written first):
  - Given a media, legal, or board inquiry reaches any team
  - When the recipient holds no spokesperson role
  - Then the recipient sends the approved line only and logs the inquiry
- Work: one memo by the end of hour one. It names one spokesperson, tells all channels to stop replies, and gives one approved holding line, for example: "We have seen the article and we are reviewing the record."
- Blocker removed: divergent replies and follow-up questions before the fact check lands.

**Step 2. Verify the record. Domain: Clear.** Reason: text comparison follows a known rule in one step, so leverage is reliable [18]. Step 3 waits on this test.
- Test (deterministic, written first):
  - Given the verified record and the article side by side
  - When each quoted passage is compared word by word
  - Then the checker marks each quote as accurate, truncated, or paraphrased, with timestamps
- Work: one hour by one person. Output: a marked table of quote verdicts.
- Blocker removed: the team stops arguing about what was said.

**Step 3. Classify and pick. Domain: Confused.** Reason: the correct response domain is unknown until materiality is known, so the meeting gathers what classifies it [18]. This step MUST NOT start until the Step 2 table exists.
- Test (evaluative, rubric written first): criteria with weights and evidence per criterion [15].
  - Quote verdict matches the record: weight 3, evidence: Step 2 table
  - Regulatory or litigation exposure: weight 3, evidence: legal read of listing and contract rules
  - Trust cost of each response option: weight 2, evidence: PR read of quote framing in press
  - Precedent cost of a formal correction: weight 2, evidence: past correction practice
  - Pass threshold: the option with the highest weighted score receives owner sign-off
- Work: one 60-minute meeting with legal, PR, and one board contact. Output: materiality tag, response owner, and one chosen response.
- Blocker removed: one owner replaces the three parallel streams.

**Step 4. Ship and probe. Domain: Complex.** Reason: the public reaction is visible only after the statement ships, so run a safe-to-fail probe with a budget and a stop condition [18]. This step MUST NOT start until Step 3 names the owner and the choice.
- Test (stochastic, written first):
  - Metric: share of the daily top-20 articles that repeat the "false" framing
  - Baseline: 1 of 1 today
  - Threshold: below 50 percent on day one
  - Sample size: 20 articles per day, two days
- Work: one approved response (correction, reaffirmation with context, or documented dispute), one channel, one voice. Then start the monitor. Stop condition: if the framing share exceeds 50 percent on day one, switch to the second-ranked option from Step 3.
- Blocker removed: the journalist fills the vacuum by default unless one message ships [judgment].

**Condition that changes this plan.** If the statement touches a regulated disclosure, replace Step 3 with a legal-led review and make the correction filing Step 4 [inference].

## Follow-up

No blocking questions: the first two steps do not depend on the unknown topic. Each assumption above names its cheapest validation test. If the topic is financial, the disclosure condition above takes control.
