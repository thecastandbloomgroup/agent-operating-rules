Headline
One owner must freeze public comment and verify the CEO statement before any reply.
Legal owns the truth check because a wrong public line creates legal risk.
PR owns one holding line so the three groups stop sending separate messages.
The board gets facts on a clock, not a live argument.

Background
A journalist published that the CEO statement from yesterday was false.
Legal, PR, and the board now message with different views.
The request is for next steps that put those groups on one path.

Relevance
The next public sentence can confirm, deny, or worsen the claim.
Therefore the groups must share facts before anyone replies.

Information
Coombs states that the response strategy should follow crisis responsibility, so the facts come before the stance.
[1]
Uncoordinated comments from several leaders raise contradiction risk [judgment].
The material does not say whether the statement was false, public, or material.

Assumption: the journalist claim is not yet verified inside the firm. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: compare the CEO words with source records in one session. If wrong: skip a correction and issue a denial with exhibits.

Assumption: no regulator has opened a file yet. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask legal for any inbound notice before the first board note. If wrong: legal leads the external reply and the holding line waits.

Assumption: the firm can name one incident lead today. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: confirm the name on the first alignment call. If wrong: the general counsel holds the role for 24 hours.

Alternative considered: publish an apology now. It loses because responsibility is not measured, and Coombs ties the stance to that measure.

An early apology can read as an admission if the report is wrong [judgment].

End
I recommend that we take these five actions.

1. Domain: Chaotic. Reason: cause and effect are not visible while three groups speak at once, so the first move stabilises.
Test:
Given an open journalist claim and no approved line,
When any staff member is asked to comment,
Then that person sends no comment and routes the ask to the incident lead.
Increment: send one freeze note to Legal, PR, the CEO office, and the board chair.
Blocker removed: further public contradiction.
Step 1 starts now.

2. Domain: Confused. Reason: the legal domain is not yet known, so this step gathers what classifies it.
Test: rubric first. Criteria: quote match 40%, record match 40%, materiality flag 20%. Pass threshold: 80% with written evidence for each scored item.
Increment: in one session, collect the exact CEO words, the exact article quotes, and the records that support or refute them.
Blocker removed: unknown truth status.
Step 2 starts with Step 1 and does not wait.

3. Domain: Complicated. Reason: after the packet, analysis can pick the practice: correct, deny, or stay silent.
Test:
Given a scored fact packet,
When legal and PR review it,
Then they sign one holding line of at most 40 words, or they log why they cannot.
Increment: one 30-minute call that produces that line.
Blocker removed: split messages to the board and the press.
Step 3 MUST NOT start until the test in Step 2 passes.

4. Domain: Clear. Reason: a known practice is a timed board brief once the line exists. Deduction from that practice sets this step.
Test:
Given a signed holding line,
When the incident lead sends the board note,
Then the note states the claim, the evidence status, the holding line, and the next check time.
Increment: one board note in the same session as Step 3.
Blocker removed: board pressure for an ad hoc reply.
Step 4 starts when Step 3 passes.

5. Domain: Complex. Reason: the journalist result is visible only afterward. This is a safe-to-fail probe, and leverage is a guess.
Test: stochastic, written first. Metric: a written reply on correction status. Baseline: no company contact. Pass threshold: one reply that states whether a correction is open. Sample size: 1 contact.
Budget: one counsel-approved email. Stop if counsel flags admission risk.
Increment: draft that email only.
Blocker removed: no path to correct the record.
Step 5 MUST NOT send until Step 3 passes.

Follow-up
Is the firm listed on an exchange? The answer decides whether securities counsel joins Step 2.
Did the CEO statement concern revenue, safety, or a deal? The answer decides whether Step 3 uses a correction or silence.
Has the journalist set a comment deadline? The answer sets the Step 3 clock.

If a regulator has already written, replace Step 5 with a counsel-only reply and do not contact the journalist.
If the packet shows the statement was false and material, replace the holding line with a correction plan before any denial.
