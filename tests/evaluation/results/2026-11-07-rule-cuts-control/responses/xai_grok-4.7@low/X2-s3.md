Headline

Close one improvement loop before you change the retrospective format.

- Score the last four meetings before you change the format.
- One owned experiment is easier to finish than a long action list.
- The next meeting should score that experiment before new topics start.
- Use an anonymous check only when finished actions still repeat one topic.

Background

A retrospective is a meeting where the team reviews its way of work and picks a change. People still attend, but the work does not change afterward. The request is for a next step that restores change without a new meeting type.

Relevance

Each cycle without a finished change trains the team to ignore the meeting. Therefore the next retrospective should prove one change, not collect more ideas.

Information

This section covers the evidence, the assumptions, and the alternative.

The Scrum Guide is the rule book for the Scrum method. It says a retrospective creates a plan to improve. It also says the team addresses the most useful improvements as soon as possible.
[1]

Derby and Larsen treat the output as an experiment, that is, a small change tried before the next meeting.
[2]

A long action list is a weaker output than one owned experiment, because no one can see a single result at the next meeting. [judgment]

Psychological safety means people can name problems without punishment. Google re:Work reports it as a top factor in team effectiveness. That report is a secondary source.
[3]

Assumption: The team can change its own meeting rules for one cycle. Why not measured: The material does not state it. Validate by: Ask the facilitator before the next retrospective. If wrong: Replace Step 2 with a written approval request.

Assumption: "Nothing shifts" means actions are not done or not reviewed. Why not measured: The material does not state it. Validate by: Score the last four notes in Step 1. If wrong: Keep the class from Step 1 and skip the mismatched step.

Assumption: Notes from the last four retrospectives exist or can be rebuilt. Why not measured: The material does not state it. Validate by: Open the board before the meeting. If wrong: Keep Step 1 Confused until one meeting is recorded in full.

Alternative considered: Drop the retrospective and use only a continuous improvement board. It loses because the team still needs a set time to review one result, and this meeting already provides that time. [judgment]

End

I recommend that we take these four actions.

1. Test first. Rubric: completion rate, weight 40 percent. Owner plus date, weight 30 percent. Repeated blocker, weight 30 percent. Evidence for each: notes from the last four retrospectives. Pass threshold: all three criteria have evidence. If any evidence is missing, the class stays Confused. In one session, score those notes and name one class. This removes the unknown cause. Domain: Confused. Reason: acting first, because cause and effect are not yet visible. If fewer than half of the actions are done, the class is open loop. If more than one in five actions lacks an owner or a date, the class is no owner. If at least half are done and the same blocker appears in three of four retrospectives, the class is repeated topic.

2. Test first.
Given the class is open loop or no owner
When the team leaves the retrospective
Then the board shows one experiment, one owner, and a due date before the next retrospective
Put that single experiment on the board in the same session. This removes the list that no one finishes. Domain: Complicated. Reason: deduction from the class in Step 1, which picks this practice. Step 2 MUST NOT start until Step 1 names open loop or no owner.

3. Test first.
Given one experiment is due
When the retrospective starts
Then the notes record done, not done, or blocked before any new topic
Use the first ten minutes for that review only. This removes the review that never happens. Domain: Clear. Reason: deduction from the rule that planned improvements are addressed soon. Step 3 MUST NOT start until the due date in Step 2 has passed.

4. Test first. Metric: blockers absent from the prior two retrospective notes. Baseline: zero such blockers. Pass threshold: at least one such blocker, with replies from at least 70 percent of people present. Sample size: one retrospective. Run one anonymous check, capped at 15 minutes. This adds a topic the open talk missed. Domain: Complex. Reason: induction from one trial, and a silent room can break the pattern. Step 4 MUST NOT start unless Step 1 names repeated topic.

If the team cannot change its own rules, replace Step 2 with one written approval request. Do not add more experiments until that request has an answer.

Follow-up

If Step 1 cannot find four sets of notes, record the next retrospective in full and repeat Step 1 after it.
