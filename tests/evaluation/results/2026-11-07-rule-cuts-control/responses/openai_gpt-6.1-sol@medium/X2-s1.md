## Headline

Treat the next retrospective as a review of one small change, not another discussion of everything that went wrong. [judgment]

- First, find where past changes stopped: selection, execution, impact, or review. [judgment]
- Next, give one change an owner, a deadline, and a success measure. [judgment]

## Background

People attend your retrospectives, but you report no resulting change. [1] That establishes the symptom, but not its cause.

## Relevance

A new meeting format will not fix a change that nobody owns or has time to complete. [judgment] Therefore, inspect what happens after the discussion before changing the discussion itself. [judgment]

## Information

### Find the point of failure

Check the last three retrospectives against these four stages. This is a diagnostic framework, not a claim about your team. [judgment]

| Stage | Evidence to inspect | Failure signal |
|---|---|---|
| Selection | Notes and decisions | No specific change was chosen |
| Execution | Owner, deadline, work record | A change was chosen but never tried |
| Impact | Outcome measure | The change happened but did not help |
| Review | Next retrospective’s agenda | The team never checked the result |

For each change, record the first stage that failed. This avoids counting one stalled change as several separate failures. [judgment]

The *Scrum Guide* describes retrospectives as a way to increase quality and effectiveness. It says teams should address the most useful improvements promptly. [2] This supports a focus on changes, not attendance alone.

**Confidence:** Moderate confidence in this starting approach, based on your reported symptom and the guide’s purpose. The actual failure stage remains unverified.

**Assumption:** The team controls at least one work practice. **Why not measured:** The material does not state it. **Validate by:** Name one change requiring no outside approval. **If wrong:** Use the next session to prepare an escalation.

**Alternative considered:** Change the facilitator or meeting format first. This loses because your report identifies absent change, not a specific problem with discussion. [judgment] Reconsider it if people cannot disagree safely or important concerns never enter the notes.

### 2. Create one complete change card

Start after Step 1 passes. Choose one change that addresses the most repeated failed stage.

**Test first, deterministic:**
```text
Given one proposed change
When the team completes its change card
Then the card names the change, owner, deadline, measure, and review date
```

Use this template:

> For the next two work cycles, **[owner]** will try **[specific change]**.  
> We will reserve **[time]** by reducing or stopping **[other work]**.  
> We will compare **[measure]** against **[current baseline]**.  
> On **[date]**, we will decide: keep, adjust, or stop.

Complete the card during the session. Do not accept “communicate better” as a specific change. [judgment]

This adds ownership and makes the work commitment visible. [judgment]

**Domain: Clear.** Deduction from the fixed template determines whether the card is complete.

### 3. Run a bounded trial and review it first

Start after Step 2 passes.

**Acceptance test first, stochastic:**

- **Metric:** The outcome measure on the change card.
- **Baseline:** The current value, recorded before the trial.
- **Pass threshold:** A numeric improvement agreed before the trial.
- **Sample size:** Two work cycles, with one observation per cycle.

Run one change only. Cap extra effort at one team-hour per cycle, unless the team explicitly replaces other work.

Stop early if the change harms delivery or exceeds that budget. At the next retrospective, review the evidence before opening new topics.

Accept the change only if it meets the threshold across both observations. Treat this as an initial signal, not proof of lasting benefit. [judgment]

This tests whether the chosen change helps, rather than merely whether someone completes it. [judgment]

**Domain: Complex.** Induction from a small trial guides the decision; workload changes can break the observed pattern. The benefit estimate remains a guess.

## Follow-up

If every useful change requires outside approval, replace the trial with one escalation: evidence, requested decision, decision owner, and response date.

If people fear consequences for speaking, address that barrier before treating the retrospective as a reliable source of concerns. [judgment]
