## Headline

Turn the retrospective into a short cycle: choose one change, give it an owner, and check the result. [judgment]

- First, trace where past decisions stopped: agreement, action, or review. [judgment]
- Next, test one change within the team’s control instead of producing another list. [judgment]
- Treat an external blocker as a request for authority, not another discussion topic. [judgment]

## Background

People attend your retrospectives, but you report no resulting changes. [1] The missing evidence is what happens between discussion and action.

## Relevance

A retrospective should lead to changes, not just shared observations. The Scrum Guide describes its purpose as planning ways to increase quality and effectiveness. [2]

Therefore, judge the next retrospective by what the team tries afterward, rather than participation alone. [judgment]

## Information

**First, separate the symptom from the cause.** Attendance without change does not identify whether the problem is ownership, capacity, authority, or the chosen action. [inference]

Confidence in any specific diagnosis is low because the available evidence contains no examples of past actions.

Use this distinction:

- **No action agreed:** narrow the discussion to one change the team can describe and test.
- **Action agreed but not tried:** examine ownership, available time, and authority.
- **Action tried but no benefit:** change the experiment rather than repeat it.
- **Benefit achieved but not sustained:** make the change part of normal work.

These are proposed checks, not findings about your team. [judgment]

**Assumption:** The team controls at least one small process change. **Why not measured:** The material does not state it. **Validate by:** Check authority before selecting the change. **If wrong:** Request a decision from someone with authority.

**Alternative considered:** Replace the facilitator or retrospective format. This loses as the first move because it changes the discussion without testing what prevents action. [judgment]

## End

I recommend that we take these three steps.

### 1. Trace recent commitments

**Test first, deterministic:**
```text
Given the available notes from the last three retrospectives
When the team traces each agreed action
Then each action shows an observed result, no action, or an explicit unknown
```

Spend no more than 20 minutes creating that record. Include the owner, what happened, and any blocker.

If no notes exist, record that gap rather than reconstruct a false history.

**Capability added:** A shared record of where commitments stop. [judgment]

**Domain: Clear.** Deduction from a fixed rule: every recorded commitment needs a visible status.

### 2. Define one small, testable change

Step 2 MUST NOT start until Step 1’s record exists.

**Test first, deterministic:**
```text
Given the action record
When the team selects its next change
Then one change has an owner, authority, time, a baseline, and a check date
```

Use one session to write this statement:

> “For the next 10 working days, [owner] will try [change]. Success means [observable result], compared with [current baseline].”

Choose a change the team controls. Reserve the required time before the session ends.

For example, replace “improve communication” with “name a reviewer when each work item starts.”

**Blocker removed:** A commitment that lacks a clear action or the means to act. [judgment]

**Domain: Complicated.** Use dependency analysis to identify who controls the change and what work it requires.

### 3. Run one limited trial and review the result

Step 3 MUST NOT start until Step 2’s test passes.

**Acceptance test first, stochastic:**

- **Metric:** Agreed changes tried by their check date.
- **Baseline:** The completion record from Step 1; mark it unavailable if notes do not establish it.
- **Pass threshold:** One of one selected changes tried within 10 working days.
- **Sample size:** One change.
- **Outcome check:** Compare the result with Step 2’s success measure.

Cap the trial at one staff-hour of extra effort. Stop if it exceeds that budget or creates a service or safety risk.

At the next retrospective, review the result before discussing new topics. Record one decision: keep, revise, or stop.

**Capability added:** A complete cycle from agreement to observed result. [judgment]

**Domain: Complex.** This is a small probe because team behavior depends on context. Induction from one trial cannot establish lasting improvement.

Workload or authority changes can break any observed pattern. Therefore, treat leverage as a guess, not a forecast. [judgment]

## Follow-up

If the team lacks authority, replace the trial with one specific request to the person who can decide.

If people fear consequences for speaking, address that before requesting more public disclosure. [judgment]
