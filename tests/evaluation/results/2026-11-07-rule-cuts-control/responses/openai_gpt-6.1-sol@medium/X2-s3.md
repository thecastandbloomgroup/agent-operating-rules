## Headline

Treat the next retrospective as a reset: trace past commitments, complete one small change, and review the result before discussing new topics.

- First, find where the chain breaks: agreement, ownership, execution, or review. [judgment]
- Next, reserve work time for one change instead of producing another list. [judgment]
- Finally, judge progress by completed changes and their effects, not attendance. [judgment]

## Background

Your team attends retrospectives, but you report that nothing changes afterward. [1] The cause remains unconfirmed: the problem could sit inside the meeting or outside the team’s control. [judgment]

## Relevance

A new meeting format does not directly address a failure to act after the meeting. [judgment] Therefore, inspect what happens between retrospectives before changing the format.

## Information

The Scrum Guide describes retrospectives as a way to improve quality and effectiveness. It says teams should address the most helpful changes as soon as possible. [2]

Use this distinction to locate the blockage:

| Where progress stops | Evidence to inspect | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Agreement | Concerns never become specific commitments | Define one observable change |
| Ownership | Commitments lack a named owner | Name one accountable owner |
| Execution | Owned commitments remain unfinished | Reserve time or escalate the dependency |
| Review | Changes happen, but nobody checks their effects | Review evidence at the next retrospective |

These are diagnostic categories, not findings about your team. [judgment] Classify each past commitment by the **first** point where progress stopped.

**Assumption:** The team can authorize at least one small work change. **Why not measured:** The material does not state it. **Validate by:** Check authority when selecting the change. **If wrong:** Seek a decision from the person who controls it.

**Alternative considered:** Change the facilitator or meeting format first. This loses because it does not directly address ownership, work time, or review. [judgment]

**Confidence:** Moderate confidence in this reset, based on your reported gap between attendance and change. Confidence in any specific cause remains low. [judgment]

## End

I recommend that we use these three steps, each limited to one working session.

### 1. Trace the last three retrospectives

**Test first, deterministic:** This tests whether the record is complete, not whether anyone deserves blame.

```text
Given the available records from the last three retrospectives
When the team traces each commitment
Then each commitment has an owner, status, evidence, and first blocked point
```

Spend 15 minutes creating that record. Mark missing information as “not recorded,” rather than guessing.

- **Increment:** One shared commitment record.
- **Blocker removed:** Uncertainty about where follow-through stops.
- **Domain:** **Clear**, because the team applies a known rule to recorded facts.
- **Sequence:** Complete this before Step 2.

### 2. Commit to one change with protected time

**Test first, deterministic:** This tests whether the commitment is ready for action.

```text
Given the commitment record
When the team records its next change
Then it names one owner, reserved time, a completion date, and a success check
```

At the next retrospective, select one change within the team’s authority. Limit the work to one working session. Reserve that time before the meeting ends.

Write the commitment as:

> By [date], [owner] will change [specific practice].  
> Completion means [observable output].  
> Improvement means [measure] changes from [baseline] to [target] by [review date].

- **Increment:** One complete action card and a calendar reservation.
- **Blocker removed:** A commitment without capacity or a clear finish.
- **Domain:** **Clear**, because the card follows fixed acceptance rules.
- **Sequence:** Step 2 MUST NOT start until Step 1 passes.

If nobody can reserve the time, record that capacity decision instead of promising the change. [judgment]

### 3. Start the following retrospective with the result

**Test first, deterministic:** This tests whether the team closes the action record.

```text
Given the action card and available result
When the next retrospective starts
Then the team records completion, observed effect, and one next decision
```

Use the first 10 minutes to inspect the result. Record one decision: keep, revise, stop, or escalate.

If the change remains unfinished, examine the obstacle before accepting another commitment.

- **Increment:** One closed review record with a next decision.
- **Blocker removed:** Repeated commitments without feedback.
- **Domain:** **Clear**, because the review follows a known completion rule.
- **Sequence:** Review Step 2’s result before creating another action card.

If the obstacle requires authority outside the team, request a named decision owner and decision date. Do not treat it as another team action. [judgment]
