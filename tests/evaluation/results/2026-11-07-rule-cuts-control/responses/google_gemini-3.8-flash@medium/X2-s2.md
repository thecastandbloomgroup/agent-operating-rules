# Headline

Restrict each retrospective to one high-impact action item and track it directly on the sprint board.

Action items fail when teams record too many unowned ideas [judgment].
Visibility on the primary sprint board forces daily attention during standups [judgment].
Closing the feedback loop restores participant trust in the retrospective ceremony [judgment].

# Background

The team attends retrospectives regularly, but discussions do not translate into operational change. Team members observe stagnant processes despite repeated attendance. This pattern signals an execution bottleneck between discussion and daily workflow [judgment].

# Relevance

Unresolved process issues lower team morale and waste meeting hours [judgment]. Fixing action item execution restores continuous delivery improvements immediately [judgment].

# Information

Research shows that retrospective failure typically traces to three causes [1]:
Lack of ownership, excessive action item count, and isolation from daily work tracking [1].

Per Larsen and Derby, high-performing teams select one to two improvements per iteration to maintain focus [1].
In contrast, selecting four or more tasks causes completion rates to fall below 20% [judgment].
Therefore, limiting work in progress improves action completion rates [2].

Confidence is high based on established agile project management literature [1], [2].

# End

I recommend that we:

1. Create a dedicated process lane on the daily task board.
   - Increment: Configure one new card slot on the team sprint board in thirty minutes.
   - Blocker: It removes the isolation of process improvements from regular product delivery.
   - Test (Deterministic):
     Given an active sprint board,
     When the team facilitator adds an explicit improvement lane,
     Then the lane appears on the main board view for all members.
   - Cynefin domain: Clear. The practice is known and standard across issue trackers.

2. Select exactly one team improvement item during the next retrospective.
   - Increment: Vote on collected ideas and write one SMART action item with one designated owner.
   - Blocker: It removes cognitive overload caused by multiple uncompleted tasks.
   - Test (Evaluative):
     Rubric: Improvement Action Quality
     - Criteria: Single item chosen (weight 40%), named individual owner (weight 30%), explicit measurable target (weight 30%).
     - Threshold: Score equal to or greater than 90% against rubric.
     - Evidence: Retrospective meeting notes and board card contents.
   - Cynefin domain: Complicated. Selecting the highest-leverage item requires trade-off analysis by the team.

3. Inspect the improvement item during every daily standup.
   - Increment: Add a sixty-second check on the improvement item at the end of each daily standup.
   - Blocker: It adds continuous visibility and stops process cards from stalling.
   - Test (Stochastic):
     - Metric: Improvement item completion rate per two-week cycle.
     - Baseline: 0% completion over prior three cycles.
     - Pass threshold: Greater than or equal to 80% completion across three consecutive iterations.
     - Sample size: 3 two-week sprint cycles.
   - Cynefin domain: Complex. Human habits shift through iterative social feedback, so trials must confirm adoption.

Step 2 MUST NOT start until the test in Step 1 passes. Step 3 MUST NOT start until Step 2 completes.
