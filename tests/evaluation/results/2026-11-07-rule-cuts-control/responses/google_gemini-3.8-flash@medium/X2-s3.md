### Headline

Limit retrospectives to one agreed improvement per cycle and place that action item directly into your sprint backlog.
Action items remain invisible when teams record them outside the primary sprint tracker [judgment].
Limiting commitments to one item prevents cognitive overload and resource competition [judgment].
Reviewing the previous cycle's action item at the start of each retrospective creates public accountability [judgment].

### Background

Your delivery team attends regular retrospectives, but the sessions no longer yield tangible process improvements.
Participants share feedback during the meetings, yet work practices remain unchanged in subsequent sprints.
This pattern indicates a breakdown between action planning and delivery execution.

### Relevance

Stagnant retrospectives waste engineering time and erode trust in continuous improvement.
Addressing this disconnect now restores team agency before disengagement turns into cynicism.

### Information

Retrospective failure usually stems from diffuse ownership and lack of integration with regular sprint work.
Derby and Larsen observe that retrospectives fail when teams select too many goals or choose items outside their direct control [1].
Furthermore, teams succeed more often when they limit work in progress to a single improvement item per cycle [2].

Assumption: The team uses a common backlog tool such as Jira for standard delivery work. Why not measured: The material does not state it. Validate by: Check the team task board during sprint planning. If wrong: Track the improvement on physical boards or the primary tracker that the team uses daily.

Alternative considered:
Assigning an external agile coach to facilitate the retrospectives.
This loses because external facilitation costs more and does not fix the lack of follow-through in the daily backlog [judgment].
