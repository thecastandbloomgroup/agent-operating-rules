Headline

Run retrospectives as a decision system, not a discussion: one owned change, one visible check, one stop rule.

- Attendance is not the failing part; conversion from insight to tested change is the failing part [judgment].
- One change per cycle beats a list because attention and ownership are limited [judgment].
- Low trust can hide causes, so add a small anonymous probe before blaming the format [judgment].

Background

The team meets, speaks, and records notes, but behavior and results stay flat. The request is how to make retrospectives produce change again.

Relevance

Repeated meetings without change teach people that input has no effect. That lowers future candor and makes later diagnosis harder [judgment].

Information

Likely causes are MECE: no single owner, too many actions, no check in daily work, and low safety [judgment]. Evidence from the query is thin, so confidence is medium: the pattern is common, but the local cause is unverified [judgment]. The fix must therefore combine a Clear follow-through rule with one Complex safety probe, per Cynefin logic [1].

Assumption: The team has authority to change at least one local work rule. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask the sponsor for one decision right this week. If wrong: move actions to escalation items.

Assumption: The retrospective is recurring and attended. Why not measured: the query states attendance happens. Validate by: review the last three invite lists. If wrong: fix attendance before redesign.

Alternative considered: replace retrospectives with a continuous improvement board. It loses because it keeps the same gap unless someone owns selection, time, and review [judgment].

End

I recommend that we do four actions in order.

1. Pick one change only.
- Increment: cap each retrospective at one team-owned experiment for the next cycle.
- Blocker removed: scattered effort across many action items.
- Test, deterministic, written first:
  - Given the last retrospective produced more than one action,
  - When the facilitator closes the meeting,
  - Then exactly one action has an owner, a due date, and a visible board card.
- Cynefin: Clear; cause and effect are known, so apply a best practice [1].

2. Review the action where work happens.
- Increment: add a two-minute check to the first daily meeting of each week.
- Blocker removed: the retrospective result disappears after the meeting.
- Test, deterministic, written first:
  - Given a board card moved to Done,
  - When the daily meeting starts,
  - Then the owner states result, blocker, and keep or revert in two minutes.
- Dependency: Step 2 MUST NOT start until Step 1 has created one card.
- Cynefin: Clear; status visibility is a known coordination rule [1].

3. Choose the next problem with a rubric.
- Increment: score candidate impediments in 15 minutes before the next retrospective.
- Blocker removed: loud topics beat costly topics.
- Test, evaluative, written first: use Kepner-Tregoe criteria [2].
  - Criteria and weights: frequency 30%, delay cost 40%, control to change 30%.
  - Evidence: last four cycles of tickets or standup notes.
  - Pass threshold: choose only items scoring 70 of 100 or more.
- Cynefin: Complicated; analysis can separate causes, and deduction from criteria is enough [1].

4. Probe safety with an anonymous pulse.
- Increment: run three anonymous questions for two retrospectives, then stop or scale.
- Blocker removed: silence about the real constraint.
- Test, stochastic, written first:
  - Metric: percent reporting “safe to raise a hard topic.”
  - Baseline: unknown; first pulse sets it.
  - Pass threshold: 70% safe across at least 8 responses, or repeat the probe once [judgment].
  - Budget: 10 minutes per survey; stop if response rate is under 50%.
- Cynefin: Complex; candor causes are visible only after trials, so use a capped safe-to-fail probe [1].

Follow-up

Conditions that change the recommendation: if no decision right exists, Step 1 becomes an escalation request. If the safety pulse stays below 50% response, pause topic work and repair trust first [judgment].
