# Headline

Retrospectives fail because actions lack owners and deadlines. Fix this with three steps: limit each retro to two action items, assign one owner per item, and review open items at the start of the next retro.

# Background

Your team holds retrospectives. Attendance is fine. Outcomes do not change. This is a common failure pattern [judgment]. The cause is usually weak follow-through, not weak discussion [judgment].

# Relevance

Retrospectives that produce no change cost about one to two hours per person per sprint [judgment]. If nothing improves, the team will disengage, and real problems will stay hidden.

# Information

Common causes of this failure, grouped:

1. **Too many actions.** Teams pick five or more items. None get done. [judgment]
2. **No owner.** "The team" owns the item. That means nobody owns it. [judgment]
3. **No review.** The next retro starts fresh. Old items vanish. [judgment]
4. **No authority.** The items need a manager's approval. The manager never sees them. [judgment]

Assumption: Your team is a software team with two-week sprints and a facilitator (Scrum Master or similar). Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask the team lead. If wrong: the same steps apply, because the pattern is process-level, not framework-specific.

Assumption: The facilitator has authority to assign owners and cap action counts. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: one conversation with the facilitator. If wrong: a manager must sponsor the change first.

Alternative considered: replace retrospectives with one-on-one feedback or an anonymous survey. This loses because it removes the shared discussion where causes surface. Surveys also face the same follow-through gap: 40 to 60% of survey action plans stall without owners [judgment, no source].

# End

I recommend that we:

1. **Cap actions at two per retro** (Clear domain: known cause and effect, best practice applies). This removes the overload blocker.
   - Test (deterministic):
   - Given a finished retrospective discussion,
   - When the facilitator records action items,
   - Then at most two items exist, each with one named owner and a deadline.

2. **Start each retro with a five-minute review of open items** (Clear domain). Each owner reports done, in progress, or dropped with a reason. This removes the vanishing-items blocker.
   - Test (deterministic):
   - Given the previous retro's action list,
   - When the new retro starts,
   - Then every open item gets a spoken status before new topics begin.

3. **Measure action completion for three sprints** (Complicated domain: needs light analysis). Metric: percent of retro action items marked done by their deadline. Baseline: count from the last three retros. Pass threshold: 80% completion across three sprints, sample size of at least six items. This adds a feedback signal.
   - Step 3 runs in parallel with Steps 1 and 2. Step 2 starts at the next scheduled retro.

If completion stays under 50% after three sprints, the blocker is authority, not process. Then escalate one item to the sponsoring manager per sprint.
