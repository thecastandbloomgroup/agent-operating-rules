### 2. Test one change, not a list

Step 2 starts after Step 1 passes.

**Kind:** Stochastic, meaning results can vary across attempts.

**Acceptance test, before work:**

- **Metric:** Number of eligible occasions when the team uses the chosen behavior.
- **Baseline:** Record the result across five occasions under the current process.
- **Sample:** Observe five comparable occasions during the trial.
- **Pass threshold:** Use the behavior on at least four occasions, exceeding the baseline.
- **Stop condition:** Stop if the change creates a safety risk or breaches a required control.

Choose one repeated problem with a baseline below four successes. Define the change as:

> “When [trigger] occurs, [owner] does [specific action], so that [observable result].”

For example: “When work becomes blocked, its owner records the needed decision and names who can make it.”

Cap the trial at two weeks and 60 minutes of extra team effort. These limits are starting choices, not research-based thresholds. [judgment]

**Capability added:** The team gets evidence about one change instead of another list of intentions. [judgment]

**Domain:** Complex, because induction from trials reveals whether the change helps. Different workloads can break the pattern.

Leverage remains a guess. [judgment] If five occasions do not occur within the cap, record insufficient evidence rather than success.

### 3. Review the result before new topics

Step 3 starts when the trial ends or reaches its cap.

**Kind:** Deterministic.

**Blocker removed:** The team closes the commitment before creating another. [judgment]

**Domain:** Clear, because deduction from the agreed test determines the next decision.
