## Headline

Stop running more retrospectives; instead cap the output at two small, owned actions and review that action list at the start of every meeting.

- Most retrospectives fail for one of three reasons: actions are too large, actions have no owner or date, or nobody reviews past actions [judgment].
- Sometimes the real blocker sits outside the meeting, for example in workload, staffing, or team interfaces [judgment].
- The cheapest fix is process discipline; the next cheapest is a short poll that shows where the blocker lives [judgment].

## Background

The team holds retrospectives. People attend, but nothing in the work changes. The request asks for a way to make the meeting produce change again.

## Relevance

A retrospective without follow-through costs one meeting per cycle. It also teaches the team that feedback changes nothing, so attendance and honesty drop over time [judgment].

## Information

Three failure patterns cover most cases [inference]:

1. Retrospectives produce action lists that are too large for one cycle.
2. Past actions have no owner, no date, and are never reviewed.
3. The real blocker sits outside the meeting, for example in staffing or interfaces with other teams.

First, check which pattern applies, so the team does not fix the wrong layer. The plan below removes patterns 1 and 2 in one cycle, then measures pattern 3 in parallel.

Assumption log:

Assumption: Past retrospectives produced action lists without owners or dates. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: read the last three action lists. If wrong: skip the action board and start with the poll.

Assumption: The team holds the retrospective on a fixed interval. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: check the meeting calendar. If wrong: fix the interval as part of Step 1.

Alternative considered: the strongest alternative is to treat the cause as low trust inside the team and run trust exercises. It loses because trust is one of several candidate causes, so a process test first gives the same information at a lower cost [judgment].

## End

I recommend that we apply four steps over two cycles.

1. **Step 1 (Clear, deterministic).** Cap each retrospective at two actions. Give each action one owner, one date, and a size of one working session. This removes the blocker of unowned, oversized action lists. Write the test before the work:
   - Given a retrospective that has ended
   - When a person reads the action board
   - Then every open action shows one owner, one date, and a size of one session
2. **Step 2 (Clear, deterministic).** Open each retrospective by reviewing the board. The team marks each past action done, moved, or dropped, and states the reason aloud. Step 2 MUST NOT start until the board from Step 1 exists. This adds a feedback loop on past actions. Write the test before the work:
   - Given an action from the last retrospective
   - When the facilitator opens the meeting
   - Then the team marks it done, moved, or dropped, and states why
3. **Step 3 (Complex, stochastic).** Run a five-question poll before each retrospective for two cycles. The poll asks each person to name their top blocker and where it sits: inside the team, outside the team, or in a process. This probe surfaces blockers that the meeting does not reach. It is a safe-to-fail probe: the budget is 30 minutes per cycle, and the stop condition is fewer than three answers per cycle. Acceptance test, written ahead: the metric is the count of named blockers that sit outside the team, the baseline is zero, the pass threshold is two or more such answers, and the sample size is five people over two cycles. Leverage is a guess; treat the result as input, not proof.
4. **Step 4 (Complicated, evaluative).** After two cycles, score candidate fixes in a Pugh matrix against the current process as the baseline column. Rubric first: the criteria are expected effect on the action closure rate (weight 3), cost in time (weight 2), and reversibility (weight 1); the pass threshold is a score that beats the baseline on two of three criteria, with the poll results and the closure data as the required evidence. This removes the blocker of choosing a structural change by opinion.

Condition that changes the plan: if the closure rate stays at zero across four cycles, stop the process steps. That result points to a cause outside the team, so escalate the poll findings to management instead.

## Follow-up

Condition that changes the recommendation: if reading the last three action lists shows that owners and dates already exist, replace Steps 1 and 2 with a capacity check, because the blocker then sits elsewhere.
