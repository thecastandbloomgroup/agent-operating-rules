### Headline

Focus each retrospective on one measurable improvement and assign it a single owner.
Teams abandon action items when lists are too long and lack clear owners [judgment].
Limiting the work in progress to one change allows the team to verify results within one sprint [1].
Reviewing the previous commitment at the start of the meeting forces accountability [judgment].

### Background

Your team holds regular retrospectives, but the discussions no longer lead to changes in practice. Team members attend the sessions, yet team behavior and delivery outcomes stay flat. You want a method to convert meeting discussions into completed team changes.

### Relevance

Wasted meetings drain team energy and lower morale. Fixing this execution gap restores team agency before members disengage from the process entirely.

### Information

Retrospectives fail to produce changes when teams generate large wish lists without accountability [1]. In agile software development, a retrospective is a recurring meeting where teams inspect their process and adapt it. When a team selects five or six action items per meeting, execution drops because daily delivery tasks take priority [judgment]. Therefore, the team treats improvement tasks as optional work.

To solve this problem, teams must treat process changes as standard delivery work [2]. First, the team must commit to exactly one action item per iteration. Next, the team must place that action item into the sprint backlog. Finally, the team must inspect the outcome during the next retrospective before choosing a new item [judgment].
