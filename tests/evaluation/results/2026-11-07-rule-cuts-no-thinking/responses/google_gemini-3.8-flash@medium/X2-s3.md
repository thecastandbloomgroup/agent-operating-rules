### Headline

Teams restore retrospective effectiveness when they limit changes to one committed action item per cycle and place that item on the work board.
First, long action lists dilute focus and create no accountability.
Second, visible board tickets ensure improvement work competes fairly with product delivery.
Third, inspecting prior commitments at the start of each meeting establishes operational discipline.

### Background

The team holds regular retrospectives, but these meetings no longer generate operational changes. 
Members attend the sessions, yet team behavior and processes remain unchanged. 
You requested an intervention to turn retrospective discussions into actual outcomes.

### Relevance

Unproductive meetings consume valuable engineering hours and erode team trust. 
Therefore, restoring this feedback loop is necessary to resolve recurring delivery blockers now.

### Information

Teams often generate too many vague tasks during retrospectives [1]. 
Consequently, members lose track of ownership once normal sprint work begins [1]. 
Furthermore, organizations frequently separate process improvement tasks from product delivery backlogs [2]. 
Delivery deadlines then displace those improvements [2]. 
Limiting commitments to one specific item per iteration raises completion rates [judgment].

Assumption log:
Assumption: The team operates on fixed iterative cycles such as two-week sprints. Why not measured: The material does not state it. Validate by: Check the team calendar. If wrong: Adjust the review cadence to match the actual team delivery cycle.

Alternative considered:
The strongest alternative is replacing retrospectives with an anonymous feedback survey. 
This option loses because surveys remove collaborative debate and reduce collective ownership of solutions [judgment].
