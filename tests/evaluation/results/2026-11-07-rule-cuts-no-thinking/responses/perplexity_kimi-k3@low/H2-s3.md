## Background

A journalist published a claim that the CEO's statement from yesterday was false. Legal, PR, and the board are sending conflicting input, and no one is aligned.

## Relevance

Silence reads as confirmation, and a fast unverified denial creates legal exposure. The next few hours set the public and legal record, so the sequence of steps matters more than speed.

## Information

**Evidence:**

- A public falsehood claim against an executive statement typically triggers securities, defamation, and duty-of-candour review before any reply [judgment].
- Retraction laws in many jurisdictions set short windows, often 48 hours to two weeks, for requesting corrections [judgment].
- Unverified denials that later prove false compound damages in both court and press coverage [judgment].

**Assumption log:**

- Assumption: the statement was recorded or published, so a verbatim record exists. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: pull the transcript or release within one hour. If wrong: verification takes longer, and the hold on comment extends.
- Assumption: the journalist's claim cites specific facts. Why not measured: the article text was not provided. Validate by: read the article and extract each factual claim. If wrong: treat it as opinion and skip the correction track.
- Assumption: no regulator has contacted the company yet. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask Legal to confirm. If wrong: Step 3 moves before Step 2, because regulatory duties outrank press handling.

**Alternative considered:** Issue a brief denial now to control the story. It loses because an unverified denial creates legal exposure if the claim holds, and credibility cost is higher than the cost of a few hours of silence with a "we are reviewing" holding statement.
