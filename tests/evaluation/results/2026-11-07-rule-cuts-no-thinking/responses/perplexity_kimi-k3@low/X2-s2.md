## Headline

Retros fail for only a few reasons, so diagnose the failure mode first, then cap output at one owned action per session.

- Retros fail in three distinct ways: actions not completed, actions too large, or low trust in the room.
- A one-hour review of recent retro notes can separate these causes.
- Capping each retro at one owned, scheduled action restores the feedback loop at low cost.
- A small anonymous safety probe checks the low-trust cause in parallel.

## Background

The team's retros happen and people attend, but no change follows. The request is a concrete plan to make retros produce change again.

## Relevance

A retro that changes nothing trains people to stop contributing. Each flat retro lowers the value of the next one, so the cost grows over time [judgment].
