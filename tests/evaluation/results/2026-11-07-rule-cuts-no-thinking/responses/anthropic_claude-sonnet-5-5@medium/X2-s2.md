## Headline

Measure what happened to past action items, find the cause, then limit each retrospective to one owned and dated change.

- The team has no baseline, so the cause of the stall is unknown.
- Missing follow-through is the most likely cause, at 70% likely [judgment].
- The fix is a small change to the process: one action, one owner, one date, in the work backlog.
- A format change is a probe, so cap its cost and test it against the baseline.

## Background

A retrospective is a recurring meeting where a team reviews its work and picks changes. Your team attends them, but no change follows. The message gives no data on past action items, team size, or meeting format.

## Relevance

A retrospective that changes nothing costs team time every cycle. It also teaches people that speaking up has no effect, so attendance may fall next.

## Information

**Evidence**

- Derby and Larsen describe a retrospective that ends with a decision on what to do, then a close [1].
- Edmondson found that psychological safety (the belief that one can speak up without punishment) relates to team learning behavior [2].
- Gollwitzer found that plans stating when, where, and how raise the rate of goal completion [3].
- Most failed retrospectives lack follow-through rather than discussion [judgment].

**Assumption log**

- Assumption: the retrospectives produce action items, and most are not done. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: count items from the last three retrospectives. If wrong: Step 3 changes to a safety or relevance fix.
- Assumption: the team has 5 to 10 people and a regular cycle of two to four weeks. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: check the team calendar. If wrong: change the sample size in Step 4.
- Assumption: the team lead can put action items into the work backlog. Why not measured: the user has not answered. Validate by: ask the lead to add one item. If wrong: Step 3 needs a sponsor with authority first.

**Alternative considered**

Stop the retrospectives and fix problems ad hoc. It loses because it removes the only shared forum, and the cause is still unknown [judgment]. An outside facilitator also loses. It costs more and treats a cause that Step 2 has not yet confirmed [judgment].

## End

I recommend that we:

1. **Audit the last three retrospectives.**
   - Increment: list each action item, its owner, its due date, and its status. This takes under one hour.
   - Blocker removed: no baseline exists.
   - Kind: deterministic. Domain: Clear. The answer comes from counting records, which is deduction from a known rule.
   - Test, written first:
     - Given the notes of the last three retrospectives
     - When the team counts items raised, owned, dated, and done
     - Then one table shows the completion rate as a number

2. **Run an anonymous five-question survey on why the retrospectives stall.**
   - Step 2 MUST NOT start until Step 1 ends, so the questions can use the audit result.
   - Increment: five questions on safety, relevance, ownership, authority, and time. Send it and collect replies in one session.
   - Blocker removed: the cause is unknown.
   - Kind: evaluative. Domain: Complicated. Analysis of the replies picks the cause, which is deduction from the data.
   - Test, written first. Rubric weights: ownership 30%, authority 25%, safety 25%, relevance 20%. Each cause is scored 0 to 5 from the replies and the audit. The pass threshold is a weighted score of at least 3.5 for one cause. If no cause passes, run Step 4 first.

3. **Change the close of the next retrospective.**
   - Step 3 MUST NOT start until Steps 1 and 2 are done.
   - Increment: end with one action, one named owner, one date, and a backlog entry. Open the next retrospective by reviewing that item.
   - Blocker removed: ideas do not become owned work.
   - Kind: deterministic. Domain: Clear. This is a known good practice, applied by deduction [1][3].
   - Test, written first:
     - Given a retrospective has ended
     - When the team checks the backlog
     - Then it holds exactly one new item with an owner and a date

4. **Run a capped probe on the retrospective format.**
   - Step 4 MUST NOT start until Step 1 gives a baseline. It runs in parallel with Step 3.
   - Increment: rotate the facilitator and use a new activity for three retrospectives.
   - Blocker removed: the format may be stale. The effect is a guess until trials run.
   - Kind: stochastic. Domain: Complex. The inference is induction from trials. Team mood or workload could break the pattern.
   - Budget: three sessions of 45 minutes. Stop after two sessions if the completion rate is below the Step 1 baseline.
   - Test, written first. The metric is the action completion rate. The baseline is the Step 1 rate. The pass threshold is the baseline plus 20 points, or at least 70%. The sample is three retrospectives, about nine items. This sample is small, so treat a pass as weak evidence.
