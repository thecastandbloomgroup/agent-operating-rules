## Headline

Make each retrospective end in one small, owned, tracked change, and measure whether the team finishes it.

- Retrospectives fail when ideas never become finished work, so the first task is to check what happened to past action items.
- One action with an owner, a due date, and a done-criterion is easier to finish than a list.
- A short trial over three retrospectives shows whether the fix works before the team commits to it.

## Background

Your team holds retrospectives (regular meetings where a team reviews how it works and picks improvements). People attend, but no change follows. The material does not state the team size, the meeting format, or what happened to past action items.

## Relevance

A retrospective that changes nothing costs team time every cycle and teaches people that speaking up has no effect. Attendance is likely to fall next.

## Information

**Evidence**

- The Scrum Guide says the retrospective should identify improvements and add the most impactful ones to the next Sprint backlog. This makes follow-through part of the event itself.

  [1]

- Derby and Larsen describe a closing phase in which the team decides what to do and commits to it. Their book is a secondary source and supports this practice.

  [2]

- The cause is likely (66 to 100%) lack of follow-through, not lack of ideas. [judgment] The team has no data yet, so Step 1 tests this.

**Assumption log**

Assumption: The team works in fixed cycles of one to two weeks and holds one retrospective per cycle. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask the facilitator. If wrong: change the trial length in Step 3.

Assumption: Past retrospectives produced ideas, but few were finished. Why not measured: no tool can reach the team's notes from here. Validate by: Step 1. If wrong: replace Step 2 with work on the meeting itself, such as safety or topic choice.

Assumption: The team can spend 30 minutes on diagnosis. Why not measured: the user has not answered. Validate by: book the slot. If wrong: split Step 1 across two days.

**Alternative considered**

Stop retrospectives and fix problems ad hoc. This loses because it removes the one regular place for team-wide change, and no evidence shows the meeting itself is the cause. [judgment]

## End

I recommend that we do these four steps in order:

1. **Audit the last three retrospectives.**
   - Increment: list every action item from the last three retrospectives with its status. This takes 30 minutes.
   - Blocker removed: the team does not know why nothing changes.
   - Kind: deterministic.
   - Test:
     - Given the notes of the last three retrospectives
     - When the facilitator lists each action item
     - Then each item shows an owner, a due date, and a status of done, open, or dropped
   - Domain: Confused. The cause is unknown, so this step gathers the data needed to classify the problem.

2. **Set a one-action rule.**
   - Increment: end the next retrospective with one action. It has a named owner, a due date inside the cycle, and a done-criterion. Put it on the team's work board.
   - Blocker removed: vague actions with no owner.
   - Kind: deterministic.
   - Test:
     - Given the retrospective is ending
     - When the team records its action
     - Then the board holds one item with an owner, a due date, and a done-criterion
   - Domain: Complicated. This follows deduction from a known practice [2]. Step 2 MUST NOT start until Step 1 is complete, because the audit decides the action's size.

3. **Run a three-retrospective trial of a review-first format.**
   - Increment: open each retrospective by reviewing the last action. Cap the added time at 10 minutes per meeting.
   - Blocker removed: no feedback loop between retrospectives.
   - Kind: stochastic.
   - Test:
     - Metric: share of actions finished by their due date.
     - Baseline: the rate found in Step 1.
     - Threshold: at least 2 of 3 actions finished, and above baseline.
     - Sample size: 3 retrospectives.
     - Stop condition: end the trial if no action is finished after 2 retrospectives.
   - Domain: Complex. This is induction from trials. A team change or a workload spike could break the pattern. The leverage is a guess. Run this in parallel with Step 2, capped at the 10-minute budget.

4. **Decide whether to keep, change, or drop the format.**
   - Increment: score the options in a 30-minute session after Step 3.
   - Blocker removed: no agreed rule for the next move.
   - Kind: evaluative.
   - Test: write the rubric before the session. Criteria and weights: completion rate 40%, team effort 30%, team view of value 30%. Pass threshold: 70 of 100. Evidence: the Step 3 data and a short team poll. Score each of the three options (keep, change, drop) against it.
   - Domain: Complicated. This is deduction from the trial data. Step 4 MUST NOT start until Step 3 ends.

If Step 1 shows that most actions were finished, replace Steps 2 and 3 with a review of the meeting's topics and safety.
