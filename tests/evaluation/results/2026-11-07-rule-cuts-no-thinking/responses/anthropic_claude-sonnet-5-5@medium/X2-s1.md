## Headline

Find out why the action items die, then fix capture, follow-up, and format in that order.

- Action items often lack an owner, a due date, or a place in daily work [judgment].
- Teams often skip the review of last retro's actions, so no one sees the gap [judgment].
- Topics outside the team's control produce actions that no one can finish [judgment].
- A stale format lowers energy, so attendance continues but candor drops [judgment].

## Background

The team holds retrospectives (regular meetings to review how the team works and agree changes). People attend, but no change follows. The request is for next steps.

## Relevance

Each retro without change costs the team's time and lowers trust in the meeting. Trust is harder to rebuild than to keep.

## Information

Evidence:
- Retrospective guidance advises few actions, each with an owner, and a review at the next meeting.

  See Derby and Larsen, Agile Retrospectives [1].

  See Snowden and Boone, HBR 2007 [2].
- The team's own records are not available, so the cause of the stall is unconfirmed [judgment].

## End

I recommend that we do these steps in order:

1. **Audit the last three retros.** Increment: one table listing each action, its owner, due date, and status. Blocker removed: the unknown cause of the stall. Domain: Complicated, because analysis of records deduces the cause. The audit is evaluative-free fact collection, so the test is deterministic.
   - Given the notes of the last three retros
   - When the table is built
   - Then every action has an owner, a due date, and a status of done, open, or blocked

2. **Cap each retro at one to two actions, each on the work board.** Step 2 MUST NOT start until Step 1 is done, because the audit shows the usual failure. Increment: a board template with owner, due date, and a first step of under two days. Blocker removed: actions that live only in notes. Domain: Clear, because a known best practice applies [1]. Deterministic test:
   - Given a retro has ended
   - When the team reviews the board
   - Then each new action has an owner, a due date, and a first step

3. **Open each retro with a review of the prior actions.** Increment: a fixed first agenda item of ten minutes. Blocker removed: no follow-up. Domain: Clear, because the practice is known [1]. Deterministic test:
   - Given a retro starts
   - When the first agenda item ends
   - Then each prior action is marked done, dropped with a reason, or carried over once

4. **Run a capped probe on format.** Step 4 MAY run in parallel with Steps 2 and 3. Increment: rotate the facilitator and change the format for four retros. Blocker removed: low candor and stale topics. Domain: Complex, because the effect of a format shows only afterward. Leverage is a guess. Induction from trials applies, and a change in team mix could break the pattern. Stochastic test:
   - Metric: share of actions done by their due date.
   - Baseline: the rate from Step 1.
   - Pass threshold: at least 70%, and at least 20 points above baseline.
   - Sample size: four retros.
   - Budget: four sessions of one hour. Stop early if the rate falls below baseline for two retros in a row.

If the audit shows that most actions were blocked by others, replace Step 4 with a request to the manager to remove those blockers.
