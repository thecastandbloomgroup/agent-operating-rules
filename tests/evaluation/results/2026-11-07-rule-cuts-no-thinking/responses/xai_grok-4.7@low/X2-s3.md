## Headline

Close the action loop before you change the meeting format.
People attend, so the gap is follow-through, not presence.
An action with no owner and no review date does not change the work.
The next meeting must start with the status of the last actions.
One owned change beats a long list of ideas.

## Background

The team runs retrospectives, and people attend.
The way of work does not change after those meetings.
The request is what the team should do next.

## Relevance

A meeting that never changes the work trains people to ignore it.
Therefore the next step is one visible change with a check.

## Information

The Scrum Guide says the team chooses improvements and handles the useful ones soon.
[1]
Derby and Larsen describe a retrospective that ends in a decision to try something, not only in talk.
[2]
However, attendance alone does not prove those changes happen.
[judgment]
The material does not show notes, owners, or completion rates.

Assumption: Past actions exist, but the next meeting does not review them. Why not measured: The material does not state it. Validate by: Read the last three note sets. If wrong: Use a silence probe before an action review.

Assumption: The team can change the agenda without outside approval. Why not measured: The material does not state it. Validate by: Ask the facilitator before the next meeting. If wrong: Ask the person who owns the meeting to approve the agenda.

Alternative considered: Stop retrospectives and use ad hoc fixes. This loses because the team already has a time slot, and the gap is a closed loop.
[judgment]

## End

I recommend that we take these four steps.

1. Domain: Complicated. Reason: analysis of the notes picks the practice.
Kind: deterministic.
Test:
Given the notes from the last three retrospectives,
When each agreed action is copied into one list,
Then every row shows owner, due date, and status, or marks that field missing.
Increment: one session to build that list.
Blocker removed: unknown status of past actions.
Step 2 must not start until this list exists.

2. Domain: Clear. Reason: deduction from a known rule to review prior actions first.
Kind: deterministic.
Test:
Given the action list from Step 1,
When the next retrospective starts,
Then the first 10 minutes cover only the status of those actions.
Increment: one agenda change for one meeting.
Blocker removed: actions that nobody checks.
This step waits on Step 1.

3. Domain: Clear. Reason: deduction from a known rule that one owner and one date finish a small change.
Kind: deterministic.
Test:
Given the retrospective ends,
When the notes are saved,
Then they contain one change, one owner, and one check date.
Increment: the output of that same meeting, after the review in Step 2.
Blocker removed: a list too large to finish.

4. Domain: Complex. Reason: induction from one probe, and one quiet meeting can break the pattern.
Kind: stochastic.
Test: The metric is blockers named only on a private note. The baseline is zero. The pass mark is one new blocker, or a note that says none. The sample size is one meeting.
Increment: a five minute private note, budget one meeting, stop if zero notes return.
Capability added: it shows silence that the action list cannot show.
Run this in parallel with Step 2. Do not expand it until the pass mark is met.

## Follow-up

If Step 1 shows owners, dates, and finished actions, replace Step 2 with a review of topics and silence.
If notes do not exist, use Step 1 for short talks with three attendees instead.
