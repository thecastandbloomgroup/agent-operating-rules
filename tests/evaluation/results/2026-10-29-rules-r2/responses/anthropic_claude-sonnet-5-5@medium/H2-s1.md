## Headline

Freeze public statements, name one decision owner, verify the facts within hours, then pick the public response.

- Control: one person decides, so Legal, PR, and the board stop giving conflicting orders.
- Facts: you cannot choose a response until you know which claims in the CEO's statement are false.
- Response: choose between correcting, clarifying, or standing by the statement, and score each option.
- Watch: track reaction while you work, so you can see if the story grows.

## Background

A journalist published today that the CEO's statement from yesterday was false. Legal, PR, and the board each send you advice, and the advice conflicts. You ask for next steps.

## Relevance

The first hours decide the shape of the story. A second inaccurate statement is likely (66 to 100%) to extend it [judgment].

## Information

**Evidence**

- Three groups give conflicting advice, so no one owns the decision [judgment].
- Whether the statement is false is unverified. Both a quick denial and a quick apology rest on that fact [judgment].
- Teams that act before they verify likely (66 to 100%) repeat the error [judgment].

**Assumption log**

A1. The article's claim is unverified. Why not measured: the material does not state whether the statement is false. Validate by: a line-by-line check in Step 2. If wrong: if the statement is true, replace Step 3 with a rebuttal request that Legal sends to the journalist.

A2. You can convene the group, or the board chair will grant that authority. Why not measured: the material does not state your role. Validate by: ask the board chair in your first message. If wrong: ask the chair to name the decision owner in Step 1.

A3. The company is not publicly listed. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask Legal. If wrong: add a disclosure-duty review to Step 2, and Legal sets the timing.

**Alternative considered**

The strongest alternative is an immediate public denial or apology to end the news cycle. It loses because you would speak before you know the facts, and a wrong reply doubles the damage [judgment].

## End

I recommend that we take these five actions in this order.

1. **Align control (within 1 hour).**
   - Increment: hold one 30-minute call with Legal, PR, the board chair, and the CEO.
   - Outcome: the call names one decision owner, freezes all outbound statements, and sends a records-retention notice.
   - Blocker removed: conflicting orders.
   - Kind: deterministic. Domain: Clear (a known practice exists).
   - Test, in Gherkin form (Given, When, Then):
     - Given the call has ended.
     - When you read the one-page decision record.
     - Then it shows one named owner, the freeze rule, and Legal's confirmation of the retention notice.

2. **Verify the facts (within 4 hours).**
   - Increment: Legal and one internal fact owner compare each claim in the CEO's statement with records.
   - Blocker removed: no one knows which claims are false.
   - Kind: evaluative. Domain: Complicated (analysis finds the answer).
   - Rubric, written first: criteria are source quality (weight 50%), claim coverage (30%), and reviewer agreement (20%).
   - Pass threshold: a score of 80 or more, and every claim marked true, false, or unclear with a record attached.
   - Step 3 MUST NOT start until Step 2 passes.

3. **Choose the response (within 6 hours).**
   - Increment: score three options (correct, clarify, stand by) in a Pugh matrix. A Pugh matrix is a table that scores options against a baseline option.
   - Baseline: "stand by".
   - Blocker removed: the PR and Legal disagreement.
   - Kind: evaluative. Domain: Complicated.
   - Criteria and weights: factual accuracy 40%, legal exposure 30%, trust effect 30%.
   - Pass threshold: the chosen option beats the baseline on accuracy and has no worse legal score.

4. **Brief the board (after Step 3).**
   - Increment: send a one-page note with the facts, the scored options, and the decision.
   - Blocker removed: board uncertainty.
   - Kind: evaluative. Domain: Clear.
   - Rubric: each of the three items is present (weight 30% each), and the note fits one page (10%).
   - Pass threshold: 100%, checked by the board chair.
   - Step 4 MUST NOT start until Step 3 passes.

5. **Run a reaction probe (start with Step 1, 24 hours).**
   - Increment: one analyst counts hourly mentions of the article.
   - Blocker removed: you cannot see whether the story grows.
   - Kind: stochastic. Domain: Complex (the effect appears only afterward).
   - Leverage: unknown. Treat any estimate as a guess.
   - Acceptance test, written first:
     - Metric: hourly mentions.
     - Baseline: the mean of the prior 7 days.
     - Threshold: escalate to the decision owner if mentions exceed 3 times the baseline for 3 consecutive hours.
     - Sample size: 24 hourly readings.
   - Budget: one analyst, 24 hours.
   - Stop condition: mentions stay below the baseline for 6 hours.
