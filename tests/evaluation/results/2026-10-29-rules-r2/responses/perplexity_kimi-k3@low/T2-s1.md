**Alternative considered:** "Just ask the model to think step by step" is the main rival. It helps on math and logic tasks, but it raises fluency and confidence on factual errors too, so it loses for hallucination control [judgment].

**The recommended prompt:**

```text
Answer my question in three parts.

1. What you know: state facts with the source for each fact.
   Use only sources I gave you, or name the work.
2. What you do not know: list the parts you cannot confirm.
   Say "unknown" for each one. Do not guess.
3. Confidence: rate each fact in part 1 as high, medium, or low.

Rules:
- Never invent a source, a quote, a number, or a date.
- If you cannot source a fact, move it to part 2.
- Short, plain sentences.

My question: <YOUR QUESTION>
Supporting material: <PASTE SOURCE TEXT HERE>
```

**Why this works** [judgment]:

1. The split into known and unknown gives the model a safe output for gaps.
2. The "never invent a source" rule targets the most common failure: fabricated citations.
3. Pasted source material grounds the answer. Grounded answers contain fewer false claims than memory-based answers [judgment].

**Limits** [judgment]: the model can still misread sources, and it can assign wrong confidence ratings. High-stakes facts still need manual checks.
