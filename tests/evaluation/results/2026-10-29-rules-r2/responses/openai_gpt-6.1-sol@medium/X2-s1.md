## Headline

Make the next retrospective produce one small, owned change, then check its effect before choosing another.

- First, find where previous decisions stopped: selection, completion, or effect.
- Next, test one change within the team’s control.
- Finally, review evidence rather than counting attendance or discussion topics.

## Background

People attend your retrospectives, but you report no resulting change. That report identifies a problem, but not where the process fails.

## Relevance

Another discussion format does not directly address the gap between a decision and a change. [judgment] Therefore, focus the next cycle on follow-through before changing the meeting format. [judgment]

## Information

The Scrum Guide describes the retrospective’s purpose as identifying changes that improve quality and effectiveness. It also says teams should address the most useful changes promptly. [1]

Use these checks to locate the failure:

| What the record shows | What to address |
|---|---|
| No specific action | Turn a complaint into an observable change. |
| An action exists but remains unfinished | Assign an owner and reserve work time. |
| An action finishes, but nobody checks its effect | Define the success test before the next action. |
| The change needs authority outside the team | Name the decision-maker and request a specific decision. |

These are diagnostic checks, not conclusions about your team. [judgment]

**Assumption log**

A1. The team controls at least one small work practice. Why not measured: The material does not state it. Validate by: Check the next proposed change. If wrong: Request an external decision instead.

**Alternative considered:** Replace the retrospective format or facilitator. This loses as the first move because your report concerns absent change, not absent participation. [judgment]

## End

I recommend that we take these three actions:

### 1. Trace the last two retrospectives to actual work

**Test first:** Given the last two retrospective records, when the review ends, then each decision has an owner, status, and evidence field. Record “absent” where needed.

Spend no more than 20 minutes checking those records against actual work. Identify whether the main gap concerns selection, completion, evidence, or outside authority.

This removes uncertainty about where to intervene. [judgment]

**Kind:** Deterministic, meaning fixed acceptance criteria.  
**Domain:** Complicated, meaning analysis can reveal cause and effect. Use the record-to-work comparison as that analysis.

If records do not exist, reconstruct the decisions with attendees. Do not treat missing records as proof that nothing happened.

### 2. Run one small change, not a list of promises

Step 2 MUST NOT start until Step 1 passes.

**Acceptance test first:**
- **Metric:** The share of eligible work occasions that use the agreed change.
- **Baseline:** Check the previous two comparable occasions.
- **Threshold:** Use the change on both next occasions.
- **Sample:** Two future occasions, within one retrospective cycle.

Choose a recurring problem within the team’s control. Write one action using this form:

> “When [event] occurs, [owner] will [specific action]. We will check [evidence] on [date].”

For example: “Before our next two planning meetings, Sam will circulate the proposed priorities one day ahead.”

Reserve the owner’s time. Cap extra effort at 60 minutes for the cycle. Stop if the change creates a safety risk or exceeds that budget.

This adds a testable change instead of another intention. [judgment]

**Kind:** Stochastic, because actual use varies.  
**Domain:** Complex, meaning the effect becomes visible after the test. Treat this as a small, reversible probe; its leverage remains uncertain.

The test checks adoption, not benefit. Record one relevant result too, such as meeting duration or unresolved decisions.

### 3. Begin the following retrospective with the result

Step 3 MUST NOT start until the probe ends or reaches its stop condition.

**Test first:** Given the probe’s evidence, when the next retrospective begins, then its first agenda item records completion, observed effect, and disposition.

Use the first ten minutes to record:
- Did the change happen?
- What result changed, compared with the baseline?
- Will the team retain, revise, or stop the change?

These prompts guide the meeting; they do not require answers now.

If the action remains unfinished, address capacity or authority before adding another action. [judgment]

**Kind:** Deterministic.  
**Domain:** Clear, meaning the agreed review procedure has known steps.

## Follow-up

If A1 is false, replace the local probe with one specific request to someone who has authority. Give that request an owner and decision date.

If people fear consequences for speaking, use a private facilitator-led route before asking for public explanations. [judgment]
