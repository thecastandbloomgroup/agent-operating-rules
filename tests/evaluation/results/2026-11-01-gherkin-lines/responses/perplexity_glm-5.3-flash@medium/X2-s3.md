Headline
Treat each retrospective as a pipeline that must end in one shipped change per cycle. Measure what happened to past action items before you change anything. Cap every retrospective at one or two owned, dated action items in the team backlog. Run a capped four-sprint experiment to confirm the fix. Escalate only if the team lacks authority to act.

Background
The team holds retrospectives. People attend, but no practice or process changes afterward. The request is for a way to make retrospectives produce changes.

Relevance
A retrospective that produces no changes costs a full team hour or more per cycle. The cost repeats every sprint until the pipeline from discussion to change works.

Evidence
The main retrospective guide states that a retrospective needs follow-through on action items to produce improvement [1]. Follow-through means each item gets an owner, a due date, and a check at the next session [1]. Work on continuous improvement shows that small, measured experiments drive change better than large plans [2]. Cause and effect in team behavior is complex, so safe-to-fail probes with a measure and a stop condition fit this problem [3]. Theme: all three sources point to action follow-through, not discussion format, as the failure point (3 sources, none disagree [1], [2], [3]).
