Headline

Stabilise external messaging first, then verify the facts, then align legal, PR, and the board on one written posture within 48 hours. Supporting points: (1) publish a holding statement today so only one voice speaks publicly; (2) complete a claim-by-claim fact review with legal before any substantive response; (3) run one alignment meeting that outputs a single decision, not three positions; (4) publish one corrected or confirmed statement on an official channel, then brief staff and the reporter.

Background

The CEO gave a public statement yesterday. A journalist today says it is false. Legal, PR, and the board each send separate messages, and no one has agreed on a posture. The request asks for concrete next steps under time pressure.

Relevance

Each hour without a single line raises the cost of the eventual correction [judgment]. Divergent internal messages also create risk if any of them leaks or reaches the reporter.

Information

First, classify the situation. Per Snowden and Boone, an event with no visible cause and effect calls for stabilisation before analysis [18].

Evidence

The main disputed fact is unknown; no transcript, article text, or document was provided in this conversation [no source]. Interviews of CEOs are widely covered, and fact-check processes inside newsrooms usually run before print [judgment]. Therefore the report is likely (66 to 100%) accurate as written. reliance on the journalist's claim without own review is the main risk [judgment].

Assumption log

A1. The statement was false on a checkable fact, not a dispute about tone. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: read the transcript and the article side by side. If wrong: the response becomes a clarification, not a correction.

A2. No regulator or court order applies in the next 7 days. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask legal in writing today. If wrong: legal hold overrides Steps 1 and 4.

A3. A recording or transcript of the statement exists. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: request the recording from the comms team. If wrong: fact review rests on testimony only, which lowers confidence.

Alternative considered

Contest the article publicly and immediately. It loses because contesting without own fact review creates a second false statement risk, and the legal exposure grows [judgment].

End

I recommend that we take these five steps, in this order.

1. Publish a holding statement and appoint one spokesperson, within 12 hours. Blocker removed: divergent voices reach press and staff. Test (deterministic):

Given the journalist's article is public,
   When the holding statement is approved and posted,
   Then every external media request receives only the approved wording,
   And the statement makes no new factual claims.

   Domain: Chaotic, because no cause and effect are visible yet and the step stabilises the message scene. Evidence that holding statements are standard practice: [judgment].

2. Run a claim-by-claim fact review, within 24 hours. It MUST NOT start until the spokesperson is named, because the spokesperson needs the fact pack for interviews. Blocker removed: nobody knows which claims are checkable. Test (evaluative). Rubric: for each disputed claim, record the exact quote (weight 30%), the supporting evidence with source (weight 40%), and a status of true, false, or unverified (weight 30%). Pass threshold: 100% of disputed claims classified with evidence attached. Domain: Confused, because the facts decide every later step.

3. Hold one alignment meeting and pick a posture: correct, contest, or correct and apologise. Blocker removed: legal, PR, and the board each pull in a different direction. Test (evaluative). Kepner-Tregoe matrix with criteria and weights: legal exposure 40%, long-term credibility 30%, speed of response 20%, stakeholder impact 10%; pass threshold: the chosen option scores highest across all four [15]. Domain: Complicated, because expertise and a structured analysis pick between good options.

4. Draft the substantive response and route it to two reviewers: one from legal and one board member, with a budget of 1 day. Blocker removed: the risk of another factual error in the response itself. Test (stochastic). Acceptance test: metric is the count of unresolved written objections; baseline is 2; pass threshold is 0 objections at a sample of 2 reviewers. Complex probes have a stop condition: stop and escalate to the board if any objection rests on legal exposure. Domain: Complex, because the reaction of reviewers and the reporter is visible only after the run; leverage is a guess [judgment]. Run this step in parallel with Step 5 preparation, capped by its 1-day budget.

5. Publish the response on the official channel, brief all staff in one email, and offer the reporter the fact pack. Blocker removed: the public record stays false and the internal story drifts. Test (deterministic):

Given the approved response,
   When it is posted to the official channel,
   Then the channel text matches the approved version word for word,
   And every staff account receives the same text within 1 hour.

   Domain: Clear, because the step is one apply-and-execute action with a known procedure.
