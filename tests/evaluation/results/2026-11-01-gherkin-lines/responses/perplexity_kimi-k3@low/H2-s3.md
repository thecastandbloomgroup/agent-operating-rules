## Headline

Stabilise first: freeze all external comment, name one decision owner, verify the facts today, then respond once from evidence.

- Mixed replies from Legal, PR, and the board are the main near-term risk, because they create the second story [judgment].
- Verified facts decide the fork: correction if the statement is false, rebuttal if it is accurate.
- One named owner ends the three-way conflict within hours [judgment].

## Background

A journalist has published that the CEO's statement yesterday was false. Legal, PR, and the board are each sending separate messages to the reader. No shared position exists.

## Relevance

Each hour of unaligned replies raises the chance of a follow-up story [judgment]. Exposure grows if the public position shifts after the facts come out, so early discipline pays [judgment].

## Information

**Evidence.** In a chaotic situation, crisis method says to act first to stabilise, then analyse.

That principle comes from the Cynefin framework, which sorts work by how cause and effect relate [1].

Structured scoring, not instinct, picks between correction and rebuttal [2].

The deterministic tests below use the Given, When, Then form [3].

**Uncertainty.** Whether the statement is false is unknown until Step 3. Treat it as about as likely as not, 33 to 66%, until the document check ends [judgment].

**Assumption log.**

A1. The truth of the CEO statement is not yet verified. Why not measured: the material does not include the article or the underlying data. Validate by: the document check in Step 3. If wrong: skip Step 3 and run Step 4 now.

A2. The reader can pull Legal, PR, and the board into one channel, or reach someone who can, within one hour. Why not measured: the material does not state the reader's role. Validate by: the answer to Q3. If wrong: the CEO's office sends the Step 1 freeze.

A3. The claim creates legal or regulatory exposure. Why not measured: the material does not state the company's listing or sector. Validate by: counsel on the first call, plus the answer to Q1. If wrong: drop the records step to normal retention and skip the disclosure check.

**Alternative considered.** An immediate public rebuttal before verification. It loses: a rebuttal that later proves wrong creates a larger second story and compounds legal exposure [judgment]. Full silence also loses: an unanswered frame hardens within one news cycle [judgment].

## End

I recommend that we run five steps today.

**Step 1, now. Freeze comment and preserve records.** Chaotic: no cause and effect is visible yet, so stabilise first.

- Increment: within one hour, send one directive to Legal, PR, and the board. No external comment. Keep all records. Route press to one spokesperson. Use one holding line until Step 4, that is, one approved sentence for all press replies.
- Blocker removed: contradictory statements and lost records.
- Test, written first, deterministic:
Given the directive is sent,
When any media inquiry arrives,
Then the reply is the holding line plus a referral to the spokesperson.

**Step 2, within two hours. Convene one crisis cell.** Chaotic: order must exist before analysis holds.

- Increment: one call with Legal, PR, one board delegate, and the CEO's office. Name one decision owner and one shared channel.
- Blocker removed: three unaligned inboxes.
- Test, written first, deterministic:
Given one named owner and one channel,
When external language needs approval,
Then only the owner approves it, within 30 minutes per item.
- Note: Step 2 runs parallel to Step 1. It does not replace it.

**Step 3, today. Verify the facts in writing.** Complicated: the truth is knowable through document analysis.

- Increment: assemble the CEO's exact words, the underlying data, and the article's cited evidence. Score the claim in one session.
- Blocker removed: nobody knows whether a correction or a rebuttal is safe.
- Test, written first, evaluative rubric:
  - Documentary match, 50%: evidence is the source documents.
  - Witness or data corroboration, 30%: evidence is interviews and data exports.
  - Strength of the journalist's evidence, 20%: evidence is the article and its materials.
  - Threshold: accurate if 80% or more of checkable elements match documents. False if under 50%. Misleading in between.

**Step 4, after Step 3. Pick the response track.** Complicated: options become knowable once the facts exist.

- Increment: one session scores three options: correction, rebuttal with evidence, or continued hold. Use Kepner-Tregoe analysis, a method that scores options against weighted criteria.
- Criteria and weights: legal exposure 35%. Match with verified facts 35%. Stakeholder impact 20%. Reversibility 10%. Threshold: the top option must lead the next by at least 10 points on a 100-point scale, or the hold continues [judgment].
- Blocker removed: a decision driven by the loudest inbox.
- Dependency: Step 4 MUST NOT start until Step 3's rubric is scored, because the facts pick the track.
- If Q1 shows a listing or a regulator, counsel runs a disclosure check in this session.

**Step 5, after Step 4. Probe with the journalist.** Complex: the media reaction is visible only after acting.

- Increment: one evidence-based contact. Request a correction, or share the verified record, per Step 4's outcome.
- Blocker removed: an uncorrected public record.
- Test, written first, stochastic: metric is story status. Baseline is today's article. Pass threshold is an update, a correction, or a scheduled follow-up within 24 hours. Sample size is this one outlet and exchange.
- Cap: one business day. Stop if counsel objects or the outlet escalates.
- Dependency: Step 5 MUST NOT start until Step 4 picks the track.

## Follow-up

**Open questions.**

Q1. Is the company listed or regulated? The answer decides whether the disclosure check in Step 4 is mandatory.

Q2. What does the article claim, and what evidence does it cite? The answer sets the Step 3 checklist.

Q3. Can the reader name the decision owner, or must the CEO's office do it? The answer decides who sends Step 1.

**Conditions that change the recommendation.** If A2 is false, the CEO's office sends the Step 1 freeze. If Step 3 finds the statement false, Step 4's output becomes a correction, not a rebuttal. If A3 is false, skip the disclosure check.
