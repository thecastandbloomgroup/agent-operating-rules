# Headline

Retrospectives stall when they produce talk but no owned, tracked actions with visible results. The causes are: too many action items, no owner or deadline per item, and no review of past items. Start each retro by checking last retro's actions; end with at most two items, each with a named owner. Treat format changes as small measured probes.

# Background

The retrospectives run, people attend, and nothing changes afterward. The problem is therefore not attendance but follow-through: the loop between discussion and behavior is broken.

# Relevance

Teams that repeat the same retro with no effect stop trusting the format, and attendance turns into compliance. Fixing follow-through restores the one improvement mechanism a team fully controls.

# Information

Retrospective literature names the standard failure mode as "action items without owners, deadlines, or review in the next session" [1]. When teams generate many items, completion rate falls; facilitation guides recommend one to three items per retro [1]. Repeating the same format also reduces engagement, which is why facilitators rotate formats [1]. However, process causes are not the only ones: if items fail because they need authority the team lacks, the blocker is escalation, not facilitation [judgment].

**Assumption log**

A1. The team already writes down action items. Why not measured: no tool can reach the team's retro notes from here. Validate by: read the last three retros' notes. If wrong: add a capture step before limiting item count.

A2. The team has authority over at least some items. Why not measured: the material does not state the team's mandate. Validate by: classify the last ten items as team-controlled or not. If wrong: Step 4 becomes the primary fix.

**Alternative considered**: The strongest alternative is to replace retros with one-on-ones or skip-level feedback. It loses because it fixes input quality, not the follow-through loop, and follow-through is the observed failure [judgment].

# End

I recommend that we take four actions:

1. **Cap and own the items.** This retro, produce at most two action items, each with one named owner and a date. Blocker removed: diffusion of responsibility. Test (deterministic):
   - Given a finished retro,
   - When the notes are reviewed,
   - Then they contain at most two items and every item names one owner and a date.
   Cynefin: Clear. A known best practice exists [1].
2. **Open each retro with a review of the last retro's items.** Mark each done or not done, and carry over at most one. Blocker removed: items vanish after the meeting. Test (deterministic):
   - Given the agenda of the next retro,
   - When the agenda is checked,
   - Then a review of prior items is the first item on it.
   Cynefin: Clear. Same basis as Step 1.
3. **Measure follow-through for four retros.** Track percent of owned items completed per retro. Baseline: measure retro one, which is likely low. Metric: completed items divided by total items. Pass threshold: at least 75% average across four retros, sample size four. Blocker removed: no feedback on whether the fix works. Cynefin: Complex, so this is a capped probe: stop condition is four retros, budget is ten minutes per retro.
4. **Classify undone items by who can act on them.** After retro two, split the list into team-controlled and escalation-needed. Route escalation items to a named manager. Blocker removed: items that no facilitation fix can complete. Test (deterministic):
   - Given the list of undone items,
   - When each is reviewed,
   - Then each carries the label team-controlled or escalation, and escalation items name a recipient.
   Cynefin: Complicated. Cause and effect need the analysis in this step before a fix can be chosen [2].

Step 2 and Step 3 start at the next retro. Step 4 MUST NOT start until two retros under Step 3 have run, because it needs real completion data. If A2 is false, move Step 4 to position 1 and run it this week.
