## Headline

Retrospectives stall when actions lack owners, deadlines, and follow-up; fix the action loop first, not the meeting format.

1. The most common cause is that agreed actions are never tracked, so the team learns nothing changes [judgment].
2. Limit each retro to one or two actions; large lists dilute effort [judgment].
3. If attendance is performative, low psychological safety is the likely root cause, which needs a different fix [judgment].

## Background

Your team holds retrospectives, people attend, but no change results. You ask what to do next.

## Relevance

A retro that produces no change costs the team one meeting per cycle and erodes trust in the process. Fixing it restores the main feedback loop a team has.

## Information

Evidence for the diagnosis:

- Themes from agile practice literature: action items without owners or review fail to complete; this theme appears across practitioner sources, and none consulted disagree [judgment].
- Psychological safety predicts whether people raise real problems; teams with low safety produce bland actions [1].
- Complex team dynamics mean cause and effect is visible only afterward, so small probes with measures fit this work [2].

Assumption log:

- A1. Actions are agreed in the retro but not reviewed at the next one. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: check the last three retro notes for action review. If wrong: focus shifts to facilitation quality.
- A2. The team picks more than two actions per retro. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: count actions in the last retro record. If wrong: skip Step 2.
- A3. Safety is moderate, not absent. Why not measured: no tool can reach it here. Validate by: one anonymous three-question pulse survey. If wrong: run Step 4 before Step 1.

Alternative considered: replace the retro with a different format, such as lean coffee or "4Ls". It loses because format changes do not fix follow-through; the action loop is the bottleneck [judgment].

## End

I recommend that we take these four steps:

1. **Add an action review to the next retro.** Increment: in one session, review the status of every open action from the last retro before new discussion. Blocker removed: invisible follow-through. Domain: Clear, because a known best practice applies. Deterministic test:
   - Given the retro agenda for the next session,
   - When the facilitator opens the meeting,
   - Then the first item lists each prior action with status done, dropped, or carried.
2. **Cap actions at two, each with a named owner and date.** Increment: enforce the cap in the next retro. Blocker removed: diluted effort. Domain: Clear. Deterministic test:
   - Given the retro closes,
   - When the record is written,
   - Then it contains at most two actions, each with one owner and one date.
3. **Measure follow-through for three cycles.** Increment: track percent of actions completed per retro. Baseline: unknown [judgment, likely below 50%]. Pass threshold: at least 80% of actions done by the next retro. Domain: Complicated, because analysis of the data picks the next fix. Deterministic test:
   - Given three retro records,
   - When the metric is computed,
   - Then each record shows completed actions divided by total actions.
4. **Probe safety with an anonymous pulse.** Increment: send three questions, one week, budget of one hour per person. Stop condition: fewer than 60% respond, in which case treat safety itself as the blocker. Domain: Complex, because reactions are visible only afterward, so this is a capped probe. Stochastic test: metric is mean safety score on a 5-point scale; baseline is none; pass threshold is a score at or below 3.5 triggering a safety intervention; sample size is all team members.

Step 3 MUST NOT conclude until three cycles pass. Steps 1, 2, and 4 can run in parallel.
