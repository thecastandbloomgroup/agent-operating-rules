## Headline

Name one decision owner now, freeze outside statements, verify the facts, and decide the response together.

- Nobody owns the decision, so the three groups pull in different directions.
- The facts of the CEO's statement are unverified, so any denial or apology is premature.
- A short holding statement protects the company while the facts are checked.
- A single decision meeting replaces three parallel message threads.

## Background

A journalist published that the CEO's statement from yesterday was false. Legal, PR (public relations), and the board each send you instructions. The instructions conflict. You asked for next steps.

## Relevance

Each hour without an owner raises the chance of a second inaccurate public statement [judgment]. The next few hours decide whether the story is "false statement" or "false statement, then a cover-up" [judgment].

## Information

First, the evidence. Three groups give conflicting advice, so no one holds decision rights [judgment]. A denial that later proves wrong adds a second error to the first [judgment]. The Cynefin framework says to stabilise first when cause and effect are not visible, then analyse [1].

Next, the assumption log.

A1. The journalist's claim is not yet verified. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: compare each claim to source records. If wrong (the CEO statement is true): replace Step 4 options with a request for correction.

A2. The company is listed, so disclosure rules apply. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask Legal for a one-line answer. If wrong: drop the disclosure check in Step 4.

A3. You can ask the board chair to name an owner. Why not measured: the user has not answered. Validate by: send the chair one message. If wrong: ask the CEO to name the owner.

Alternative considered: the CEO issues a full denial or defense today. It loses because a false statement followed by a second false one costs more than a few hours of delay [judgment].

## End

I recommend that we take these five actions in this order.

1. **Name one decision owner and freeze outside statements for 24 hours.** Increment: one message to the board chair, Legal, and PR, sent within 30 minutes. Blocker removed: three groups issue conflicting instructions. Domain: Chaotic, because no one owns the decision and the instructions conflict, so stabilise first. Kind: deterministic. Test:
   ```gherkin
   Given the board chair, Legal, and PR have each received the message
   When any of them asks who decides
   Then all three name the same person
   ```

2. **Build a fact table, after Legal issues a document hold.** Increment: a one-page table that lists each claim in the article and the CEO statement. Mark each claim true, false, or unverified, with a record reference. Blocker removed: nobody knows what is true. Domain: Complicated, because records and expertise settle the facts. Kind: deterministic. Test:
   ```gherkin
   Given the article, the CEO statement, and the source records
   When Legal and the CEO's office compare each claim to the records
   Then every claim has a status and a record reference
   ```
   Step 4 MUST NOT start until Legal signs off the table.

3. **Issue a holding statement.** Increment: three sentences that say the company is reviewing the claims and will report by a stated time. Blocker removed: silence invites speculation. Domain: Complicated, because PR and Legal practice defines safe wording. Kind: deterministic. Test:
   ```gherkin
   Given the draft holding statement
   When Legal and PR review it
   Then it contains no claim of fact beyond the review itself
   ```
   Step 3 MUST NOT go out until the owner from Step 1 approves it.

4. **Hold one decision meeting on the response.** Increment: a 45-minute meeting with the owner, Legal, PR, and the board chair. Options: correct, retract, or defend. Blocker removed: no shared basis for choosing. Domain: Complicated, because the fact table makes the options analysable. Kind: evaluative. Test: score each option with a Kepner-Tregoe layout [2].
   - Criteria and weights: factual accuracy 40, legal exposure 25, trust of public and staff 20, reversibility 15.
   - Pass threshold: a total of 70 out of 100, and an accuracy score of at least 8 out of 10.
   - Evidence: the fact table, a Legal memo, and PR notes on press reaction.

   Step 4 MUST NOT start until Step 2 is signed off.

5. **Run a probe: brief 10 key customers and partners by phone.** Increment: 10 calls in two hours, each using only the holding statement. Blocker removed: unknown stakeholder reaction. Domain: Complex, because reaction is visible only afterward. Leverage is a guess. Kind: stochastic. Test:
   - Metric: the share of contacts with no escalation within 24 hours.
   - Baseline: none exists. The threshold of 8 out of 10 is [judgment].
   - Sample size: 10.
   - Stop condition: stop after 2 escalations.
   - Budget: two hours.

   Run Step 5 in parallel with Steps 2 to 4.

If A1 is false, replace Step 4 options with a request that the journalist correct the article. Gherkin form follows [3].

## Follow-up

Conditions that change the recommendation:

- If A2 is false, skip the disclosure check in Step 4.
- If the fact table shows the CEO statement was false, the CEO should not front the response alone.
