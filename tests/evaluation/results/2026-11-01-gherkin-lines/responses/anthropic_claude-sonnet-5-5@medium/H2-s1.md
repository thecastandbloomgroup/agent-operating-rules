## Headline

Stop all outbound messages, name one decision owner, verify the facts under legal privilege, then pick a response within 24 hours.

- **Who decides:** one named owner ends the conflict between Legal, PR (public relations), and the board.
- **What is true:** a claim-by-claim check shows whether the journalist or the CEO is right.
- **What to say:** one approved message, chosen by a scored comparison, replaces three competing drafts.
- **Whether it works:** a small read test checks the message before release.

## Background

A journalist published a claim that the CEO's statement from yesterday was false. Legal, PR, and the board each send you advice, and the advice conflicts. No one owns the decision.

## Relevance

Each hour without one owner raises the chance of a contradictory public message. A contradiction creates a second story on top of the first. [judgment]

## Information

**Evidence**

- The article is the only source for the claim that the statement was false. The company has not checked it yet. [no source]
- Confidence that the statement is false: low, one source (the article).
- It is likely (66 to 100%) that conflicting messages from three groups will reach the press if no one is in charge. [judgment]
- The 24-hour deadline matches the usual news cycle. [judgment]
- Cause and effect are unclear now, so Step 1 stabilises first (Cynefin "Chaotic" domain).

**Assumption log**

A1. The statement is at least partly inaccurate. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: Legal compares each claim with source records. If wrong: replace Step 3 options with "defend with evidence".

A2. You hold enough authority to convene the CEO, Legal, PR, and one board member. Why not measured: the user has not answered. Validate by: send the invitation and see who accepts. If wrong: ask the board chair to convene the call.

**Alternative considered**

Defend the statement and wait. It loses because a denial of a true claim adds a second false statement. [judgment]

## End

I recommend that we take these five steps, in this order:

**Step 1. Stabilise the decision process (Chaotic: the groups conflict and no cause and effect is visible).**
- Increment: hold one 30-minute call with the CEO, Legal, PR, and one board member. Produce a one-page decision record.
- Blocker removed: no one owns the decision.
- Kind: deterministic. Test, written first:
  - Given the call has ended
  - When the decision record is sent
  - Then it names one decision owner, one freeze on outbound statements, and one hold on related documents
  - And each participant has confirmed receipt in writing

**Step 2. Verify the facts (Complicated: experts can establish the facts with analysis).**
- Increment: Legal builds a table of every claim in the CEO statement. Each claim gets a rating: supported, partly supported, or false.
- Blocker removed: nobody knows what is true.
- Kind: evaluative. Rubric, written first:
  - Evidence completeness, weight 40%.
  - Independence of the source documents, weight 30%.
  - Reviewer sign-off by Legal, weight 30%.
  - Pass threshold: 80 of 100, and every claim rated.
  - Evidence needed: one source document per claim.
- Step 2 MUST NOT start until the Step 1 test passes.

**Step 3. Choose the response path (Complicated: analysis picks among good practices).**
- Increment: score four options in a Pugh matrix [3]. The options are correct, clarify, defend, and no comment. "No comment" is the baseline.
- Blocker removed: three groups push three different messages.
- Kind: evaluative. Rubric, written first:
  - Accuracy against Step 2, weight 5.
  - Legal and disclosure exposure, weight 4.
  - Trust of staff, customers, and the board, weight 4.
  - Speed, weight 2.
  - Pass threshold: the winner beats the baseline and scores no worse on accuracy.
  - Evidence needed: the Step 2 table and Legal's written view.
- Step 3 MUST NOT start until the Step 2 rubric score reaches 80.

**Step 4. Update the board in writing (Clear: standard governance practice applies).**
- Increment: send one written note with the facts, the chosen path, the decision owner, and the time of the next update.
- Blocker removed: board members send separate messages to you.
- Kind: deterministic. Test, written first:
  - Given the Step 3 decision is made
  - When the note is sent to all board members
  - Then it holds the four items above
  - And no board member needs to ask a follow-up to learn the decision

**Step 5. Test the message on a small group (Complex: public reaction is visible only afterward).**
- Increment: draft the approved message. Ask 10 staff outside the crisis team to read it.
- Blocker removed: no check that readers understand the message.
- Kind: stochastic. Acceptance test, written first:
  - Metric: readers who restate the key fact correctly.
  - Baseline: 0 of 10, because no message exists yet.
  - Pass threshold: 8 of 10.
  - Sample size: 10 readers.
  - Budget: 1 hour and 1 person.
  - Stop condition: fewer than 8 of 10 after two rewrites, then return to Step 3.
- Run this probe in parallel with Step 4. Its leverage is a guess.

If A1 is false, replace the Step 3 options with "defend with evidence" and keep the rest.
