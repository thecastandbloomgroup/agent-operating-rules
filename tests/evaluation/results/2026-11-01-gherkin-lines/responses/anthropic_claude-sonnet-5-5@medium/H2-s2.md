## Headline

Stop all outside statements now, verify the facts, then choose one response that Legal, PR, and the board score the same way.

- Control: one person must own all outside messages until the facts are known.
- Facts: a claim-by-claim check shows what is true, false, or unverified.
- Decision: a shared scoring sheet replaces three competing opinions.
- Message test: a small read-through checks the draft before it goes public.

## Background

A journalist published a report that the CEO's statement from yesterday was false. Legal, PR, and the board each send you different advice. No one has authority over the whole response.

## Relevance

Each hour without one message gives the story more time to spread. Conflicting replies from your side may also become part of the story. [judgment]

## Information

**Evidence**

- Whether the article is correct is unknown. Confidence is low, because no document has been reviewed yet.
- Disagreement among Legal, PR, and the board is the main blocker. Each group guards a different risk: liability, reputation, and oversight. [judgment]
- The action labels use the Cynefin framework [1]. It sorts work by how clearly cause and effect link. That is why Step 1 is Chaotic and Step 4 is a capped probe.

**Assumption log**

A1. The article is not yet verified as right or wrong. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: read the CEO statement next to the source records. If wrong: if the article is clearly right, choose a correction in Step 3.

A2. You can ask the CEO or board chair to name an incident lead. Why not measured: the user has not answered. Validate by: send one message to the board chair. If wrong: ask the CEO's chief of staff to name the lead.

A3. The company may have legal duties to disclose corrections (for example, as a listed company). Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask Legal one yes/no question. If wrong: add a disclosure-timing check to Step 3.

**Alternative considered**

The strongest alternative is to defend the CEO at once. It loses because, if the article is right, a defense adds a second false statement. [judgment]

## End

I recommend that we take these four steps, in this order.

1. **Freeze outside messages (Chaotic: no one leads and facts are unknown).**
   - Increment: within 60 minutes, send one message that names an incident lead. It also stops all posts, comments, and replies to the journalist. It also tells Legal to preserve documents.
   - Blocker removed: conflicting messages.
   - Kind: deterministic. Test written first:

   ```gherkin
   Given an incident lead is named by the board chair or CEO
   When the freeze message is sent to Legal, PR, the board, and the CEO office
   Then each recipient confirms receipt in writing
   And no outside statement is published until the lead approves it
   ```

2. **Check every claim (Complicated: experts can find the answer from records).**
   - Increment: within 2 hours, build a table with the CEO's statement in one column and the article's claim in the next. Add the evidence and a verdict for each line.
   - Blocker removed: no shared facts.
   - Kind: deterministic. Test written first:

   ```gherkin
   Given the CEO statement is split into single claims
   When Legal and the statement's source team review each claim against records
   Then each claim is marked true, false, or unverified
   And each verdict links to one document
   ```

3. **Choose the response (Complicated: a scoring method picks among known options).**
   - Step 3 MUST NOT start until the test in Step 2 passes.
   - Increment: score four options with a Kepner-Tregoe sheet [judgment on weights]. The options are correct, clarify, defend, and hold with "no comment yet".
   - Blocker removed: three-way disagreement.
   - Kind: evaluative. Rubric written first:

   | Criterion | Weight | Evidence needed |
   |---|---|---|
   | Factual accuracy | 40 | Step 2 table |
   | Legal exposure | 25 | Legal's written view |
   | Trust of public and staff | 20 | PR's view of coverage so far |
   | Reversibility | 15 | Can we change course in 24 hours? |

   - Pass threshold: a weighted score of 70 out of 100 or more. Factual accuracy MUST also score at least 30 of its 40 points. Legal, PR, and one board member each score alone, then compare.

4. **Test the draft on a small group (Complex: public reaction shows only after release).**
   - Step 4 MUST NOT start until Step 3 produces a draft.
   - Increment: send the draft to 5 trusted readers, such as staff, a customer, and a former journalist. Run it in parallel with Step 3 edits.
   - Blocker removed: unknown public reaction. Leverage is a guess.
   - Kind: stochastic. Acceptance test written first:
     - Metric: share of readers who restate the facts correctly.
     - Baseline: the article's version.
     - Pass threshold: 4 of 5 readers.
     - Sample size: 5.
     - Budget: 60 minutes.
     - Stop condition: 2 readers report that the draft sounds evasive. Then return to Step 3.

If A1 is false and the article is clearly right, replace "defend" and "clarify" in Step 3 with a correction only. If A3 is false, add a disclosure-timing check to Step 3.
