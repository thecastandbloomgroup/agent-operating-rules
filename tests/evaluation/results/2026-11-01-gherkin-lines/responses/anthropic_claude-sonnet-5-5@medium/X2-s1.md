## Headline

I recommend that we first measure why actions die, then fix follow-through with a small, visible commitment loop.

- **Diagnose:** No data shows whether actions are missing, weak, or ignored, so the audit comes first.
- **Commit:** Fewer actions with one owner and one date raise the chance that they finish.
- **Track:** Reviewing last retro's actions at the start of the next one makes follow-through visible.
- **Probe:** A changed format tests whether the meeting itself is stale, at a capped cost.

## Background

Your team holds retrospectives (regular meetings to review how the team works). People attend, but no change follows. The request gives no data on how many actions the team raises or finishes.

## Relevance

A retrospective that changes nothing costs team time and trust. Each empty meeting lowers the chance that people raise real problems in the next one [judgment].

## Information

**Evidence**

- Retrospective practice ends with a short list of chosen actions, not a long list of complaints [1]. 
- Teams often raise more actions than they can finish in one iteration (one work cycle) [judgment].
- Cause and effect here are not yet known. Cynefin (a framework that sorts problems by how clear cause and effect are) calls this Confused until data arrives [2].

Confidence in the causes above is low. The basis is zero team data in the request.

**Assumption log**

A1. The team already writes down some actions. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: read the notes of the last three retros. If wrong: skip Step 2 and write actions first.

A2. Most actions are inside the team's control. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: tag each past action as "ours" or "needs others". If wrong: add an escalation step for outside blockers.

A3. A team lead can protect time for one action per iteration. Why not measured: the user has not answered. Validate by: ask the lead in the next planning. If wrong: Step 2 fails and the cause is capacity, not the retro.

**Alternative considered**

Replace the retrospective with a different ritual, such as a monthly review. It loses because the audit may show that the format is fine and follow-through is the gap [judgment].

## End

I recommend that we take these four steps:

1. **Audit the last three to five retros.** Increment: one working session to list every action, its owner, and its status. Blocker removed: no one knows why actions fail. Domain: Confused, because the cause is unknown and this step gathers what we need to classify.
   Kind: deterministic. Test, written first:
   ```gherkin
   Given the notes of the last five retrospectives
   When the team lists each action with owner and status
   Then the share of finished actions is known
   And each unfinished action has a tagged cause
   ```

2. **Limit each retro to one action with a named owner and a due date, placed in the normal work backlog.** Step 2 MUST NOT start until the Step 1 test passes. Increment: apply this at the next retro. Blocker removed: too many vague actions with no owner. Domain: Complicated, because the audit result picks the right limit.
   Kind: deterministic. Test, written first:
   ```gherkin
   Given a finished retrospective
   When the team reviews the backlog
   Then it holds exactly one retro action
   And that action has an owner and a due date
   ```

3. **Open every retro by reviewing the last action.** Increment: add one agenda item to the next retro. Blocker removed: no one sees whether actions finish. Domain: Clear, because a standard practice applies [1].
   Kind: deterministic. Test, written first:
   ```gherkin
   Given the agenda of a retrospective
   When the meeting starts
   Then the first item is the status of the previous action
   ```

4. **Run a capped probe: change the retro format for two iterations.** Run it in parallel with Steps 2 and 3. Increment: swap one activity, for example a new question set. Blocker removed: the meeting may feel stale. Domain: Complex, because the effect shows only after the meeting. Leverage is a guess.
   Kind: stochastic. Test, written first:
   - Metric: share of retro actions finished by the next retro.
   - Baseline: the rate from Step 1.
   - Pass threshold: baseline plus 25 percentage points.
   - Sample size: two retros. This is small, so treat a pass as a signal and not as proof.
   - Budget: two retros. Stop condition: revert to the old format if the rate falls below baseline.

If A3 is false, replace Step 2 with a request to the lead for protected time, and do not judge the retro format until that time exists.
