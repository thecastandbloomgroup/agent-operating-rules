## Headline

**Make each retrospective end in one owned, scheduled change, and check that change at the next retrospective.**

- The likely cause is weak follow-through, not weak discussion (A2).
- Step 1 tests that cause before the team changes anything else.
- Small, owned actions placed in the sprint plan fix follow-through.
- A format change is a capped probe, not a commitment.

## Background

The team holds retrospectives (regular meetings to review how the team works). People attend, but no process change follows. The material gives no retro records, team size, or cadence.

## Relevance

An empty retrospective costs team time each sprint. It also teaches people that speaking up has no effect, so participation will fall further.

## Information

**Evidence**

Retrospectives need a follow-up step to create change. The source below states this.

Derby and Larsen describe closing the retrospective with committed actions [1].

The Scrum Guide says the most impactful improvements should be addressed soon. It says they may enter the next sprint backlog.

Scrum Guide 2020, section "Sprint Retrospective" [2].

The team has no data yet. Therefore the claim that follow-through is the cause is likely (66 to 100%), based on [judgment] of the common pattern. Confidence is low until Step 1 runs.

**Assumption log**

A1. The team runs sprints and holds one retrospective per sprint. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask the facilitator. If wrong: adapt the review timing in Step 3.

A2. Retrospectives produce ideas, but the actions are not finished. Why not measured: no retro records were provided. Validate by: count the action items from the last three retrospectives and their status. If wrong: if no action items exist, replace Step 2 with a facilitation fix.

A3. The team has authority to change its own process. Why not measured: the user has not answered. Validate by: mark which of the last three action items needed outside approval. If wrong: add a step to take blocked items to the manager.

**Alternative considered**

The strongest alternative is to replace the retrospective format at once. It loses because a new format does not fix missing owners or dates [judgment]. Step 4 tests the format as a probe.

## End

I recommend that we take these steps in order:

1. **Audit the last three retrospectives.** Increment: list every action item, its owner, its due date, and its status, in one session. Blocker removed: we do not know why nothing shifts. Domain: Confused, because we cannot tell the cause yet [3]. Kind: evaluative.
   Rubric (pass: score 70 or more of 100):
   - Items have an owner (weight 30). Evidence: owner field filled.
   - Items have a due date (weight 30). Evidence: date field filled.
   - Items are done (weight 40). Evidence: completed status.

   The result sets the baseline completion rate for Step 4.

2. **Limit each retrospective to one to three actions, each with an owner and a due date, and add them to the sprint backlog.** Increment: change the retro template and apply it at the next retrospective. Blocker removed: actions with no owner or date. Domain: Complicated, because several good practices exist and Step 1 picks among them. Step 2 MUST NOT start until Step 1 is done. Kind: deterministic.
   ```gherkin
   Given a retrospective has ended
   When the facilitator reads the action list
   Then each action has one owner, one due date, and a sprint backlog entry
   ```

3. **Open each retrospective by reviewing the previous actions.** Increment: add a five-minute agenda item. Blocker removed: no one checks past actions. Domain: Clear, because the practice is well known. Kind: deterministic.
   ```gherkin
   Given the retrospective has started
   When the facilitator opens the agenda
   Then every previous action is marked done, dropped, or carried over with a reason
   ```

4. **Run a probe: change the format for three retrospectives.** For example, rotate the facilitator and vote on one topic only. Blocker removed: unknown effect of format on engagement. Domain: Complex, because the effect shows only afterward. Leverage is a guess. Kind: stochastic. Run Step 4 in parallel with Steps 2 and 3.
   - Metric: share of actions done by their due date.
   - Baseline: the Step 1 result.
   - Pass threshold: 20 percentage points above baseline.
   - Sample size: three retrospectives (small, so treat a pass as weak evidence).
   - Budget: 30 extra minutes of preparation in total.
   - Stop condition: end the probe after two retrospectives if the rate falls below baseline.

If A2 is false, replace Step 2 with a facilitation fix, because the real problem is then idea generation.
