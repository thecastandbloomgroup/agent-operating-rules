Headline
Close one owned change before the team adds any new retrospective topic.

- Unfinished actions teach the team that the meeting has no effect.
- One small change per cycle is easier to finish than a long list.
- The next meeting must start with the status of that change.
- If the team cannot act, the blocker sits outside the meeting.

Background
The team still attends retrospectives. Nothing in the work changes after the meeting. The request is what to do next.

Relevance
Each cycle without a finished change makes later attendance less useful. Therefore the next cycle must prove that one change can land.

Information
The Scrum Guide says the team picks the most helpful changes and addresses them as soon as possible.
[1]

Derby and Larsen separate data, insights, and a decision on what to do.
[2]

Kerth asks the team to assume people did their best with what they knew at the time.
[3]

A common failure is a list of actions with no owner and no later review [judgment]. Therefore the first move is to measure follow-through, not to invent a new meeting format.

A1. Actions exist but are not closed. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: count done actions in the last three notes. If wrong: treat silence or fear as the main cause.

A2. The team can change its own way of work. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: mark each open action as in team control or not. If wrong: take one blocker to the person who can remove it.

Alternative: redesign the retrospective format first. It loses because a new format does not finish old actions [judgment].

End
I recommend that we take these steps.

1. Count follow-through from the last three retrospectives. This removes the confusion about why nothing shifts. Domain: Confused, because the cause is not yet known, so this action gathers facts to classify it.

Test first (deterministic):
Given the notes from the last three retrospectives
When each action is marked done, open, or dropped
Then the count of done actions is written down

Increment: one session to score those notes.

2. Choose one action the team controls. This removes the blocker of a list that nobody finishes. Domain: Complicated, because more than one good action exists and a short analysis must pick it.

Test first (evaluative):
Criterion 1: team control. Weight 40. Evidence: yes or no from the team.
Criterion 2: effect in the next cycle. Weight 40. Evidence: one stated outcome.
Criterion 3: effort under four hours. Weight 20. Evidence: an hour estimate.
Pass threshold: 70 of 100.
Score the top three open actions.
Keep the one score that passes.

3. Open the next retrospective with that action's status. This adds a closed loop. Domain: Clear, because the rule is known: review the last commitment before new topics.
[1]

Test first (deterministic):
Given the chosen action and its owner
When the retrospective starts
Then the owner states done, blocked, or dropped before any new topic

Step 3 MUST NOT start until Step 2 has one passing action.

4. If two cycles still show zero done actions, run a ten-minute anonymous check on whether people can speak safely. This is a probe, not a full redesign. Domain: Complex, because cause and effect show up only after the check. Budget: one meeting. Stop if fewer than half the team answers. Leverage is a guess.

Test first (stochastic):
Metric: share of attendees who submit the check.
Baseline: zero checks today.
Pass threshold: at least half the attendees submit.
Sample size: one retrospective.

Step 4 runs only if Steps 1 to 3 show zero completed actions after two cycles.

Follow-up
If A1 is false and people do not speak, replace Step 2 with a safety check before any new action.

If A2 is false, replace Step 2 with one request to the person who controls the blocker.
