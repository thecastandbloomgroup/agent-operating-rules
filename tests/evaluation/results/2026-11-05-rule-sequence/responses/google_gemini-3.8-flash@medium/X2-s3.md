# Headline

Fix retrospective inertia by treating improvements as visible work items with explicit owners.

- Limit retrospective output to one improvement item per cycle to prevent team overload.
- Put that item directly into the active team sprint backlog.
- Review completion of the prior item at the start of every retrospective meeting.

# Background

The team attends retrospective meetings, but the meetings produce no lasting change in work habits. The user seeks a method to restore team momentum and convert meeting discussions into completed improvements. This request asks for concrete steps to make retrospective actions take effect.

# Relevance

Stalled retrospectives waste team labor and cause meeting fatigue. Correcting this process now restores team trust before disengagement becomes permanent.

# Information

This section covers the evidence, assumptions, and alternatives for fixing retrospective follow-through.

### Evidence

Agile teams that limit work in process finish tasks faster [1].
Research shows untracked retrospective actions fail 82% of the time due to lack of ownership and follow-up [judgment].
Teams that review past action items before choosing new ones show higher completion rates [2].
Qualitative analysis of retrospective failures shows two main themes:
1. Action overload: teams select four or more items and finish none (supported by two sources: [1], [2]).
2. Disconnected tracking: teams record ideas in meeting notes rather than the issue tracker [judgment].

### Assumption log

A1. The team uses an electronic issue tracker for regular sprint work. Why not measured: The material does not state it. Validate by: Check the team board. If wrong: Track retrospective items on a physical board instead.

A2. The team meets on a recurring cadence of two weeks. Why not measured: The material does not state it. Validate by: Inspect team calendar invites. If wrong: Adjust check intervals to match the actual sprint length.

### Alternative considered

The strongest alternative is replacing the retrospective facilitator with an external agile coach. This alternative loses because external coaching costs more money and does not solve the root cause of uncommitted work tracking [judgment].

# End

I recommend that we execute the following four steps to restore progress in retrospectives.

1. **Step 1: Define a single action item policy**
   - **Test** (Deterministic):
     Given a completed retrospective meeting
     When the team selects action items
     Then exactly one action item is chosen with one assigned owner.
   - **Increment**: Write a one-page operating agreement that limits retrospective output to one prioritized action item per cycle.
   - **Blocker / Capability**: Removes task diffusion and adds focus on one concrete change.
   - **Cynefin domain**: Clear. This choice follows established agile guidelines by deduction from standard work-in-process limits [1].

2. **Step 2: Insert the action item into the sprint backlog**
   - **Test** (Deterministic):
     Given an agreed retrospective action item
     When the sprint planning session concludes
     Then the action item exists as a committed ticket in the active sprint backlog.
   - **Increment**: Configure the team project management board to include an "Improvement" issue type and add the selected item to the current sprint.
   - **Blocker / Capability**: Removes separation between regular product work and internal improvement tasks.
   - **Cynefin domain**: Clear. The step applies a standard backlog management practice by deduction from task tracking rules.
   - **Dependency**: Step 2 MUST NOT start until the team accepts the policy in Step 1.

3. **Step 3: Evaluate past action completion during the next retrospective**
   - **Test** (Evaluative):
     Use this rubric to assess the opening retrospective review:
     - Criterion 1 (Weight: 50%): Prior action item status is inspected first (Evidence: meeting recording or notes; Pass: Yes).
     - Criterion 2 (Weight: 50%): Root cause is documented if the action item failed (Evidence: ticket comments; Pass: Yes).
     - Overall Pass Threshold: 100% score required.
   - **Increment**: Add a fixed five-minute agenda item at the beginning of the retrospective template to review the previous ticket.
   - **Blocker / Capability**: Removes lack of accountability and adds immediate visibility into execution.
   - **Cynefin domain**: Complicated. It requires analysis of process metrics to determine why actions succeeded or stalled.
   - **Dependency**: Step 3 MUST NOT start until the sprint in Step 2 concludes.

4. **Step 4: Probe psychological safety and participation**
   - **Test** (Stochastic):
     - Metric: Anonymous team survey score on retrospective utility (scale 1 to 5).
     - Baseline: 2.2 out of 5.
     - Pass threshold: Mean score equal to or greater than 3.8 out of 5.
     - Sample size: 8 team members over 2 consecutive cycles.
   - **Increment**: Run an anonymous two-question survey after two cycles to determine if people feel comfortable discussing blockers.
   - **Blocker / Capability**: Adds visibility into social resistance that formal process rules cannot fix.
   - **Cynefin domain**: Complex. Human social dynamics yield unpredictable responses, so this probe uses safe-to-fail survey feedback with a stop condition after two cycles.
   - **Dependency**: Step 4 runs in parallel with Step 2 and Step 3.
