# Headline

Focus each retrospective on one experiment placed directly into the sprint backlog.

- Limiting improvement work to one item prevents divided effort.
- Booking the item as a backlog ticket gives it visible sprint capacity.
- Reviewing the result at the next meeting maintains team accountability.

# Background

The team holds regular retrospectives, but the discussions fail to generate real changes in daily work [judgment]. Team members attend the meetings without seeing tangible progress afterward [judgment]. Consequently, engagement drops because unaddressed friction repeats across sprints [judgment].

# Relevance

Unresolved retrospective items waste meeting hours and erode team trust in continuous improvement [judgment]. Therefore, fixing this execution loop restores team agency before disengagement becomes permanent [judgment].

# Information

Agile teams struggle when retrospectives generate long task lists without delivery support [1]. Limiting work in progress improves cycle completion rates in knowledge work [1]. Furthermore, teams report that lack of follow-up is the main cause of retrospective fatigue [2]. When teams treat improvement tasks as secondary work, delivery pressure displaces them [judgment]. It is very likely that unassigned tasks will be forgotten within three days of the meeting [judgment].
