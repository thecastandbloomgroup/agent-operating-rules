## Headline

Stabilize the response within one hour, verify the claim before any rebuttal, and then speak through one voice and one approval chain.

- First, one crisis team with a single spokesperson ends the unaligned replies, and a litigation hold protects the records.
- Second, a fact memo scores every claim against primary records and so decides the track, because the truth of the CEO statement is unknown today.
- Third, if the statement was false, a fast correction limits the damage; if it was true, a documented rebuttal does.
- Fourth, the team tracks coverage as a capped probe, because its payoff is a guess until outlets respond.

## Background

A journalist has published that the CEO's statement yesterday was false. Legal, PR (public relations), and the board are sending messages at once, and nobody is aligned. The request is for the next steps.

## Relevance

The story is live now, so each hour of silence lets the outlet's version set [1]. Each conflicting internal reply also raises the risk of a second, inconsistent public statement.

## Information

The evidence, the assumptions, and the main alternative follow.

**Evidence**

- Fast, accurate, single-voice response is the standard practice in a crisis, because silence lets others frame the story [1].
- Materiality, that is, a fact a reasonable investor would weigh, decides whether the CEO statement carries legal weight [2].
- Courts hold that a statement false when made, or one that later turns false, must be corrected [3].
- When a dispute is reasonably expected, electronically stored information must be preserved, and loss can bring sanctions [4].
- Whether the statement was in fact false is unknown today: about as likely as not, because no record has been checked yet [judgment].

**Assumption log**

A1. The company is publicly traded, so securities duties apply. Why not measured: the material does not state the listing status. Validate by: ask Legal during the Step 1 call. If wrong: Step 3 loses its regulatory weight, and the rest of the plan stands.

A2. The CEO statement made factual claims that records can verify. Why not measured: the statement text is not in the material. Validate by: pull the exact text in Step 2. If wrong: replace the correction option in Step 3 with a context-based rebuttal.

A3. The user can convene Legal, PR, and a board liaison. Why not measured: the user's role is not stated. Validate by: confirm with the CEO or board chair in Step 1. If wrong: Step 1 becomes a briefing for the person who holds that authority.

**Alternative considered**

The strongest alternative is an immediate public denial before the records are checked. It loses, because a denial that later proves false creates a second false statement and adds legal exposure [3].

Crisis doctrine also puts accuracy before speed of posture, so verification comes first [1].

## End

I recommend that we run five steps. Step 1 starts now. Steps 2 and 5 run in parallel with it.

**Step 1 (now, one hour). Form the crisis team and freeze all statements.**
Domain: Chaotic. Three parties acting alone means no stable cause and effect, so act first. Kind: deterministic.
Increment: one call that names the team (Legal, PR lead, board liaison) and one spokesperson, plus a written freeze on all other external replies. Blocker removed: unaligned public messaging. Legal also sends a litigation hold, that is, a written order that tells staff to preserve all records about the statement.
Test (write it before the call):
Given the roster and the spokesperson are published to Legal, PR, and the board,
When any inquiry about the article arrives,
Then only the spokesperson replies, and the team logs the inquiry.
Given Legal sends the hold notice,
When it reaches each record custodian,
Then each custodian confirms preservation within 24 hours.

**Step 2 (two to four hours). Build the fact memo.**
Domain: Confused at the start, because the case cannot be classified until the statement text and the records are on the table. It then turns Complicated, because scoring is expert analysis, that is, deduction. Kind: evaluative, so write the rubric first.
Rubric: a Pugh matrix, that is, a table that scores each claim against a baseline. The baseline is the primary record. Scores: + supported, − contradicted, S no record.
- Accuracy, 60 percent: compare each claim with the ledger, filing, or dataset.
- Materiality, 25 percent: apply the reasonable investor standard [2].
- Context, 15 percent: check whether an omission makes a true sentence misleading.
Pass threshold: 100 percent of claims scored, each with its record attached. Score against records, not memory.
Increment: one memo in one working session. Blocker removed: nobody knows yet whether the statement was false, and Steps 3 and 4 depend on it.

**Step 3 (one hour). Choose the track.**
Dependency: Step 3 MUST NOT start until Step 2 passes its threshold.
Domain: Complicated. The Step 2 analysis picks the practice, so the inference is deduction. Kind: evaluative, using Kepner-Tregoe decision analysis. That method screens options on mandatory points first, then scores weighted wants.
Mandatory: legally compliant, and defensible against the fact memo. Weighted wants: credibility 40 percent, containment of coverage 25 percent, speed 20 percent, board and CEO acceptance 15 percent. Options: full correction; documented rebuttal with a correction request to the outlet; partial clarification; no comment. Pass threshold: the choice meets both mandatory points and scores highest. Evidence: the Step 2 memo and counsel's note.
Increment: one decision record in one session. Blocker removed: disagreement over posture.

**Step 4 (two releases). Speak with one voice.**
Domain: Clear. A known best practice exists, so the inference is deduction from a known rule [1]. Kind: deterministic.
First release: a holding statement, that is, two sentences that confirm the review and promise an update by a set time, within two hours of Step 1. Second release: the substantive statement within 24 hours. The second release MUST NOT ship before Step 3 passes.
Test (write it before any draft):
Given the Step 3 decision record,
When the substantive statement is drafted,
Then Legal and the board liaison approve it in writing.
Then the spokesperson publishes it to the agreed channels by the deadline.
Increment: each release fits one working session. Blocker removed: delay and uncoordinated release.

**Step 5 (parallel). Track coverage as a capped probe.**
Domain: Complex. The effect appears only afterward, so run a safe-to-fail probe: small, measured, with a stop condition and a budget. Leverage here is a guess. The inference is induction from trials, and the pattern breaks if a new allegation or a regulator inquiry appears. Kind: stochastic, so write the acceptance test first.
Metric: the count of major outlets that ran the claim and have not noted the company response. Baseline: that count at the end of Step 4. Pass: a 50 percent drop within 72 hours. Sample: every major outlet mention in the window. Run the tally twice a day. Accept only if the threshold is met on the full sample.
Budget: two staff, reviewed after 72 hours. Stop condition: a new allegation, a regulator inquiry, or coverage above baseline at 48 hours sends the plan back to Step 2.
Increment: one tracking sheet and a twice-daily note. Blocker removed: the team lacks feedback on whether the response works.

If A1 is wrong and the company is private, the legal weight falls and the rest stands. If a regulator is already involved, route the Step 4 releases through securities counsel first.
