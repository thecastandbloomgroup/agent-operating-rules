## Headline

Stop the external reaction and first establish whether the journalist's claim is true; everything else depends on that fact.

- Step 1 is a single internal evidence check, likely completable within hours [judgment].
- Legal, PR, and the board must work from one fact base, not three narratives.
- No public response goes out before counsel reviews it.

## Background

A claim that the CEO's statement was false is live, and three stakeholders are messaging separately. The risk now is a split response that creates legal and reputational damage.

## Relevance

The first 24 hours set the narrative. An unaligned response makes a bad claim worse; a correct fact base makes the response defensible either way.

## Information

Supporting points:

A1. The truth of the journalist's claim is unknown from the material. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: check the CEO's statement against internal records with legal present today. If wrong (that is, if the statement was false), the plan shifts from rebuttal to correction, stated in the End section.

Alternative considered: issue a fast denial to control the story. It loses because a denial that later proves wrong converts a one-day story into a credibility crisis, at likely higher cost than a delay of several hours [judgment].

Chaotic crisis logic applies: stabilise before analyzing [1]. Press silence during a short verification window is standard crisis practice with no demonstrated downside at this scale [judgment].

## End

I recommend that we:

1. Tell Legal, PR, and the board within the next hour to send nothing externally and route all contact through one spokesperson. Blocker removed: conflicting messages. Test (deterministic, Clear domain, deduction from crisis best practice):
   - Given a request for comment from any outlet,
   - When it arrives at any of the three parties,
   - Then it is logged and answered only by the designated spokesperson with "We are reviewing this and will respond today."
2. Convene a two-hour fact check today: legal, the CEO's office, and whoever holds the records behind the statement. Capability added: one verified fact base. Test (evaluative, Complicated domain, analysis picks the practice): a rubric with one criterion, "statement matches the records," weighted 100%, pass means every material claim in the statement is documented; any undocumented claim counts as a fail.
3. Step 3 MUST NOT start until Step 2 reports. If the statement holds, have legal approve and PR issue a point-by-point rebuttal with cited records the same day. Blocker removed: uncorrected news cycle. Test (deterministic, Clear domain):
   - Given the verified records,
   - When the rebuttal is drafted,
   - Then each sentence cites a record and counsel signs off before send.
4. If the statement fails Step 2, replace Step 3 with a prompt correction drafted by PR and approved by legal, plus a board decision on who speaks. Cynefin domain: Complex; treat the correction wording as a capped probe (one draft, one review cycle, one business day budget) because audience reaction is visible only after release. Test (stochastic): metric is follow-up coverage tone, baseline is today's article, pass threshold is no new factual challenge in the next five published pieces across the outlets that covered the story.
5. After the response ships, run a 30-minute debrief to fix the approval chain so the next incident starts aligned. Cynefin domain: Clear. Test (deterministic):
   - Given the incident log,
   - When the debrief ends,
   - Then a written escalation map names one decision owner for fact, legal, and message.

## Follow-up

If A1 resolves as "statement false," Step 4 replaces Step 3, as noted.

How fast can the records behind the statement be pulled? The answer decides whether Steps 1 to 3 complete today or the holding statement runs into tomorrow.
