| Stage | Check | Response if absent |

### 2. Launch one small, protected experiment

Step 2 MUST NOT start until Step 1 passes.

**Kind:** Stochastic, meaning the result depends on people and context.

**Acceptance test first:**

- **Metric:** One agreed action completed, with evidence that its target problem improves.
- **Baseline:** Record the current condition before the change, using one relevant measure.
- **Pass threshold:** Complete the action and meet a target agreed before the trial.
- **Sample size:** One trial, ending at the next retrospective.
- **Evidence:** An observed count, elapsed time, or direct check of the changed work.

For example, test whether a named reviewer reduces the number of work items waiting over one day.

**Work:** Use one session to agree the action, owner, target, and review date. Reserve the owner’s time in the normal work plan. Cap the experiment at 60 person-minutes of extra effort. Stop if it disrupts delivery or creates a safety concern.

**Blocker removed:** A commitment without time, scope, or a test. [judgment]

**Domain:** Complex. Use a small, reversible trial because cause and effect need observation. Any claimed benefit remains a guess until measured.

### 3. Close the loop before discussing new problems

Step 3 MUST wait until the trial’s review date.

**Kind:** Deterministic, using the agreed acceptance rule.

**Test first:**

Given the trial’s target and evidence,  
When the next retrospective starts,  
Then record “met,” “not met,” or “insufficient evidence,” and assign the next decision.

**Work:** Use the first 10 minutes to review the result. Keep the change if it passes. Otherwise, stop it or define one revised trial. If authority blocked execution, name the person who must resolve that blocker.

**Capability added:** An explicit decision instead of an action that disappears. [judgment]

**Domain:** Clear. Deduction from the agreed acceptance rule determines the recorded result.

## Follow-up

If people avoid naming problems because they expect punishment, address that condition before an open group experiment. [judgment]

One successful trial supports that change, not a claim that the whole retrospective process now works. [judgment]
