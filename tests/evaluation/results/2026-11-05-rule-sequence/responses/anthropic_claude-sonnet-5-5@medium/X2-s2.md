## Headline

Run a short audit of past action items, then change the retrospective so each session ends with one owned, dated, tracked action.

- The most likely cause is a follow-through gap: the team talks but does not finish actions.
- A cap of one action per retrospective, with an owner and a due date, fixes the follow-through gap directly.
- A format change tested as a small trial shows whether the sessions themselves need to change.

## Background

Your team holds retrospectives (regular meetings to review the last work period). People attend, but no change follows. You ask what to do.

The material gives no data on action items, team size, or meeting format. This response therefore starts with a cheap diagnosis and then applies a known fix.

## Relevance

A retrospective that produces no change costs team time and lowers trust in the process. Attendance without effect often leads to silence, then to skipped sessions.

## Information

**Evidence**

1. The Scrum Guide states that the retrospective exists to plan ways to increase quality and effectiveness [1]. A session with no change fails this stated purpose.

2. Derby and Larsen advise the team to pick a few items and assign them, because long lists do not get done [2]. This is a secondary source.

3. The cause is a follow-through gap with 50% likelihood. [judgment] The other causes are unsafe discussion, wrong topics, and no authority to act.

4. No team data exists yet. Step 1 below produces the baseline.

**Assumption log**

A1. The team runs a retrospective every one to two weeks. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: check the calendar. If wrong: the trial in Step 4 needs a longer window.

A2. The team lists action items but does not finish them. Why not measured: no tool can reach the team's board. Validate by: Step 1. If wrong: raise the weight of Step 4 and Step 3.

A3. The team can spend about 30 minutes per week on action items. Why not measured: the user has not answered. Validate by: ask the team lead. If wrong: cap the action at a 15-minute task.

**Alternative considered**

Replace the retrospective with a different ritual, such as a monthly review. It loses because the cause is unknown. A new ritual with the same follow-through gap fails the same way. [judgment]

## End

I recommend that we take these four actions in this order.

1. **Audit the last three retrospectives.**
   - Increment: list every action item and mark it done, open, or dropped. This takes one session.
   - Blocker removed: no one knows whether the problem is idea quality or follow-through (A2).
   - Kind: deterministic. Test:
     - Given the notes of the last three retrospectives
     - When the team counts the action items by status
     - Then a table shows items raised, done, and dropped, and the completion rate
   - Domain: Complicated. Analysis of past records, by deduction, shows the cause.

2. **Cap each retrospective at one action item.** Step 2 MUST NOT start until Step 1 is complete, because its result sets the baseline.
   - Increment: at the next retrospective, end with one action. Give it one owner and a due date within the sprint. Start the next session by reviewing it.
   - Blocker removed: long action lists with no owner.
   - Kind: deterministic. Test:
     - Given a retrospective has ended
     - When the team checks the board
     - Then exactly one action exists with an owner and a due date
   - Domain: Clear. A known best practice applies [2], by deduction from a known rule.

3. **Survey the team on why the sessions fail.** Run this in parallel with Step 2.
   - Increment: send three anonymous open questions. Read the answers in one session and group them into themes.
   - Blocker removed: unknown causes other than follow-through, such as low safety.
   - Kind: evaluative. Rubric, written first: criteria are theme support (weight 60%) and dissent noted (weight 40%). Pass threshold: each reported theme cites at least 2 responses, and every disagreeing response is listed. Evidence: the raw answers.
   - Domain: Complicated. Thematic analysis picks the practice [3], by analysis.

4. **Trial one format change for two retrospectives.** Step 4 MUST NOT start until the Step 3 themes exist, because they choose the change.
   - Increment: change one thing, for example a rotating facilitator or one focus topic per session.
   - Blocker removed: sessions that feel the same each time.
   - Kind: stochastic. Test, written first:
     - Metric: share of action items done by the next retrospective.
     - Baseline: the rate from Step 1.
     - Pass threshold: 20 percentage points above the baseline in both sessions.
     - Sample size: two sessions. This is small, so the result is low confidence.
   - Domain: Complex. Cause and effect show only afterward, so this is a probe by induction. A change in team staffing could break the pattern.
   - Budget: 30 extra minutes of preparation per session. Stop condition: revert if the rate falls below the baseline after one session. Leverage is a guess.

## Follow-up

**Open questions:** none.

**Conditions that change the recommendation**

- If A2 is false (the team raises few ideas), replace Step 2 with a session that changes how topics are chosen.
- If Step 3 shows low safety, run Step 4 as a facilitator change led by someone outside the team.
