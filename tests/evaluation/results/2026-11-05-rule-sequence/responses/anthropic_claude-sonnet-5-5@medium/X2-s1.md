## Headline

Fix follow-through first: measure what happened to past action items, then limit, assign, and review them.

- Retrospectives produce change only when their actions get done, so the loop from decision to result needs repair.
- The cause is unconfirmed, so a short audit of past actions comes before any redesign.
- Small rules (one action, one owner, one date) are cheap and fix the most common failure.
- A format change is uncertain, so run it as a capped trial.

## Background

The team holds retrospectives, and people attend. The team sees no change after them. The query gives no data on team size, cadence, or past action items.

## Relevance

Each retrospective that ends without change costs the team its meeting time. It also teaches people that speaking up has no result, which lowers future input [judgment].

## Information

**Evidence**

- A retrospective has five phases: set the stage, gather data, generate insights, decide what to do, and close [1]. Most failures to change appear in the "decide what to do" phase and after it [judgment].
- It is likely (66 to 100%) that the main cause is weak follow-through, not poor discussion [judgment]. The basis is that attendance is steady while outcomes are absent. No team data supports this yet.
- The Cynefin framework [2] fits here. The cause is not yet known, so the first step must gather facts.

**Assumption log**

A1. The team works in fixed cycles, such as two-week sprints. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask the team lead in one message. If wrong: replace "sprint backlog" with the team's own work list.

A2. Past retrospectives did produce action items. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: open the notes of the last three retrospectives. If wrong: the cause is in the "decide" phase, so Step 3 moves up.

A3. The team may change its own meeting rules without outside approval. Why not measured: the user has not answered. Validate by: ask the team lead. If wrong: take the proposal to the person who owns the process.

**Alternative considered**

The strongest alternative is to pause retrospectives for a few cycles or replace them with written feedback. It loses because it removes the one shared channel, and it does not fix the missing follow-through [judgment].

## End

I recommend that we take these four actions, in this order:

1. **Audit the last three retrospectives.** Cynefin domain: Confused, because the cause is not yet known. This step gathers the data to classify the problem. Kind: deterministic. Increment: one session to list every action item, its owner, and its result.
   - Blocker removed: we do not know the completion rate.
   - Test (Gherkin [3]):
     - Given the notes of the last three retrospectives
     - When we count the action items and the completed ones
     - Then we have a completion rate and a list of reasons for each miss

2. **Limit each retrospective to one action, with one owner and one date.** Cynefin domain: Clear, because this is an established practice (deduction from a known rule). Kind: deterministic. Step 2 MUST NOT start until Step 1 is complete.
   - Increment: one session to add the rule and a template field to the notes.
   - Blocker removed: too many vague actions with no owner.
   - Test:
     - Given a retrospective has ended
     - When we read the notes
     - Then they show one action, one named owner, and one due date

3. **Put the action into the sprint backlog and review it first at the next retrospective.** Cynefin domain: Clear, because the action is a known practice. Kind: deterministic. Step 3 MUST NOT start until the test in Step 2 passes.
   - Increment: one session to create the backlog item and add a first agenda line.
   - Blocker removed: actions sit outside the daily work.
   - Test:
     - Given the next retrospective starts
     - When the facilitator opens the agenda
     - Then the team reviews the prior action before any new topic

4. **Run a trial of one new retrospective format for two cycles.** Cynefin domain: Complex, because we learn the effect only after the trial (induction from trials). The pattern could break if the cause is not format. Leverage is a guess. Run this step in parallel with Steps 2 and 3.
   - Kind: stochastic.
   - Increment: one session to choose the format, such as focusing on one problem in depth.
   - Blocker removed: sessions that repeat the same talk.
   - Acceptance test, written first:
     - Metric: share of actions completed by the next retrospective.
     - Baseline: the rate from Step 1.
     - Pass threshold: 20 points above baseline.
     - Sample size: two retrospectives. This is too small for proof, so treat a pass as a signal only.
     - Stop condition: stop if a team survey shows lower engagement after the first trial.
     - Budget: 30 extra minutes per retrospective.

## Follow-up

**Conditions that change the recommendation**

- If A2 is false, replace Step 1 with a review of why no actions were set, and move Step 2 first.
- If Step 1 shows most actions were done but had no effect, replace Step 4 with a review of how actions are chosen.
