## Headline

Retrospectives fail when they find problems but do not turn them into owned, scheduled work, so fix the follow-through first.

- Action items likely lack an owner, a date, or a place in the sprint backlog [judgment].
- The team has not measured how many past actions were completed, so the real cause is unknown.
- A small change to the format can be tested within four retrospectives.

## Background

The team holds retrospectives, and people attend. Nothing changes afterward. The request is for a course of action. The material gives no data on past action items, team size, or retrospective format.

## Relevance

Each retrospective without change costs the team an hour per person. It also teaches people that reflection has no effect. Agile practice expects the team to "tune and adjust its behavior" after reflection (principle 12) [1].

## Information

**Evidence**

- Retrospective guidance treats the close of the meeting as the step that turns insight into action [2]. This is a secondary source. Its claim is a practice recommendation, not a measured rate.
- No team data is available. The completion rate of past actions is the number that decides the cause.
- Four candidate causes cover the space without overlap [judgment]:
  1. Actions have no owner or date.
  2. Actions are too large or too vague to finish.
  3. The problems lie outside the team's control.
  4. People do not feel safe to name the real problem.

**Assumption log**

A1. Past retrospectives produced action items, but few were finished. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: count the items in the last three retrospective notes. If wrong: skip Step 3 and go to the safety cause in Step 2.

A2. The team runs sprints with a shared backlog. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask the team lead. If wrong: use the team's task board instead.

A3. The team has fewer than 12 people. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: read the roster. If wrong: split the retrospective into smaller groups in Step 4.

**Alternative considered**

The strongest alternative is to stop retrospectives for a period. It loses because it removes the only channel for team feedback, and it does not fix the missing follow-through [judgment].

## End

I recommend that we do these steps in order:

1. **Audit past actions (Step 1).**
   - Increment: list every action item from the last three retrospectives with its owner and status. This takes one session.
   - Blocker removed: the team does not know its completion rate. The audit also gives the baseline for Step 4.
   - Kind: deterministic. Test, written first:
     - Given the notes of the last three retrospectives
     - When we tag each action as done, open, or dropped
     - Then a table shows the completion rate and the count of actions with no owner
   - Domain: Clear. The count follows from a known rule (deduction).

2. **Find the cause (Step 2).** Step 2 MUST NOT start until the table in Step 1 exists.
   - Increment: run a three-question anonymous survey and score the four causes.
   - Blocker removed: the team would otherwise fix the wrong cause.
   - Kind: evaluative. Rubric, written first:

   | Criterion | Weight | Evidence needed |
   |---|---|---|
   | Missing owner or date | 30% | Step 1 count |
   | Action too large or vague | 30% | Step 1 text review |
   | Outside team control | 20% | Survey answers |
   | Low safety | 20% | Survey answers |

   - Pass threshold: a cause scores 60% or more of its weight, with support from at least two evidence items.
   - Domain: Complicated. Analysis of the survey picks the practice (deduction from data).

3. **Move actions into the backlog (Step 3).** Step 3 MUST NOT start until Step 2 confirms the cause is owner, date, or size.
   - Increment: add a rule that each retrospective ends with at most two actions, each with one owner and one date, entered in the sprint backlog.
   - Blocker removed: actions live only in meeting notes.
   - Kind: deterministic. Test, written first:
     - Given a retrospective has ended
     - When we open the sprint backlog
     - Then it holds at most two new items, and each item has an owner and a date
   - Domain: Clear. The rule is a known best practice (deduction).

4. **Probe a new format (Step 4).** Step 4 MAY run in parallel with Step 3 but MUST NOT start until Step 1 gives the baseline.
   - Increment: for four retrospectives, spend the full hour on one problem chosen by vote. Use a root-cause method such as the five whys.
   - Capability added: deeper analysis of one problem instead of shallow lists.
   - Kind: stochastic. Acceptance test, written first:
     - Metric: share of actions finished within one sprint.
     - Baseline: the Step 1 rate.
     - Pass threshold: 70% or higher.
     - Sample size: four retrospectives.
   - Stop condition: end the probe if the rate is below the baseline after two retrospectives. Budget: four hours of team time. Leverage is a guess [judgment].
   - Domain: Complex. The effect shows only after trials (induction). Team turnover or a heavy sprint could break the pattern.

If A1 is false, replace Step 3 with a team-safety exercise based on the Step 2 survey.

## Follow-up

Open questions: none. The assumptions above replace them.

Conditions that change the recommendation:
- Step 1 shows a completion rate above 70%. Then the problem is the size of the changes, not follow-through.
- Step 2 ranks "outside team control" highest. Then add a step to escalate those items to management.
