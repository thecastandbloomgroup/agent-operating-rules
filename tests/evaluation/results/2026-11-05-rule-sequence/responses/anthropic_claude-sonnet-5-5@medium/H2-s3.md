## Headline

Freeze all outside statements today, confirm the facts within hours, and choose the public response only after that.

- One decision owner and one spokesperson must exist before anyone speaks.
- The facts decide the response, so check the CEO's statement against records first.
- A short holding statement protects the company while the check runs.
- The final choice (correct, clarify, or defend) follows a scored comparison, not the loudest voice.

## Background

A journalist published that the CEO's statement from yesterday was false. Legal, PR, and the board each send you separate advice. No one agrees on the next step.

## Relevance

Each hour without one owner raises the chance of a second, conflicting statement. A second false or contradictory statement is harder to repair than the first.

## Information

**Evidence**

- Three groups (Legal, PR, board) give you advice, and none agree. This is the count of sources in your message. [judgment] The cause is that no one owns the decision.
- Crisis research says the response should match how much the public holds the organization responsible. Strong responsibility calls for correction or apology, not denial. This comes from the theory in [1].
- Whether the journalist's claim is fully true is about as likely as not (33 to 66%). [judgment] The material gives no facts, so the fact check in Step 2 decides it.

**Assumption log**

A1. The journalist's claim is not yet verified. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: compare each line of the statement with source records. If wrong: if the claim is already confirmed false, skip to Step 4 with the correction option.

A2. The company has disclosure duties, for example as a listed company. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask Legal one yes/no question. If wrong: remove the disclosure check from Step 4.

A3. You can convene the four parties today. Why not measured: the user has not answered. Validate by: send the call invitation now. If wrong: ask the board chair to convene it.

**Alternative considered**

The strongest alternative is an immediate public rebuttal led by PR. It loses because a rebuttal made before the fact check can become a second false statement. [judgment] It also ignores the match between response and responsibility in [1].

## End

I recommend that we take these five actions in this order:

1. **Stabilise: name one decision owner and one spokesperson, and freeze outside statements.**
   - Increment: one call of 30 minutes with Legal, PR, a board representative, and the CEO. Send a written freeze notice after it.
   - Blocker removed: three groups give conflicting advice and no one decides.
   - Domain: Chaotic. Cause and effect are not visible, so act first and stabilise. [2]
   - Kind: deterministic. Test, written first:
     - Given Legal, PR, and the board send separate advice
     - When the owner and the freeze are confirmed in writing
     - Then each group confirms receipt and no outside statement goes out without the owner's sign-off

2. **Check the facts: compare the CEO's statement with source records.**
   - Increment: a one-page fact sheet that marks each claim true, false, or unknown. Allow two hours.
   - Blocker removed: no one knows if the journalist is right.
   - Domain: Complicated. Analysis of records finds the answer.
   - Kind: evaluative. Rubric, written first:
     - Criteria and weights: a source record exists for each claim (40); a second reviewer confirms (30); every claim in the article is matched to a line of the statement (30)
     - Pass threshold: 80 of 100
     - Evidence needed: the records and the reviewer's sign-off
   - Step 2 MUST start once Step 1 passes its test.

3. **Publish a holding statement.**
   - Increment: a three-sentence statement. It acknowledges the report, says the company is reviewing, and gives a time for the next update.
   - Blocker removed: silence leaves the story to the journalist.
   - Domain: Clear. A known best practice exists for holding statements.
   - Kind: deterministic. Test, written first:
     - Given the owner approved the text
     - When the statement is published
     - Then it names the report, states a review, gives an update time, and makes no claim that the CEO's statement was true or false
   - Step 3 MUST NOT start until the test in Step 1 passes. It does not wait for Step 2.

4. **Choose the response: correct, clarify, or defend.**
   - Increment: one scored comparison, completed in one session.
   - Blocker removed: the three groups argue from opinion, not from a shared score.
   - Domain: Complicated. A structured analysis picks among several good options. [3]
   - Kind: evaluative. Rubric, written first:
     - Criteria and weights: accuracy against the fact sheet (35); legal and disclosure exposure (25); trust of staff, customers, and investors (25); reversibility (15)
     - Pass threshold: the top option scores at least 70 of 100 and leads the next by 10
     - Evidence needed: the Step 2 fact sheet and Legal's written view on A2
   - Step 4 MUST NOT start until the test in Step 2 passes.

5. **Probe the reaction with a small briefing.**
   - Increment: a short message to 10 key contacts (for example top customers, senior staff). Budget: two hours.
   - Capability added: an early read on how the holding statement lands.
   - Domain: Complex. Reactions show only afterward, so run a safe-to-fail probe by induction. The pattern can break if a contact shares the message outside. The leverage of this probe is a guess.
   - Kind: stochastic. Acceptance test, written first:
     - Metric: contacts who can restate the company position correctly
     - Baseline: none measured
     - Threshold: 8 of 10
     - Sample: 10 contacts
   - Stop condition: any contact escalates to a regulator or the press. Run Step 5 in parallel with Steps 2 to 4.
