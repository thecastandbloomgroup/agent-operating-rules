## Headline

Choose Northwind Analytics, but lock the price before you sign.

- Cost: Northwind costs 372,000 to 399,000 USD, which fits the 400,000 budget, while Bluepeak (455,000, uncapped) does not.
- Risk: Northwind offers a 99.9% SLA, while Cedar has no SLA and depends on one engineer.
- Performance: Northwind's 2.1 s median sits between Bluepeak (1.3 s) and Cedar (3.4 s).
- Gap: the margin under budget is thin, so a contract change and a test must come first.

## Background

Three vendors proposed a data warehouse for a 400,000 USD, three-year budget. Current data is 14 TB and grows about 3 TB per year [1, Proposal 1]. No vendor states the staff cost to run the system or the price after year 3 [1, closing note].

## Relevance

The thin margin under budget means a small error in growth or price changes the answer. The decision needs a contract fix and a test before signature.

## Information

**Cost check (all figures USD).**

- Northwind: the licence covers 20 TB. Data reaches 17, 20, and 23 TB at the end of years 1 to 3. Year 3 is 3 TB over, so the extra cost is 27,000 and the total is 399,000 [1, Proposal 1].
- Bluepeak: 455,000 is 55,000 (13.75%) over budget at current query volume, and no cap exists [1, Proposal 2].
- Cedar: 286,000 is 114,000 under budget, and hardware fits 30 TB. Staff cost is missing [1, Proposal 3].

**Assumption log.**

A1. Northwind counts data size at year end, so only year 3 has an overage. Why not measured: the material does not state when size is counted. Validate by: ask Northwind in writing. If wrong: with start-of-year counting, the total is 372,000.

A2. Growth is 3 TB per year. Why not measured: the material gives only our team's estimate. Validate by: check the last 12 months of storage logs. If wrong: at 4 TB per year, the total is 444,000, which is over budget.

A3. Cedar needs at least one more engineer. Why not measured: the material does not state staff cost. Validate by: get a loaded salary figure from HR. If wrong: Cedar's cost advantage grows, but the SLA gap remains.

**Alternative considered.** Cedar is the cheapest at 286,000. It loses because it has no SLA, one engineer, and the slowest queries (3.4 s) [1, Proposal 3]. It scores 59 against 78. Its 114,000 cost margin is unproven until staff cost is known (A3).

## End

I recommend that we take these steps, in this order:

1. Negotiate a price cap with Northwind. Ask for a 25 TB tier at the same fixed price and a capped renewal price after year 3. This removes the thin 1,000 USD margin, and it is the largest blocker. Domain: Complicated, because contract terms are knowable by analysis. Kind: deterministic. Increment: one written quote request and one reply.
   - Given a written Northwind quote
   - When we compute the 3-year cost at 4 TB growth per year
   - Then the total is at most 400,000 USD and the year-4 price is capped

2. Test Northwind speed on our own data. Load a 14 TB copy and run the 12 reference queries. This removes the doubt about the 10 TB vendor benchmark. Domain: Complicated, because a measurement gives the answer. Kind: stochastic. Increment: one test session.
   - Metric: median time of the 12 reference queries.
   - Baseline: 2.1 s (vendor benchmark).
   - Pass threshold: median at most 2.6 s [judgment].
   - Sample size: 30 runs per query, 360 runs in total.

3. Run a migration probe in parallel with Step 2. Move 1 TB of real data to a Northwind trial. This finds data quirks before the 10-week move. Domain: Complex, because data problems appear only after the move. Budget: 2 working days. Stop condition: the load is not finished after 2 days. Kind: deterministic.
   - Given a 1 TB extract of our production data
   - When we load it into the Northwind trial
   - Then all row counts match the source

4. Sign the Northwind contract. Step 4 MUST NOT start until Step 1 and Step 2 pass. Step 4 SHOULD wait for the Step 3 result. Domain: Clear, because signing follows a known procedure. Kind: deterministic. Increment: one signed contract.
   - Given passed tests from Steps 1 and 2
   - When legal reviews the final contract
   - Then the contract holds the capped price and the no-fee Parquet exit
