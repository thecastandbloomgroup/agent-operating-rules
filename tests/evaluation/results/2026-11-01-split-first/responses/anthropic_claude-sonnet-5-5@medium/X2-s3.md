## Headline

Retrospectives (regular team meetings that review work and agree changes) stall when ideas do not become owned, tracked work, so fix follow-through first.

- First, audit the action items from the last three retrospectives to find the real cause.
- Next, give every action item one owner, one due date, and a place in the team backlog.
- Then open each retrospective by reviewing the last actions.
- Last, test a format change as a small probe, not a commitment.

## Background

The team attends retrospectives, but nothing changes afterward. The request is for a path that restores real change. The material gives no data on past action items, team size, or meeting rhythm.

## Relevance

Each retrospective without change costs the team meeting time and trust. Attendees learn that speaking up has no effect, so the next session produces less.

## Information

**Evidence**

- The standard five-phase retrospective ends with a "decide what to do" phase that turns insight into concrete actions [1].

- Many retrospectives skip that phase or leave actions without owners. [judgment]

- Safety to speak is a precondition for useful data in a retrospective [2].

- Theme: the stall has two plausible causes, weak follow-through and low safety. Zero team sources are available, so the weighting is a guess. The cause is follow-through: about 60% likely. [judgment]

- The problem is Confused in Cynefin terms until the audit shows the cause [3]. The Cynefin domain describes how cause and effect relate in the work.

2. **Set the action rule (Clear: a known good practice applies).** Increment: in one session, write a rule that each action has one named owner, one due date, and a backlog ticket.    - When the facilitator closes the meeting

4. **Probe the format and safety (Complex: the effect of a change on people shows only afterward).** Increment: for two retrospectives, add an anonymous pre-survey and a changed format.
