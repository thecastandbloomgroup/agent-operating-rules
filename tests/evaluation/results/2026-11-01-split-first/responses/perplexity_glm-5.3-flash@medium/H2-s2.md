## Headline

Hold every external statement now, build a one-page fact record, then answer once under a single spokesperson within 24 hours of the record being ready. Support: (1) a message hold ends the legal, PR, and board conflict over who speaks; (2) the fact record decides between correction, rebuttal, or silence; (3) a fast, single-voice response reduces the damage from the first story [19]; (4) monitoring after the response decides whether a second step is needed.

## Background

A journalist published that a CEO statement from yesterday is false [judgment, based on the message]. Legal, PR, and the board each send directives. No single decision owner exists.

## Relevance

The first hours fix the record: other outlets copy the first story while it stands unanswered [19]. Mixed messages do more harm than the original claim.

## Information

Crisis research advises a fast response and one consistent voice, because delay lets other parties define the story [19]. Source type: peer-reviewed theory, built on case studies [S].

This is a crisis: cause and effect are not visible in real time, so the first action stabilises before any message goes out [18] [P].

Assumption log:

A1. The statement contained a factual claim with operational or financial weight. Why not measured: the message does not state the topic. Validate by: read the article and the exact CEO words. If wrong: treat this as routine PR and skip step 2's legal input.
A2. The journalist based the claim of falsity on evidence, not opinion. Why not measured: the article is not in this session. Validate by: obtain the article and its cited sources. If wrong: a document-backed rebuttal becomes the likely option in step 3.
A3. The company is not publicly listed and no disclosure deadline applies. Why not measured: the message does not state this. Validate by: one yes-or-no check with legal. If wrong: the response window shrinks from 24 hours to about 4, and disclosure counsel leads.

Alternative considered: have legal draft a denial today and send it. It loses because an unverified denial can create a second false statement, and legal alone lacks the press view [judgment].

## End

I recommend that we act in this order:

1. Impose a message hold. Route all external statements through one named owner; legal, PR, and board submit input to that owner only. Blocker removed: divergent messages from the three groups. Domain: Chaotic, because cause and effect are not visible in real time, so stabilisation comes first [18]. Kind: deterministic. Test, written first:
   Given the hold is announced to all three groups,
   When a team drafts an external line,
   Then the line carries the owner's approval before release.
2. Build the fact record before any reply. One page: the exact CEO words, the exact journalist claim, timestamps, and internal documents for each. Blocker removed: the group cannot choose a response without shared facts. Domain: Confused, the record is what classifies the rest of the problem [18]. Kind: deterministic. Test: a second reviewer marks the page complete against the same checklist. This step MUST NOT start its scoring until the page passes that review.
3. Score three options against the record: correct the statement, rebut the article, or say nothing. Blocker removed: the decision itself. Domain: Complicated, because analysis by legal, PR, and the owner selects the practice [18]. Kind: evaluative. Rubric, written first, Kepner-Tregoe format [15]: legal exposure, weight 0.4; press credibility, weight 0.3; stakeholder impact, weight 0.3. Pass threshold: the chosen option scores highest and no criterion falls below 2 on a 5-point scale. Evidence per criterion: the fact record from step 2.
4. Publish the chosen response under one spokesperson within 24 hours of the record passing step 2's test. Blocker removed: the unanswered first story. Domain: Complex for the press reaction, because media response is visible only afterward [18]. Kind: the draft is deterministic, so test first:
   Given the owner approves the text,
   When the publishing window is open,
   Then the exact approved text goes out under the named spokesperson. Budget: one statement, one 24-hour window.
5. Monitor coverage as a parallel probe with the steps above, capped by its budget. Metric: count of top-10 outlets using "false statement" framing. Baseline: today's count. Pass threshold: no increase within 48 hours after publication. Sample: the top 10 outlets. Stop condition: if framing rises, the owner convenes the three groups within 4 hours. Domain: Complex, effect shows only after the probe [18]. Kind: stochastic.

Conditions that change this recommendation: if A3 is false, deadline in step 4 becomes about 4 hours and disclosure counsel writes the text; if A2 is false, step 3 most likely lands on the rebuttal option.

Follow-up questions:

1. Is the company publicly listed, and does the statement touch financials? The answer sets the step 4 deadline and whether disclosure counsel leads.
2. Send the article link and the exact CEO wording. The answer tests A1 and A2 before step 2 starts.
