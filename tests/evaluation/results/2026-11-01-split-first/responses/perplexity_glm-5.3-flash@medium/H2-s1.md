## Background

Legal, PR, and the board are each sending you conflicting messages after a journalist called the CEO statement false. Nobody holds a shared plan. You need an order of steps, not another opinion.

## Relevance

Unverified public messages within the first 24 hours shape the story and the legal record. Order matters more than speed on the content itself. [judgment]

## Information

The problem splits into three parts that do not overlap: process (who decides), facts (what is true), and message (what to say).

Assumption log:

- A3. No formal fact verification with legal review has happened between publication and now. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: one question to the communications team. If wrong: skip the result part of Step 3 and move to the decision meeting.

Alternative considered: publish an immediate public rebuttal. It loses because an unverified rebuttal risks a second false statement, and fast rebuttals extend news cycles more often than they end them. [judgment]

## End

I recommend that we run these five steps in this order.

1. **Freeze external and internal statements (Chaotic)**, because cause and effect are not visible here and the first job is to stop the spread. Increment: within 2 hours, send one notice that names a single owner of all media contact. This removes the blocker of parallel messages. Test:

   Given the freeze notice is sent
   When any employee receives a media inquiry
   Then they forward it to the named owner and give no comment

2. **Name the decision owner and one meeting time (Clear)**, because a known best practice exists: one incident owner, one decision point. Increment: one email with the owner's name and a fixed daily 30-minute meeting. This removes the blocker of three unaligned channels. Test:

   Given the owner is named
   When legal, PR, or a board member raises a decision
   Then it routes to the owner only

3. **Audit the disputed facts (Complicated)**, because picking the facts needs analysis with access to records. Increment: today, one table that lists each disputed claim in the article, the matching CEO statement text, and the source record. This adds the capability to respond with verified facts. Test:

   Given the article text and the statement transcript
   When each disputed claim is checked against a source document
   Then the table marks the claim true, false, or unverifiable, with a citation

4. **Choose the response with a scored rubric (Complicated, evaluative)**, because more than one good response exists and analysis must pick one. Increment: within 24 hours, one decision meeting that scores options with a Kepner-Tregoe rubric: legal exposure weight 0.4, credibility weight 0.4, speed weight 0.2, pass threshold 70 of 100, evidence from the Step 3 table. This removes the blocker of alignment. Score first, then decide.

5. **Publish through one controlled channel (Complex, stochastic)**, because the audience reaction shows only after release. Probe, not full commitment: one holding statement first on one channel. Budget: one statement, 24 hours, one channel. Measure: statement accuracy confirmed against the Step 3 table, sentiment of follow-up coverage. Stop condition: if coverage intensifies, revert to no further comment and go back to Step 4. The final substantive response MUST NOT start until the Step 3 table is complete and the Step 4 decision is scored.

## Follow-up

Conditions that change this plan: if the article points to legal exposure that counsel rates as severe, Step 5 stops and counsel directs the sequence. If the statement leaked through a private channel, the audit scope widens to that channel and the freeze notice extends to internal chats.
