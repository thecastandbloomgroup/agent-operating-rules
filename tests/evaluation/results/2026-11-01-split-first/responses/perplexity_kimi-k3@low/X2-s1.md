# Headline

Retrospectives fail when they produce discussion instead of owned, tested changes, so fix the output loop, not the meeting. First, cap each retrospective at one or two action items with owners and due dates. Second, open every retrospective by reviewing the previous action items. Third, treat any format change as a small probe with a measure and a stop condition.

## Background

The team holds retrospectives, people attend, and nothing changes afterward. The request is how to make retrospectives produce change again.

## Relevance

A retrospective that produces no change costs one to two hours per sprint per person [judgment]. It also teaches the team that improvement talk is safe to ignore, which lowers trust in the format itself [1].

## Information

Per the Scrum Guide, the purpose of the sprint retrospective is to plan ways to increase quality and effectiveness, and the team identifies the most helpful changes to improve its effectiveness [1]. Derby and Larsen describe the same failure pattern: retrospectives decay when action items lack owners, when too many items are attempted at once, and when outcomes are never reviewed [2].

A common root cause is that the team generates items outside its authority [judgment]. Such items die silently. The fix is to split items into "team can do" and "needs escalation" and to route the second group to a named manager.

**Assumption log**

A1. The team holds a regular retrospective at a fixed cadence. Why not measured: the material does not state it, but the question implies it. Validate by: ask the facilitator once. If wrong: the advice still applies to whatever improvement meeting exists.

A2. The team has authority to change its own working agreements. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: check the team charter or ask the manager. If wrong: add an escalation step before Step 1.

A3. The completion rate of past retro action items is low (under 50%). Why not measured: no tool can reach it here. Validate by: review the last three retros' notes. If wrong: the problem is item quality, not follow-through, so replace Step 3 with a root-cause session.

Confidence: high confidence that the output loop (owners, caps, review) is the primary fix, two independent sources [1], [2].

**Alternative considered**

Drop retrospectives and hold improvement talks only when a problem appears. This loses because scheduled cadence protects improvement time; ad hoc improvement is displaced by delivery pressure [2] [judgment].

## End

I recommend that we take four actions:

1. **Cap and assign action items.** Increment: at the next retrospective, select at most two action items, each with one named owner and a due date before the next retro. Blocker removed: items without owners never get done [2]. Cynefin domain: Clear, because this is a known best practice. Test (deterministic, write first):
   - Given the retrospective has ended,
   - When the notes are published,
   - Then they contain at most two action items, each with one owner and one due date.

2. **Open with a review of past items.** Increment: put a five-minute review of the previous action items first on every retro agenda, starting next session. Blocker removed: no feedback loop, so non-completion has no cost [1]. Cynefin domain: Clear, cause and effect are known. Test (deterministic):
   - Given a retrospective begins,
   - When the facilitator opens the agenda,
   - Then the status of every prior action item (done, dropped, carried) is recorded.

3. **Run a format probe.** Action type: stochastic. Increment: for the next three retros only, collect improvement topics anonymously before the meeting. Metric: share of attendees who contribute at least one topic. Baseline: current share (measure at the probe start). Pass threshold: 20 percentage points above baseline across three retros. Stop condition: drop the change after three retros if the threshold is not met. Budget: 15 minutes of facilitator prep per retro. Cynefin domain: Complex, because participation effects are visible only afterward; leverage is a guess.

4. **Escalate items outside team authority.** Increment: label each action item "team" or "escalate" during the retro, and send each "escalate" item to a named manager within two working days. This step depends only on Step 1. Blocker removed: items the team cannot act on dying silently [judgment]. Cynefin domain: Complicated, because routing requires analysis of who owns each blocker. Test (deterministic):
   - Given an action item is labeled "escalate",
   - When two working days pass,
   - Then the named manager has confirmed receipt in writing.

## Follow-up

The recommendation changes if A3 is false: if past completion was already high, run a root-cause session on item quality instead of Step 3.
