# Headline

Stabilise the facts before anyone speaks again, so the company responds once, with one verified position, within the next 24 hours.

1. Stop all outbound communication until the CEO's statement is checked against primary evidence. A second false claim is worse than a delayed reply [judgment].
2. Run a fast three-track review: Legal checks exposure, PR checks the journalist's evidence, a board delegate agrees what can be said.
3. Issue one holding statement today and one corrective or defensive statement once the fact-check completes.

# Background

A journalist published that the CEO's yesterday statement is false. Legal, PR, and the board message in parallel without a shared position.

# Relevance

Every hour of public silence and internal disagreement raises legal and reputational cost. Misaligned replies can create defamation, disclosure, or retraction exposure [judgment].

# Information

## Evidence

- The journalist's claim exists; its accuracy is unverified because the material does not confirm it [no source].
- A false statement followed by a false defence compounds liability; a prompt, verified correction limits it [judgment].
- Crisis communication research supports one spokesperson and one message [no source, tertiary basis].

## Assumption log

A1. The journalist published today or yesterday, and no reply has gone out yet. Why not measured: the material does not state timing precisely. Validate by: check the article timestamp and the press inbox, 10 minutes. If wrong: adjust Step 3 timing only.
A2. The statement is verifiable against documents, data, or recordings. Why not measured: the statement content is unknown. Validate by: ask the CEO office for the source material. If wrong: the response becomes a judgment dispute, and Legal leads instead of the fact team.
A3. The material does not name the jurisdiction, so no disclosure rules are assumed. Why not measured: company domicile is unstated. Validate by: confirm with Legal in Step 1. If wrong: insert a regulator notification step before any public statement.

## Alternative considered

The strongest alternative is an immediate public rebuttal by PR. It loses because publishing before the fact-check risks a second false statement, which converts a media problem into a legal one [judgment].

# End

I recommend that we act in this order:

1. **Freeze outbound communication for 24 hours.** Send one line to Legal, PR, board, and CEO office: no external comment until Step 4. Blocker removed: conflicting public voices. Domain: **Chaotic**, because no cause and effect are visible while four parties talk at once; stabilise first. Test (deterministic): **Given** the freeze notice is sent, **When** any party drafts external text, **Then** the draft routes to the single response owner.
2. **Verify the statement in a 4-hour fact team.** One lawyer, one PR lead, one board delegate compare the CEO's words against primary records. Blocker removed: unknown truth. Domain: **Complicated**, because cause and effect are knowable through document analysis. Test (evaluative, mini-rubric): verdict is true, partly false, or false; each rating needs a cited record; pass requires agreement of all three members.
3. **Build one decision tree before publication.** Pre-draft three statements: correct, clarify, defend. Blocker removed: slow response after the verdict. Domain: **Clear**, because drafted-then-approved statements are a known best practice. Test (deterministic): **Given** the verdict from Step 2, **When** the matching draft is selected, **Then** it is within 200 words and quotes a primary record.
4. **Publish through one channel and one spokesperson.** Step 4 MUST NOT start until Step 2 passes. Blocker removed: fragmented narrative. Domain: **Clear**, for the same reason as Step 3. Test (stochastic): acceptance is zero conflicting statements from any spokesperson within 72 hours of publication, sample of all public posts and press contacts.
5. **Probe the journalist follow-up.** One reply offering evidence; stop condition is any legal threat, which routes to counsel. Domain: **Complex**, because the journalist's reaction is known only afterward; leverage here is a guess, not a plan basis. Budget: one reply, one business day.

If Step 2 finds the statement true, replace the correction path with the defence path; the rest holds.

# Follow-up

Open questions (answer changes the action):

1. What exactly did the CEO say, and what evidence does the journalist hold? (This decides whether Step 2 leads to correction or defence.)
2. Is the company publicly listed or regulated, which would add a disclosure duty before any statement?

Conditions that change the recommendation: a legal threat escalates Step 5 to counsel first; an unverifiable statement moves leadership from the fact team to Legal.
