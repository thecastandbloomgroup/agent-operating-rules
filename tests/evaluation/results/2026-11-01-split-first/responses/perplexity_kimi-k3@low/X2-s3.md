## Headline

Retrospectives fail because insights never become owned, tracked actions, so fix the loop between discussion and follow-through. Three mechanisms drive the failure: no single owner per action, no review of past actions, and topics people do not feel safe to raise [judgment]. One root-cause check, plus three process changes, restores change within about two sprints.

## Background

Your team holds retrospectives. Attendance is fine, but the meetings produce no changes in behavior or process. The request: actions to restart improvement.

## Relevance

A retrospective that produces nothing costs meeting time and teaches the team that speaking up changes nothing [judgment]. Fixing the follow-through loop is cheaper than replacing the format.

## Information

Evidence is thin on the specific team, so the plan rests on a widely used retrospective method [1] and judgment.

- Common failure pattern: actions are agreed but owned by "the team", so no one acts [judgment].
- Actions not reviewed at the next retrospective fade; Derby and Larsen make reviewing past actions a step in the format [1].
- Fear of naming real problems is likely (60% likely). A one-question anonymous pulse test checks it cheaply [judgment].

Assumption log:

`A1. Retrospectives already produce action items, but they are not tracked. Why not measured: the user has not answered. Validate by: read the last two retrospectives' notes. If wrong: drop Step 2 and focus on topic quality, Step 4.`

`A2. The team has a facilitator or lead who can run the changes. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask in the next standup. If wrong: assign a rotating facilitator first.`

Alternative considered: replacing the retrospective format, for example with structured workshops. It loses because format changes do not fix ownership or follow-through; the failure is in the loop after the meeting [judgment].

## End

I recommend that we take these actions.

1. Diagnose the loop. Read the last two retrospective notes and count actions agreed, actions completed, and actions with a named owner. Increment: one 30-minute review. Blocker removed: unknown root cause (Confused; we cannot yet see cause and effect). Test, evaluative rubric written first: criteria are completion rate below 50%, unnamed owners above 50%, and repeated topics; each weights one third; two or more criteria true means the loop, not the format, is the defect. Evidence: the notes themselves.
2. Cap actions at one per retrospective, each with one named owner and a due date. Increment: apply at the next retrospective. Blocker removed: diffusion of responsibility (Clear; known best practice [1]). Test, deterministic:
   Given a retrospective ends with action items,
   When the facilitator records them,
   Then each item has exactly one owner and one date, and the count is one.
   Step 2 starts whatever Step 1 finds, because ownership is low-risk.
3. Open each retrospective with a two-minute review of the previous action. Increment: add the agenda item. Blocker removed: old actions disappear (Clear; this is part of the standard format [1]). Test, deterministic:
   Given the last retrospective recorded an action,
   When the next retrospective starts,
   Then the owner reports done, not done, or blocked before new topics open.
   Step 3 MUST NOT be skipped even if the action is unfinished.
4. Run one anonymous round, for example cards or a chat poll, on "what are we not saying". Increment: one round in the next two retrospectives, capped at 15 minutes. Blocker removed: unsafe topics stay hidden (Complex probe; cause and effect visible only afterward, so treat the round as a safe-to-fail probe with a stop condition: stop if participation stays at zero after two tries). Test, stochastic: metric is new topics never raised in open discussion; baseline is zero from recent notes; pass threshold is one or more new topics; sample size is two retrospectives. Runs in parallel with Steps 2 and 3.

Hedge: if A2 is false, add a step before Step 2 to assign a facilitator.

## Follow-up

If Step 1 shows actions were tracked and completed but nothing changed, the defect is topic quality. Raise that as a new question.
