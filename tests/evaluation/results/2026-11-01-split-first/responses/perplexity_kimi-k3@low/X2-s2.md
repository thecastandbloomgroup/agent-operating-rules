## Headline

Your retrospectives fail at the loop from insight to action, so repair that loop in four moves: close, diagnose, focus, probe.

- Actions are forgotten when no owner, done-check, or review carries them into the next retro [1].
- A single repeated format dulls participation, so the meeting collects only shallow topics [1].
- Effort scatters on problems outside the team's control, so the work shows no result [3].
- People withhold real topics when speaking up feels unsafe, so the true blockers stay hidden [2].

## Background

Your team holds retrospectives (retros) on a regular cadence, and people attend. The same topics return, and behavior does not change. You ask how to make the meetings produce change again.

## Relevance

A retro that changes nothing teaches people to invest nothing in the next one. Therefore input quality falls with each cycle, and the cost of waiting grows [inference].

## Information

The practitioner literature names four failure modes, and together they explain the symptom.

First, follow-through. Derby and Larsen instruct facilitators to end every retro with specific, owned commitments and to review them later [1]. The reason: insight without a tracked commitment changes nothing [inference]. The cap of two open actions in Step 1 is [judgment]; the principle of a cap comes from [1].

Second, format fatigue. Derby and Larsen supply a catalog of varied activities because repetition dulls participation [1]. Kerth structures his review method around varied activities as well [4]. Theme count: two sources support it, and none disagree.

Third, control. Covey separates what a team controls from what it only influences [3]. Work on the second group without an escalation path produces no visible result [inference].

Fourth, safety. Edmondson shows that psychological safety predicts whether people raise real problems [2]. Psychological safety means the belief that speaking up brings no punishment. This theme rests on one source here. It still matters because Step 2 tests it locally at low cost.

Quantitative evidence is not available without access to your retro records. Step 2 creates the local numbers.

Confidence: the follow-through mode is the likely main cause (66 to 100%). Basis: the reported symptom is that nothing shifts, which is an execution and review failure [inference].

Assumption log

A1. The team runs retros at a steady cadence of one to four weeks. Why not measured: the request does not state cadence. Validate by: check the team calendar. If wrong: adjust the timing of Steps 1 to 4.

A2. Missing follow-through is the dominant failure, ahead of low safety. Why not measured: no tool can reach the retro notes. Validate by: the Step 2 scoring. If wrong: run Step 4 before Step 3.

A3. The team has authority to change its own working agreements. Why not measured: the request does not state constraints. Validate by: confirm with the manager or the working agreement. If wrong: the Step 3 escalation path becomes the main track.

Alternative considered

The strongest alternative is to cancel the meetings and keep only a standing improvement board. It loses because a board tracks tasks but removes the group reflection that surfaces systemic topics [1]. The broken loop would follow the work into any new forum [judgment].

## End

I recommend that we run four steps.

Step 1, close the loop (Clear, deterministic). Increment, one working session: build a visible improvement board. Each action gets one row with an owner, a done-check, and a date. Cap open actions at two. A done-check is a visible proof that the action happened. Blocker removed: actions are forgotten after the meeting. Domain: Clear, because a best practice exists and its effect is known [1]. Test first:
Given a retro has ended,
When five working days have passed,
Then every open action shows one named owner, a done-check, and a date.
Also open each retro with a five-minute board review.

Step 2, diagnose the failing stage (Complicated, evaluative). Increment, one working session: two reviewers score the notes of the last three retros and a five-question anonymous pulse. Blocker removed: Steps 3 and 4 target a stage instead of a guess. Domain: Complicated, because the cause is knowable through this analysis. Rubric first: criteria are the four loop stages, weights are equal, and each stage scores 0 to 2 against the notes and the pulse. A stage at 0 or 1 counts as failing, and the lowest stage directs Steps 3 and 4.

Step 3, focus on controllable items (Clear, deterministic). Increment, inside the next retro: sort each item into team controls, team influences, or neither. Act on the first two classes, and assign an escalation owner for the third. Blocker removed: effort spent where the team has no leverage. Domain: Clear, because the triage is an established practice [3]. Test first:
Given the team lists an improvement item,
When the facilitator asks who can change this,
Then the item lands in one of the three classes and carries an owner or an escalation owner.

Step 4, probe for safety (Complex, stochastic). Run only if Step 2 flags the input stage. Increment: one retro with anonymous written input within the next two cycles. Budget: two retros and no extra meetings. Blocker removed: fear of consequences hides real topics. Domain: Complex, because team openness shows its causes only afterward, so leverage here is a guess. Acceptance test first: metric is the count of first-time topics per session. Baseline is that count in the last three retros, set by Step 2. Threshold is three or more per session. Sample size is two sessions. Stop condition: if both sessions stay below the threshold, end the anonymous format and rotate the facilitator.

Sequencing. Step 1 runs this week. Step 2 runs the same week, in parallel. Step 3 runs at the next retro. Step 4 MUST NOT start until the Step 2 scoring flags the input stage. If A2 proves false, Step 4 moves ahead of Step 3.

## Follow-up

Open question 1: What share of actions from the last three retros reached done? Use of the answer: above one half, treat A2 as false and run Step 4 before Step 3.

Conditions that change the recommendation: a Step 2 flag on the input stage moves Step 4 first. A3 false turns the Step 3 escalation path into the main track.
