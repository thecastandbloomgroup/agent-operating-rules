# Headline

**Find the stage where your retrospectives fail, then fix that stage with small, owned actions.**

1. A one-hour audit of past action items (changes the team agreed to make) shows which of four stages fails: raise, decide, do, or check.
2. One action per retrospective, with an owner and a place in the backlog (the list of planned work), targets the decide and do stages.
3. A five-minute review of the last action at the start of each retrospective targets the check stage.
4. A probe (a small, safe-to-fail test) with anonymous cards tests whether the raise stage fails.

# Background

Your team holds retrospectives, that is, regular meetings to review how the team works. Members attend, but the meetings no longer lead to changes.

# Relevance

Each retrospective uses the work time of every member. When nothing changes, members also learn that the problems they raise lead nowhere [judgment].

# Information

This section covers the four stages, the evidence, the assumptions, and the alternative.

**Four stages.** A retrospective produces change only when all four stages work. A failure belongs to the first stage that does not work [judgment].

| Stage | Question | Sign of failure |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Raise | Do members say the real problems? | The same safe topics come up each time. |
| 2. Decide | Does the meeting end with a concrete action? | Items are vague, for example "communicate better". |
| 3. Do | Does someone complete the action? | Items have no owner or are not in the backlog. |
| 4. Check | Does the team see whether the action worked? | No one reviews past items. |

Your team's failing stage is not known. Confidence in any single stage is low, because the material contains no team data. Therefore the first step collects that data.

**Theme A: a retrospective needs a separate step that turns insight into a few actions in the work plan.** Two sources support it. No source in this session disagrees.

- Derby and Larsen make "decide what to do" one of five stages. They also advise the team to choose only a few actions:
  [1]
- The Scrum Guide tells the team to address the improvements with the most effect as soon as possible. It allows the team to add them to the next Sprint Backlog (the plan for one iteration):
  [2]

**Theme B: members raise real problems only when they feel safe.** Two sources support it. No source in this session disagrees.

- Edmondson studied 51 work teams. She defines psychological safety as "a shared belief that the team is safe for interpersonal risk taking." She found that it links to how much a team learns:
  [3]
- Kerth's Prime Directive asks members to assume that everyone did the best job they could:
  [4]

Theme B has only two sources. However, it matters, because a failure at stage 1 blocks all later stages [judgment].

**Assumption log**

A1. The team keeps notes or a board from past retrospectives. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: open the notes from the last six meetings. If wrong: Step 1 rebuilds the list from member memory, and its confidence drops to low.

A2. The team has authority to change its own way of work. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask the team lead whether a change needs outside approval. If wrong: add a step that sends blocked items to the person with authority.

A3. The team has 4 to 10 members and works in iterations of 1 to 4 weeks. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: count the members and check the iteration length. If wrong: change the sample size and dates in Step 4.

**Alternative considered.** The strongest alternative is to change the retrospective activities first, for example with new games or a new room. It loses because new activities act mainly on stage 1, and the failing stage is not yet known [judgment].

# End

I recommend that we take four actions in this order. Each step names its Cynefin domain, that is, how cause and effect relate in the work:
[5]

**Step 1. Audit the last six retrospectives.**

- **Increment:** list each action item from the last six meetings. Mark the stage where it stopped. This takes about one hour [judgment].
- **Blocker removed:** the team does not know which stage fails.
- **Kind:** deterministic. The test comes first:
  - Given the notes from the last six retrospectives,
  - When we list every action item,
  - Then each item has one status: vague, no owner, owned but not done, done but not checked, or done and checked,
  - And the status counts equal the total number of items.
- **Domain:** Confused. This step collects what the team needs to classify the other steps. A stage 1 failure leaves no item, so Step 4 covers it.

**Step 2. End the next retrospective with one action.**

- **Increment:** choose one action. Name one owner, a done condition, and a review date. Put the action in the backlog for the next iteration.
- **Blocker removed:** actions that stay vague or have no owner (stages 2 and 3).
- **Kind:** deterministic. The test comes first:
  - Given the next retrospective ends,
  - When the facilitator closes the meeting,
  - Then the backlog holds one retrospective action,
  - And that action has one owner, a done condition, and a review date.
- **Domain:** Clear. Two sources describe this practice:
  [1]
  [2]
- **Dependency:** Step 2 MUST NOT start until Step 1 records the status counts.

**Step 3. Start each retrospective with a review of the last action.**

- **Increment:** use the first five minutes of the meeting for the action from Step 2. Record whether the owner completed it and whether it changed anything.
- **Blocker removed:** no one checks the effect of an action (stage 4).
- **Kind:** deterministic. The test comes first:
  - Given an action from the previous retrospective,
  - When the next retrospective starts,
  - Then the notes record "done" or "not done" for that action,
  - And the notes record "effect seen" or "no effect seen".
- **Domain:** Clear [judgment]. The practice is common, but no source in this session states it.
- **Dependency:** Step 3 MUST NOT start until Step 2 produces one action.

**Step 4. Run a probe with anonymous cards.**

- **Increment:** for two retrospectives, collect problems on anonymous cards before the discussion. The budget is 15 minutes per meeting.
- **Capability added:** a way to see problems that members do not say aloud (stage 1).
- **Kind:** stochastic. The test comes first:
  - **Metric:** number of distinct problems raised per meeting.
  - **Baseline:** the count from the last retrospective, from the Step 1 notes.
  - **Pass threshold:** at least 50% more distinct problems than the baseline, and at least one problem the team has not discussed before [judgment].
  - **Sample size:** two meetings, with at least half the members submitting cards in each meeting.
- **Stop condition:** stop after the first meeting if fewer than half the members submit cards.
- **Domain:** Complex. The effect of anonymity on this team is visible only afterward, so the leverage of this step is a guess.
- **Order:** run Step 4 in parallel with Steps 2 and 3, within its budget.

# Follow-up

**Open questions:** none.

**Conditions that change the recommendation:**

- If Step 1 shows that most items stopped because they need outside approval, add a step that sends each blocked item to the person with authority (see A2).
- If Step 1 shows that most items were done and checked, the failure is in stage 1. In that case, make Step 4 the main step and extend it to four meetings.
