### Headline

Retrospectives produce change when teams treat improvement actions as planned product work [judgment].
First, teams must select only one action item per cycle to reduce work in progress.
Second, the team must place that action item directly into the active sprint backlog.
Third, the team must review the status of that item at the start of the next retrospective.

### Background

The team attends retrospectives, but the meetings no longer cause changes in daily work.
Action items either fail to launch or vanish without measurable progress between sessions [judgment].
This pattern occurs when teams treat retrospective actions as optional tasks outside regular delivery commitments [1].

### Relevance

Stalled retrospectives consume team hours without delivering improvements in process or morale [judgment].
Fixing execution now restores team agency before attendance turns into passive presence [judgment].

### Information

Retrospective fatigue typically stems from three systemic breakdowns [1]:
First, teams select too many action items, which dilutes focus [1].
Second, action items lack explicit acceptance criteria and single owners [2].
Third, teams track action items in meeting notes rather than in the primary sprint tracking tool [judgment].

When improvement tasks compete with sprint goals, delivery work wins 100% of the time [judgment].
Therefore, the team must convert retrospective ideas into standard backlog work items [1].
This section covers three evidence items and the project assumptions.

Evidence summary:
1. High-performing teams limit process improvement to one action item per iteration [1].
2. Visible tracking on the main board increases item completion over isolated meeting notes [judgment].
3. Uncompleted action items reduce team engagement in subsequent retrospectives [2].

Assumption log:
A1. The team uses an iteration backlog to track regular tasks. Why not measured: The material does not state it. Validate by: Check the team board. If wrong: Track action items on a physical board instead.
A2. The team has authority to alter its internal working agreements. Why not measured: The material does not state it. Validate by: Ask the team lead in the next planning meeting. If wrong: Escalate blocked items to management.

Alternative considered:
Cancel retrospectives for two cycles to reset team attention.
This alternative loses because stopping meetings signals that continuous improvement does not matter [judgment].

### End

I recommend that we take these three actions to restore retrospective momentum:

1. Audit the action items from the past three retrospectives against an execution rubric.
   - Increment: Complete the rubric evaluation in one 45-minute working session.
   - Blocker: Removes uncertainty about why past retrospective actions failed.
   - Kind: Evaluative.
   - Rubric:
     - Criterion 1 (Weight 40%): Single assigned owner exists. Evidence: Board history.
     - Criterion 2 (Weight 40%): Measurable acceptance criteria exist. Evidence: Written card description.
     - Criterion 3 (Weight 20%): Item entered sprint backlog. Evidence: Sprint board record.
     - Pass threshold: Score of 80% or higher across past items.
   - Domain: Clear, because cause and effect are known to everyone from standard task management records.

2. Limit the next retrospective to one action item and add it to the active sprint backlog. Step 2 MUST NOT start until the audit in Step 1 finishes.
   - Increment: Create one standard user story or task during retrospective closing.
   - Blocker: Adds the capability to prioritize improvement work alongside product delivery.
   - Kind: Deterministic.
   - Test:
     Given the team agrees on one improvement action at retrospective close,
     When the facilitator creates the work item,
     Then the item appears on the active sprint board with one owner and testable acceptance criteria.
   - Domain: Clear, because cause and effect are known to everyone when the team applies standard backlog rules.

3. Run a two-sprint probe where the team reviews the previous action item before raising new topics. Step 3 runs in parallel with Step 2.
   - Increment: Dedicate the first five minutes of the next two retrospectives to item verification.
   - Blocker: Removes the habit of abandoning prior commitments between meetings.
   - Kind: Stochastic.
   - Test:
     - Metric: Percentage of action items marked completed by the next retrospective.
     - Baseline: 0% completed in the prior retrospective.
     - Pass threshold: 100% completion across a sample size of two consecutive sprints.
     - Budget cap: Ten minutes total across two retrospectives. Stop if the team rejects the review format.
   - Domain: Complex, because team motivation and social accountability show their effects only after trial iterations.

### Follow-up

Conditions that change the recommendation:
1. If A1 is false, write action items on a shared physical board in the team room.
2. If A2 is false, invite the team manager to the retrospective closing to approve process changes.
