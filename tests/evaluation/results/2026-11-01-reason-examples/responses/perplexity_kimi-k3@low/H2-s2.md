# Headline

Stabilise communication within hours, verify the facts against a record, then align one response through one spokesperson.

- First, freeze external statements and open one coordination channel, because mixed messages cause the most damage now [judgment].
- Second, verify the statement against the primary record, because every later step depends on the facts.
- Third, run one decision process with Legal, PR, and the board, because you need one agreed position.
- Fourth, respond once through one spokesperson, because a single voice limits exposure.

# Background

A journalist published a claim that the CEO's statement from yesterday was false. Legal, PR, and the board contact you separately and do not agree. You need the next steps in order.

# Relevance

Each hour of unaligned messaging increases legal and reputational exposure [judgment]. A fast, verified, single response limits that exposure.

# Information

**Assumption log**

A1. The CEO's statement exists in a retrievable record (transcript, recording, or release). Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask the CEO's office for the text or recording. If wrong: Step 1 changes to a reconstruction of what was said, from attendees.

A2. The journalist's article names the specific claim called false. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: read the article. If wrong: request clarification from the journalist before Step 3.

A3. A general counsel and a head of communications exist and can join one call today. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: one message to both. If wrong: route decisions to the board chair directly.

**Alternative considered**

Respond at once with a denial or retraction before verification. This loses because a denial of a true claim, or a retraction of a true statement, doubles the damage [judgment]. Verification takes hours; the cost of a wrong response is far higher.

# End

I recommend that we take these five actions, in this order:

1. **Stabilise.** Send one holding instruction to Legal, PR, and the board: no external comment until the fact check closes. Cynefin domain: Chaotic, because three parties act without coordination and no shared cause is visible. Increment: one message sent in this session. Blocker removed: unaligned public statements. Test (deterministic):
   - Given the holding instruction is sent to Legal, PR, and the board,
   - When any inquiry arrives from media or staff,
   - Then all parties reply with the agreed holding line only.

2. **Verify the record.** Collect the CEO's statement, the source data behind it, and the article's specific claim. Cynefin domain: Complicated, because cause and effect are knowable through document analysis. Increment: one fact memo. Blocker removed: factual uncertainty. Test (deterministic):
   - Given the statement text and the underlying data,
   - When each sentence of the statement is compared line by line with the data,
   - Then each sentence is marked supported, contradicted, or unverifiable, with a citation.

3. **Decide the position.** Hold one 60-minute meeting with counsel, communications, and a board delegate. Cynefin domain: Complicated, because several valid responses exist and structured analysis must pick one. Increment: one decision record. Blocker removed: no agreed response. Test (evaluative, Kepner-Tregoe [15]): score options against legal risk, factual accuracy, and reputation, each weighted equally; pass threshold: one option scores at least 70% of the maximum and has no legal blocker.

   Step 3 MUST NOT start until the fact memo in Step 2 is complete.

4. **Respond once.** Issue one statement through one spokesperson, matched to the fact memo: correction, clarification, or substantiation. Cynefin domain: Clear, because crisis-response practice gives one known form per finding. Increment: one published statement and a Q&A sheet. Blocker removed: media silence feeding the story. Test (deterministic):
   - Given the decision record from Step 3,
   - When the statement is drafted,
   - Then counsel and communications both sign off before release, and every factual sentence traces to the fact memo.

5. **Set a watch.** Monitor coverage and stakeholder replies for 72 hours. Cynefin domain: Complex, because audience reaction is visible only afterward. This is a capped probe: a two-person watch, a daily summary, and a stop condition. Stop condition: no new erroneous reports in two consecutive cycles. Budget: two staff, three days. Test (stochastic): metric: number of uncorrected false repetitions of the article's claim; baseline: today's count; pass threshold: zero for two consecutive days; sample: all outlets that carried the original article.

   Step 5 MAY run in parallel with Steps 3 and 4. Leverage for Step 5 is a guess; it is capped for that reason.

# Follow-up

1. Does the article cite evidence for its claim? The answer sets how much of Step 2 is document review versus new data collection.
2. Was the CEO's statement made under oath, in a filing, or on the record to regulators? The answer changes the legal stakes of Step 4.

Conditions that change this plan: if A1 fails, replace Step 2 with witness interviews within 24 hours; if the fact memo shows the statement was false, Step 4 becomes a prompt correction, because delay raises legal exposure [judgment].
