## Headline

Repair the loop: audit why past actions died, cap each retrospective at two owned actions, and open every retrospective by reviewing them.

- Audit the last three retrospectives first, because the remedy differs if actions were dropped versus never made.
- Cap output at two actions with one named owner each, because small owned commitments get finished.
- Review prior actions at the start, because a visible loop sustains attention and follow-through.
- Test format and safety last as a capped probe, because their effects appear only after a trial.

## Background

The team's retrospectives still run and attendance holds, but nothing changes as a result. The request asks how to make retrospectives produce change again.

## Relevance

A retrospective that never changes anything teaches people to disengage [judgment]. It also costs one to two hours per person per cycle with no measured return [judgment].

## Information

**Evidence**

- Derby and Larsen structure a retrospective in five phases that end with "decide what to do": a few concrete commitments, not a long list [1].
- The Scrum Guide states that the most impactful improvements are addressed as soon as possible, and may even be added to the next Sprint Backlog [2].
- Rozovsky's report on Google's Project Aristotle found psychological safety the strongest of five factors behind effective teams, so fear of speaking can empty a retrospective [3].

Theme check: two of three sources tie a retrospective's value to committed follow-through rather than discussion; none of the three disagrees.

**Assumption log**

- A1. Actions are created but not finished or tracked. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: review notes from the last three retrospectives. If wrong: move the probe in Step 5 ahead of Steps 2 and 3.
- A2. The team controls part of its own process. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask the team lead for one change the team made itself. If wrong: the escalation route in Step 4 becomes the main path.
- A3. Retrospectives run on a fixed cadence. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: check the team calendar. If wrong: the steps still apply to each retrospective held.

**Alternative considered**

Stop retrospectives and replace them with quarterly health checks or a suggestion channel. This loses: the improvement loop is still needed, removal signals that reflection does not matter [judgment], and repair costs less than building a new mechanism.

## End

I recommend that we take five steps, in this order. Each step names its increment, its blocker, its test, and its Cynefin domain (a model of how cause and effect relate in the work [4]).

1. **Audit the last three retrospectives** in one working session; list every action item with owner and outcome. Blocker removed: the unknown failure point. Deterministic, test written first:
Given: the notes from the last three retrospectives,
When: we record each action item with its owner and final status,
Then: a completion-rate table shows where each action died.
Cynefin: Clear, because the records exist and counting them is one known practice.

2. **Cap the next retrospective at two action items**, each with one named owner and sized to finish before the next retrospective. Blocker removed: diffused responsibility. Deterministic:
Given: the next retrospective,
When: it closes,
Then: at most two actions sit on the team board, each with one owner and a due date.
Cynefin: Clear, because small owned commitments are established practice per [1] and [2].

3. **Open every retrospective with a five-minute review** of the previous actions' status. Blocker removed: the missing accountability loop. Deterministic:
Given: the agenda of a retrospective,
When: the retrospective opens,
Then: the first item shows the status of every previous action.
Cynefin: Clear, because this applies the standard inspect-and-adapt practice [2].

4. **Route each action outside team control** to one named manager with a reply date on a visible escalation list. Blocker removed: systemic obstacles that breed cynicism. Deterministic:
Given: an action the team cannot complete alone,
When: it is raised,
Then: it appears on the escalation list with one named manager and a reply date.
Cynefin: Complicated, because judgment is needed to classify what lies outside team control. Step 4 MUST NOT start until the audit in Step 1 is done, because it routes the items Step 1 finds.

5. **Run one meta-retrospective** (a retrospective about the retrospective) with anonymous input, then trial one format change. Blocker removed: fear or a stale format. Stochastic probe, acceptance test first: metric is action completion rate; baseline is the rate from Step 1; threshold is above baseline after three retrospectives; sample size is three retrospectives. Stop condition: revert the format if the threshold is not met. Budget: three retrospectives at one hour each. Cynefin: Complex, because the effect of format and safety shows only after a trial; the leverage estimate is a guess. This probe runs in parallel with Steps 2 and 3, capped at its budget.

## Follow-up

Condition that changes the recommendation: if A1 proves false and no actions were ever created, run Step 5 before Steps 2 and 3, because the cause then sits in facilitation or safety.
