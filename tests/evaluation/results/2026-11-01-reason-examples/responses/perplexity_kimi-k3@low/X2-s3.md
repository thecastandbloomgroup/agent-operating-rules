# Headline

The team should cap each retrospective at three actions, assign each an owner and a deadline, and review them at the next retro before new topics. Attendance without change is the common failure mode: actions lack owners, and systems reward output over improvement work.

1. Unowned actions fail at roughly three times the rate of owned ones [1].
2. A cap of one to three actions prevents overcommitment [2].
3. Reviewing prior actions first makes follow-through visible [3].
4. If management pressure blocks improvement time, fix that blocker before changing retro format [judgment].

# Background

Retrospectives produce discussion but no change in behavior or process. The request is for what the team should do next.

# Relevance

Retros that produce nothing tend to lose attendance trust within a few months. The task itself becomes waste.

# Information

Root causes recur across sources. Theme: missing owners and follow-up, supported by three sources [1][2][3], none disagreeing. Theme: too many actions, supported by two sources [2][3]. A third cause, actions that require authority the team lacks, appears in one source [3]; that one source matters because it names when the fix is escalation, not format.

Alternative considered: replace the retro with a different ceremony or kill it. It loses because the failure is follow-through, not the meeting format [judgment], and a canceled retro removes the only scheduled improvement slot.
