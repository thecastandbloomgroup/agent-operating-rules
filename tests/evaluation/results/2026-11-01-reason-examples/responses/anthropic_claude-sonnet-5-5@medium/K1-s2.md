## Headline

Raise the database pool size from 8 per pod to a value the new connection limit allows, then confirm the fix with a one-pod trial.

- The pool cut from 50 to 8 connections per pod causes most of the delay.
- The shorter cache TTL (time to live, the seconds a cached entry stays valid) adds load, but it is a smaller factor.
- The ORM (object-relational mapper) change does not explain the delay.
- A full rollback is the fallback, not the first move.

## Background

The API p95 latency (the 95th percentile of request time) rose from 120 ms to 900 ms after release 2026.10.21. That release changed the pool size, the cache TTL, and one ORM query. The pool is the set of reusable database connections each pod holds.

## Relevance

The error rate rose from 0.02% to 0.40% [2]. Each failed request returns a 503 after 5 s. The problem continues until the pool is fixed.

## Information

Evidence:

- The p95 of `db.pool.acquire` rose from 3 ms to 690 ms. That is 687 ms of the 780 ms total rise, or 88% [inference].

  Source: traces.txt, summary table [2].

- The query spans did not change. `db.query orders` p95 went from 52 ms to 58 ms.

  Source: traces.txt, summary table [2].

- Of the errors in window B, 96% are `PoolTimeout`.

  Source: traces.txt, error line [2].

- The pool shows 8 of 8 connections in use, with 6 to 14 waiters. Warnings rose from none to between 1,800 and 2,400 per hour.

  Source: logs.txt, 2026-10-22 entries and note [1].

- The diff cuts `max_size` from 50 to 8 and `acquire_timeout_s` from 30 to 5.

  Source: release.diff, `config/db.yaml` [3].

- Peak demand in window A was 31 active connections. Across 4 pods, that is about 8 per pod, so a pool of 8 has no headroom [inference].

  Source: traces.txt, last line [2].

- The cache miss rate rose from 12% to 31% after the TTL fell from 300 s to 60 s. The `cache.get` span stayed at 2 ms.

  Source: release.diff, `config/cache.yaml` [3].

  The cache change adds database load. It adds no latency of its own.

- The `selectinload` change adds no measurable cost. `db.query order_lines` p95 went from 30 ms to 26 ms.

  Source: traces.txt, summary table [2].

Confidence: the pool is the main cause. This is very likely (90 to 100%). Basis: three independent signals agree (span times, error type, connection counts). Nothing in the material disagrees.

Assumption log:

- A1. The connection limit in OPS-412 is at least 64 for the service (16 per pod times 4 pods). Why not measured: the ticket text is not attached. Validate by: read OPS-412. If wrong: set `max_size` to the limit divided by 4, and add a connection pooler if demand exceeds it.
- A2. The trace figure of 8 active connections is per pod. Why not measured: traces.txt does not state the scope. Validate by: count connections per client in the database. If wrong: the pool limit may apply to the whole service, and Step 2 needs a new size.
- A3. Someone cut the TTL to meet a freshness need. Why not measured: the diff gives no message for `config/cache.yaml`. Validate by: ask the release author. If wrong: skip Step 3.

Alternative considered: roll back the whole release. It loses because the old pool of 50 per pod totals 200 connections. That may break the new limit that OPS-412 describes [judgment]. A rollback also undoes the safe ORM change.
