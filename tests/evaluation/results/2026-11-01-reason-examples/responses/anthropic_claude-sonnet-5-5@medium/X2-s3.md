## Headline

Stop treating the retrospective as a talking event and make it a commitment process with an audit of why past actions stalled.

- Past actions probably stall at the handoff from meeting to work, not in the discussion itself [judgment].
- One owned, dated action per retro is easier to finish than a list.
- A format change helps only if you measure it, so test it as a small trial.

## Background

Your team attends retrospectives, but nothing changes afterward. The material gives no data on past action items, team size, or meeting format. The request asks what to do.

## Relevance

A retrospective that produces no change costs team time each cycle and teaches people that speaking up has no effect. The longer it runs, the harder it is to restore trust.

## Information

Evidence:

1. A retrospective exists to produce improvements that the team then carries out.

   Source: the Scrum Guide, Sprint Retrospective section [1].

2. Facilitation guides treat follow-through on agreed actions as part of the retrospective, not an extra.

   Source: Derby and Larsen [2].

3. The cause in your team is unknown. No data was supplied, so the likely causes (no owner, items too large, no time, items outside team control) are [judgment].

4. No themes from team interviews exist yet. The audit in Step 1 produces them.

Assumption log:

A2. The team runs retros on a regular cycle of two weeks or less. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: check the calendar. If wrong: use longer trial windows in Step 4.

Alternative considered: cancel retrospectives for a period. It loses because it removes the only recurring place to raise problems, and it does not address why actions stall [judgment].

## End

I recommend that we take these four actions in this order:

1. **Audit past actions (Confused: the cause of the stall is not visible, so we gather facts first).**
   - Increment: in one session, list every action item from the last three retros. Record owner, size, due date, and status. Tag each stalled item with a cause.
   - Blocker removed: we do not know why nothing shifts.
   - Kind: evaluative. Test, written first:
     - Criteria and weights: record completeness 50%, cause tagged on every stalled item 50%.
     - Pass threshold: 80% total score.
     - Evidence: retro notes, the tracker, and a short check with the facilitator.

2. **Commit to one action per retro (Clear: a known practice exists, because each retro ends with one owner, one date, and one backlog entry).**
   - Increment: update the retro template in one session with fields for owner, due date, and backlog link.
   - Blocker removed: actions with no owner or date.
   - Kind: deterministic. Test, written first:
     - Given a retro has ended
     - When the notes are published
     - Then exactly one action shows an owner, a due date, and a backlog link
   - Step 2 MUST NOT start until Step 1 passes, because the audit decides whether the cause is ownership.

3. **Route items outside team control (Complicated: more than one route fits, and analysis of the Step 1 tags picks one).**
   - Increment: take the tagged items that the team cannot fix. Name one escalation owner for each.
   - Blocker removed: actions that stall because nobody on the team can act.
   - Kind: evaluative. Test, written first:
     - Criteria and weights: a named owner 40%, a stated decision date 30%, a reply received 30%.
     - Pass threshold: 70%.
     - Evidence: messages sent and replies received.
   - Step 3 MUST NOT start until Step 1 passes.

4. **Run a format probe (Complex: the effect on engagement and follow-through shows only after a trial).**
   - Increment: for two retros, discuss one problem in depth instead of many. Start each retro by reviewing the last action.
   - Budget: two 60-minute sessions. Stop if attendance drops or the team asks to stop.
   - Kind: stochastic. Test, written first:
     - Metric: share of committed actions done by the next retro.
     - Baseline: the Step 1 audit result.
     - Pass threshold: baseline plus 30 percentage points.
     - Sample size: two retros. This is too small for a firm conclusion, so repeat for four retros before you adopt the format.
   - Run Step 4 in parallel with Steps 2 and 3. Leverage is a guess [judgment].

If A3 is false, drop Step 4 and put all effort into Step 3.
