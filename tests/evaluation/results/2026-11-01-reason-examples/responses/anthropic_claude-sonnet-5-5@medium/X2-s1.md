## Headline

Make every retrospective end with one owned, scheduled change, and review that change at the next meeting.

- First, find where changes die, because the cause is not yet known.
- Next, give each change an owner, a due date, and a place in the team backlog.
- Then review the last change at the start of each retrospective.
- In parallel, run a small trial of a new meeting format.

## Background

A retrospective is a regular team meeting that reviews how the work went and agrees on changes. Your team attends, but the changes do not happen. The request does not say how many items the meetings produce, who owns them, or where they are tracked.

## Relevance

A retrospective that changes nothing costs team time every cycle. It also teaches people that raising problems has no effect, so they stop raising them.

## Information

**Evidence**

- The Scrum Guide says the team should plan improvements during the retrospective. It treats those improvements as part of the team's work plan, not as side notes. This supports the backlog step.

  Per the Scrum Guide 2020, section "Sprint Retrospective" [1].

- Derby and Larsen describe a retrospective in five phases. The last phase closes with a small number of chosen actions.

  See Agile Retrospectives, 2006 [2].

- Follow-through gaps are the main cause. This is likely (about 70%) [judgment]. No team data supports it yet. Action 1 tests it.

- Cynefin is a framework that sorts work by how cause and effect relate. Each action below carries its Cynefin label.

  See Snowden and Boone, 2007 [3].

**Assumption log**

A1. The team works in sprints (fixed work cycles) and keeps a shared backlog. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask the team lead one question. If wrong: Action 2 uses the team's own tracker instead.

A2. Retrospectives produce action items, but the items are not finished. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: Action 1. If wrong: if no items appear, replace Action 3 with a probe on why people stay silent.

A3. The team can make most changes without outside approval. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: Action 1 records any item that waited on an outside party. If wrong: move those items to a separate list for the manager.

**Alternative considered**

Pause retrospectives for a few cycles. This loses because it removes the only regular forum for change, and no evidence shows that the meeting itself is the fault [judgment].

## End

I recommend that we take these actions in this order.

1. **Audit the last three retrospectives.** This is the Confused domain, because the cause of the stall is not visible yet. The increment is a 30-minute review of the old notes. It removes the blocker of not knowing where changes die. The task is deterministic. Step 2 MUST NOT start until this test passes.

   ```text
   Given the notes from the last three retrospectives
   When we tally each action item as done, dropped, or never started
   Then every item has one status, an owner or "none", and a note if it waited on an outside party
   ```

2. **Attach each change to an owner, a due date, and the backlog.** This is the Clear domain, because the Scrum Guide gives one known practice: plan the improvement as work [1]. The increment is one agreed template and one item entered in the backlog at the next meeting. It removes the blocker of items with no owner. The task is deterministic.

   ```text
   Given a retrospective has ended
   When the team lists its chosen actions
   Then at most two actions exist, and each has a named owner, a due date, and a backlog entry
   ```

3. **Open each retrospective by reviewing the last action.** This is the Clear domain, because the practice is known: check the result before you raise new topics. The increment is a 10-minute agenda slot. It adds the capability of seeing whether change happened. The task is deterministic. Step 3 MUST NOT start before Step 2 produces at least one owned action.

   ```text
   Given the previous retrospective chose an action
   When the new retrospective starts
   Then the first agenda item states that action as done, partly done, or not done, with the reason
   ```

4. **Run a capped trial of a one-problem format, in parallel with Steps 2 and 3.** This is the Complex domain, because the effect of a new format on this team shows only after a trial. Leverage is a guess. The increment is a probe: spend the full meeting on one problem and find its cause. The budget is three retrospectives. The task is stochastic. The metric is the share of chosen actions done by their due date. The baseline comes from Step 1. The pass threshold is 100% of actions done in at least two of the three trials. The sample is three, so the result is low confidence. Stop the trial early if attendance drops or if two retrospectives in a row produce no action.

If A2 is false, replace Step 4 with a probe on why people stay silent.

## Follow-up

**Conditions that change the recommendation**

- If Step 1 shows most items waited on an outside party, escalate those items first and delay Step 4.
- If the team does not use a backlog (A1), track actions in the team's chat or task tool.
