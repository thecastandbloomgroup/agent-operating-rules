## Headline

Replace the usual retrospective with a short review of past actions, then test one small change with a named owner.

- First, find where change stops: no decision, no execution, or no useful effect. [judgment]
- Then, judge the retrospective by what changes afterward, not by attendance. [judgment]

## Background

People attend your retrospectives, but you report that nothing changes. [1] The missing detail is whether the team selects actions, completes them, or checks their effects.

## Relevance

Another discussion will not resolve an unknown break in that chain. [judgment] Therefore, use the next session to locate the break before changing the meeting format.

## Information

The Scrum Guide describes a retrospective’s purpose as improving quality and effectiveness through useful changes. It says teams should address the most helpful changes promptly. [2]

Use three distinct cases:

| What the review finds | What to change |
|---|---|
| No concrete action leaves the meeting | Choose one change, with an owner and a date. |
| An action exists, but nobody completes it | Check capacity, authority, and dependencies before accepting another action. |
| People complete actions, but nothing improves | Define an outcome measure before choosing the next action. |

These are diagnostic categories, not findings about your team. [judgment]

**Facilitation safeguard:** Ask, “What prevented this action?” rather than, “Why didn’t you do it?” This keeps the review focused on constraints. [judgment]

**Assumption log**

A1. The team can test one small change within two weeks. Why not measured: The material does not state it. Validate by: Check authority and capacity when selecting the change. If wrong: Escalate one specific request instead.

**Alternative considered:** Replace the retrospective format with a new exercise. That addresses repetitive discussion, but not necessarily missing authority or execution. [judgment]

**Confidence:** Moderate confidence in this approach, because your report establishes the symptom but not its cause.

## End

I recommend that we take these three steps:

### 1. Trace the last three retrospectives

**Kind:** Deterministic, a task with fixed acceptance criteria.

**Test, before work:**

Given the available retrospective notes,  
When the team reviews each agreed action,  
Then each action has a recorded status: not started, completed without a result check, or completed with a result check.

**Action:** Spend 15 minutes tracing decisions into results. Record “no action selected” separately. If notes are unavailable, reconstruct the most recent session together.

**Blocker removed:** The team stops treating several different problems as one problem. [judgment]

**Domain:** Clear, because the task checks records against fixed categories.

### 2. Write one change the team can execute

Start only after Step 1 passes.

**Kind:** Deterministic.

**Test, before work:**

Given the constraint found in Step 1,  
When the team writes its change,  
Then it names one owner, one due date, one outcome measure, and one review date.

**Action:** Spend 15 minutes writing one change. Confirm that the owner has capacity and authority. Limit the team to one active retrospective action for this trial. [judgment]

For example: “For two weeks, flag blocked work daily; Sam owns the trial; compare blocked-work age before and afterward.”

If the team lacks authority, replace the change with one specific request to the person who has authority.

**Capability added:** The team creates an executable commitment rather than a general intention. [judgment]

**Domain:** Clear, because the action uses a fixed checklist.

### 3. Run a small trial and inspect the result

Start only after Step 2 passes.

**Kind:** Stochastic, meaning the outcome varies with people and circumstances.

**Acceptance test, before work:**

- **Metric:** The outcome measure selected in Step 2.
- **Baseline:** Its value before the trial, using the same measurement method.
- **Pass threshold:** A numerical improvement the team agrees is worth the effort.
- **Sample:** One two-week trial, with a baseline measurement and an end measurement.
- **Decision:** Keep the change only if it meets the threshold without breaking the agreed guardrails.

**Action:** Run the trial for two weeks. Cap extra effort at 30 minutes per person. Stop early if it threatens a delivery or safety commitment.

At the next retrospective, review this result first. Record “keep,” “revise,” or “stop” before selecting another change.

**Capability added:** The team connects discussion to observable results. [judgment]

**Domain:** Complex, because the effect on team behavior becomes visible after the trial. Its expected benefit remains a guess.

One trial supports a local decision, not a strong causal claim. [judgment]

## Follow-up

If the trial reveals that leaders repeatedly block agreed changes, shift the discussion to decision authority rather than meeting technique. [judgment]
