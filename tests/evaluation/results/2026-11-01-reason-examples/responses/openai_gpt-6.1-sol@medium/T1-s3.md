## Headline

Replace repeated SQL style debates with a shared rule: automate format choices, and reserve human review for code quality.

- Give one maintainer authority over the format standard, with a fair path for changes. [judgment]
- Test the policy in one repository before treating it as a team-wide solution. [judgment]

## Background

The team disagrees about SQL format, and those disputes cause friction in every review. [1] The request is to change that pattern, not choose where commas belong. [1]

## Relevance

A formatter alone does not settle who decides or how people challenge decisions. [judgment] Therefore, the change needs both a tool and a working agreement.

## Information

**Treat this as a decision problem, not a personality problem.** That is the best starting point given the reported disputes. [judgment]

The report establishes recurring friction, but it does not establish its cause. [1] Confidence is low that format rules alone will resolve the conflict, because no review examples or team accounts are available.

### Proposed working agreement

> **SQL review agreement**
>
> - The repository’s formatter defines SQL format, including indentation, capitalization, and line breaks.
> - Format preferences do not block a review when the formatter passes.
> - Reviewers still challenge correctness, security, performance, and ease of understanding.
> - Comments identify either a required change with a reason or an optional suggestion.
> - Anyone can propose a format change in a separate issue, with an example and its cost.
> - One named maintainer decides those proposals after a two-working-day comment period.
> - The same rules apply to every contributor, including senior staff.
> - No one must defend a personal preference as the price of merging correct code.

This agreement separates automatic checks from human judgment without banning useful review. [judgment] However, harmful conduct still needs a direct response; a formatter does not address insults or retaliation. [judgment]
