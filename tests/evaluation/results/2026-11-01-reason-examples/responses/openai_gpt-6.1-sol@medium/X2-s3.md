## Background

Your team attends retrospectives, but you report no resulting change. [1] That describes the outcome, but it does not establish the cause.

## Relevance

A new meeting format does not, by itself, fix missing ownership, authority, or time. [judgment] Therefore, inspect what happens after the discussion before changing the discussion itself. [judgment]

## Information

**Use three checks to locate the problem.** [judgment]

| Where progress stops | Evidence to seek | Response |
|---|---|---|
| No change gets chosen | Notes contain concerns but no specific action | Choose one action within the team’s control |
| A chosen change never starts | An action exists, but nobody attempts it | Name an owner and reserve time |
| A change happens without review | Someone tries it, but nobody checks the result | Define success and review the evidence |

Also check whether people can disagree without personal consequences. Do not interpret silence as agreement. [judgment]

For teams using Scrum, the Scrum Guide explicitly connects retrospectives with prompt action on the most useful improvements. [2] This supports a focus on follow-through rather than collecting more suggestions.

### 2. Agree on one small change

**Test first, deterministic:**

Given the record from Step 1,  
When the team selects one change,  
Then record its owner, reserved time, expected result, review date, and required authority.

**Action:** Spend 15 minutes writing that agreement. Choose work the team can complete before the next retrospective.

For example: “For five workdays, reserve ten minutes daily to review blocked work.”

**Blocker removed:** The agreement makes responsibility and the next action explicit. [judgment]

**Domain:** Clear, because each required field has a direct completion check.

Step 2 MUST NOT start until Step 1’s record exists.

### 3. Run a capped trial across two retrospective cycles

**Acceptance test first, stochastic:**

- **Metric:** Chosen changes attempted before their review date, plus the outcome measure agreed in Step 2.
- **Baseline:** The comparable completion rate from Step 1; mark it unknown if records do not support one.
- **Sample:** Two consecutive retrospective cycles, with one change per cycle.
- **Pass threshold:** Attempt and review both changes; at least one must meet its agreed outcome target.
- **Evidence:** Record the attempt, result, and decision to keep, adjust, or stop each change.

**Action:** Run the trial after Step 2 passes. Cap each change at two person-hours unless the team explicitly approves more.

**Capability added:** The team tests whether its retrospective now produces useful action. [judgment]

**Domain:** Complex, because the effect on team behavior becomes visible only after the trial. The benefit remains uncertain.

Stop a change if it causes harm or exceeds its budget. Treat two cycles as a practical signal, not proof. [judgment]
