Headline
Retrospectives stop producing change when the action loop stays open.
- The break is one of three kinds: no action, an undone action, or an action with no effect.
- Name that break before you change the meeting.
- Give one action one owner and one date.
- Check that action before you add another.

Background
The team still holds retrospectives, and people attend. A retrospective is a meeting where the team plans one improvement. The request is what to do when those meetings do not change the work.

Relevance
An open action loop wastes the meeting and trains people to ignore it. Therefore a closed loop this cycle gives the next meeting proof of change.

Information
This section covers the evidence, the assumptions, and the alternative.

Derby and Larsen require a phase called decide what to do [1].
The Scrum Guide says the team addresses the most impactful improvements as soon as possible [2].
Edmondson links learning behavior in teams to psychological safety [3].
Therefore a retrospective without an owned action does not meet that bar. [inference]
Three modes cover the request, and they do not overlap. [judgment]
The three-mode split is likely to cover this team.
Basis: the request states attendance without change, and the modes split that gap. [judgment]

A1. The team already holds retrospectives on a fixed cadence. Why not measured: The material does not state it. Validate by: Ask who schedules the meeting. If wrong: Set a cadence before Step 2.
A2. Notes from the last three retrospectives still exist. Why not measured: The material does not state it. Validate by: Open the last three notes. If wrong: Use memory from the last meeting only.
A3. One owner can finish one small action inside one cycle. Why not measured: The material does not state it. Validate by: Ask the owner for a date inside the cycle. If wrong: Shrink the action until the date fits.

Stop the meetings and use daily talk instead. That alternative loses because the team still needs a set point to inspect and plan one improvement [2].

End
I recommend that we take these four steps.

Step 1. Classify the break. This step is Confused, because the request does not say which break stops change.
Test first. Rubric: actions recorded (weight 40), actions finished (weight 40), finished actions changed the work (weight 20). Pass when the notes show exactly one primary break. Evidence is the last three retrospective notes.
Increment: one working session and one classification page. Blocker removed: an unknown break.
Step 2 MUST NOT start until this test passes.

Step 2. Assign one action. This step is Clear, because one owner and one date is a known practice.
Test first.
Given the classification names a missing or undone action,
When the team leaves the session,
Then the notes show one action, one owner, and one date inside the next cycle.
Increment: one decision in one session. Blocker removed: no owned next step.
Step 3 MUST NOT start until this test passes.

Step 3. Check the action at the next retrospective. This step is Clear, because a check at the start has one known practice.
Test first.
Given the owned action from Step 2,
When the next retrospective starts,
Then the team marks that action done or dropped before any new action is added.
Increment: the first ten minutes of the next retrospective. Blocker removed: actions that never return.

Step 4. Run a safety probe only if Step 1 shows that people do not speak. This step is Complex, because the effect on speech shows only after a trial. Leverage is a guess.
Test first. Metric: share of attendees who speak at least once. Baseline: the last retrospective. Pass threshold: half of attendees speak. Sample size: one meeting. Stop if a person names a personal risk. Budget: 15 minutes.
Increment: one 15 minute check, not a format change. Capability added: a signal on whether people will speak.

Follow-up
If A1 is false, schedule one retrospective before Step 2.
If Step 1 shows finished actions with no effect, replace Step 2 with a measure for that action.
If Step 1 shows silence, do Step 4 before Step 2.
