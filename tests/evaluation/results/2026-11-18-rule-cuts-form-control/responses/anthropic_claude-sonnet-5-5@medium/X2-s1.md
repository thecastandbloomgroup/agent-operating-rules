## Headline

**Fix the follow-through after the meeting, not the meeting itself.**

- First, find out where past action items died, because the cause decides the fix.
- Next, agree on fewer changes, each with one owner, a due date, and a place in the sprint plan.
- Then, review the previous changes at the start of every retrospective.
- Last, test one format change as a small, capped probe.

## Background

A retrospective is a team meeting that reviews how work went and picks improvements. Your team attends, but no change follows. The material does not say how actions are chosen, tracked, or closed.

## Relevance

A meeting that produces no change costs time and teaches people that speaking up has no effect. Attendance then falls and trust drops.

## Information

**Evidence**

- The Scrum Guide says the team identifies the most helpful changes. It says the team may add them to the Sprint Backlog (the list of work for the next sprint) [1].
- A retrospective method from Derby and Larsen closes with a clear plan for action, not only discussion [2].
- Silent loss of action items is the likely cause (70% likely) [judgment]. The material has no data on it.
- Cynefin (a framework that sorts problems by how cause and effect relate) sets the action types below [3].

**Assumption log**

`Assumption: The team raises ideas, but the actions are not completed. Why not measured: The material does not state it. Validate by: Count actions in the last three retrospective notes. If wrong: Replace Step 1 with a format probe, because the problem is idea generation.`

`Assumption: The team works in sprints of fixed length. Why not measured: The material does not state it. Validate by: Ask the team lead. If wrong: Put actions in the main work tracker instead of the sprint backlog.`

`Assumption: Notes from past retrospectives exist. Why not measured: The material does not state it. Validate by: Search the team wiki. If wrong: Start the count in Step 2 and use Step 4 data as the baseline.`

**Alternative considered**

The strongest alternative is to change the format, for example with a rotating facilitator or games. It loses because a new format does not give an action an owner or a due date [judgment]. Step 4 tests it cheaply.

## End

I recommend that we take these four actions in order:

1. **Audit the last three retrospectives (one session, 60 minutes).**
   - Blocker removed: we do not know why actions die.
   - Kind: deterministic. Domain: Complicated. Analysis of past records picks the fix (deduction).
   - Test, written first:
     - Given the notes of the last three retrospectives
     - When we list each action with its owner, due date, and status
     - Then we know the completion rate and one cause for each dropped action

2. **Set a limit of two actions per retrospective, each with one owner and one due date (one session, 15 minutes at the next retrospective).**
   - Blocker removed: too many vague actions.
   - Kind: deterministic. Domain: Clear. Scrum practice already states the rule [1].
   - Step 2 MUST NOT start until the audit in Step 1 is complete.
   - Test, written first:
     - Given a retrospective has ended
     - When we read the sprint backlog
     - Then it holds at most two retrospective actions, each with an owner and a due date

3. **Open each retrospective with a five-minute review of the previous actions (one session, edit the agenda).**
   - Blocker removed: nobody checks whether the last change happened.
   - Kind: deterministic. Domain: Clear. The fix is a known practice.
   - Test, written first:
     - Given a retrospective starts
     - When the facilitator opens the agenda
     - Then each previous action shows as done, dropped, or carried over, with a reason

4. **Run a capped probe on format over three retrospectives (45 minutes each).**
   - Capability added: a measured answer on whether a new format raises completion.
   - Kind: stochastic. Domain: Complex. Cause and effect show only after trials (induction). The pattern could break if blockers sit outside the team. Leverage is a guess.
   - Test, written first:
     - Metric: share of actions completed by the next retrospective.
     - Baseline: the rate from Step 1.
     - Threshold: baseline plus 25 percentage points.
     - Sample size: three retrospectives, about six actions.
   - Stop the probe if the rate stays at or under baseline after two retrospectives.
   - Run Step 4 in parallel with Steps 2 and 3, within its budget.

If the audit shows that most dropped actions need people outside the team, replace Step 4 with a monthly escalation list sent to the owner of that outside work.
