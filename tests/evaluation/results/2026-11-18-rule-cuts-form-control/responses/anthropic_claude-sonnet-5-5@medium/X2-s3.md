## Headline

Retrospectives stall when their output never reaches the team's real work, so the fix is to link each retro to a small, tracked action.

- First, measure what happened to past action items, because the cause is unknown.
- Next, cap each retro at one or two actions, each with an owner, a date, and a backlog ticket.
- Then open each retro by reviewing the last actions.
- Last, run one capped trial of a changed retro format.

## Background

The team holds retrospectives (regular meetings to review how the team works). People attend, but no change follows. The material gives no data on past action items, team size, or meeting format.

## Relevance

A retro without change costs team time and teaches people that speaking up has no effect. Attendance will likely fall next.

## Information

Evidence:

- The Agile principle on reflection requires that the team "tunes and adjusts its behavior" after it reflects. A retro with no change fails this test.

  [1]

- Facilitation guidance treats follow-through on chosen actions as part of the retro design, not an extra.

  [2]

- Likely causes, split so they do not overlap: [judgment]
  1. Weak output: actions are vague or too many.
  2. No capacity: actions do not enter the sprint backlog.
  3. No follow-up: nobody reviews the last actions.
  4. Wrong scope: the issues lie outside the team's control.

- Confidence that causes 2 and 3 drive the stall: medium confidence, based on pattern knowledge and no team data. [judgment]

Assumption log:

- Assumption: the team controls most of the issues it raises. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: tag each past item as "in team control" or not. If wrong: add an escalation step for outside issues.

Alternative considered: stop retros and use ad hoc problem solving. It loses because it removes the only regular reflection point, and the cause is not yet shown to be the meeting itself. [judgment]
