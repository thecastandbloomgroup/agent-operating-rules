## Headline

Find where the action loop breaks, then fix that break with small tests before you change the retrospective format.

- The team has no data yet on what happens to retrospective actions after the meeting.
- The likely causes fall into four groups: unclear choice of actions, no capacity, no follow-up, and no authority.
- Small, capped trials will show which group applies, with low cost.

## Background

A retrospective is a recurring team meeting that reviews the last work period and picks improvements. Your team attends, but no change follows. The material gives no data on past actions, team size, or cadence.

## Relevance

Each retrospective costs the whole team's time. For example, an hour with eight people uses eight person-hours per sprint [judgment]. Without change, the meeting loses trust, and attendance becomes a habit with no value.

## Information

**Evidence**

- The retrospective method ends with a step that decides what to do, so the output is an action, not a discussion [1].
- The Scrum Guide says the team may add top improvements to the next Sprint Backlog (the list of work for the next work period) [2].
- Cause ranking: follow-up and ownership gaps are the most common failure in practice [judgment]. Confidence is low, because the team has given no data. This cause is about as likely as not to be the main one.
- All four cause groups cover the loop from choice to result without overlap:
  - Choice: the action is vague or too large.
  - Capacity: no time is set aside for the action.
  - Follow-up: nobody reviews the action later.
  - Authority: the fix needs people outside the team.
- Only a book and the Scrum Guide support these claims. No team data supports them yet [inference].

## End

I recommend that we take these four steps, in this order.

1. **Audit the last three retrospectives.** Increment: one table of every action item, marked done, open, or dropped. Blocker removed: no data on where the loop breaks. Domain: Confused, because the cause is unknown. This step gathers what is needed to classify the problem. Kind: deterministic. Test, written first:

   ```gherkin
   Given the notes of the last three retrospectives
   When the team marks each action item as done, open, or dropped
   Then a table shows the completion rate and a count for each status
   ```

2. **Score the four cause groups.** Increment: one scored rubric, built in a 30-minute session. Blocker removed: no agreed cause. Domain: Complicated. Deduction from the Step 1 table picks the cause. Kind: evaluative. Rubric, written first:
   - Fit to the audit evidence: weight 40. Evidence: the Step 1 table.
   - The team can fix it alone: weight 30. Evidence: who owned each action.
   - Cheap to test in two retrospectives: weight 30. Evidence: the time cost of a trial.
   - Pass threshold: 70 of 100. Score each cause group. Take the highest score.

3. **Run a follow-up probe.** Increment: at the next retrospective, pick one action only. Give it one owner and one date. Review it first at the following retrospective. Blocker removed: no follow-up. Domain: Complex. Induction from trials. A repeating pattern could break if outside events block the action. Kind: stochastic. Acceptance test, written first:
   - Metric: share of actions done before the next retrospective.
   - Baseline: the Step 1 completion rate.
   - Threshold: at least 80%.
   - Sample: three retrospectives.
   - Budget: 15 extra minutes per retrospective. Stop after three retrospectives.
   - Leverage is a guess. The sample is small, so confidence stays low.

4. **Run a participation probe in parallel with Step 3.** Increment: before each retrospective, every person writes one issue anonymously, and the facilitator role rotates. Blocker removed: few voices raise issues. Domain: Complex. Induction from trials. A pattern could break if the same two people still dominate. Kind: stochastic. Acceptance test, written first:
   - Metric: number of distinct people who raise an issue.
   - Baseline: the count in the Step 1 notes.
   - Threshold: at least 80% of the team.
   - Sample: two retrospectives.
   - Budget: 10 minutes per retrospective.

Steps 3 and 4 MUST NOT start until Step 1 gives the baseline. Step 2 runs in parallel with them. Use the Step 2 result to decide which probe to continue after the stop point.
