## Headline

Name one incident lead now, freeze all outside statements, and verify the claim against records before anyone picks a public position.

- Three groups give conflicting advice, so first stop the noise with one decision owner.
- Next, establish whether the statement is false, because every option depends on that fact.
- Then issue a short holding statement while the facts are checked.
- Last, put the options in one comparison so legal, communications, and the board decide from the same page.

## Background

A journalist published a report that the chief executive officer's (CEO) statement from yesterday (Tuesday, November 17, 2026) was false. Legal, communications, and the board each send you advice. The groups do not agree.

## Relevance

A news story spreads within hours. A second inconsistent statement from the company would add a new story. A fast, aligned, factual response limits that risk. This reasoning is [judgment].

## Information

**Evidence**

- The material gives no facts about the claim, the company, or the disagreement. Any claim about the truth of the statement is therefore unverified.
- The chance that the statement is wholly false is about as likely as not (33 to 66%) [judgment]. Low confidence, because no record has been checked.
- The Cynefin framework [1] says the situation is Chaotic while no one owns decisions. It becomes Complicated once facts are checked and one owner exists.
- A Pugh matrix [2] compares options against a baseline. This suits a dispute where each group values different criteria.

**Assumption log**

- Assumption: the company is not under a legal duty to disclose within hours. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask legal for a yes or no in Step 2. If wrong: move the holding statement in Step 3 earlier and add legal's wording.
- Assumption: the CEO and the journalist rely on documents that the company can reach today. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask the incident lead to list the documents in the first hour. If wrong: Step 2 takes longer, and the holding statement says the review is ongoing.
- Assumption: you hold enough authority to name an incident lead. Why not measured: the user has not answered. Validate by: confirm with the CEO or board chair in one message. If wrong: ask the board chair to name the lead.

**Alternative considered**

Reply to the journalist at once with a firm denial. This loses because a denial before verification risks a second false statement. That risk is [judgment].

## End

I recommend that we take these five actions, in this order.

1. **Name one incident lead and freeze outside statements.** This is the first action because it removes the largest blocker: three groups with no shared owner.
   - Increment: send one message to legal, communications, and the board chair, stating the lead and the freeze. This takes under one hour.
   - Kind: deterministic.
   - Domain: Chaotic. Act first, because no cause and effect is visible while the groups conflict.
   - Test:
     - Given legal, communications, and the board each send different advice
     - When the incident lead and the freeze are announced in one message
     - Then each group confirms in writing, and no statement leaves without the lead's approval

2. **Verify the claim against primary records.** This adds the fact that all options depend on.
   - Increment: the lead collects the documents behind the CEO's statement and the journalist's claim. Legal answers the disclosure question. Target: four hours.
   - Kind: evaluative.
   - Domain: Complicated. Analysis of records decides the answer.
   - Rubric first. Score each criterion from 0 to 10 for evidence strength.

     | Criterion | Weight | Evidence needed |
     |---|---|---|
     | A record contradicts or supports the statement | 40% | Primary document |
     | What the CEO knew when speaking | 30% | Emails, briefing notes |
     | Scope of the error | 20% | Figures, dates |
     | Other claims in the article | 10% | Records for each claim |

   - Pass threshold: a verdict of "accurate", "partly accurate", or "false" needs a weighted score of 7 or higher. Below 7, the verdict is "unverified".
   - Step 2 MUST NOT start until Step 1 passes.

3. **Draft and approve a holding statement.** This reduces the risk of silence while Step 2 runs.
   - Increment: a text of 100 words or fewer. It says the company is reviewing the report and will share findings by a stated time.
   - Kind: deterministic.
   - Domain: Complicated. Established crisis practice offers several good forms, and legal reviews the choice.
   - Test:
     - Given the draft statement
     - When legal and communications review it
     - Then it contains no claim that Step 2 has not verified, has a stated update time, and has the lead's approval
   - Step 3 MUST NOT start until the test in Step 1 passes.

4. **Write a one-page decision memo for the board.** This aligns the groups on one comparison.
   - Increment: a Pugh matrix [2] of four options: correct publicly, clarify publicly, hold position, and wait for more facts. The baseline is "hold position".
   - Kind: evaluative.
   - Domain: Complicated. Analysis of the Step 2 verdict decides the best option.
   - Rubric first. Criteria and weights: legal exposure 35%, trust of customers and staff 35%, disclosure duty 20%, speed 10%. Score each option as better (+1), same (0), or worse (-1) than the baseline.
   - Pass threshold: an option wins if it is not worse than the baseline on disclosure duty and has the highest weighted total.
   - Step 4 MUST NOT start until Step 2 gives a verdict or the four-hour limit passes.

5. **Run a capped probe on how the story spreads.** This tells us whether to escalate faster.
   - Increment: one person checks 20 named news outlets and the staff question inbox at three set times today.
   - Kind: stochastic.
   - Domain: Complex. Spread is visible only afterward. Leverage here is a guess. A safe-to-fail probe fits.
   - Acceptance test first:
     - Metric: number of outlets that repeat the claim.
     - Baseline: the count at publication.
     - Threshold: escalate to a board call if the count doubles within four hours.
     - Sample: 20 outlets, three checks.
   - Stop condition: end the probe after the third check. Budget: one person, 90 minutes total.
   - Run this step in parallel with Steps 2 to 4.

If Step 2 shows that the statement is accurate, replace Steps 3 and 4 with a written request to the outlet for a correction.
