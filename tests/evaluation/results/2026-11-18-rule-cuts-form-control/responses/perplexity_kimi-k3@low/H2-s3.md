# Headline

Designate one decision-owner now, freeze all outbound statements, then verify the journalism with legal before you respond publicly.

- Nobody is aligned, so the first act is to stop independent replies, not to draft one.
- The journalist built a story on a named statement, so fact-check against the specific record before you concede or rebut.
- Legal must gate any retraction or correction, because an uncontrolled public admission creates liability.
- A single spokesperson and a holding statement buy 24 hours without conceding anything.

# Background

A published article disputes the CEO's statement from yesterday. Legal, PR, and the board are each contacting you separately with no shared plan. This is a live crisis: multiple parties can act on their own, and each uncoord­inated message can contradict the others.

# Relevance

The next few hours decide whether this becomes a one-day story or a prolonged reputational and legal problem. The largest risk today is an uncoord­inated public response, not the article itself.

# Information

The article itself is not available in this session, so its claims cannot be assessed here. Patterns from crisis-communication practice apply: organisations that respond early with an unverified position routinely retract within 48 hours, which doubles the coverage [judgment].

Cynefin classification [18]: the current situation is Chaotic. Catch-effect links are not visible: you do not know whether the statement was false, who will react, or whether the journalist has more material. Chaotic situations require action to stabilise before analysis.

Validate by: confirm in your next message to PR and legal. If wrong: route the recommendations through the CEO's office instead.
- Assumption: The CEO's statement exists as a written or recorded artifact. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: retrieve the transcript or press release in Step 2. If wrong: rely on the journalist's quotation and the note-taker's record, with lower confidence.

Alternative considered: an immediate public rebuttal rallying around the CEO. It loses because, with no fact-check complete, a rebuttal is a guess (about as likely as not to be wrong), and a wrong rebuttal converts a press problem into a credibility and possible legal problem [judgment].

# End

I recommend that we:

1. **Install a single command structure in the next hour.** Tell legal, PR, and the board chair that one person (you or the designated crisis lead) holds final sign-off on all external statements, and that nobody speaks to the journalist until Step 2 completes. This removes the core blocker: uncoord­inated action. Domain: Chaotic, because the priority is to stop uncontrolled activity before any analysis.
   - Test (evaluative, rubric first): Criterion 1, every named party confirms the freeze in writing (weight 60). Criterion 2, one spokesperson is named (weight 40). Pass at 100%. Evidence: written confirmations.

2. **Fact-check the CEO's statement line by line within one working session.** Pull the exact statement, compare each claim in the article against primary records (filings, transcripts, internal data), and have legal mark each claim as true, false, or unsure. This adds the capability to respond on facts, not guesses. Domain: Complicated, because the truth is knowable through analysis; deduction from records applies.
   - Test (deterministic):
     - Given the article and the CEO's statement
     - When each disputed claim is matched to a primary record
     - Then every claim carries a legal-verified status of true, false, or unsure

3. **Issue a 2-sentence holding statement within 2 hours, only if press is already asking for comment.** State that the company is reviewing the facts and will respond by a named time. This removes the blocker of a vacuum that invites worse coverage. Domain: Clear, because a holding statement is standard crisis practice and dependable. Step 3 MUST NOT start until Step 1 passes.
   - Test (deterministic):
     - Given an incoming press inquiry before fact-check completes
     - When the holding statement is sent
     - Then it contains no factual claim beyond the review and the response deadline

4. **Pick the response path by the fact-check result.** If the CEO was wrong, legal drafts a precise correction; if the article is wrong, supply the cited records and request a correction in writing; if mixed, address each part separately. Domain: Complex, because outcomes depend on the journalist's and market's reaction and are visible only afterward. Cap the probe: release the response once, monitor for 48 hours, and do not escalate without new evidence. Step 4 MUST NOT start until Step 2 passes.
   - Test (stochastic): Metric: share of follow-up coverage using the company's framing. Baseline: 0% (pre-response). Pass: more than 50% within 48 hours across the first 10 verified follow-up reports, reviewed in one sitting.
