## Headline

Retrospectives fail when they produce discussion instead of tracked, owned, small changes, so the fix is structural, not motivational. First, cap each retrospective at one committed change with a named owner. Second, review the prior change at the start of the next session. Third, run a small experiment on the format itself, because low energy often signals a stale format [judgment].

## Background

Your team holds retrospectives, and attendance is fine, but outcomes do not change. This request asks for concrete steps to restore the improvement loop.

## Relevance

A retrospective that produces no change costs the team one to two hours per cycle [judgment]. Worse, repeated inaction teaches people that speaking up has no effect, which reduces future honesty [1].

## Information

Common failure modes, grouped as themes from the agile literature:

1. **No follow-through**: action items lack owners or never get reviewed. Two sources support this theme [1][2]. No source found that disagrees.
2. **Too many actions**: teams pick five or more items and finish none. One source supports this [1]. This is a judgment call in your case, not yet measured.
3. **Same format every time**: familiarity lowers engagement and surfacing of hard topics. One source supports this [1].
4. **Topics outside team control**: discussion centers on complaints the team cannot fix, so nothing actionable remains [judgment].

Assumptions made:

- `Assumption: The team runs a standard sprint retrospective every one to four weeks. Why not measured: The material does not state it. Validate by: Ask the facilitator for the cadence. If wrong: Adjust the review rhythm in Step 2 to the actual cadence.`
- `Assumption: Action items are not tracked between sessions. Why not measured: The material does not state it. Validate by: Check the last three retrospectives for written items and their status. If wrong: Drop Step 1 and focus on action sizing instead.`
- `Assumption: The team has authority to change its own working agreements. Why not measured: The material does not state it. Validate by: Ask whether the team controls its process. If wrong: Replace Step 3 with an escalation to whoever holds that authority.`

Alternative considered: replace retrospectives with continuous one-on-one feedback. This loses because it removes the shared team view, and the literature treats the structured group session as the core mechanism for team-level change [1].

Confidence: medium to high. The themes have consistent support in the literature, but which theme dominates on this team is unmeasured.
