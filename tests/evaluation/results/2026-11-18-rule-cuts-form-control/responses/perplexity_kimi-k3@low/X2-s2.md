## Headline

Retrospectives fail when actions lack owners and follow-up; cap actions at one per retro and review completion first each meeting. Support points:

1. Actions compete with delivery work, so volunteers absorb them and drop them [judgment].
2. Follow-up makes completion visible, which gives the meeting purpose [1].
3. Silence can signal low safety, which hides the real causes [1].

## Background

Your team holds retrospectives and people attend, but the same problems return. Nothing changes between meetings. This pattern has known causes and a known fix [1].

## Relevance

A retro without outcomes teaches the team that speaking up wastes time. Fix the action pipeline now, before attendance also drops.

## Information

Common causes, from literature and practice:

- Too many actions per meeting. Teams pick five items and finish none [1].
- No named owner. Everyone owns it, so no one owns it [judgment].
- No review step. The next retro starts fresh, so stale actions vanish [1].
- Unsafe room. People skip the real topic and propose trivial actions [1].

Assumption log:

- Assumption: The retro already produces action items. Why not measured: The material does not state it. Validate by: Read the last three retro notes. If wrong: The cause is facilitation, not follow-through, so add a facilitator action.
- Assumption: Actions compete with sprint work that has deadlines. Why not measured: Measuring costs more than the decision is worth. Validate by: Ask one team member. If wrong: Severity is lower, but the fix still applies.
- Assumption: The team has ten or fewer members and meets weekly or biweekly. Why not measured: The material does not state it. Validate by: Count attendees at the next retro. If wrong: Larger teams need breakout facilitation, per [1].

**Alternative considered**: Replace retros with a different forum. It loses because the flaw is the follow-up loop, not the meeting format; a new forum inherits it [judgment].

## End

I recommend that we:

1. **Review prior actions first at every retro** (Clear domain; the rule and its effect are known, so deduction applies). Increment: add a five-minute standing agenda item. Blocker removed: actions disappear without review.

   *Deterministic test:*

5. **Run a safety probe if actions stay trivial** (Complex domain; cause and effect are visible only afterward, so use a probe). Increment: one anonymous pre-retro survey, capped at one retro cycle, budget one hour. Stop when two participants decline to answer. Blocker removed: hidden causes stay unspoken.
   Step 5 runs only if the metric in Step 4 stays below 80% after two retros.

If the first assumption is wrong, add a facilitator before Step 1, per the format guidance in [1].
