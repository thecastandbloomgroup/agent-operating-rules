Yes. Turn on gzip for JSON responses of 400 KB, and prefer brotli when `Accept-Encoding` includes `br`.

Use gzip level 5 or 6 for compatibility, or brotli level 4 to 5 for smaller output. Set a minimum size near 1 KB to 5 KB. Do not compress already compressed content.

A repetitive 400 KB JSON body is likely to fall near 40 KB to 160 KB, a 5x to 10x reduction [judgment]. At 100 Mbps, 400 KB needs about 32 ms of transfer time before overhead. Compression cuts transfer time but adds CPU and can raise tail latency.

Keep `Vary: Accept-Encoding` for caches. Send `Content-Encoding: gzip` or `br`. Stream large bodies when memory matters.

Check BREACH-style risk only if secrets are mixed with attacker-controlled reflected input and compression. Most authenticated JSON APIs have lower risk than cookie-based HTML pages.

Measure before rollout: baseline p50/p95 latency, response bytes, CPU per request, and error rate. After one day, keep encryption on only if median bytes fall at least 50%, p95 latency does not rise, and CPU stays within budget.
