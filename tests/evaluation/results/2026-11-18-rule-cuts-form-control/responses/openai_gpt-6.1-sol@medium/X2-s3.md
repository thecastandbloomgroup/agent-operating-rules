## Headline

Replace open-ended discussion with one small, owned change, then check what happens before choosing another. [judgment]

- First, find where past actions stopped: selection, execution, or review. [judgment]
- Then, test one change within the team’s control, with an owner and a review date. [judgment]

## Background

People attend your retrospectives, but you report no resulting change. [1] The cause remains untested: the team needs to inspect what happens after the discussion. [judgment]

## Relevance

A new meeting format does not address an action that lacks authority, time, or follow-through. [judgment] Therefore, inspect the path from discussion to action before changing the format. [judgment]

## Information

The *Scrum Guide* says retrospectives should identify useful changes and address the most useful ones promptly. [2] That supports a focus on action, but it does not establish why your team has stalled.

Use three distinct failure points to inspect recent actions:

- **Selection:** Did the team choose a specific change it could influence?
- **Execution:** Did someone have ownership, time, and authority to act?
- **Review:** Did the team check the result and decide whether to continue?

This is a diagnostic framework, not a finding about your team. [judgment] Confidence in any specific cause is low because the available evidence describes the outcome, not the action history.

**Assumption:** The team can authorize one small work-process change. **Why not measured:** The material does not state it. **Validate by:** Check authority when selecting the change. **If wrong:** Seek a sponsor’s decision instead of assigning another team action.

**Alternative considered:** Change the facilitator or retrospective format first. This loses because better discussion alone does not repair a broken action process. [judgment]

## End

I recommend that we take these three steps, in order. The limits below are proposed experiment settings, not research-derived thresholds. [judgment]

### 1. Trace three recent actions

**Test first, deterministic:**

```gherkin
Given three recent retrospective actions, or all available if fewer
When the team checks their history
Then each has a recorded owner, status, obstacle, and review result
```

Spend 15 minutes completing that record. If no actions exist, record “no action selected” rather than inventing a history.

This removes uncertainty about where follow-through stops. Describe observable facts, not personal failings: “No time allocated,” rather than “People do not care.”

**Domain: Complicated.** Analysis of the action history distinguishes selection, execution, and review failures.

### 2. Write one change agreement

Step 2 MUST NOT start until Step 1’s record is complete.

**Test first, deterministic:**

```gherkin
Given the recorded obstacle
When the team selects one change
Then its agreement names an owner, authority, work time, measure, and review date
```

Use 15 minutes of the next retrospective to complete this card:

> **Problem:** What specific event should happen less often?  
> **Change:** What will the team do differently?  
> **Owner:** Who coordinates the change?  
> **Capacity:** What work time is reserved, and what gets reduced?  
> **Measure:** What observation would support continuation?  
> **Review date:** When will the team decide?

This replaces a broad intention with an executable agreement. Choose an action within the team’s authority; otherwise, name the decision needed from a sponsor.

**Domain: Clear.** Deduction from the checklist establishes whether the agreement contains each required field.

### 3. Run a bounded trial and review it first

Step 3 MUST NOT start until Step 2’s agreement passes its test.

**Acceptance test first, stochastic:** The result varies with team behavior.

- **Metric:** Planned uses of the change completed, with an outcome recorded.
- **Baseline:** Document the current practice before the trial.
- **Sample:** Two planned opportunities to use the change.
- **Pass threshold:** Complete both opportunities and record both outcomes.
- **Benefit decision:** Continue only if the card’s agreed measure supports continuation.

Run this as a small probe. Cap the effort at one team-hour, excluding normal work. Stop if the change creates harm or exceeds that budget.

At the next retrospective, review this trial before collecting new topics. Decide: continue, adjust, or stop. Do not describe two observations as proof of lasting benefit.

This adds a feedback step rather than another promise.

**Domain: Complex.** Learn through trials; workload changes or staff absence can break the observed pattern. Leverage remains a guess. [judgment]

## Follow-up
