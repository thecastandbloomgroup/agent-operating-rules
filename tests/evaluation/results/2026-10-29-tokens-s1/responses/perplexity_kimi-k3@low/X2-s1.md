## Headline

Fix the follow-through loop, not the meeting format.

- Audit the last three retros to find which failure mode applies.
- Cap each retro at two action items, each with an owner and a done test.
- Open every retro with a status review, and escalate items beyond team control.
- Probe safety with one anonymous retro only if the audit shows silence.

## Background

The team holds retros, people attend, and the same problems return. A retro (retrospective) is the team's regular improvement meeting. The request does not state whether its action items are written, completed, or effective. So the fix depends on that pattern.

## Relevance

A seven-person team spends about 10 person-hours on a 90-minute retro each sprint [judgment]. A retro that changes nothing wastes those hours, so it also erodes trust in every other improvement effort [judgment].

## Information

Evidence theme: action items die without an owner, a small scope, or a closing review. Derby and Larsen support this theme [1]. Kerth supports it too [2]. No source was found that disagrees.

Derby and Larsen advise one or two action items per retro and a review of outcomes at the next one [1].

Kerth links candid discussion to safety through his prime directive: everyone did their best given what they knew [2].

Practitioner experience ranks broken follow-through as the most common retro failure mode [judgment]. Confidence: likely (66 to 100%); basis is two secondary sources.

Cynefin is a framework that classifies work by how cause and effect relate [3]. The steps below name their domain.

Assumption log:

- A1. Action items are written but not completed, or completed without effect. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: the Step 1 audit, one 30-minute session. If wrong: replace Step 2 with a root-cause analysis (5 Whys) on one recurring topic.
- A2. The team controls its own process inside the sprint. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: one question to the manager. If wrong: Step 4 becomes the primary action.
- A3. Minimal safety exists, so at least some real topics surface. Why not measured: no tool can reach it from here. Validate by: count distinct speakers in the last retro notes. If wrong: Step 5 runs before Step 2.

Alternative considered: change the format, for example new exercises or a new facilitator. It loses because boredom with the format explains low energy, not unchanged outcomes [judgment]. Without owners and review, a new format produces the same result [judgment].

## End

I recommend that we run five steps in this order. Step 1 diagnoses the failure mode. Steps 2 to 4 follow by leverage. Step 5 runs only as a triggered probe.

1. Audit the last three retros (30 minutes, one session). Cynefin domain: Confused, that is, the cause is not yet known. It gathers the facts that classify the problem.
   Test first (deterministic; Gherkin, a Given-When-Then format):
   Given the notes of the last three retros,
   When the team lists each action item with owner and outcome,
   Then each item shows one status: done, not done, or effect observed.
   Blocker removed: the failure mode is unknown today.

2. Cap the next retro at two action items (decided inside that one retro). Cynefin domain: Clear. A best practice exists [1].
   Test first (deterministic):
   Given the retro has ended,
   When the improvement list is final,
   Then at most two action items exist.
   Each has a named owner, a date inside the sprint, and a done check.
   Blocker removed: too many vague items compete for attention.

3. Open every retro with a 10-minute review of the previous action items. Cynefin domain: Clear.
   Test first (deterministic):
   Given the retro has started,
   When the agenda runs,
   Then the first 10 minutes cover the status of the previous items.
   The completion rate goes on the team board.
   Blocker removed: the loop never closes, so old items fade. Step 3 starts at the first retro after Step 2.

4. Create an escalation lane for items outside team control (one 30-minute session with the manager). Cynefin domain: Complicated. It needs management agreement.
   Test first (deterministic):
   Given an item sits outside team control,
   When the retro ends,
   Then the item appears on a shared escalation list.
   The list shows a management owner and a review date.
   Blocker removed: systemic issues have no path out.

5. Run one anonymous retro as a safety probe (one retro, budget one sprint). Cynefin domain: Complex. It is a safe-to-fail probe.
   Acceptance test first (stochastic): metric is the share of the team that submits at least one anonymous topic. Baseline: distinct topic-raisers in the last retro. Pass threshold: 70% or more. Sample size: the full team, one retro. Stop condition: participation stays at baseline; then escalate the safety concern to the manager.
   Blocker removed: silence hides the real topics. Start Step 5 only when the Step 1 audit shows fewer than half the team raising topics.

## Follow-up

Conditions that change the recommendation:

- If A1 is wrong, replace Step 2 with a 5 Whys session on one recurring topic.
- If A2 is wrong, run Step 4 first.
- If A3 is wrong, run Step 5 before Step 2.
