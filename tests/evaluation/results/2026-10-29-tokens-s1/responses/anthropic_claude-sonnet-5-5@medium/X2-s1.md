## Headline

Fix follow-through first: retrospectives stop changing things when actions have no owner, no date, and no review.

- The cause is likely follow-through, not a stale meeting format (70% likely, [judgment]).
- First, count what happened to past actions, so you know which cause applies.
- Next, limit each retrospective to one action with one owner and one date.
- Last, test a format change as a small side probe.

## Background

Your team holds retrospectives (regular meetings to review how work went and agree changes). People attend, but nothing changes. You ask what to do.

## Relevance

A retrospective that changes nothing costs team time every cycle. It also teaches people that raising problems has no result, so they stop raising them.

## Information

**Evidence**

The Agile Manifesto says a team reflects at regular intervals, then "tunes and adjusts its behavior". A meeting without change fails this purpose.

Agile Manifesto principles, principle 12 [1]

Derby and Larsen describe a retrospective in stages. The last stages decide what to do and close the meeting. Those decisions need follow-up.

Derby and Larsen, 2006 [2]

Three causes cover all cases (MECE):
1. The team chooses no actions.
2. The team chooses actions, but nobody finishes them.
3. The team finishes actions, but they have no effect.

Cause 2 is the most common case in my experience of team practice [judgment]. Your report ("nothing shifts") fits it best.

**Assumption log**

A1. The team works in a regular sprint cycle and keeps retrospective notes. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask the facilitator for the last three sets of notes. If wrong: Step 1 needs a short survey in place of notes.

A2. The team produces action items in retrospectives. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: Step 1. If wrong: replace Step 2 (see Follow-up).

A3. The team controls most of the problems it raises. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: in Step 1, mark each item as inside or outside team control. If wrong: add an escalation path to a manager.

**Alternative considered**

The strongest alternative is to change the meeting format first (new questions, new games). It loses because a new format does not fix missing owners or dates [judgment]. It stays as a capped probe in Step 4.

## End

I recommend that we take these four steps in order.

1. **Audit the last three retrospectives.** Increment: one 60-minute session. Blocker removed: we do not know which of the three causes applies. Kind: deterministic. Cynefin: Confused, so this step gathers what we need to classify the work.
   - Given the notes of the last three retrospectives
   - When we list each action with its owner and status
   - Then each action shows one label: "not chosen", "not done", or "done, no effect"

2. **Set one action per retrospective, with one owner and one date.** Increment: one retrospective. Blocker removed: actions have no owner. Kind: deterministic. Cynefin: Complicated; the audit in Step 1 picks the practice. Step 2 MUST NOT start until Step 1 is complete.
   - Given a retrospective ends
   - When the team picks an action
   - Then the action has one owner, one due date, and one ticket in the sprint backlog

3. **Open each retrospective with a status check on the last action.** Increment: one agenda change. Blocker removed: nobody reviews past actions. Kind: deterministic. Cynefin: Clear. Step 3 MUST NOT start until the test in Step 2 passes.
   - Given a retrospective starts
   - When the facilitator opens the meeting
   - Then the team reports the last action as done, not done, or dropped before any new topic

4. **Run a format probe in parallel with Steps 2 and 3.** Increment: change 15 minutes of each retrospective, for three retrospectives. Capability added: evidence on whether format matters. Kind: stochastic. Cynefin: Complex, so this is a safe-to-fail probe. The leverage is a guess.
   - Metric: share of members who rate "this retrospective leads to a change I will see" as 4 or 5 on a 5-point scale.
   - Baseline: the share in the first retrospective, held in the old format.
   - Pass threshold: 20 percentage points above baseline.
   - Sample size: all members, in three retrospectives.
   - Budget: 15 minutes per retrospective.
   - Stop condition: stop if the share falls below baseline twice.

## Follow-up

Conditions that change the recommendation:
- If A2 is false, replace Step 2 with a step that forces a choice: the team votes on one problem before the meeting ends.
- If A3 is false, add a step that sends out-of-control items to a manager with a reply date.
- If the audit shows "done, no effect" for most items, replace Step 2 with a review of how the team picks problems.
