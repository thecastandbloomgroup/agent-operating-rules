## Headline

Treat the retrospective as a closed loop: produce one owned action per meeting, and review that action at the start of the next meeting. Three supporting points:

1. Retros fail when the actions have no owner, no date, and no review, so the same issues repeat [1], [2].
2. A visible action log and a fixed agenda slot for the last action turn discussion into tracked change.
3. A completion-rate measure over the next three meetings proves whether the loop works.

## Background

The team runs retrospectives. People attend, but no process or behavior changes after the meetings. The request asks for a way to make the meetings produce change.

## Relevance

Each empty retrospective costs a full meeting slot and lets known problems accumulate. After three or four such meetings, people stop expecting change, and they disengage [1].

## Information

**Evidence.** A baseline comparison: the current action-implementation rate is 0 actions per retro [inference from the request]. The target is a rate the team can measure. Two themes emerge from the practice literature, and both describe what disappears when a retro ends:

- Theme 1: actions without an owner and a check date do not get done, and teams that review past actions at the next retro close more of them. Sources that support it: 2 [1], [2]. Sources that disagree: none found in session.
- Theme 2: candor depends on safety; members who expect blame stay quiet, so the retro discusses symptoms, not causes. Sources that support it: 2 [3], [4]. Sources that disagree: none found in session.

**Assumption log.**

- A1. The team has authority to change its own working practices. Why not measured: the request does not state it. Validate by: one question to the team lead. If wrong: escalate blocked actions to management inside the retro instead.
- A2. Attendance includes real candor; silence is not the hidden blocker. Why not measured: the request does not say why items stall. Validate by: a two-question anonymous survey. If wrong: run the safety probe in Step 3 first, before the output contract.

**Alternative considered.** Cancel retros and replace them with an anonymous survey. It loses because a survey cannot reveal root causes through follow-up questions, and the practice literature ties change to the team inspecting its own work together [1]. [judgment]

## End

I recommend that we rebuild the retro as a three-part cycle, with the contract first and the probe alongside.

1. **Agree the output rule.** One action per meeting, one named owner, one date before the next retro. This removes the "no accountability" blocker. Test: deterministic, write the test before the meeting format changes.

   Given a retro has ended
   When the facilitator closes the meeting
   Then the action log shows one new entry with an owner name and a date

   Domain: Clear, because the rule is a known practice, applied by deduction from the literature [1].

2. **Add the action review.** Open every retro by reviewing the last action: done, moved with a new date, or escalated. This makes the loop visible and removes the "issue repeats" blocker. Test: deterministic, same Gherkin check, extended to review the oldest open entry first.

   Given a retro has started
   When the facilitator opens the agenda
   Then the first item is the previous action, marked done, moved, or escalated

   Domain: Clear, deduction from the same known practice [1]. Step 2 does not start the first meeting until Step 1 is written down.

3. **Run one deep-dive probe.** Pick the most repeated issue, count its occurrences over the past two sprints, and run a 90-minute root-cause session on that issue alone. Measure: the session names one root cause the team controls. Stop condition: if the session names no controllable change, stop and escalate to management. Budget: 90 minutes, one meeting. Domain: Complex, because root causes are visible only after the discussion; a discovery based on induction, capped by the time budget. Run Step 3 in parallel with Steps 1 and 2, per A2 and per the probe rules.

4. **Measure the completion rate.** After Step 2 has run three times, compare the number of actions completed against the baseline of 0 per retro [inference]. Accept the new format when at least 2 of the first 3 actions are done on their dates. Sample size: 3 actions. Test: stochastic, acceptance test with the metric, baseline, threshold, and sample size stated above. Domain: Clear, deduction from the data after collection. Step 4 starts only after the third retro with the new format.

## Follow-up

**Conditions that change the recommendation.** If A1 is false, Step 3 becomes the first action and its escalation rule replaces the output contract. If A2 is false, run a blameless-format retro and re-test candor before the completion measure. If the completion rate stays below the threshold after three meetings, the problem is upstream of the retro, and the next investigation targets planning and follow-through, not the meeting.

**Open questions.** None; A1 and A2 cover the material gaps.
