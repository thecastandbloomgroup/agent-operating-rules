# Headline

Stabilise communications first, verify the facts second, then release one aligned statement from a single channel.

1. Freeze all external statements until the facts are checked, and route messaging through one coordinator (see End, Step 1).
2. Verify the journalist's claim against primary records before any public response (Step 2).
3. Have legal assess exposure based on those verified facts, not on reports (Step 3).
4. Issue a single aligned statement to the board, staff, and media from one accountable owner (Step 4).

## Background

The request: a journalist has published that a statement by the CEO yesterday was false. Legal, PR, and the board are each messaging separately, and nobody is aligned. The need is a sequence of next steps that removes the misalignment without adding a second public error.

## Relevance

Uncoordinated responses within the first hours of a dispute compound the harm, because each channel commits the company to wording that the others contradict [judgment, based on crisis practice themes in 18]. The sequence below gives each function a role and one owner.

## Information

**Evidence.** Cognitive-load and message-consistency research shows that audiences judge a source by consistency across channels; mixed messages lower trust in each channel [18]. Crisis practice guidance supports acting to stabilise first, with small, measured probes when cause and effect are unclear [18]. No document in this conversation provides the facts of the CEO statement.

**Assumption log.**

- A1. No verified answer exists yet on whether the CEO statement was false. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: the record check in Step 2. If wrong: skip Step 2 and move legal analysis forward.
- A2. The company faces formal disclosure duties, so a public false statement carries legal exposure beyond reputation. Why not measured: the material does not state the company type. Validate by: one question to counsel (see Follow-up). If wrong: Step 3 covers reputation only and is faster.
- A3. Legal, PR, and board members are acting within hours without a shared record. Why not measured: the user reports misalignment, and measuring further costs more than the decision is worth. Validate by: confirmation that the Step 1 coordinator has named an owner per function. If wrong: Step 1 shortens to a single call.

**Alternative considered.** Immediate public rebuttal of the journalist: it loses because the facts are not verified, so a rebuttal risks a second false statement on top of the first [judgment].

## End

I recommend that we run four actions in this order.

**Step 1: Stabilise communications.** (Chaotic; cause and effect are not yet visible, so the action stabilises first, per [18].)
- Increment: within 30 minutes, name one coordinator and one accountable owner per function (legal, PR, board). Issue a freeze: nobody sends external wording until it routes through the owner.
- Blocker removed: ends the parallel, contradicting messages.
- Kind and test (deterministic; test written first, in Gherkin form [14]):
  - Given the freeze notice with named owners
  - When a team member drafts external wording for any channel
  - Then the draft reaches the single coordinator for sign-off before sending
- Reason for the domain: no rule exists for this dispute yet, so the action is stabilisation, not analysis [18].

**Step 2: Verify the claim.** (Confused; the output decides the domain and wording of every later step, so it comes next [18].)
- Increment: one working session, one person who is not the CEO compares the journalist's published claim with primary records: the verbatim transcript or recording of the statement, the source data it described, and the journalist's quote.
- Blocker removed: replaces competing opinions with one checked answer.
- Kind and test (evaluative; rubric first, Kepner-Tregoe layout [15]):
  - Criteria and weights: match between the statement and the primary record, 50%; match between the journalist's quote and the transcript, 30%; timeline consistency of dates and numbers, 20%.
  - Evidence needed: the transcript itself, the source data itself, and the published article itself.
  - Pass threshold: two independent records agree, and the result is stated as true, false, or mixed with the evidence attached [15].
- Reason for the domain: this is interpretation, not a known rule, so a rubric governs the judgment.
- Dependency: Step 3 MUST NOT start until this test produces a stated result.

**Step 3: Assess legal exposure.** (Complicated; analysis by an expert picks the response, so the action names the analysis [18].)
- Increment: one working session, same day as Step 2. Counsel scores exposure categories with the Step 2 result as input.
- Blocker removed: tells the board what is at stake before any public wording is chosen.
- Kind and test (evaluative; rubric first, Kepner-Tregoe layout [15]):
  - Criteria and weights: exposure under disclosure rules if A2 holds, 40%; accuracy of the Step 2 record under legal review, 30%; risk of precedent from silence or from correction, 30%.
  - Evidence needed: the Step 2 written result and the applicable rules counsel identifies.
  - Pass threshold: a written "go" or "amend" verdict with reasons, signed by counsel.
- Reason for the domain: a known body of rules exists, so expertise and deduction apply [18].

**Step 4: Release one aligned statement.** (Complicated; the wording choice follows the Step 2 and Step 3 analysis [18].)
- Increment: one working session. Draft three variants from the Step 2 result: a correction with the accurate facts, a mixed-facts clarification, or an evidence-based rebuttal. The coordinator selects one, the board endorses it, and the named owner sends it to media, staff, and the board at the same time.
- Blocker removed: ends the misalignment with one message and one owner.
- Kind and test (evaluative; rubric first, Pugh matrix across the three variants [16]):
  - Criteria and weights: consistency with the Step 2 record, 40%; consistency with the Step 3 verdict, 30%; clarity for a reader with no prior context, 20%; one-ownership of the channel, 10%.
  - Pass threshold: the chosen variant meets all four criteria, confirmed by legal sign-off.
- Reason for the domain: the choice depends on the prior analyses, so it ranks and depends, not guesses [18].

Confidence in this sequence: high confidence that Steps 1 and 2 are correct regardless of outcome, because they follow from the reported misalignment itself and from the stabilise-first rule for unclear cause and effect [18]. Confidence in the wording of Step 4 depends on the Step 2 result, stated in A1.

## Follow-up

Open questions:

1. Is the company publicly listed, and which disclosure rules apply? This sets the weight of Step 3.
2. Does a verbatim record of the CEO statement exist? Without one, Step 2 needs a stated fallback method.
3. Has the journalist published further claims since? This changes the Step 4 media option.

Conditions that change the recommendation (see A1 and A2): if Step 2 proves the journalist's report false with records attached, replace the Step 4 correction variant with the rebuttal variant within one news cycle. If counsel finds disclosure exposure, the Step 4 variant goes to disclosure counsel before release.
