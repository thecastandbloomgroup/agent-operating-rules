## Headline

Name one decision owner and verify the facts claim by claim before you answer the journalist.

- Control: one owner and one holding line stop the conflicting messages.
- Facts: a claim-by-claim check shows what, if anything, is false.
- Alignment: Legal, public relations (PR), and the board need one shared fact sheet.
- Response: choose to correct, defend, or clarify only after the check.

## Background

A journalist published that the chief executive officer's (CEO's) statement from yesterday was false. Legal, PR, and the board each send you separate guidance. The group has no shared view of the facts or of the owner.

## Relevance

Each conflicting message raises the chance of a second inaccurate statement. Therefore the next hours matter more than the final wording.

## Information

Evidence:

- A second statement that the facts later contradict costs more than a short delay. Confidence: likely (66 to 100%) [judgment].
- Fast action before the facts are known raises the risk of that second error [inference].
- The Cynefin framework sorts the actions below by how well cause and effect are known.

[1]

Assumption log:

A1. You have authority to convene Legal, PR, and the board chair. Why not measured: the material does not state your role. Validate by: ask the board chair to confirm in one message. If wrong: Step 1 moves to the person with that authority.

A2. The claim is not yet verified as true or false. Why not measured: the material does not include the article or the statement. Validate by: read both side by side (Step 3). If wrong: if the claim is true, skip the defend option in Step 4.

A3. The company has no stock-exchange disclosure duty today. Why not measured: the material does not state the listing status. Validate by: ask Legal one yes/no question. If wrong: add disclosure counsel to Step 1 and Step 4.

Alternative considered: issue a firm denial now. It loses because the facts are unverified (A2). A denial that proves wrong makes the story larger [judgment].

## End

I recommend that we take these four actions in order:

1. **Name one decision owner and set a hold rule (next 30 minutes).**
   - Increment: send a one-page note naming the owner, with the rule that no one speaks externally without the owner's approval.
   - Blocker removed: conflicting messages from three groups.
   - Kind: deterministic. Test, written first [2]:
     - Given Legal, PR, and the board chair hold the same note
     - When any of them receives a request for comment
     - Then the request reaches the owner within 15 minutes
   - Domain: Clear. Deduction from a known rule: crises need one voice.

2. **Approve a holding line (next 2 hours).**
   - Increment: write two sentences that say the company is reviewing the report and will update at a stated time.
   - Blocker removed: silence, which invites speculation.
   - Kind: deterministic. Test, written first:
     - Given the owner and Legal approved the holding line
     - When the journalist or staff ask for comment
     - Then they receive only that line and the update time
   - Domain: Clear. Deduction from a known rule: a holding line makes no factual claim.
   - Step 2 MUST NOT start until the test in Step 1 passes.

3. **Check the statement against the article, claim by claim (same session as Step 2).**
   - Increment: one table with each claim, the evidence for it, and a verdict.
   - Blocker removed: no shared facts.
   - Kind: evaluative. Rubric, written first:
     - Criteria and weights: source document exists (40%), wording matches the claim (30%), date of evidence fits (15%), Legal review done (15%).
     - Pass threshold: every claim scores 3 or more out of 5 on a stated document.
     - Evidence needed: transcripts, emails, data, and the CEO's notes.
   - Domain: Complicated. Deduction by analysis of records that exist.

4. **Decide the response and brief the board (after Step 3).**
   - Increment: one Pugh matrix comparing correct, defend, and clarify against the baseline "hold".
   - Blocker removed: no agreed path.
   - Kind: evaluative. Rubric, written first:
     - Criteria and weights: factual accuracy (40%), legal exposure (30%), trust (20%), speed (10%).
     - Pass threshold: the chosen option beats the baseline on accuracy and is no worse on legal exposure.
     - Evidence needed: the Step 3 table and Legal's written view.
   - Domain: Complicated. Deduction by analysis, using the Step 3 table as input.
   - Step 4 MUST NOT start until the Step 3 rubric passes.
   - If A2 is false, remove the defend option from the matrix.

Conditions that change the recommendation:

- If the story spreads faster than the Step 3 check, shorten the update time in Step 2.
