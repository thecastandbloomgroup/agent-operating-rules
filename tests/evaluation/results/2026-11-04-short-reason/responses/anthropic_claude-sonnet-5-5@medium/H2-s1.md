## Headline

Verify the facts first, set one decision owner, then align Legal, PR, and the board on a single written position.

- Facts decide the response, so a fast, documented check of the CEO statement (chief executive officer) comes first.
- The misalignment is a control problem, so one owner and one outbound voice remove it.
- Legal risk grows with delay, so preserve records now.
- The three groups disagree on goals, so a shared scoring sheet turns opinions into a choice.

## Background

A journalist published a report today. It says the CEO's statement from yesterday was false. Legal, PR, and the board each send you instructions. No one has set a common position.

## Relevance

Each hour without one voice raises the chance of a second inconsistent statement. A second statement is harder to fix than the first [judgment].

## Information

**Evidence**

- The report and the statement are the primary sources. No one has compared them line by line yet [judgment].
- Cynefin sorts work by how cause and effect relate [1]. Here the facts are unknown, so the case is Confused until the check ends.
- A Pugh matrix scores options against a baseline option [2]. It suits a group that disagrees on goals.

**Assumption log**

A1. The claim is not yet verified as true or false. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: read the report against the statement and the source records. If wrong: skip Step 4 options that defend the statement.

A2. You have the authority to convene Legal, PR, and the board chair. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask the CEO's office who owns the response. If wrong: send Step 2 to the person with that authority.

A3. No one has made a public reply yet. Why not measured: no tool can reach it. Validate by: search the company channels and press mentions. If wrong: add the reply to the Step 1 comparison.

**Alternative considered**

Issue a firm denial now. It wins on speed. It loses because a denial before the check risks a second false statement [judgment].

## End

I recommend that we take these five steps in order.

1. **Verify the facts (Confused).** Compare each claim in the report with the statement and its source records.
   - Increment: one table of claims, one row each, done in one session.
   - Blocker removed: no one knows whether the statement was false.
   - Domain reason: deduction from records, because we cannot classify the case until the facts are known.
   - Kind: evaluative. Rubric first. Criteria: source match (weight 50%), date match (25%), wording match (25%). Pass threshold: every row scored with a named record. Evidence: one primary record per row.

2. **Name one decision owner and freeze outbound messages (Clear).**
   - Increment: a one-line written order from the CEO or board chair, sent today.
   - Blocker removed: three groups give you conflicting orders.
   - Domain reason: deduction from a known rule, because single command is standard crisis practice [judgment].
   - Kind: deterministic. Test:

   ```gherkin
   Given the owner is named in writing
   When Legal, PR, or a director wants to send a statement
   Then the owner approves it before release
   ```

   Step 3 MUST NOT start until this test passes.

3. **Issue a legal hold on related records (Clear).**
   - Increment: one notice to the teams that hold emails, drafts, and notes about the statement.
   - Blocker removed: loss of records raises legal exposure.
   - Domain reason: deduction from a known rule, because Legal already uses hold procedures.
   - Kind: deterministic. Test:

   ```gherkin
   Given the hold notice is sent
   When a team member deletes a related record
   Then the system blocks it or logs it for Legal
   ```

4. **Align the three groups with a Pugh matrix (Complicated).**
   - Increment: one page that scores three options: correct, clarify, or stand by the statement.
   - Blocker removed: no shared basis for the choice.
   - Domain reason: deduction through analysis, because the Step 1 table gives the facts to score.
   - Kind: evaluative. Rubric first. Criteria and weights: legal exposure (35%), factual accuracy (30%), trust with the public (20%), board risk (15%). Pass threshold: the option with the highest weighted score, with no group rating it unacceptable. Evidence: the Step 1 table, Legal's written view, and PR's media count.
   - Step 4 MUST NOT start until Step 1 passes.

5. **Run a capped probe on the public reaction (Complex).**
   - Increment: a one-hour watch of the top 20 outlets and social posts after the holding line goes out.
   - Blocker removed: no data on how the story spreads. Leverage is a guess [judgment].
   - Domain reason: induction from trials. The pattern breaks if a larger outlet or a regulator joins the story.
   - Kind: stochastic. Acceptance test first. Metric: share of new items that repeat the claim. Baseline: the share in the first six hours. Threshold: a drop of 20 points or more, or a rise that triggers Step 4 review. Sample: all items in the 20 outlets over 24 hours. Budget: one analyst. Stop condition: Step 4 produces a decision.
   - Run Step 5 in parallel with Steps 1 to 4.

If A1 is false, replace the Step 4 option "stand by the statement" with "defend with evidence," and make Legal the lead on wording.
