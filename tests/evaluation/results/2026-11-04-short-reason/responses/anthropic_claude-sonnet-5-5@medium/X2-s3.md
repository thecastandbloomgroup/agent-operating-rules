## Headline

Find out why the actions die, then fix follow-through before you change the meeting format.

- Retrospective output has three stages: choose an action, finish it, and learn from it. Each stage can fail.
- Most stalls happen after the meeting, because nobody owns the action or has time for it. [judgment]
- A small, owned action per retrospective is the cheapest fix.
- A format change is a trial, so run it as a capped experiment.

## Background

The team holds retrospectives, but attendance does not lead to change. The request asks what to do. The material gives no data on past actions, team size, or sprint length.

## Relevance

Each retrospective without change costs the team time and lowers trust in the next one. [judgment] People who see no result stop raising real issues. [inference]

## Information

**Evidence**

- The Scrum Guide says the team should plan improvements and may add the most impactful ones to the next sprint backlog (Scrum Guide, Retrospective section).

[2]

- Derby and Larsen advise a short list of experiments that the team can finish, and a review of past items at each retrospective.

[1]

- Confidence that follow-through is the main cause: about as likely as not (33 to 66%). The basis is one practitioner book, one standard text, and no team data.

**Causes (MECE, by stage)**

1. Choice: actions are vague, too large, or outside the team's control.
2. Follow-through: actions have no owner, no date, or no time in the sprint.
3. Climate: people do not raise real issues, so the actions miss the problem.

**Assumption log**

A1. The team keeps no record of past action items beyond meeting notes. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask who holds the notes from the last three retrospectives. If wrong: skip the record search in Step 1.

A2. The team can change its own process without outside approval. Why not measured: the user has not answered. Validate by: check one past action that needed approval. If wrong: add an escalation action for blockers.

**Alternative considered**

Replace the facilitator or the format at once. It loses because a new format does not fix missing owners or missing time. [judgment]

## End

I recommend that we take these steps in order.

**Step 1. Audit the last three retrospectives.**
- Increment: list every action item and mark it done, partly done, or dropped. This takes under one hour.
- Blocker removed: nobody knows the real completion rate.
- Kind: deterministic.
- Cynefin: Clear. Deduction from a known rule: count each item by status.
- Test:
  - Given the notes of the last three retrospectives
  - When one person lists each action with an owner, a date, and a status
  - Then every action has exactly one status and the completion rate is a number

**Step 2. Diagnose the cause.** Step 2 MUST NOT start until the test in Step 1 passes.
- Increment: score the three causes against the audit and a five-question anonymous poll.
- Blocker removed: the team does not know which stage fails.
- Kind: evaluative.
- Cynefin: Complicated. Deduction by analysis: the audit and poll data point to one cause.
- Rubric, written first:
  - Criteria and weights: audit evidence 50%, poll evidence 30%, facilitator observation 20%.
  - Evidence: the completion rate by cause, poll scores, and notes from the last meeting.
  - Pass threshold: a cause scores at least 60 of 100 and leads the next cause by 15 points.

**Step 3. Limit each retrospective to one or two actions, each with an owner, a date, and a sprint backlog ticket.**
- Increment: apply the rule at the next retrospective.
- Blocker removed: actions with no owner or no time.
- Kind: deterministic.
- Cynefin: Clear. A known practice [2]: put the action in the backlog.
- Test:
  - Given a retrospective has ended
  - When the team reviews the action list
  - Then each action has one owner, one date, and one backlog ticket

**Step 4. Open the next retrospective with a review of the previous actions.** Step 4 MUST NOT start until Step 3 is in place.
- Increment: reserve the first ten minutes for the review.
- Blocker removed: no feedback on whether actions worked.
- Kind: deterministic.
- Cynefin: Clear. A known practice from [1]: review before you plan.
- Test:
  - Given a retrospective starts
  - When the facilitator opens the agenda
  - Then the first item is the status of each prior action

**Step 5. Run one format probe in parallel with Steps 3 and 4, if Step 2 names climate as the cause.**
- Increment: try one new format, such as anonymous input, for two retrospectives.
- Blocker removed: people do not raise real issues.
- Kind: stochastic.
- Cynefin: Complex. Induction from trials; a different team mix could break the pattern. Leverage is a guess.
- Acceptance test, written first:
  - Metric: share of actions finished by the next retrospective.
  - Baseline: the Step 1 completion rate.
  - Threshold: baseline plus 20 percentage points.
  - Sample size: at least six actions across two retrospectives.
  - Stop condition: end the probe after two retrospectives or after any team member reports harm.
  - Budget: no more than 30 extra minutes per retrospective.

If A1 is false, replace Step 1 with a one-question poll on what the team remembers acting on.
