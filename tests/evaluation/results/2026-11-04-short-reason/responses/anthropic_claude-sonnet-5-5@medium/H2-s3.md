## Headline

Name one decision owner now, verify the statement against records, and say nothing new in public until the check is done.

- Authority: one owner and one channel stop Legal, PR, and the board from issuing separate advice.
- Facts: a line-by-line check decides whether the company corrects or defends the statement.
- Outside words: a short holding statement with no new factual claims buys time.
- Alignment: a scored options brief gives all three groups one shared basis for the decision.

## Background

A journalist published a report that says the CEO's statement from yesterday was false. Legal, PR, and the board each send you separate guidance. No one owns the response, and no one has yet compared the statement with the records.

## Relevance

Each hour without an owner adds the risk of a second, conflicting statement. A second statement turns one dispute into two.

## Information

**Evidence**

- Crisis research finds that the response should match how much responsibility the public assigns to the organisation. Correction or apology fits when responsibility is high [1].
- A holding statement that adds no new facts is safer than a full rebuttal before the facts are known. [judgment]
- Confidence in this ordering: medium, one secondary source plus judgment.

**Assumption log**

A1. The CEO statement contains a factual claim that records can confirm or refute. Why not measured: the material does not state the claim. Validate by: compare the claim with source documents (Step 2). If wrong: if the dispute is about opinion, replace Step 3 with a statement that explains the CEO's meaning.

A2. You have the authority to convene Legal, PR, and the board chair. Why not measured: the material does not state your role. Validate by: send one message to the chair and the CEO to confirm. If wrong: ask the chair to name the owner in Step 1.

A3. No securities or regulatory disclosure duty applies today. Why not measured: the material does not say whether the company is public. Validate by: ask Legal for a yes or no in Step 1. If wrong: Legal sets the timing of Step 3, and Step 3 follows Legal's disclosure rules.

**Alternative considered**

The alternative is to rebut the article at once and defend the CEO. It loses because a defense of a false statement raises the cost of the later correction [1]. It also commits the company before any fact check. [judgment]

## End

I recommend that we take these four actions in this order.

1. **Stabilise authority (Chaotic).**
   - Increment: in the next hour, the CEO or board chair names one decision owner and one channel. Every outside statement needs the owner's sign-off.
   - Blocker removed: three groups give conflicting direction.
   - Domain: Chaotic, because no cause and effect is visible, so act first to stabilise [2].
   - Kind: deterministic. Test, written first [3]:
     - Given Legal, PR, and the board have each sent guidance
     - When the owner and channel are announced in writing
     - Then each group confirms the owner by reply and no outside statement is sent without sign-off

2. **Verify the statement (Confused).**
   - Increment: within two hours, Legal and one neutral reviewer compare each sentence of the CEO statement with primary records. The output is a one-page table: claim, evidence, status (true, false, unclear).
   - Blocker removed: no one knows who is right.
   - Domain: Confused, because the truth is unknown, so gather facts to classify [2].
   - Kind: evaluative. Rubric, written first:
     - Criteria and weights: every disputed claim covered (50%), each status backed by a primary record (30%), two reviewers agree independently (20%).
     - Pass threshold: score of at least 80%, and no claim left uncovered.
     - Evidence needed: document names, dates, and reviewer initials.

3. **Issue a holding statement (Clear).**
   - Increment: send a statement of three sentences or fewer. It acknowledges the report, says a review is under way, and gives a time for the next update.
   - Blocker removed: silence leaves the journalist's account as the only account.
   - Domain: Clear, because holding statements are an established best practice, applied by deduction from that rule [2].
   - Kind: deterministic. Test, written first [3]:
     - Given the owner has approved the draft
     - When the statement is sent to the journalist and posted
     - Then it contains no new factual claim and states one update time

   Step 3 MUST NOT start until Step 1 passes. Step 3 MAY run in parallel with Step 2.

4. **Align the three groups with a scored brief (Complicated).**
   - Increment: build a Pugh matrix [4] of three options: correct, defend, stay silent. Use the Step 2 table as evidence. Take "hold and wait" as the baseline.
   - Blocker removed: Legal, PR, and the board have no shared basis to decide.
   - Domain: Complicated, because analysis of the Step 2 evidence picks among good practices by deduction.
   - Kind: evaluative. Rubric, written first:
     - Criteria and weights: factual accuracy (40%), legal exposure (30%), public trust (20%), speed (10%).
     - Pass threshold: one option beats the baseline on accuracy and does not score worse on legal exposure.
     - Evidence needed: a Step 2 table row or a Legal note for each score.

   Step 4 MUST NOT start until the Step 2 rubric passes. The owner MUST NOT issue the final public response until Legal, PR, and the board chair each sign the chosen option.
