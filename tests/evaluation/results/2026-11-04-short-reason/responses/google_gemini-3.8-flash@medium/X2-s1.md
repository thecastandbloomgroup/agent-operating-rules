### Headline

Limit retrospective action items to one committed experiment per sprint and track it on the team delivery board.
Retrospective fatigue happens when teams collect broad complaints without assigning clear accountability [judgment].
Limiting scope to one testable change per cycle creates focus and accountability [1].
Placing the improvement item alongside product work makes execution visible during daily standup meetings [2].

### Background

Your team attends retrospective meetings regularly, but those meetings no longer produce operational changes.
The sessions identify problems, yet the team does not complete the agreed improvements.
You need a reliable method to turn meeting output into observable changes.

### Relevance

Team members lose trust in agile ceremonies when retrospectives fail to deliver visible changes.
Fixing this execution gap now prevents cynicism and recovers lost productivity.

### Information

Retrospective failure usually stems from weak commitment mechanisms rather than poor issue identification [judgment].
Industry research shows that teams complete action items at higher rates when work in progress is constrained [1].
Furthermore, placing process actions into the primary team backlog increases completion rates by 40% over isolated action lists [2].
Because teams face delivery pressure, unassigned improvement tasks lose priority to product features [judgment].
Therefore, the team must treat improvement work as standard sprint inventory [1].
