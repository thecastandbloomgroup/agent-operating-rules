### Background

Your team holds regular retrospective meetings and team members attend as scheduled. However, these sessions fail to produce visible changes in daily work. Therefore, you need a method to convert team discussions into completed improvements.

### Relevance

Stalled retrospectives waste team hours and reduce engagement in continuous improvement. Resolving this execution gap now prevents team members from abandoning the ritual completely.

### Information

Retrospective decay occurs when teams treat action items as optional discussion notes instead of sprint commitments [1].
Furthermore, teams that select more than two action items complete them less than 30% of the time [judgment].
Therefore, limiting work in process applies to team process changes just as it applies to software delivery.
See Section 5.2 for the operational assumptions behind this assessment.

A1. The team operates in one-week or two-week iterations. Why not measured: The material does not state it. Validate by: Inspect the team calendar. If wrong: Adjust the review cycle to match the release cadence.

A2. The team has authority to modify internal workflow practices. Why not measured: The material does not state it. Validate by: Confirm mandate with engineering management. If wrong: Focus actions only on items inside local team control.

The strongest alternative considered is replacing retrospective meetings with written feedback forms. This alternative loses because forms eliminate collaborative root cause analysis and fail to fix execution tracking [judgment].

### End

I recommend that we execute the following sequence:

1. Step 1: Audit open action items from previous meetings to clear abandoned commitments.
   - Test (Evaluative): Evaluate existing retrospective items using this rubric.
     - Criteria: Business value (weight 40%), owner assigned (weight 30%), age under 30 days (weight 30%).
     - Pass threshold: Score 70 out of 100 points.
     - Evidence: Retrospective meeting notes and issue tracker records.
   - Increment: One 45-minute working session with the team facilitator.
   - Blocker removed: This step clears backlog clutter and ends passive guilt over dead commitments.
   - Cynefin domain: Complicated. Cause and effect require analysis of past meeting records to identify recurring failure patterns.

2. Step 2: Configure one dedicated process improvement card on the primary sprint board. Step 2 MUST NOT start until Step 1 passes.
   - Test (Deterministic):
     Given an agreed retrospective action item from Step 1
     When the sprint planning meeting concludes
     Then the sprint board displays one improvement card in the active backlog with an assigned owner
   - Increment: One 15-minute configuration task during sprint planning.
   - Capability added: This step makes process improvements visible alongside standard product backlog tasks.
   - Cynefin domain: Clear. Cause and effect follow a known rule that visible board items receive regular attention.

3. Step 3: Run a safe-to-fail probe that inspects the improvement card during daily standups. Step 3 runs in parallel with Step 2, capped at a two-week sprint budget.
   - Test (Stochastic):
     - Metric: Daily standup mentions of the improvement card.
     - Baseline: Zero mentions across ten consecutive business days.
     - Pass threshold: At least seven mentions across ten consecutive business days.
     - Sample size: Ten business days.
   - Increment: A five-minute review block inside daily standup meetings for one sprint.
   - Blocker removed: This step prevents the team from ignoring process commitments between meetings.
   - Cynefin domain: Complex. Cause and effect in team habits appear only after running the trial probe.
