### Headline

Limit retrospectives to one owned improvement item per cycle, track it on the main delivery board, and review it before new discussion.
First, limiting work in process prevents action items from being abandoned.
Second, placing improvement tasks on the active sprint board makes execution visible.
Third, reviewing the prior action item at the start of each meeting establishes accountability.

### Background

The team attends retrospective meetings consistently.
However, these sessions fail to produce visible operational changes.
The user seeks an intervention to restore effectiveness to the process.

### Relevance

Unproductive meetings consume team time without delivering improvements.

### Information

Retrospective decay typically occurs when teams generate long lists of unassigned action items [1].
Without limits on work in progress, teams complete zero improvements [judgment].
In contrast, focusing on one item per iteration raises completion rates [2].

A thematic review of agile coaching case studies shows two recurring causes for retrospective stagnation [3].

Second, teams do not inspect past commitments before creating new ones [3].
Zero sources in that review dispute these two failure modes [3].
