## Headline

There is no single "best" prompt, but the most effective general one instructs the model to say "I don't know" instead of guessing.

- Hallucination is reduced by permission to abstain, not by demands for accuracy.
- Prompt engineering alone cannot eliminate hallucination; retrieval of source documents helps more.
- The prompt below combines the two techniques with tested effect.

## Background

AI chatbots sometimes invent facts, called hallucination. Many users try to fix this with a magic phrase, but research shows instruction wording only reduces, not removes, the problem [1].

## Relevance

If the model's errors affect your work, a reliable answer style matters now. A short, copyable prompt is the cheapest first fix [inference].

## Information

Evidence: a study of instruction prompts found that telling the model it may answer "I don't know" raises the rate of correct abstention on unanswerable questions, compared with prompts demanding accuracy alone [1]. Theme count: this conclusion rests on one secondary source, so confidence is medium (one study, judgment on generalization).

- A1. Your model accepts a system or instruction prompt. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: paste the prompt and test. If wrong: adapt the wording to your interface.
- A2. Your failures are fact errors, not reasoning errors. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: review two or three past wrong answers. If wrong: add "show your step-by-step reasoning" instead.

Alternative considered: retrieval-augmented generation (give the model the source text and require citations). It reduces hallucination more than prompting alone, but it needs setup, so the plain prompt wins on cost for a first step [judgment].

Copy this prompt:

```text
Answer only when you know the answer with confidence. If you are
not sure, say "I don't know" and state what information would let
you answer. Do not invent facts, dates, names, or references.
When you use a source I provide, cite it by name.
```

## End

Domain: Clear, because the cause and effect (abstention instruction reduces guessing) is a known pattern [1]. 3. If errors continue, attach your source documents and require citations. This adds retrieval grounding. Domain: Complicated, because you pick the sources by analysis. Test: given an attached document, when you ask about its content, then each claim carries a citation you can verify.
