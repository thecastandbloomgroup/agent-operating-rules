## Headline

Retrospectives stop producing change when actions lack owners, deadlines, and follow-up at the next session. Three moves fix most cases: limit actions to one or two, assign a named owner and a check date, and open the next retrospective by reviewing the last actions [1]. A fourth move, rotating the format, addresses decline that comes from ritual fatigue [2].

## Background

The team holds retrospectives and people attend, but no behavior or process changes afterward. The request is for concrete steps to restore the improvement loop.

## Relevance

A retrospective that produces no change costs the meeting time without a return. Left alone, attendance and candor decline next, which removes the team's main feedback channel [1].

## Information

Evidence from practice literature:

1. Derby and Larsen: actions fail when they are vague, unowned, or too many; they advise one to two actions per session with visible tracking [1]. [S]
2. A common anti-pattern is "no follow-through": teams that review prior actions at the start of each session complete more of them [1]. [S]
3. Kerievsky and others note that repeating one format lowers energy and idea quality; rotating formats restores participation [2]. [S]
4. Psychological safety research: people raise fewer real problems when prior input had no visible effect [3]. That is likely happening here. [S]

Assumption log:

A1. Actions from past retrospectives are not reviewed in later sessions. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask the facilitator for the last three action lists. If wrong: the cause is action quality, not follow-up; shift weight to Step 2.

A2. The facilitator is a team member, not a dedicated coach. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask who runs the sessions. If wrong: a coach can apply Step 4 without team effort.

A3. The team has authority to change its own process. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: pick one proposed action and check whether it needs outside approval. If wrong: escalate blockers to the manager who holds that authority, per Section 6.8.

Alternative considered: stop retrospectives and collect feedback another way, for example one-to-ones. This loses because one-to-ones lack shared ownership of process problems, and the team loses its one forum for group decisions [1]. [judgment]

Confidence: high for Steps 1 and 2, based on consistent guidance across two independent sources [1][2]. Medium for Step 4, because fatigue is an inference about this team. About as likely as not [4]. Statement of uncertainty: the safe-to-fail probe in Step 4 manages this.

## End

I recommend that we take four actions, in this order:

1. **Review the last three retrospectives and list every action item with its status.** Increment: one 30-minute document review. Blocker removed: no baseline on follow-through. Test (deterministic):
   - Given the notes of the last three sessions,
   - When each action is marked done, partially done, or not started,
   - Then a completion rate exists and the anti-pattern is confirmed or ruled out.
   Cynefin: Clear. Cause and effect are known; reading notes and tallying outcomes follows one established practice [1].

2. **Adopt an action contract for the next retrospective: at most two actions, each with a named owner, a visible location, and a check date.** Increment: one facilitated session rule change. Blocker removed: vague, unowned actions. Test (deterministic):
   - Given the next retrospective ends,
   - When the action list is published in the team's visible workspace,
   - Then each action has exactly one owner and one date, and the list has at most two items.
   Cynefin: Clear. A best practice exists and applies directly [1]. Step 2 MUST NOT start until Step 1 confirms the follow-through rate, because the rate decides the phrasing of the opening review.

3. **Open every retrospective with a five-minute review of the previous actions.** Increment: add one fixed agenda item. Blocker removed: actions disappear after the meeting. Test (stochastic): metric is action completion rate; baseline is the rate from Step 1; pass threshold is at least half of actions completed; sample size is four consecutive retrospectives. Accept only at that sample size. Cynefin: Complicated. The pattern is known, but the team must adapt the review to its workflow; analysis of Step 1 data picks the format [1].

4. **Probe one format change as a safe-to-fail experiment.** Increment: one session run in a different format, budget one hour. Blocker addressed: participation decline from ritual repetition. Stop condition: cancel the probe if participation in the next standard session returns to normal without it. Test (evaluative, rubric first): criteria are number of topics raised, number of speakers, and actions proposed; each weighted one third; pass threshold is improvement over the average of the last three sessions on at least two criteria. Cynefin: Complex. Cause and effect on team energy are visible only after the trial, so this is a capped probe, not a commitment. Leverage is a guess [5]. Run it in parallel with Steps 2 and 3.

Hedge: if A3 is false, replace Step 2 with "send the action list to the approving manager within one day."
