## Headline

Retrospectives fail when decisions lack owners, deadlines, and follow up; fix the loop, not the meeting. Support:
- Most stalled retros share one cause: action items have no owner, so nothing changes.
- Feedback loops work only when the next retro reviews the last retro's outcomes.
- Fewer, tested actions beat long wish lists because teams finish small increments.

## Background

The team's retrospectives happen, but they produce no visible change. Attendance is fine, so the problem sits in follow through, not participation.

## Relevance

Repeated reflection without change teaches the team that speaking up is wasted. Trust in the format erodes, and attendance will drop next.

## Information

Evidence:
- Studies of continuous improvement show action tracking and review predict follow through more than meeting quality [1].
- Theme from agile practice literature: the dominant failure pattern is unowned action items, supported by two sources [2]; no source disagrees.
- [judgment] People attend, which signals the cause is not hostility to the ritual.

A1. Actions currently lack explicit owners. Why not measured: the user did not describe it. Validate by: check the last three retros' notes. If wrong: focus on follow up cadence instead of ownership.

A2. The team has authority to change its own process. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask the manager. If wrong: add a step to secure approval first.

Alternative considered: replace retros with another format, such as lean coffee. It loses because format is not the failure point; weak follow through would transfer to any new format [judgment].

## End

I recommend that we:

1. Cap each retro at three actions, each with a named owner and a date. This removes the blocker of unowned items. Deterministic, Clear domain, because ownership rules follow directly.
   - Given a retro ends,
   - When actions are recorded,
   - Then each action carries one owner, one date, and one sentence of expected change.

2. Open every retro with a five minute review of prior actions. This adds accountability for outcomes. Deterministic, Clear domain, because the review rule is a best practice.
   - Given the last retro's action list,
   - When the new retro starts,
   - Then each prior action is marked done, in progress, or dropped with a reason.

3. Make one action per retro a two week experiment with a measure and a stop condition. This rounds guesswork into evidence. Stochastic, Complex domain, because cause and effect in team behavior appears only after trials. Acceptance test: baseline satisfaction score from a three question pulse survey, target plus one point on a five point scale, sample of all team members, two sprints.

4. Cancel or shorten the retro if nothing new surfaces for two sessions. This protects the ritual from becoming empty habit. Evaluative, Complicated domain. Rubric: novelty of items (50%), energy in discussion (30%), decisions made (20%); pass threshold 60%; evidence is retro notes.

Step 2 MUST NOT start until Step 1 passes. Step 3 runs in parallel. If A2 is false, add a step to get a manager to sponsor the change list.
