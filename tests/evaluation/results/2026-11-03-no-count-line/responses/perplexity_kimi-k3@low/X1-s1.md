## Background

Your support tool added an LLM summariser. Agents say it is "sometimes wrong". No metrics, labeled examples, or feedback capture exist yet.

## Relevance

Complaints without data cannot confirm or reject any fix. So a baseline turns each future change into a verifiable test.

## Information

Error analysis of raw outputs is the standard first step for LLM quality work [1]. A starter taxonomy for support summaries has five classes [judgment]. Adjust the classes against real tickets in Step 1.

1. Unsupported claim: the summary states a fact absent from the ticket.
2. Wrong detail: a number, name, or date is incorrect.
3. Missing key content: the resolution or the next step is absent.
4. Stale content: the summary uses an older message, not the final state.
5. Format or tone fault: the text is correct but unusable for the agent.

Sample size follows from the standard formula: 96 summaries give a ±10% margin at 95% confidence, and 384 give ±5% [inference]. A GPT-4 judge matched human preferences at 80% agreement or better in MT-Bench tests [2]. Therefore an automated judge is fit for use only after local calibration [judgment]. The NIST AI framework orders measurement before risk management [3]. Effects that appear only after the fact call for small probes, not full commitments [4].

1. Define the failure taxonomy and label 100 recent summaries.
   - Domain: Complicated, because the classes are knowable by analysis of the outputs.
   - Increment: one session with two reviewers to write class rules, label 20 shared summaries, then split 80 more.
   - Blocker removed: "wrong" has no shared definition and no baseline.
   - Test first (evaluative), rubric with pass threshold 0.8 of 1.0 weighted:

     - Reliability, weight 40%: Cohen's kappa of 0.7 or more on the 20 shared summaries. Evidence: the agreement sheet.
