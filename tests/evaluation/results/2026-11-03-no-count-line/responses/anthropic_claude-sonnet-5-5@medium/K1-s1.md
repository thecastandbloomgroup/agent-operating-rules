## Headline

Raise the database pool size back to a value the database limit allows. That change removes most of the added latency.

- The pool change caused most of the slowdown: `db.pool.acquire` p95 rose 687 ms of the 780 ms total rise (88%) [2].
- The cache TTL cut is a smaller second cause: the miss rate rose from 12% to 31% [2].
- The ORM change is unlikely to be a cause, because query spans did not change [2].

## Background

The API p95 latency rose from 120 ms to 900 ms after release 2026.10.21. That release cut the pool `max_size` from 50 to 8, the acquire timeout from 30 s to 5 s, and the cache TTL from 300 s to 60 s. It also added `selectinload` (a query option that loads child rows in one extra query) to the ORM (object-relational mapper) code [3].

## Relevance

The error rate is 0.40%, up from 0.02%. Of those errors, 96% are `PoolTimeout` [2]. Users receive 503 responses after 5 s now.

## Information

**Evidence**

- Pool wait is the main delay. The p50 acquire time rose from 1 ms to 140 ms, and p95 from 3 ms to 690 ms [2].
- The logs agree. Pods report "8 of 8 connections in use" with 6 to 14 waiters, and `PoolTimeout` errors follow [1].
- Pool warnings rose from none on 2026-10-20 to 1,800 to 2,400 per hour on 2026-10-22 [1].
- The database is not the bottleneck. Its peak CPU moved from 38% to 41% [2].
- The query spans did not change. `db.query orders` p95 moved from 52 ms to 58 ms, and `order_lines` fell from 30 ms to 26 ms [2].
- Load did not change: peak 410 requests per second before, 405 after [2].
- Active connections from this service fell from 31 to 8 at peak [2].
- The cache lookup is fast (p95 4 ms to 5 ms). The cost of a miss is the extra database work and the pool demand it adds [2].
- The commit message says the pool was lowered to match a new database connection limit [3]. The limit value is not in the material.

Confidence that the pool change is the main cause: high confidence, with three independent signals (trace spans, error mix, pool warnings).

Confidence that the cache TTL adds a smaller share: about as likely as not. The miss rate rose, but no trace isolates its effect.

**Assumption log**

A1. The new database limit allows more than 8 connections for this service. Why not measured: the material does not state the limit. Validate by: read the limit in ticket OPS-412 or query `max_connections` on the database. If wrong: Step 2 uses a smaller value, and the fix is partial.

A2. The pool size of 8 applies per pod (4 pods, 32 workers). Why not measured: the material gives 8 active connections for the service, and the logs show "8 of 8" on each pod. These two facts conflict. Validate by: check the pool metric labels on one pod. If wrong: the pool is shared by all pods, and the limit is 8 for the whole service.

**Alternative considered**

Roll back the whole release. It loses because it also reverts the ORM change without evidence of harm. It may also break the new connection limit, since the old pool allowed up to 200 connections across 4 pods [3]. Keep it as the fallback if Step 2 fails.
