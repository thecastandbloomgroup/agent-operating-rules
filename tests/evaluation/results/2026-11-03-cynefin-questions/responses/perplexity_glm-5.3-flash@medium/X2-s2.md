## Headline

Make retrospective output a tracked commitment: one owner and one due date for each action, and review the result at the next meeting.
1. Agreed actions without owners do not get done; ownership moves work into the backlog.
2. Cap output at two actions per retrospective, because a long list has no owner per item.
3. Measure the completion rate over three cycles as the pass condition.

## Background

Team retrospectives still produce agreed actions, but the actions do not reach the work. Attendance continues, so the people and the cadence exist. The failure sits between the meeting and the work.

## Relevance

Each retrospective that ends without follow-through trains the team to forget its output. One process change can stop this within three cycles.

## Information

**Root causes.** The failure has three non-overlapping parts [judgment]: no owner per action, no place the actions are tracked, and no review of past actions at the next meeting.

**Practice.** Derby and Larsen describe follow-through as part of the retrospective cycle: select a small number of actions, place them in the team backlog, and reopen them at the next retrospective [1]. This is a published practice, not an observation of your team.

**Assumption log.**

- A1. The retro participants are the people who would carry out the actions. Why not measured: the request does not state who the participants are. Validate by: list owners at the next retro. If wrong: route actions to planning instead of the team.
- A2. The cadence is at most two weeks. Why not measured: the request does not state the cadence. Validate by: read the dates in past meeting notes. If wrong: three cycles span a longer period, so state the threshold per cycle, not per month.

**Alternative considered.** Run a larger, better facilitated year-style retrospective [judgment]: it loses because the blocker is follow-through after the meeting, not the quality of discussion inside it.

## End

I recommend that we make the output a tracked commitment, in these steps.

1. **Cap and own.** Increment: in the next retrospective, select at most two actions, each with one owner and one due date. Blocker: unowned output. Test (write first, deterministic, Gherkin):
   Given the end of the next retrospective,
   When the action list is written,
   Then no more than two actions appear, and each line shows one owner and one due date.
   Domain: Clear, because the rule is known to everyone and applies in one step.

2. **Track.** Increment: move the two actions onto the team board as backlog items in the same session. Blocker: actions that live only in meeting notes. Test:
   Given the board after the session,
   When a team member searches for the two actions,
   Then both appear as items with owner and due date.
   Domain: Clear, because it applies one known practice [1]. Step 2 MUST NOT start until the test in Step 1 passes.

3. **Review.** Increment: open each following retrospective with the status of the previous two actions. Blocker: no feedback loop between meetings. Test:
   Given the start of retrospective cycle n,
   When the team reviews the two actions of cycle n-1,
   Then each action is marked done, or dropped with a stated reason, or rescheduled with a date.
   Domain: Clear, because it reopens the previous output as Derby and Larsen recommend [1].

4. **Probe the cause if progress stalls.** Increment: after three cycles with zero completed actions, ask each participant one question, "what stops your own action?" Blocker: the real cause is unknown. Acceptance test (write first, stochastic): metric is survey response count, baseline is 0, pass threshold is answers from at least two thirds of participants, sample is one session. Stop condition: close after one session and read the answers. Budget: ten minutes of the next retro.
   Domain: Complex, because the cause shows only after the trial. Leverage is a guess; the probe is capped at one session.

Step 4 MUST NOT start until Step 3 has run three cycles.

**References**

[1] E. Derby and D. Larsen, Agile Retrospectives: Making Good Teams Great. Pragmatic Bookshelf, 2006. [S]

## Follow-up

Conditions that change the recommendation: if participants cannot do the actions themselves, Step 1 routes the actions to the planning meeting instead (see A1).
