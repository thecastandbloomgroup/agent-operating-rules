## Headline

Test output tracking first, then safety and format, in that order. The most likely fault is that retrospective actions have no owner and no follow-up, so no change survives the meeting [judgment].

1. Three faults explain a stuck retrospective without overlapping: output has no owner or tracking, people hold back real issues, and the format produces vague actions.
2. The cheapest test is an audit of the last three action lists, because it shows which fault dominates.
3. Two small probes then test the remaining faults: an anonymous safety check, and a format rotation, each with a measure and a stop condition.

## Background

A team runs retrospectives on a fixed schedule. People attend. No action from these meetings changes how the work happens.

## Relevance

The same problems repeat every sprint while the team pays meeting time without return. The gap between discussion and action also teaches people that speaking up changes nothing.

## Information

**Evidence**

E1. Derby and Larson make follow-up the fifth stage of a retrospective, after the meeting ends. The stage covers assigning owners and checking each action at the next meeting [1].

E2. A study of more than 180 teams at Google found psychological safety the largest single factor in team effectiveness. The New York Times article is secondary for that internal study [3].

E3. Edmondson measured work teams and found that teams with higher rated safety raised more problems and showed more learning behavior [2].

E4. Theme with limited support [judgment]: repeated use of one format reduces the quality of output, because facilitation practice recommends varying the format. Sources that disagree are not known to the response.

**Assumption log**

A1. Follow-through is the main fault, not attendance or intent. Why not measured: the material does not state what the last action lists contain. Validate by: the audit in Step 1. If wrong: move the probe in Step 3 to the first position.

A2. At least some attendees hold back real issues. Why not measured: the material does not state this. Validate by: the anonymous check in Step 3. If wrong: drop the probe and keep only Steps 1 and 2.

**Alternative considered**

Stop retrospectives and solve problems inside the sprint instead. It loses because a sprint has no step that reviews the process itself, so issues lose their pusher and their owner, per the five-stage model [1]. [judgment]

## End

I recommend that we run four small steps. Steps 3 and 4 run in parallel with Steps 1 and 2.

**Step 1. Audit the last three action lists.** Increment: one working session. Blocker removed: the unknown follow-through state. Kind: deterministic. Domain: Clear, because giving each action an owner is one known practice, and the inference is deduction.

Test, written first:

```gherkin
Given the action lists from the last three retrospectives
When the audit session ends
Then each listed item has one owner, one due date, or a closed state
```

**Step 2. Track exactly one action per retrospective.** Increment: a rule for the next meeting, then each meeting after it. Blocker removed: vague, untracked output and the missing review step. The action goes into the backlog as one item, small enough for one sprint. Step 2 MUST NOT start until Step 1 closes every item without an owner. Kind: deterministic. Domain: Clear, deduction from the five-stage model [1].

Test, written first:

```gherkin
Given one retrospective has just ended
When the facilitator records the action
Then the backlog holds exactly one new item with one owner
And the item has an acceptance criterion and a due date inside one sprint
And the next retrospective opens with a status check of that item
```

**Step 3. Probe psychological safety in parallel.** Increment: an anonymous two-question check at each of the next four retrospectives, five minutes each. Blocker addressed: held-back issues. Metric: the number of new, concrete issues named per session. Baseline: the average count across the three sessions audited in Step 1. Pass threshold: the count rises in at least two of four sessions. Sampling: four sessions. Stop condition: stop if the response rate falls below 60% for two sessions. Budget: four sessions, 20 minutes total. Domain: Complex, because the effect shows only after a trial; the inference is induction, and the pattern breaks if anonymity is not real in a small room.

**Step 4. Rotate the format in parallel.** Increment: use three formats across the same four sessions: start, stop, continue; 4Ls; and a five-whys session on one theme. Blocker addressed: vague output from one worn-in format. Metric: the number of new, concrete issues named per session, against the same baseline from Step 1. Pass threshold: at least one format beats the baseline. Sampling: four sessions. Stop condition: stop after four sessions and rank the formats. Budget: no extra time beyond the existing meetings. Domain: Complex, with the same induction risk as Step 3.

## Follow-up

Conditions that change the recommendation:

1. If the audit in Step 1 shows that actions already had owners and due dates, drop Steps 1 and 2 and move Step 3 to the first position.
2. If no session in the probes names a new issue, bring in a facilitator from outside the team, because the fault is then the discussion itself, not the tracking. [inference]
