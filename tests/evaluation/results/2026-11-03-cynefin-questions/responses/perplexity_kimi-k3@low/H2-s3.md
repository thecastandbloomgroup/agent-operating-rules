## Background

A journalist published a claim that yesterday's CEO statement was false. Legal, PR, and the board are sending separate messages, and no one agrees on a response.

## Relevance

The first 24 hours set the public record. One wrong reply can turn a factual dispute into a credibility crisis, so alignment must come before any response.

## Information

Two evidence paths exist. First, the factual record: the transcript, recording, or release of yesterday's statement, and the journalist's evidence for falsity. Second, the stakeholder messages already received. Neither is verified yet [inference]. Crisis communications research shows that early, coordinated, provisional statements reduce reputation damage more than silence or unverified denials [1].

Assumption log:

`A1. No verified transcript of the CEO statement exists yet in your hands. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: request the recording or release within one hour. If wrong: Step 2 becomes faster, no other change.`

`A2. No legal threat (subpoena, regulator contact) has arrived. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask Legal directly now. If wrong: Legal leads all decisions and external contact stops until counsel approves.`

`A3. You have authority to convene Legal, PR, and the board. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: send the convening message; the replies confirm or refute. If wrong: escalate to the person who has that authority before Step 3.`

Alternative considered: issue an immediate public denial. It loses because an unverified denial, if the claim proves partly true, doubles the damage [judgment].

## End

I recommend that we act in this order:

1. **Convene one decision channel within 30 minutes.** One chat or call with named leads for Legal, PR, and the board. Test: Given all three parties, when the channel opens, then each names one decision-maker. Type: deterministic. Domain: Chaotic, because uncoordinated replies cause harm now, so stabilization comes first.
2. **Freeze all external statements until Step 1 confirms compliance.** Issue one holding line through PR only: "We are reviewing the claim and will respond with the facts." Test: Given the holding line, when Legal approves it, then PR publishes nothing else. Type: deterministic. Domain: Chaotic, because it stops further harm before analysis.
3. **Verify the factual record before any correction or denial.** Legal pulls the statement's source within two hours and compares it to the journalist's evidence. Test: Given the source material and the article, when Legal reviews both, then the team gets a written verdict: accurate, partially accurate, or inaccurate. Type: evaluative; rubric: accuracy 50%, legal risk 30%, evidentiary strength 20%; pass threshold: Legal's signed verdict. Domain: Complicated, because an expert with the data can answer this by deduction.
4. **Decide the response from the verdict in Step 3.** Accurate: publish the evidence. Partially accurate: correct the specific point. Inaccurate: correct with apology and a plan. Each path needs Legal sign-off before release. Test: Given the verdict, when the named response is drafted, then Legal and the board approve it in writing. Domain: Complex for the public reaction, because the effect shows only after the response ships; cap exposure by releasing through one channel and monitoring for four hours.
5. **Assign daily governance after the first response.** One spokesperson, one approval chain, one log of decisions. Test: Given the written protocol, when the next external request arrives, then it follows the chain. Domain: Clear, because a known practice applies.

Step 3 MUST NOT start until Step 1 confirms each lead. Step 4 MUST NOT release until the verdict in Step 3 is written.
