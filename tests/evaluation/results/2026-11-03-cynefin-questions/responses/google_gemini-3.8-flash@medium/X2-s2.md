### Headline

Focus the team on one measurable change per sprint and review past commitments at the start of each retrospective.
This practice creates accountability for previous agreements before the team creates new items.
Limiting work in progress prevents team fatigue and ensures capacity for process changes.
Assigning single owners to specific tasks removes ambiguity about execution.

### Background

The team attends retrospectives regularly, but these sessions fail to produce process changes.
Attendance remains steady while outcomes stagnate [judgment].
The user wants an actionable plan to restore effective change through retrospectives.

### Relevance

Unproductive meetings consume working hours and reduce team morale.
When meetings produce no visible change, team members stop investing effort into continuous improvement [1].

### Information

Retrospectives fail to produce results primarily because teams select too many vague actions without ownership [1].
Furthermore, teams rarely review past commitments before creating new action lists [judgment].
When teams treat retrospective actions as unallocated side tasks, normal sprint work displaces them [1].
Therefore, process improvement requires explicit sprint capacity and single-item focus [1].

I assess it as very likely (90 to 100%) that capping retrospective actions to one item per sprint restores progress.

### End

I recommend that we:

1. Audit all open retrospective items from the last three months to establish a baseline.
   - Increment: Complete the audit during one 45-minute working session.
   - Capability: Clears stale commitments and identifies recurring failure patterns.
   - Test (Evaluative):
     - Rubric:
       - Item age recorded: Weight 30%, Pass threshold 100% of items, Evidence: board records.
       - Completion status verified: Weight 40%, Pass threshold 100% of items, Evidence: git commits or ticket logs.
       - Root cause categorized: Weight 30%, Pass threshold 80% of items, Evidence: team notes.
       - Total pass threshold: 85% overall score.
   - Cynefin domain: Clear. One known practice applies here because counting closed items uses standard data collection.

2. Introduce a strict gate at the start of the next retrospective to review the previous action item.
   - Increment: Update the retrospective meeting agenda template in one 15-minute session.
   - Capability: Prevents creating new tasks until the team assesses previous commitments.
   - Test (Deterministic):
     Given a retrospective meeting begins
     When the facilitator presents the agenda
     Then the first agenda topic is the status of the prior action item
   - Cynefin domain: Complicated. Cause and effect are knowable through process analysis, and expert facilitation guides the agenda design.
   - Dependency: Step 2 MUST NOT start until Step 1 finishes.

3. Limit the team to exactly one retrospective action item for the upcoming cycle with a dedicated owner.
   - Increment: Select and schedule one action item during the next retrospective session.
   - Capability: Removes task overload and establishes clear personal responsibility.
   - Test (Deterministic):
     Given the retrospective reaches the decision phase
     When the team agrees on candidate improvements
     Then exactly one action item is placed on the active sprint board with one assigned owner
   - Cynefin domain: Complicated. Selecting the highest-leverage item requires expert analysis of team constraints.
   - Dependency: Step 3 MUST NOT start until Step 2 passes.

4. Run a two-sprint probe where the team allocates five percent of sprint capacity directly to that action item.
   - Increment: Reserve story points or hours for process improvement during one sprint planning session.
   - Capability: Protects process improvement work from being displaced by feature delivery.
   - Test (Stochastic):
     - Metric: Action item completion rate.
     - Baseline: 0% completion across past retrospectives.
     - Pass threshold: 100% completion (2 out of 2 items finished).
     - Sample size: 2 consecutive sprints.
   - Cynefin domain: Complex. Team dynamics and delivery pressures show their interaction only through trial; therefore, this probe uses a cap of two sprints.
   - Dependency: Step 4 MUST NOT start until Step 3 passes.

### Follow-up

If A2 is false, replace Step 3 with an escalation session to request process ownership from leadership.
