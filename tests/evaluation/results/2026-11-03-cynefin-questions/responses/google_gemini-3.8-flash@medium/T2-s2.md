### Headline

No single prompt stops hallucinations completely, but grounding prompts that enforce citation and explicit refusal reduce errors.

1. Constraining the model to cite provided reference text prevents unsupported claims.
2. Instructing the model to admit ignorance stops confabulation when context lacks the answer.
3. Requiring step-by-step verification separates fact extraction from text synthesis.

### Background

You asked for the best prompt to stop hallucinations in model outputs. Generative language models predict tokens based on probability rather than verified knowledge bases [19]. Therefore, prompting techniques reduce hallucination rates, but they cannot eliminate errors entirely [20].

### Relevance

Relying on a single prompt to stop hallucinations creates an illusion of complete factual accuracy. Implementing verified prompt structures allows you to suppress ungrounded claims immediately.

### Information

Language models optimize for probability rather than truth [19]. Consequently, models confabulate facts when data is missing [19]. Studies show that grounding prompts with explicit refusal instructions reduce hallucination rates by up to 80% compared to open-ended generation [20]. However, completely stopping hallucinations requires external retrieval and validation mechanisms [judgment].
