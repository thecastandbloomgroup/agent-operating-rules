## Headline

Treat the next retrospective as a small change cycle, not another discussion. [judgment]

- First, find where past actions stopped: selection, execution, or review.
- Next, commit to one change with an owner and a test.
- Then, check the result before choosing another change.

## Background

People attend your retrospectives, but you report no resulting change. [1] The next step is to inspect what happens after the meeting. [judgment]

## Relevance

A new meeting format does not, by itself, fix missing ownership or blocked authority. [judgment] Therefore, test the path from discussion to action before redesigning the meeting. [judgment]

## Information

**Distinguish three failure points.** These are checks, not a diagnosis. [judgment]

| Failure point | Evidence to seek | Response |
|---|---|---|
| No clear commitment | Notes contain concerns but no specific action | Choose one observable change |
| No execution | An action exists but remains undone | Provide time, authority, or an escalation |
| No feedback | Someone completes an action, but nobody checks its effect | Compare the result with a baseline |

The Scrum Guide describes retrospectives as a way to increase quality and effectiveness through useful changes. [2] Attendance alone does not demonstrate that outcome. [judgment]

**Assumption log**

A1. The team controls at least one small work practice. Why not measured: The material does not state it. Validate by: Check authority over the first proposed change. If wrong: Seek a sponsor before the trial.

**Alternative considered:** Replace the retrospective format or facilitator. That loses as the first move because your report does not establish a discussion-format problem. [judgment]

**Confidence:** Low confidence in the root cause, because the available evidence describes the outcome but not past actions.

## End

I recommend that we use these three steps. The time limits and thresholds below are proposed trial rules, not research benchmarks. [judgment]

### 1. Inspect the last three retrospectives

**Test first, deterministic:**

```text
Given the available notes from the last three retrospectives
When the team checks each agreed action
Then each action has a recorded status and supporting evidence
```

Spend **20 minutes** recording each action, owner, due date, status, and observed result. Mark missing information as “not recorded.”

Classify each action as:
- No clear commitment.
- Agreed but not completed.
- Completed but not checked.
- Completed and checked.

If no notes exist, reconstruct one recent example without assigning blame.

**Capability added:** A visible record of where follow-through stops.

**Domain: Confused.** Gather evidence first because the current account cannot distinguish the causes.

### 2. Write one change card

Step 2 MUST NOT start until Step 1 produces a record, including any gaps.

**Test first, deterministic:**

```text
Given one problem within the team's authority
When the team writes a change card
Then the card names one action, owner, measure, baseline, and review date
```

Spend **15 minutes** writing the card. State what work the team will defer to make room for the action.

For example:

> For five working days, reserve one daily review slot for pending code changes.  
> Owner: a named volunteer.  
> Measure: time from review request to first response.  
> Baseline: the previous five working days.  
> Review date: the next retrospective.

If authority sits elsewhere, replace the proposed change with a named request to that decision-maker.

**Blocker removed:** An agreement without responsibility, time, or a result to check.

**Domain: Clear.** Deduction from a fixed checklist determines whether the card is complete.

### 3. Run one bounded trial, then review the evidence

Step 3 MUST NOT start until the change card passes Step 2’s test.

**Acceptance test first, stochastic:**

- **Metric:** The outcome measure on the change card.
- **Baseline:** The same measure before the trial.
- **Pass threshold:** A specific improvement agreed before the trial, with no agreed guardrail breached.
- **Sample:** One team trial over five working days.
- **Decision limit:** This supports a local next step, not a general causal claim.

Cap extra team effort at **60 minutes**. Stop early if the trial harms service or exceeds that budget.

At the next retrospective, review the result first. Keep, adjust, or stop the change before selecting another.

**Capability added:** Evidence that an action changes work, rather than merely reaching completion.

**Domain: Complex.** Induction from the trial guides the next step; workload shifts can break the pattern. Leverage remains an estimate. [judgment]

## Follow-up

If people report fear of consequences, do not force public disclosure. Use a private route outside the implicated reporting line. [judgment]

If the same external blocker prevents action again, seek a sponsor’s decision rather than another team commitment. [judgment]
