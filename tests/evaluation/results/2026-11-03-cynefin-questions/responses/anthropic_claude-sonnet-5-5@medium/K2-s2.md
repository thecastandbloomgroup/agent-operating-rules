## Background

Three vendors proposed a data warehouse. The budget is 400k USD over three years. The data is 14 TB today and grows by about 3 TB per year [1].

## Relevance

Northwind leaves only about 1k of margin, so a small error in growth or in the overage rule can break the budget. No vendor states staff cost or the price after year 3.

Next, the Northwind overage. Data reaches 17 TB, 20 TB, and 23 TB at the end of years 1, 2, and 3 (A1). Year 3 then has 3 TB over the 20 TB limit. At 9,000 per TB, that adds 27k, so the total is about 399k.

The break point is 3.1 TB of overage (28k divided by 9k). Growth of 3 TB per year is "about" a stated value. So the chance that Northwind stays under 400k is about as likely as not (33 to 66%).

Cedar's hardware holds 30 TB. That is 7 TB above the year 3 volume of 23 TB. The extra 114k of budget equals 38k per year. A second database engineer costs more than 38k per year [judgment]. So Cedar's saving is likely to vanish if the team must add staff.

Availability: 99.9% allows 8.76 hours of downtime per year. 99.95% allows 4.38 hours. Cedar offers no SLA.

Performance: Bluepeak is 38% faster than Northwind. Cedar is 62% slower. The benchmarks use 10 TB, but year 3 data is 23 TB (A3).

Exit: Northwind and Cedar charge no export fee. Bluepeak charges 0.08 per GB. At 23 TB, that is about 1,880 USD [inference].

Bluepeak's cost has no cap, and its 455k already assumes today's query volume. Its estimate is therefore a floor, not a limit.
