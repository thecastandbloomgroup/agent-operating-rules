## Headline

I recommend fixing the path from retrospective to change first, because the likely cause is weak follow-through, not the meeting itself.

- Action items need an owner, a date, and reserved time. [judgment]
- A short audit of past retrospectives shows which cause is real.
- Format changes are small probes, not a redesign.
- Items the team cannot fix need a route to someone who can.

## Background

The team holds retrospectives (regular meetings that review how the team works). People attend, but no change follows. The material gives no data on past action items, team size, or format.

## Relevance

A retrospective that changes nothing costs team time and teaches people that speaking up is pointless. Each further empty session makes that belief harder to reverse. [judgment]

## Information

**Evidence**

1. Retrospective guidance advises few action items per session, so the team can finish them.

   [1]

2. The Scrum Guide says the Scrum Team plans the most impactful improvements into upcoming work.

   [2]

3. Team learning behavior depends on psychological safety (the belief that speaking up is safe). The study covers 51 work teams.

   [3]

4. Follow-through is among the causes of the stall: likely (66 to 100%), based on common patterns, not on your data. [judgment]

5. Only tertiary and secondary sources support these claims. No source here measures your team. [inference]

**Assumption log**

A1. The team works in sprints and keeps a backlog. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask the team lead one question. If wrong: replace "backlog" with the team's own task list in Step 1.

A2. At least six past retrospectives left notes. Why not measured: no tool can reach the team's records. Validate by: count the notes in the wiki. If wrong: run Step 2 on the notes that exist, and lower the confidence in its result.

A3. The team has a manager or sponsor who can act on outside blockers. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: name the person in Step 4. If wrong: Step 4 fails, so find the nearest person with authority.

**Alternative considered**

The strongest alternative is to replace the retrospective format at once (new games, new facilitator). It loses because a new format with no owner or time still produces no change. [judgment]

## End

I recommend that we take these four actions in this order:

1. **Set action-item rules.**
   - Increment: one 30-minute session to write the rules and apply them to open items.
   - Blocker removed: items have no owner, date, or reserved time.
   - Kind: deterministic. Test written first:

     Given a retrospective ends with action items
     When the facilitator closes the session
     Then each item has one owner, a due date, and a backlog entry, and the total is at most two

   - Domain: Clear. One known practice applies, and deduction from that rule gives the action [1].

2. **Audit the last six retrospectives.**
   - Increment: one working session to read the notes and score them.
   - Blocker removed: the true cause is unknown.
   - Kind: evaluative. Rubric written first. Score each cause 0 to 3 from the evidence. A cause counts as present at a score of 2 or more.
   - Pass threshold: at least one cause is present in 3 of 6 retrospectives.

   | Cause | Weight | Evidence needed |
   |---|---|---|
   | No owner or date | 25 | Items in the notes lack a name or date |
   | No time reserved | 25 | Items never appear in the backlog |
   | Item outside team control | 20 | Items name other teams or managers |
   | Same topics recur | 15 | A topic appears in 3 or more notes |
   | Low safety | 15 | Few voices in notes, or a survey score below 3 of 5 |

   - Domain: Complicated. An expert can find the answer by analysis of the notes. This step MUST finish before Step 3 starts.

3. **Probe one format change.**
   - Increment: change one element for three retrospectives (for example, start with a review of last session's items).
   - Blocker removed: low engagement with the meeting.
   - Kind: stochastic. Acceptance test written first.
     - Metric: share of items done by the next session.
     - Baseline: the rate found in Step 2.
     - Pass threshold: at least 75%.
     - Sample size: about six items over three sessions, which is small.
   - Stop condition: end the probe if the 1 to 5 "worth my time" rating drops 1 point below the first session.
   - Budget: 15 minutes of preparation per session.
   - Domain: Complex. The result shows only after the trial. Induction from trials applies, and a new element could break the pattern if the team treats it as a gimmick. Leverage is a guess. This probe runs in parallel with Steps 1 and 2.

4. **Probe an escalation route.**
   - Increment: invite the sponsor from A3 for the last 15 minutes of two retrospectives.
   - Blocker removed: items outside team control stall.
   - Kind: stochastic. Acceptance test written first.
     - Metric: escalated items that get a reply within five working days.
     - Baseline: zero, since no route exists today (A3).
     - Pass threshold: 2 of 3 items.
     - Sample size: three items.
   - Stop condition: end the probe if the sponsor misses both sessions.
   - Domain: Complex. The sponsor's response shows only after the trial. Induction applies, and a sponsor who lacks authority could break the pattern. Leverage is a guess.

Step 3 MUST NOT adopt the format change until Step 2 confirms that engagement, not follow-through, is a cause. If Step 2 shows low safety as the main cause, replace Step 3 with an anonymous input round [3].
