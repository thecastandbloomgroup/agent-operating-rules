## Headline

**Choose Northwind Analytics, and sign only if its year-3 overage is capped so the total stays at or under 400,000.**

- Cost: Northwind totals about 399,000, Bluepeak 455,000 breaches the budget, and Cedar at 286,000 excludes staff time.
- Operations: Northwind and Bluepeak offer an SLA (service level agreement, a promised uptime), but Cedar offers none and relies on one engineer.
- Performance: all three figures are vendor benchmarks on 10 TB, and Northwind's 2.1 s is second of three.
- Exit: Northwind exports free, while Bluepeak charges about 1,840 to export 23 TB.

## Background

The team has three bids for a data warehouse. The budget is 400,000 over three years. The data is 14 TB today and grows about 3 TB per year.

## Relevance

The Northwind margin under the budget is about 1,000. A small change in growth or in billing rules decides if the choice holds.

## Information

**Evidence.** Source for the figures below: proposals.txt, all proposals [1].

| Item | Northwind | Bluepeak | Cedar |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stated 3-year cost | 372,000 | 455,000 | 286,000 |
| Cost at 23 TB in year 3 | about 399,000 | 455,000 or more | 286,000 |
| SLA | 99.9% | 99.95% | none |
| Median query time (10 TB) | 2.1 s | 1.3 s | 3.4 s |
| Migration time | 10 weeks | 16 weeks | 12 weeks |
| Capacity | 20 TB, then 9,000 per TB | no cap | 30 TB |

- Northwind data grows to 23 TB by year 3 (14 + 3 + 3 + 3). That is 3 TB over the 20 TB tier, so the overage is 3 × 9,000 = 27,000. The total is 372,000 + 27,000 = 399,000 [inference].
- Bluepeak is 55,000 over budget at current query volume. Its price has no cap, so growth adds cost [inference].
- Cedar leaves 114,000 of headroom, or 38,000 per year. Part-time database work by an engineer likely costs more than that [judgment].
- A 99.9% SLA allows 8.76 hours of downtime per year (0.001 × 8,760 h) [inference].
- Cedar has no SLA, and uptime depends on one database engineer [1].
- No vendor states staff cost or the price after year 3 [1].
- Northwind stays within 400,000: about as likely as not, because the margin is 0.25% and growth is only "about" 3 TB [judgment].

**Rubric (Kepner-Tregoe [2]).** Musts: total at or under 400,000, and a written uptime SLA.

- Northwind passes both musts.
- Bluepeak fails the cost must.
- Cedar fails the SLA must.

Northwind also has a free export and the shortest migration, 10 weeks, which supports the choice.

**Assumption log**

A1. Growth is 3 TB per year, and Northwind bills overage on year-end size. Why not measured: the material states only "about 3 TB" and does not state the billing date. Validate by: ask Northwind in writing how it measures size. If wrong: the total passes 400,000, and Step 1 must change the terms.

A2. The 400,000 budget covers vendor charges only, not staff time. Why not measured: the user has not answered. Validate by: ask finance (Step 3). If wrong: Cedar fails the budget test even more clearly, and Northwind's 1,000 margin disappears.

A3. The 10 TB benchmark times hold at 14 to 23 TB. Why not measured: no tool can reach the vendor systems. Validate by: run the 12 reference queries in a trial (Step 2). If wrong: the performance ranking can change.

**Alternative considered.** Cedar is the cheapest bid and has the most room to grow. It loses because it has no SLA, depends on one engineer, and has an unstated staff cost [judgment].

## End

I recommend that we take these four steps, in this order.

1. **Cap the Northwind overage in writing.**
   - Increment: one negotiation session that ends with a draft clause.
   - Blocker removed: the 1,000 margin and the unclear billing date (A1).
   - Domain: Complicated. An expert can deduce the answer by calculation from the contract terms.
   - Kind: deterministic. Test written first:

   ```gherkin
   Given the draft Northwind contract
   When the data grows from 14 TB by 3 TB per year
   Then the three-year total is at most 400,000
   And the clause states the date when size is measured
   ```

2. **Run a Northwind trial with our 12 reference queries at 14 TB.**
   - Step 2 runs in parallel with Step 1.
   - Increment: five working days, no vendor fee.
   - Blocker removed: unverified benchmark (A3).
   - Domain: Complex. The result shows only after a trial, and a different data shape could break the pattern. The leverage is a guess.
   - Kind: stochastic. Acceptance test written first: the median query time must be at most 2.5 s, against the 2.1 s baseline [1]. The sample is 60 timings (12 queries × 5 runs).
   - Stop condition: stop if the trial needs a paid commitment.

3. **Ask finance if the 400,000 includes staff time.**
   - Increment: one written answer.
   - Blocker removed: A2.
   - Domain: Clear. Finance applies one known budget rule.
   - Kind: deterministic. Test written first:

   ```gherkin
   Given the 400,000 budget line
   When finance reads the line
   Then finance states in writing if staff time counts
   ```

4. **Sign with Northwind.** Step 4 MUST NOT start until the tests in Steps 1 and 2 pass and Step 3 gives its answer.
   - Increment: one signed contract.
   - Blocker removed: no decision.
   - Domain: Clear. Signing follows a known practice once the checks pass.
   - Kind: deterministic. Test written first:

   ```gherkin
   Given Steps 1, 2 and 3 are complete
   When the contract is signed
   Then it contains the overage cap and the 99.9% SLA
   ```
