## Headline

Fix follow-through first: the retrospective produces talk but no owned, tracked change.

- The cause is likely missing ownership and tracking of actions, not a lack of ideas [judgment].
- A short audit of past actions confirms or refutes this cause within one session.
- A strict action rule and a review of last time's actions then close the gap.
- A format change is a separate, capped trial, not the main fix.

## Background

The team holds retrospectives (regular meetings to review work and plan changes). People attend, but behavior and results do not change. No data on past sessions came with the request.

## Relevance

A retrospective that changes nothing costs team time each cycle. It also teaches people that raising problems has no effect, so they stop raising them.

## Information

**Evidence**

1. A retrospective has five phases: set the stage, gather data, generate insights, decide what to do, and close. The "decide what to do" phase is where change begins [1].

2. Teams often skip or rush that phase. Most sessions then end with talk and no owned action [judgment].

3. The likelihood that missing follow-through is the main cause is "likely (66 to 100%)". Confidence is low, because there is no team data [judgment].

4. Domain choice per action uses the Cynefin framework, which sorts work by how cause and effect relate [2].

**Assumption log**

A1. The team holds retrospectives on a regular cycle, about every two weeks. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: check the calendar for the last three sessions. If wrong: Step 3 needs a new timing.

A2. The team can complete most of its own actions without outside approval. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: Step 1 tags each undone item as "outside team control" or not. If wrong: add an action to escalate blockers to a sponsor.

A3. The main cause is weak follow-through, not poor topics or low trust. Why not measured: the user has not answered, and no tool can reach team records. Validate by: Step 1 audit. If wrong: replace Steps 2 and 3 with a trust and topic review.

**Alternative considered**

The strongest alternative is to replace the retrospective with a new format at once. It loses because a new format does not fix actions that lack an owner. Its payoff is also a guess [judgment].

## End

I recommend that we take these four actions, in this order.

1. **Audit the last three retrospectives.** Kind: evaluative. Domain: Confused. The cause is unknown, so we cannot yet tell whether analysis or a trial fits. This step gathers what we need to classify.
   - Increment: one 60-minute session. List each past action with owner, due date, and status. Tag each undone item with a cause: no owner, too large, no time, or outside team control.
   - Blocker removed: the unknown cause.
   - Test (rubric, written first): criteria and weights are status evidence per item (40), cause tag per undone item (40), and a ranked count of causes (20). The pass threshold is 80 of 100. Evidence is the backlog, meeting notes, and chat history.

2. **Adopt an action rule.** Kind: deterministic. Domain: Clear. One known practice applies, and we deduce it from the "decide what to do" phase [1]. Step 2 MUST NOT start until Step 1 passes its rubric.
   - Increment: apply the rule at the next retrospective.
   - Blocker removed: actions with no owner or date.
   - Test:
     - Given the retrospective has ended
     - When the facilitator reads the action list
     - Then each action has one owner, one due date, and one backlog ticket
     - And the list has at most two actions

3. **Open each retrospective with a review of last time's actions.** Kind: deterministic. Domain: Clear. The practice is known, and we deduce it from the rule in Step 2. Step 3 MUST NOT start until Step 2 is in use.
   - Increment: a 10-minute agenda item at the next session.
   - Blocker removed: no one checks whether actions finish.
   - Test:
     - Given a previous retrospective produced actions
     - When the new session opens
     - Then the team states the status of each action before any new topic

4. **Run a capped trial of a new format.** Kind: stochastic. Domain: Complex. The result shows only after a trial, so we use induction from trials. The pattern could break if team size or workload changes. Leverage is a guess. This step runs in parallel with Steps 2 and 3.
   - Increment: use a "one problem, deep dive" format with a rotating facilitator for two sessions.
   - Blocker removed: shallow discussion that yields no concrete action.
   - Test (written first): the metric is actions done by the next session. The baseline is the Step 1 rate. The pass threshold is 3 of 4 actions done across two sessions. The sample size is 4 actions, which is small, so the result is a signal and not proof.
   - Stop condition: revert if fewer than 1 of 4 actions finish after the first session. Budget: 30 extra minutes of preparation per session.

## Follow-up

Condition that changes the recommendation: if A3 is false, replace Steps 2 and 3 with a trust and topic review.

Condition that changes the recommendation: if Step 1 shows most undone items are outside team control, add an escalation step before Step 2.
