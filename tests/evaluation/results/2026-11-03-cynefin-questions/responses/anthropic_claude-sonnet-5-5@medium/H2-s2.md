## Headline

Name one decision owner now, hold all outside comment, verify the facts, then send one approved response.

- **Control:** Three groups give three different orders, so a single owner and channel must come first.
- **Facts:** The response depends on whether the CEO's statement was false, so a documented fact check must come before any position.
- **Position:** After the facts, choose one stance (stand by, clarify, or correct) with a scored comparison, not by who pushes hardest.
- **Message:** Test the approved message on one small audience first, then widen it.

## Background

A journalist published a report today that says the CEO's statement from yesterday was false. Legal, PR, and the board each give you separate guidance. No one owns the decision.

## Relevance

Each hour without one owner raises the chance of a second, conflicting statement. A conflicting statement would add a new error to the story.

## Information

**Evidence**

- The conflict is about who decides, not about what is true. [judgment]
- The situation has live harm and no agreed process. That fits the Chaotic domain of the Cynefin framework, where the first move is to stabilise [1].
- Fact checking is work an expert can do from documents, so it fits the Complicated domain [1].
- The reaction to a new message shows only after you send it. That fits the Complex domain, so a small probe is the right form [1].

**Assumption log**

A1. The article is at least partly accurate: about as likely as not (33 to 66%). Why not measured: the material does not state the facts. Validate by: compare each disputed claim with its source document in a one-hour review. If wrong: Step 3 shifts from "correct" to "stand by", and legal drafts a rebuttal request.

A2. You hold enough rank to convene the CEO, legal, PR, and the board chair. Why not measured: the user has not stated a role. Validate by: ask the board chair to confirm the owner in writing. If wrong: Step 1 becomes a request to the board chair to name the owner.

A3. The company faces disclosure rules, for example as a listed or regulated firm. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask counsel in one message. If wrong: drop the disclosure item from the Step 2 rubric.

**Alternative considered**

The strongest alternative is an immediate public denial that backs the CEO. It loses because under A1 it is about as likely as not that the claim is wrong, and a second false statement would cost more than a delay of a few hours. [judgment]

## End

I recommend that we take these four actions in order:

1. **Name one decision owner and one channel, and hold all outside comment.**
   - Kind: deterministic. Domain: Chaotic, because harm is live and there is no time to analyze, so we act first.
   - Increment: one written message to the CEO and board chair that asks for a named owner, sent within the hour.
   - Blocker removed: three groups give conflicting orders.
   - Test:
     - Given legal, PR, and the board each send separate instructions
     - When the board chair names the owner in writing
     - Then each group confirms in the channel, and no outside statement leaves without the owner's sign-off

2. **Verify the disputed claims against source documents.**
   - Kind: evaluative. Domain: Complicated, because counsel and the business owner can deduce the answer from documents.
   - Increment: a one-page table with one row per disputed claim.
   - Blocker removed: no one knows which parts of the report are true.
   - Test (rubric, pass threshold 80 of 100 per claim to label it "supported"):
     - Source document exists and is dated: weight 40. Evidence: the document itself.
     - Independent confirmation from a second person or record: weight 30. Evidence: a name or file.
     - Wording and context match the CEO's statement: weight 20. Evidence: the transcript.
     - Disclosure exposure reviewed by counsel (A3): weight 10. Evidence: counsel's note.
   - Step 3 MUST NOT start until Step 2 is scored for every claim.

3. **Choose the response position: stand by, clarify, or correct.**
   - Kind: evaluative. Domain: Complicated, because the Step 2 scores let an expert deduce the best option.
   - Increment: a Pugh matrix [P1 below is not used; see note] with three options scored against the baseline "hold, no comment".
   - Blocker removed: no shared basis for the decision.
   - Test: criteria are accuracy (weight 40), legal exposure (30), trust of staff and investors (20), and speed (10). The owner approves only an option that scores above the baseline on accuracy and on the weighted total.
   - Step 4 MUST NOT start until the owner and the board chair approve the position.

4. **Probe the approved message on employees only.**
   - Kind: stochastic. Domain: Complex, because the reaction shows only after we send the message. This is induction from one trial, and a new leak or a second report could break the pattern.
   - Increment: one short note to staff, with a budget of two working hours.
   - Blocker removed: no evidence of how the message lands.
   - Test: the metric is the share of staff replies coded "unclear or distrustful". The baseline is the 100% of staff who have heard nothing. The pass threshold is 20% or less. The sample is at least 30 replies, or all replies after 24 hours.
   - Stop condition: stop and return to Step 3 if a new claim or leak appears.
   - Run Step 4 in parallel with the Step 2 follow-up work, and do not widen the audience until the threshold is met.

- If regulators or investors contact us, add counsel as a co-owner of Step 3.
