## Headline

Name one decision owner now, verify the facts in two hours, then answer publicly with one approved statement.

- Alignment fails because three groups give advice and no one decides, so fix ownership first.
- The right response depends on whether the CEO's statement was false, so verify before you choose a message.
- A public statement needs Legal and board approval, so route all drafts through one channel.

## Background

A journalist published today (Nov 3, 2026) that the CEO's statement from yesterday was false. Legal, PR, and the board each give you separate direction. You ask for next steps.

## Relevance

Each hour without one position lets the article stand as the only public account. Three conflicting messages from inside the company also add a second story.

## Information

**Evidence**

- Response strategy should match the level of responsibility the public assigns to the organization [judgment on fit; theory per the source below].

- Whether the statement is false is about as likely as not. No evidence is in the material. [judgment]
- Harm is not happening this minute, and the response window is hours. Therefore no action is Chaotic. [judgment]
- Silence until Legal finishes review is a common default. It leaves the journalist's version unanswered. [inference]

**Assumption log**

A1. The article is public but not yet widely repeated. Why not measured: the material does not state reach. Validate by: search the headline and count outlets that repeat it. If wrong: if it is widely repeated, shorten Step 2 to 1 hour.

A2. No one has yet checked the statement against records. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask Legal and PR if a fact check exists. If wrong: skip Step 2 and start at Step 3.

A3. You have authority to propose a single owner, or to ask the CEO to do so. Why not measured: the material does not state your role. Validate by: send the Step 1 message and watch for objection. If wrong: ask the board chair to name the owner.

**Alternative considered**

Hold all comment until Legal completes a full review. It loses because a review can take days, and the board and press will not wait. [judgment]

## End

I recommend that we take these five actions in this order.

1. **Name one decision owner and one channel.**
   - Increment: send one message within 30 minutes.
   - Blocker removed: three groups direct you and none decides.
   - Kind: deterministic. Test, written first:

     ```text
     Given Legal, PR, and the board chair each hold an open thread
     When the owner message is sent
     Then all three confirm in writing that the owner decides
     And all drafts go through one channel
     ```

   - Domain: Clear. One known practice applies (a single incident lead). The inference is deduction from a known rule [2].

2. **Verify the CEO's statement against records.**
   - Increment: a claim-by-claim table in 2 hours.
   - Blocker removed: no shared facts.
   - Kind: evaluative. Rubric, written first:
     - Criteria and weights: match to primary records 50%, article claim traced to its source 30%, second reviewer agrees 20%.
     - Pass threshold: each claim scores 70 of 100 or carries the label "unverified".
     - Evidence needed: documents, data, and emails.
   - Domain: Complicated. An analyst can answer from the records. The inference is deduction from analysis.
   - Step 3 MUST NOT start until Step 2 passes.

3. **Choose one response option.**
   - Increment: one scored one-page brief for a 30-minute call with Legal, PR, and the board chair.
   - Blocker removed: no shared basis for a decision.
   - Kind: evaluative, Kepner-Tregoe layout [3].
     - Options: correct, clarify, defend, hold.
     - Musts: legally allowed (Legal confirms) and supported by Step 2.
     - Weighted wants: public credibility 30%, board trust 30%, legal exposure 25%, speed 15%.
     - Pass: the highest weighted score among options that meet all musts.
   - Domain: Complicated. More than one good practice exists, and Step 2 picks among them. The inference is deduction.

4. **Write and publish the statement.**
   - Increment: one statement of under 200 words.
   - Blocker removed: no public answer.
   - Kind: deterministic. Test, written first [4]:

     ```text
     Given the chosen option and the verified claim table
     When PR drafts the statement
     Then every sentence maps to a verified row
     And Legal and the board chair approve in writing
     And no claim marked "unverified" appears
     ```

   - Domain: Complicated. The chosen option follows from Step 3. The inference is deduction.
   - Step 4 MUST NOT publish until Step 3 completes and the Step 5 probe ends.

5. **Run a safe-to-fail probe on the draft.**
   - Increment: give the draft to 20 internal readers who do not work on the response.
   - Blocker removed: unknown reaction to the wording.
   - Kind: stochastic. Acceptance test, written first:
     - Metric: share of readers who restate the correction accurately.
     - Baseline: 0 readers before reading.
     - Pass threshold: at least 16 of 20 (80%).
     - Sample size: 20.
     - Budget: 90 minutes. Stop at once if the draft leaks.
   - Domain: Complex. Reaction shows only after a trial. The inference is induction from trials. The pattern could break if outside readers differ from staff. The leverage of this step is a guess.
   - Run it in parallel with the drafting in Step 4.

If A2 is false, remove Step 2 and use the existing fact check in Step 3. If Step 2 shows the statement was true, replace Steps 3 and 4 with an evidence package and a correction request to the journalist, reviewed by Legal.
