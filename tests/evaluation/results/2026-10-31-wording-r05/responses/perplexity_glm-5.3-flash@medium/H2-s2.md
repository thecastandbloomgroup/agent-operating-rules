## Headline

Stabilize first, then align, then respond. One fact-freeze directive stops the bleeding within one hour. One joint call aligns legal, PR, and the board on facts within two hours. One evidence pack decides the strategy. One approved statement reaches the public on day one.

## Background

The company faces a public statement from the CEO that a journalist reports as false. Legal, PR, and the board are messaging independently. No alignment exists as of now.

## Relevance

Each uncoordinated message raises legal and reputational risk. The first two hours decide whether the company speaks with one voice or three.

## Information

- Every external channel is a live risk surface: an uncontrolled message cannot be retracted. [judgment]
- The falsity of the statement is unverified in both directions: the article may be right, wrong, or right in part. [judgment]
- Split internal responses produce the strongest hostile story: "the company does not agree with itself". [judgment]

**Confidence**

## End

I recommend that we run one coordinated response with five steps.

1. **Freeze all external messages.** Each spokesperson (CEO, legal, PR, board members) sends no new public message for 4 hours. It removes the blocker of conflicting statements. Test, written before the work:
   - Given the directive is sent to all four groups, when 60 minutes pass, Then zero new external messages appear.
   - Deterministic, because the count of outgoing messages has a known cause and effect. Domain: Chaotic, because it stabilizes before any analysis.
2. **Convene the joint alignment call.** Legal, PR, one board delegate, and the CEO office meet within 2 hours. Output: one named owner and a written one-page fact summary. It removes the blocker of three independent message tracks. Dependency: step 2 MUST NOT start before the directive in step 1 is sent. Test, written before the work:
   - Given all parties confirm attendance, when the call ends, Then one written owner name and one fact summary exist and all parties accept them.
   - Evaluative, scored against a rubric: owner named (weight 40%), facts agreed (40%), availability confirmed (20%), pass threshold 80% with party sign-off as evidence. Domain: Chaotic, because it restores order by directive. Domain label: Chaotic, because stabilization precedes analysis.
3. **Collect the evidence pack.** Transcript or recording of the statement, the article, and internal records, shared in one place. It removes the blocker of judging the statement without material. Dependency: step 3 uses the owner named in step 2. Test, written before the work:
   - Given the response owner requests the sources, when 4 hours pass, Then one pack exists with each item dated and sourced.
   - Deterministic, because item presence is checkable. Domain: Confused, because this step gathers what is needed to classify the true situation.
4. **Run the fact review and pick the strategy.** Legal analyzes the evidence against the article. Three strategies: publicly confirm the article, correct the article, or take no public reply. Rubric first: criteria are legal exposure (weight 40%), factual accuracy (40%), audience reach (20%), pass threshold 2 of 3 criteria at majority source support. Test, written before the work:
   - Given the evidence pack from step 3, when the scored rubric exists, Then legal agrees that one of the three strategies is selected, in writing.
   - Evaluative. Domain: Complicated, because analysis and legal expertise pick among more than one good practice.
5. **Release the approved statement, then probe audience reaction.** The named owner publishes the statement through one channel and one spokesperson. Then run a capped probe: measure coverage and sentiment for 48 hours on a sample of 20 articles, baseline = coverage tone in the 24 hours before release, pass threshold = no increase in negative share. Stop condition: stop the probe when the 20-article sample is complete. Budget: one analyst, 48 hours. Test, written before the work: acceptance test above, with metric, baseline, threshold, and sample size stated. Stochastic, because reception varies run to run. Domain: Complex, because reaction is visible only afterward; the probe stays small and does not commit further action.

Dependency rule: steps 4 and 5 MUST NOT start before the test in step 3 passes.

If the evidence pack shows the CEO was factually correct, replace step 5 with the silent-wait alternative in Information.
