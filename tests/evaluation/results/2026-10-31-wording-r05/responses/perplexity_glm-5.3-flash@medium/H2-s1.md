## Headline

Stabilize first: freeze all messaging, name one decision owner and one response channel today, then verify the claim against the record before any public statement. Supporting points:

1. One 30-minute decision call today ends the parallel messages from legal, PR, and the board.
2. A same-day fact review decides, from the record, whether the statement was false.
3. A short holding statement buys time; the full response follows only after the fact review.
4. One spokesperson and one channel carry every message from now on.

## Background

The company issued a CEO statement yesterday. Today a journalist published that it was false, and three internal groups are sending the user different directions. The user asks for next steps.

## Relevance

The first hours decide whether the story grows or ends. Mixed internal messages leak, and a leaked conflict becomes a second story [judgment].

## Information

Evidence: this assessment is qualitative, built on crisis-response practice; the analysis behind each step is [judgment]. Two themes emerge:

- Theme: one decision owner prevents the parallel-channel problem. Supported by 2 of 3 framing sources [3], [5]; none disagree.
- Theme: verify before you speak, because a second retraction compounds the first error. Supported by 2 of 3 [3], [4]; none disagree.

The numbers and the themes agree, so confidence is high on the process, not on the facts. One fact is unknown: whether the statement was false. That drives the final response, and the record review in Step 2 is the cheapest test of it.

Assumption log:

A1. The article is public and reaching audiences. Why not measured: the material gives no URL or reach. Validate by: pull the article link and today's coverage count. If wrong: the holding statement in Step 4 becomes optional.

A2. A verbatim record of the statement exists (recording, transcript, or written text). Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask the chief of staff for the record today. If wrong: verification moves to witness interviews, and the timeline roughly doubles [judgment].

A3. The claim centers on a checkable fact (a number, a date, an event), not on a projection or intention. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: read the article's exact wording against a copy of the statement. If wrong: the response changes from correction to clarification, and Scenario B in Step 5 changes.

Alternative considered: publish a full correction or denial immediately, before verification. It loses because a public reversal of a retraction would damage credibility more than one day of silence [judgment].

## End

I recommend that we take these five steps, in this order:

1. **Decision call (Chaotic, because cause and effect are not yet visible and the job is to stabilize).** Convene a 30-minute call today with the general counsel, the comms lead, and the board chair. Its output: one named decision owner and one response channel, both written down. Test, written first (deterministic):
   - Given the call has ended, When anyone checks the written call summary, Then the summary names one decision owner and one media channel.
2. **Fact review (Complicated, because the answer is knowable with analysis).** Collect the CEO statement verbatim, the article verbatim, and any underlying document, and compare them line by line. Output: a one-page memo that says which statement is false, if any, with citations to the documents. This step waits on Step 1 only for the owner assignment. Test, written first (evaluative rubric): criteria are verbatim match (weight 0.4), external document support (weight 0.4), and residual ambiguity (weight 0.2); pass threshold is 0.8 of 1.0; evidence is the documents themselves, scored in the memo. If the facts stay ambiguous, say so in the memo and treat the domain as Confused: the next step is to gather what classifies the claim.
3. **Freeze and route (Clear, because the best practice is known to everyone).** One step: all outbound statements stop, and every inbound media question goes to the single channel. Step 3 starts when Step 1 ends. Test, written first (deterministic):
   - Given the freeze notice is sent, When legal, PR, or the board receives a media question, Then each routes it to the named channel without an individual reply.
4. **Holding statement probe (Complex, because the audience reaction is visible only afterward; leverage is a guess).** Publish a short statement: the company is aware of the report, is reviewing the record, and will respond. Run it as a safe-to-fail probe with a measure (count of follow-up stories in 24 hours against today's baseline), a stop condition (escalate to a corrective release if follow-ups rise), and a budget (24 hours). Step 4 runs in parallel with Step 2 and does not depend on it. Acceptance test, written first (stochastic): metric is count of follow-up stories, baseline is today's coverage count, pass threshold is no increase, sample size is all coverage in 24 hours.
5. **Scenario drafts (split into two parts).** Part 5a, deterministic: draft the correction statement and the rebuttal statement, both today. Test, written first:
   - Given the two drafts exist, When anyone reads each draft, Then each cites the record or the article, and neither names an unverified source. Part 5b, evaluative: select one draft as a gate after Step 2's memo passes its threshold, scored on the Step 2 rubric criteria. If the memo says the article is wrong, the rebuttal goes to the journalist with a correction request. If the memo says the statement was false, the correction comes from the CEO first, then to the board before publication.

## Follow-up

Conditions that change the recommendation: if A2 is false and no verbatim record exists, insert witness interviews before Step 2 and extend the holding statement period. If the reporter offers an advance briefing of the article's evidence, take it before Step 2 closes. If regulators or auditors are involved, the general counsel takes the decision owner role in Step 1.
