# Headline

Recommend Proposal 1: Northwind Analytics.
- It fits the 400,000 USD budget even at the projected year-3 volume of 23 TB, with capped pricing.
- Bluepeak is 13.8% over budget, and its consumption price has no cap.
- Cedar is cheapest on paper, but it adds run cost to a team with one database engineer and no service level agreement (SLA).
- Northwind has the only vendor-run, 10-week migration in the set.

# Background

Three vendor proposals for a data warehouse arrived on 2026-10-10. The task is to recommend one within a budget of 400,000 USD over three years. All vendor figures come from proposals.txt [1].

# Relevance

The choice fixes the three-year cost, the operations load, and the exit path. Terms can be negotiated only before a signature.

# Information

**Evidence**

1. **Cost against the budget of 400,000 USD.** The bids price at 372,000, 455,000, and 286,000 USD [1]. Northwind is 7.0% under budget, Bluepeak is 13.8% over, and Cedar is 28.5% under [inference: arithmetic against the stated budget].
2. **Growth against the licence cap.** Current data is 14 TB, and the team states growth of about 3 TB per year [1]. The projected volume at the end of year 3 is 23 TB [inference]. Northwind's licence covers up to 20 TB, and each extra TB costs 9,000 USD per year [1], so year 3 adds up to 27,000 USD [inference]. The three-year total stays at or under 399,000 USD under assumption A1 [inference].
3. **Query performance.** All vendors benchmarked the same 12 queries on the same 10 TB sample [1]. That is a like-for-like baseline: Bluepeak is 38% faster than Northwind, and Cedar is 62% slower [inference]. The file states no latency requirement, so see A2.
4. **Availability.** A 99.9% SLA allows about 8.8 h of downtime per year, and 99.95% allows about 4.4 h [inference]. Cedar offers no SLA, so uptime depends on the team [1].
5. **Cedar total cost with staff time.** No vendor prices staff time [1]. At a judged 0.3 FTE (full-time equivalent) for self-hosting, about 40,000 USD per year, Cedar reaches about 406,000 USD over three years and loses its lead [judgment].
6. **Themes in the file's statements.** One theme is supported by three statements from one source: Cedar's running cost sits with the team, because it has no SLA, a headcount of one database engineer, and next-day response for severity 1 faults [1]. No statement in the file disagrees. One source is few, but the theme drives the score because staff time is the stated gap in all three bids [1].

Type: proposal 1 is a managed warehouse, proposal 2 is consumption-priced, proposal 3 is self-hosted open source with a support contract [1].

**Assumption log**

- A1. Growth stays at about 3 TB per year. Why not measured: the material states only a team estimate. Validate by: monthly volume reports for 6 months (Step 4). If wrong: growth near 5 TB per year pushes Northwind overflow near 100,000 USD and breaches the budget.
- A2. A median query time of 2.1 s is acceptable. Why not measured: the user has not stated a latency requirement. Validate by: confirm with query owners, then the Step 2 proof of concept (POC). If wrong: Bluepeak, 1.3 s, becomes the target, and it is 13.8% over budget.
- A3. Northwind's managed service needs about 0.1 FTE of team time [judgment]. Why not measured: the material does not state managed-service staffing. Validate by: log ops hours during the POC. If wrong: more staff time narrows Cedar's comparison but does not change the budget fit.
- A4. Year-4 prices stay proportionate. Why not measured: no vendor states post-year-3 pricing. Validate by: request a written renewal price in Step 1. If wrong: this affects renewal, not the three-year decision.

**Alternative considered**

The strongest alternative is Bluepeak with a negotiated spend cap. It loses because 455,000 USD is 13.8% over budget [1], and a spend cap is a new commercial term that no proposal offers.

Confidence in the cost ranking is high, because all figures come from one primary file, and each is a direct comparison against the stated budget. The recommendation depends on A1 and A2.

# End

I recommend that we sign Northwind Analytics, executed in the four steps below. Each test is written before the work.

**Step 1: Negotiate the headroom and the renewal price.**
- Increment: one written Northwind quote covering up to 25 TB and a stated year-4 price.
- Blocker: removes the overflow cost risk (A1) and the unknown renewal price (A4).
- Test, Gherkin form [2]: "Given a written Northwind quote, When we price the projected year-3 volume of 23 TB, Then the three-year total is at or under 400,000 USD."
- Domain: Clear, because the commercial step and its outcome are known in advance. Kind: deterministic.

**Step 2: Run a two-week POC on the Northwind tenant.**
- Increment: load a 1 TB sample, run the 12 reference queries, trial-move one schema, and export the sample to Parquet.
- Blocker: tests A2, the 10-week migration claim, and the no-fee exit claim.
- Rubric, written first: criteria and weights are median query latency 40%, migration hours per TB 30%, weekly ops hours 20%, export effort 10%. Pass threshold: weighted score of at least 3.5 of 5, scored against timed runs and ops logs.
- Domain: Complicated, because analysis of the benchmark results must pick the practice. Kind: evaluative.

**Step 3: Sign and start the migration.**
- Increment: signatures plus a migration start date.
- Blocker: starts the vendor-run migration of the live 14 TB.
- Test: "Given the Step 1 test passes and the Step 2 score is at least 3.5 of 5, When the migration completes, Then all 14 TB are live within 10 weeks."
- Domain: Clear, because the vendor states a 10-week plan and the trigger conditions are fixed [1]. Kind: deterministic.
- Dependency: Step 3 MUST NOT start until the Step 1 test passes and the Step 2 rubric meets its threshold.

**Step 4: Check growth against the headroom for six months.**
- Increment: one monthly volume report, six readings.
- Blocker: validates A1 and confirms the 25 TB headroom is enough.
- Acceptance test, written first: metric is TB growth per 6 months; baseline is 1.5 TB; pass threshold is at or under 1.5 TB; sample size is 6 monthly readings. A reading above the threshold triggers a headroom purchase.
- Domain: Clear, because the measure and the response are agreed in advance. Kind: stochastic.

Condition that changes the recommendation: if the Step 2 rubric fails on the latency criterion, re-quote Bluepeak with a spend cap before any signature.
