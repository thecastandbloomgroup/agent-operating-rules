# Headline

Restore the connection pool settings from release 2026.10.21, and verify the OPS-412 limit first; keep the ORM change and the cache change is secondary. The evidence: the pool cut added the wait, the timeout cut turned the wait into errors, and the cache and ORM changes did not slow the queries. The measured split: pool acquire wait is 690 ms of the 900 ms p95, and pool exhausted warnings rose from none to 1,800 to 2,400 per hour [1][3].

## Background

P95 latency rose from 120 ms to 900 ms after release 2026.10.21. The release touched three parts: the ORM query, the cache TTL, and the connection pool.

## Relevance

The error rate rose from 0.02% to 0.40%, and 96% of the errors are pool timeouts [3]. That means lost requests now, not only slow ones.

## Information

The three changes and their measured effects:

| Change | Before | After | Measured effect | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pool max_size | 50 | 8 per pod | acquire p95 3 ms to 690 ms; warnings 0 to 1,800 to 2,400 per hour | Primary cause [1][2][3] |
| acquire_timeout_s | 30 | 5 | PoolTimeout is 96% of errors; 503s at 5,000 ms | Converts waiting into errors [1][3] |
| Cache TTL | 300 s | 60 s | miss rate 12% to 31%; DB CPU 38% to 41% | Adds load, secondary [3] |
| ORM selectinload | not set | set | query p95 flat: orders 52 to 58 ms, order_lines 30 to 26 ms | Not a cause; keep [3] |

A capacity check supports the verdict. Per pod: about 101 requests per second (405 over 4 pods) and 40 to 60 ms of DB work hold about 4 to 6 connections at once [judgment]. With only 8 slots per pod, bursts push hold counts past 8, so requests queue. The database itself has headroom, because CPU went from 38% to 41% and query latencies stayed flat [3]. Therefore the bottleneck is the pool, not the database.

`A2. Connections are held only during queries. Why not measured: traces do not report hold time. Validate by: compare connection checkin time with query end time in one trace. If wrong: a leak holds connections, and config alone does not fix it.`

Alternative considered: the ORM selectinload caused the regression. It loses because exact per-query latencies stayed flat and the added wait sits in pool acquire, not in queries [3].
