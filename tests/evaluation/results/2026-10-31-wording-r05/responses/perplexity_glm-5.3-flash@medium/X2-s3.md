## Headline

Diagnose where retrospective action items die, then cap each retrospective at one owned, dated, and reviewed action, with a safety probe in parallel.

1. Stalled retrospectives fail at follow-through more than at discussion [judgment], high confidence, because the method's own guide makes tracked items its core step [1].
2. A cap of one action item, with a named owner and a due date, closes the follow-through gap [1].
3. A short anonymous poll tests the second cause: topics that people avoid raising in the room [2].

## Background

The team holds retrospectives, which are regular meetings that review how the work went. People attend, but no process change follows. The request asks for a fix.

## Relevance

Each wasted session costs about an hour per attendee [judgment]. Without a fix, known problems stay open and the meeting stays a cost with no return.

## Information

**Evidence:**

- The retrospective method's guide says to pick one or two improvement items, assign an owner, and check them at the next meeting [1].
- Team effectiveness research at Google ranks psychological safety first of five factors [2].
- Attendance that holds while changes stall points to follow-through or safety as the two candidate causes [judgment]. Both fit the sources above, so the plan covers both.

**Assumption log:**

A1. Retros produce written action items today. Why not measured: the message does not state the current format. Validate by: read the notes of the last two retros. If wrong: skip the audit in Step 1 and build a written actions log first.
A2. The team can change at least some of its own practices without sign-off. Why not measured: no team charter exists in the material. Validate by: one question to the team lead. If wrong: all systemic routes in Step 4 go through that manager only.

**Uncertainty:** Which cause holds is about as likely as not to be follow-through rather than safety before the audit runs. Therefore both run in parallel.

**Alternative considered:** Replace the retrospective with a different meeting format. It loses because no measurement yet shows the meeting itself fails; the audit in Step 1 costs one session and decides with data [judgment].

## End

I recommend that we:

1. **Audit the last six retros.** Domain: Complicated, because analysis must pick the failing stage before later steps order by leverage. Increment: one tally table, done in one session. Blocker: without it, fixes aim at the wrong stage. Test (deterministic): Given the notes of the last six retros, When each action item gets a tally for owner, due date, and status at the following retro, Then the table shows a completion count per stage.
2. **Cap the next retro at one action item.** Domain: Clear, because a known best practice exists [1]. Increment: one format change at the next session. Blocker removed: unowned and untracked items. Test (deterministic): Given a retro capped at one item, When the next retro starts, Then that item opens the agenda and receives a status of done or not done.
3. **Run an anonymous safety poll for three retros.** Domain: Complex, because the cause shows only after the run [judgment], so this is a capped probe. Increment: a two question anonymous poll before each of the next three retros, budget 5 minutes per session. Metric: share of poll themes absent from the open discussion; baseline is 0 because no poll exists. Pass: at least one blocked theme reaches the action list within three polls. Stop condition: participation under half of attendees after two polls, then switch to an outside facilitator.
4. **Route systemic items with an external owner and a weekly check.** Domain: Clear, because the routing rule is a standard apply step. Increment: one routing note per item found by the audit. Blocker removed: items that die because the team lacks authority over them [judgment]. Test (deterministic): Given a systemic item from the audit, When the weekly check runs, Then the external owner records a status.

Sequencing: Steps 3 and 1 run in parallel with Steps 2 and 4. Step 4 depends on the audit output from Step 1. Step 1 decides whether the cap in Step 2 stays at one item or rises to two.

## Follow-up

- If A1 is false, replace Step 1 with building a written actions log first.
- Condition that changes the recommendation: if the audit shows completed items and no improvement, the failure is in topic selection, not follow-through; Step 2 then changes to a recurring problem theme review [judgment].
