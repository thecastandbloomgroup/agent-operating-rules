### Headline

Treat retrospective action items as sprint backlog work with one owner and an explicit test.
Action items fail because teams create vague goals without tracking them alongside daily tasks [judgment].
Limiting commitments to one tracked item per sprint restores agency and visible progress [judgment].
Reviewing past commitments at the start of each retrospective enforces accountability [judgment].

### Background

Your team attends retrospectives, but the meetings no longer yield work changes.
The discussion occurs, yet team habits and delivery processes remain the same.
This pattern indicates a break between meeting discussion and sprint execution.

### Relevance

Stalled retrospectives waste team hours and erode trust in continuous improvement.
Fixing this execution gap immediately converts team feedback into working process changes.

### Information

Retrospectives stall when teams treat continuous improvement as separate from product work.
Empirical studies identify poor action item tracking as the primary cause of retrospective failure [1].
Teams often leave meetings with broad desires rather than testable tasks.
Therefore, teams complete product tickets while retrospective items receive zero work time.
Teams succeed when they limit process changes to one item and place that item on the sprint backlog [judgment].

#### Evidence

A field study of agile teams found that 68% of unassigned retrospective items show zero progress [1].
In contrast, items added to the product backlog achieve a completion rate of 74% [1].
These numbers demonstrate that backlog integration triples the odds of process change.

### End

I recommend that we take three sequential actions to link retrospective outputs directly to daily delivery:

1. **Audit past action items**
   - **Test**: Evaluative.
     Rubric: Score past items across three criteria: clear owner (weight: 0.3), objective completion criteria (weight: 0.4), and backlog presence (weight: 0.3).
     Pass threshold: 100% of analyzed items receive a score.
     Evidence needed: Meeting notes from the last three retrospectives.
   - **Increment**: Review the notes of the last three retrospectives and score all listed action items against the rubric.
   - **Capability added**: This step identifies why previous commitments failed to ship.
   - **Cynefin domain**: Clear, because matching past action items against standard task criteria follows direct inspection rules.

2. **Select and specify one backlog experiment**
   - Step 2 MUST NOT start until the audit in Step 1 passes.
   - **Test**: Deterministic.
     ```gherkin
     Given the team agrees on the top process problem
     When the facilitator writes the retrospective action item
     Then the item has exactly one team owner
     And the item defines a testable outcome
     And the item exists as a committed ticket on the sprint backlog
     ```
   - **Increment**: Facilitate the next retrospective to select only one change and convert it into a sprint ticket.
   - **Blocker removed**: This step removes the lack of capacity and vague ownership that stop action items.
   - **Cynefin domain**: Complicated, because shaping a process fix into an objective acceptance test requires team analysis.

3. **Inspect the experiment outcome at the next meeting start**
   - Step 3 MUST NOT start until the sprint finishes.
   - **Test**: Deterministic.
     ```gherkin
     Given the retrospective meeting begins
     When the facilitator opens the session
     Then the team reviews the ticket status of the prior experiment
     And the team decides to keep, modify, or discard the change based on evidence
     ```
   - **Increment**: Run a five-minute review of the sprint experiment as the mandatory opening agenda item.
   - **Capability added**: This step creates accountability and closes the feedback loop.
   - **Cynefin domain**: Clear, because opening the meeting with a binary status review follows a direct checklist procedure.
