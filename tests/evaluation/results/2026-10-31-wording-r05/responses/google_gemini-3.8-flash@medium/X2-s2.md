# Headline

Limit retrospective output to one prioritized action item placed directly into the sprint backlog with an assigned owner.

Unfinished action items create fatigue because teams generate more tasks than they finish.
Treating retrospective commitments as real sprint work removes the barrier between reflection and delivery.
Tracking completion in the daily standup makes progress visible to the entire team.

# Background

The team attends regular retrospectives, but the meetings fail to produce lasting process improvements.
Discussions happen during the session, yet team behavior and delivery patterns remain unchanged afterward.
This pattern indicates that the breakdown occurs during execution rather than during discussion.

# Relevance

Stalled retrospectives waste team time and erode trust in continuous improvement.
Fixing this execution gap restores team autonomy before cynicism causes members to disengage completely.

# Information

Research shows that retrospectives fail when teams do not integrate action items into primary workflow tools [1].
Teams often generate three to five action items per retrospective, which exceeds their capacity for process change [judgment].
When items lack dedicated sprint capacity, delivery pressure forces team members to abandon them [2].

A1. The team has authority to modify its internal delivery processes. Why not measured: The material does not state it. Validate by: Ask the team lead during the next standup. If wrong: Reframe actions to focus on external escalation.

A2. Team members attend meetings willingly and identify real problems. Why not measured: The user states people attend but nothing shifts. Validate by: Review the notes from the last two retrospectives. If wrong: Replace execution steps with psychological safety interventions.

Alternative considered:
Replace the retrospective facilitator with an external coach. This alternative loses because external facilitation costs more and fails to solve the work-tracking breakdown [judgment].

# End

I recommend that we take these three actions:

1. Step 1: Audit the last three retrospectives to establish baseline completion rates.
   - Increment: Complete an audit sheet that lists all action items from the past three retrospectives in one 45-minute session.
   - Blocker removed: Removes ambiguity about where previous commitments stalled.
   - Kind and test: Evaluative. Write a rubric with three criteria: clear owner assigned (weight 30%), status tracked in backlog (weight 30%), and verified completion (weight 40%). Pass threshold is an audit of 100% of recorded items against these criteria.
   - Cynefin domain: Clear, because locating past notes and recording status follows a known administrative process.

2. Step 2: Enforce a single-item limit and add that item directly to the active sprint backlog. Step 2 MUST NOT start until Step 1 completes.
   - Increment: Draft a rule agreement and create one user story for the top retrospective item in one 30-minute working session.
   - Capability added: Adds capacity protection by treating improvement work as standard sprint scope.
   - Kind and test: Deterministic.
     Given a completed retrospective with three proposed improvements,
     When the team votes on the highest-priority item,
     Then the team selects exactly one item, assigns one owner, and inserts it into the current sprint backlog.
   - Cynefin domain: Complicated, because selecting the single highest-leverage improvement requires team analysis and consensus.

3. Step 3: Run a two-sprint safe-to-fail probe that reviews the action item at every daily standup. Step 3 MUST NOT start until Step 2 creates the backlog item.
   - Increment: Track daily status mentions for the action item over two consecutive sprints with a maximum budget of two weeks. Stop the probe if standup duration increases by more than two minutes.
   - Capability added: Adds visibility so that process commitments do not get forgotten between meetings.
   - Kind and test: Stochastic. The metric is the completion rate of retrospective action items. The baseline is 0% completion from past retrospectives. The pass threshold is 100% completion of the single selected item across two sprints with a sample size of two sprints.
   - Cynefin domain: Complex, because team social accountability yields unpredictable engagement that requires an empirical probe.

# Follow-up

If A1 is false, replace Step 2 with an escalation request to management for process autonomy.
