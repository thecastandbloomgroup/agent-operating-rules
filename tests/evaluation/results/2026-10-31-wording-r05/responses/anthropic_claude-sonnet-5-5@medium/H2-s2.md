## Headline

Verify the facts first, give one person the decision, and release only a short holding statement until the facts are known.

- Legal, PR (public relations), and the board disagree because no one owns the decision. One owner fixes that.
- The journalist's claim is either true or false. Only a fact check settles it, so every message before that check adds risk.
- A holding statement buys time without a second claim that could be wrong.
- One brief and one scored comparison of options replace three separate opinions.

## Background

A journalist published that the CEO's statement from yesterday (Saturday, October 31, 2026) was false. Legal, PR, and the board each send you advice, and the advice conflicts. You ask for next steps.

## Relevance

Each hour without one owner and one set of facts gives each group more room to act alone. A conflicting public message from any group would worsen the problem. A follow-up story within 24 hours is likely (66 to 100%) [judgment].

## Information

**Evidence**

- Fact: the dispute is about whether the statement was accurate. No one has yet compared it with records [judgment].
- Fact: three groups give conflicting advice, so the decision rights are unclear [judgment].
- Domain: the situation has an urgent, unstable part (conflicting messages) and a knowable part (the facts). Cynefin separates these cases and sets different actions for each [1].

**Assumption log**

A1. You can convene Legal, PR, and a board representative today. Why not measured: the material does not state your authority. Validate by: send one meeting invite and see who accepts within 30 minutes. If wrong: Step 1 moves to the person who holds that authority.

A2. No one has checked the CEO's statement against source records. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask Legal in one message. If wrong: skip Step 2 and go to Step 3.

A3. The company may have legal disclosure duties, for example as a listed company. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask Legal one yes or no question. If wrong: remove the disclosure criterion from the Step 3 rubric.

**Alternative considered**

The strongest alternative is an immediate public denial that defends the CEO. It loses because a denial of a true claim becomes a second false statement and doubles the damage [judgment].

## End

I recommend that we take these five steps, in this order:

1. **Freeze and assign an owner (Chaotic: the groups act at cross purposes and no cause and effect is clear, so stabilise first).**
   - Increment: within one hour, send one message that stops all outside comms and names one decision owner.
   - Blocker removed: three groups acting alone.
   - Kind: deterministic.
   - Test: Given the message is sent, When any group drafts outside comms, Then it goes to the owner first. Step 2 MUST NOT start until all three groups confirm in writing.

2. **Verify the facts (Complicated: records and experts can answer it).**
   - Increment: Legal and the CEO's office compare each disputed line with source records. Output is a one-page table of line, record, and match or mismatch.
   - Blocker removed: no shared facts.
   - Kind: evaluative.
   - Rubric: criteria are source quality (40%), completeness (40%), and independent review (20%). Pass at 80 of 100. Each criterion needs a named record.
   - Step 3 MUST NOT start until the table passes.

3. **Choose the response (Complicated: a scored comparison picks the practice).**
   - Increment: score four options (correct, clarify, defend, no comment) in a Kepner-Tregoe table [judgment on the weights].
   - Blocker removed: the disagreement between Legal, PR, and the board.
   - Kind: evaluative.
   - Rubric: accuracy 40%, legal exposure 25%, stakeholder trust 20%, speed 15%. Score 1 to 5 per criterion. Pass at 70 of 100 for the chosen option. Evidence for each score comes from the Step 2 table.
   - The owner decides. The board gets the table before the decision.

4. **Release a holding statement (Complicated: the practice is known, but the wording needs expert review).**
   - Increment: one statement of three sentences or fewer, approved by Legal and the owner.
   - Blocker removed: silence while facts are checked.
   - Kind: deterministic.
   - Test: Given the draft, When Legal reviews it, Then it has no claim that Step 2 has not confirmed. Also, Given the draft, When PR reviews it, Then it names the date of the next update.
   - Release it only after Step 1 passes. It MAY go out before Step 3 ends.

5. **Run a reaction probe (Complex: media and public response show only afterward).**
   - Increment: count outlets that repeat the claim at the time of publication and every 6 hours.
   - Budget: one analyst for 24 hours.
   - Blocker removed: guesses about how far the story spreads.
   - Kind: stochastic.
   - Acceptance test: metric is the number of outlets repeating the claim. Baseline is the count at publication. Pass threshold is a rise of no more than 20% across checks. Sample size is four checks. Stop early if a major outlet runs the story, and then go to Step 3 again.
   - Leverage here is a guess. Run this in parallel with Steps 2 to 4.

## Follow-up

**Open questions:** none. A1 to A3 cover the gaps.

**Conditions that change the recommendation:**
- If A2 is false and a check already exists, replace Step 2 with a review of that check.
- If Step 2 shows the statement was true, replace Step 3 with a plan to publish the supporting records.
- If Step 2 shows the statement was false, add a correction to the options in Step 3 and weight it highest on accuracy.
