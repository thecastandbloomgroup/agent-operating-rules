## Headline

Find out why retro actions fail, then fix follow-through before you change the meeting format.

- Three causes cover all cases: the team agrees on no actions, the team agrees on actions but does not finish them, or the team finishes them with no effect.
- Weak follow-through is the most likely cause [judgment].
- A short audit of past retros names the actual cause at low cost.
- A fix that works for most causes is one owned action per retro, tracked in the normal work backlog and reviewed first at the next retro.

## Background

A retrospective (retro) is a recurring team meeting that reviews recent work and agrees on improvements. Your team attends the retros, but no change follows. The request is for a plan to restore the link between retro and change.

## Relevance

A retro that produces no change costs the team meeting time each cycle. It also teaches people that speaking up has no result, so engagement falls further.

## Information

**Evidence**

1. The Scrum Guide says the most impactful improvements should be addressed as soon as possible. It also says the team may add them to the sprint backlog. See the Sprint Retrospective section.

   [2]

2. Derby and Larsen describe a retro as a sequence of stages that ends with a clear choice of what to do next.

   [1]

3. Weak follow-through is the most likely cause. Evidence: none from your team. Confidence: low, because no retro records were seen. [judgment]

4. The share of cause 1, 2, and 3 is unknown. Each is about as likely as not to be the main cause until the audit runs.

**Assumption log**

A1. The team works in fixed cycles and holds a retro each cycle. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask the team lead for the calendar. If wrong: Step 3 needs a different review slot.

A2. Past retros left written notes. Why not measured: no tool can reach them. Validate by: search the team wiki or chat for the last six retros. If wrong: Step 1 starts with a recall session.

A3. The team has a shared work backlog. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: open the backlog tool. If wrong: Step 2 uses a visible board instead.

**Alternative considered**

The strongest alternative is to change the retro format first, for example new activities or a new facilitator. It loses because a new format does not fix missing owners or missing time to do the work [judgment]. Step 4 tests it as a small probe.

## End

I recommend that we take these four actions in this order.

1. **Audit the last six retros.** Increment: one session of 60 minutes or less. Label each action item as done with effect, done without effect, or not done. Blocker removed: we do not know which of the three causes applies. Kind: deterministic. Cynefin domain: Confused, because the cause is not yet known, so this step gathers the data to classify it. Test:
   - Given the notes of the last six retros
   - When we label every action item
   - Then each item has exactly one status
   - And the status counts add up to the total number of items

2. **Set one action per retro, with an owner and a date, and put it in the work backlog.** Increment: one retro, using one template. Blocker removed: nobody owns the change and nobody schedules time for it. Kind: deterministic. Cynefin domain: Complicated, because several good practices exist and the audit from Step 1 picks the one that fits. Step 2 MUST NOT start until Step 1 is complete. Test:
   - Given a finished retro
   - When we read the backlog
   - Then it holds one item with an owner, a due date, and the label "retro action"

3. **Open every retro with a review of the last action.** Increment: a standing 10-minute first agenda item, starting at the next retro. Blocker removed: the team never checks results. Kind: deterministic. Cynefin domain: Clear, because the practice is well known and has a known effect. Test:
   - Given a retro starts
   - When the facilitator opens the agenda
   - Then the first item shows the status and effect of the last action

4. **Run a probe that changes the format.** Increment: two retros in which the team picks one problem and finds its root cause, instead of listing many topics. Blocker removed: Steps 2 and 3 fail if the chosen actions do not address real causes. Kind: stochastic. Cynefin domain: Complex, because we see the effect only after the retros. Leverage is a guess. Run this step in parallel with Steps 2 and 3.
   - Metric: share of retro actions finished within one cycle.
   - Baseline: the rate from Step 1.
   - Pass threshold: baseline plus 30 percentage points.
   - Sample size: four retros. Accept only after four.
   - Budget: 15 extra minutes per retro.
   - Stop condition: revert to the old format if the rate is below baseline after two retros.

If A2 is false, replace Step 1 with a recall session, and treat its counts as low confidence.
