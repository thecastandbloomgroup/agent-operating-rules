## Headline

Find out why actions die before you change the retrospective format.

- First, measure what happens to actions after each retrospective, because no data on that exists yet.
- Next, ask each member why nothing shifts, because the cause decides the fix.
- Then test a smaller commitment per retrospective, because too many unowned actions is a likely cause [judgment].
- Last, escalate blockers the team cannot remove, because the team cannot fix those alone.

## Background

The team holds retrospectives (regular meetings to review work and agree on improvements). People attend, but no changes follow. The message gives no data on past actions, team size, or format.

## Relevance

Each retrospective without change costs the team time and lowers trust in the meeting. Trust falls further with every empty cycle [judgment].

## Information

**Evidence**

- Attendance is present and change is absent. This points to a follow-through problem more than a participation problem [inference].
- Retrospective guidance advises a small number of concrete actions per session.

  Source: [1].
- The cause is not known, so the work is a diagnosis first. Cynefin (a framework that sorts problems by how cause and effect relate) calls for analysis or probes here.

  Source: [2].
- Likely causes, from most to least probable [judgment]:
  1. Actions have no owner or date.
  2. Actions are too big or too many.
  3. The blocker sits outside the team.
  4. Members do not believe change is possible.

  These four overlap little, and together they cover the usual causes. Confidence is low, because no data from this team exists.

**Assumption log**

A1. The team records retrospective actions somewhere. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask the facilitator for the last six outputs. If wrong: Step 1 becomes a rebuild from memory, and the baseline is weaker.

A2. The team has 5 to 12 members. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: count the attendees. If wrong: for a team over 12, split the interviews into a sample.

A3. The team lead is willing to act on blockers. Why not measured: the user has not answered. Validate by: ask the lead in Step 4. If wrong: replace Step 4 with a request to the lead's manager.

**Alternative considered**

Replace the format at once (new facilitator, new activities). It loses because a new format does not fix a missing owner or an outside blocker [judgment]. It also adds change before the cause is known.

## End

I recommend that we take these steps in order:

1. **Audit the last six retrospectives.** Increment: one table of action, owner, due date, and status, built in 45 minutes. Blocker removed: no baseline exists. Kind: deterministic. Domain: Clear, because counting records has a known method.
   - Test: Given the last six retrospective records, When we list every action, Then each row shows owner, date, status, and the table gives the share of actions done.
2. **Interview each member for 15 minutes about why nothing shifts.** Increment: coded themes from all interviews in one session. Blocker removed: the cause is unknown. Kind: evaluative. Domain: Complicated, because thematic analysis [3] picks the cause. Step 2 MUST NOT start until Step 1 ends, so that questions can use the audit.
   - Rubric: criteria are theme support (weight 50%), link to audit data (30%), and fixability by the team (20%). The pass threshold is a weighted score of 70 out of 100. Each criterion needs the count of members who raised the theme and one matching audit row.
3. **Run a probe: one action per retrospective, with one owner and one date.** Increment: the next retrospective only, with 10 minutes at its start to review the last action. Capability added: visible follow-through. Kind: stochastic. Domain: Complex, because team response shows only after the trial. Leverage is a guess. Step 3 MUST NOT start until Step 2 names the main cause.
   - Acceptance test: the metric is the share of actions done by the next retrospective. The baseline is the Step 1 result. The pass threshold is 80% done. The sample size is three retrospectives.
   - Stop condition: stop and redesign the format if fewer than 50% are done after two retrospectives. The budget is 10 minutes per session.
4. **Escalate blockers outside team control.** Increment: one written list of blockers sent to the lead, built from Steps 1 and 2 in 30 minutes. Blocker removed: the team cannot fix the item alone. Kind: evaluative. Domain: Complicated, because the lead's options need analysis. Run Step 4 in parallel with Step 3.
   - Rubric: criteria are blocker cost in hours per month (40%), owner outside the team named (30%), and a requested decision date (30%). The pass threshold is 70 out of 100. Each criterion needs a number from the audit or interviews.

If A1 is false, replace Step 1 with a one-page recall exercise held with the team.
