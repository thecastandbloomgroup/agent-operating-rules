## Headline

Restore the connection pool size and the cache TTL (time to live), and keep the ORM change.

- The pool cut from 50 to 8 connections explains 690 of the 900 ms p95, because pool wait is the span that grew.
- The cache TTL cut from 300 s to 60 s raised the miss rate from 12% to 31%, so more requests need the pool.
- The ORM query spans did not grow, so the ORM change is not the cause.

## Background

The orders endpoint p95 rose from 120 ms to 900 ms after release 2026.10.21. The release changed the pool, the cache TTL, and one ORM query. The attached logs, traces, and diff are the evidence.

## Relevance

Error rate rose from 0.02% to 0.40% [3]. Each error is a 503 after a 5 s wait, so users see failures now.

## Information

Evidence:

- Pool wait p95 rose from 3 ms to 690 ms. Total p95 rose by 780 ms. Pool wait is therefore 88% of the rise.

  Source: traces.txt, span table [3].

- Query spans stayed flat. The orders query p95 went from 52 ms to 58 ms.

  Source: traces.txt, span table [3].

- The release cut the pool from `max_size: 50` to 8 and the acquire timeout from 30 s to 5 s.

  Source: release.diff, config/db.yaml [2].

- The release cut the cache TTL from 300 s to 60 s. The miss rate rose from 12% to 31%.

  Source: release.diff, config/cache.yaml [2].

  Source: traces.txt, cache miss rate row [3].

- Traffic is flat at 410 and 405 requests per second, and DB CPU is 38% and 41%. Load and the database are not the cause.

  Source: traces.txt, last two lines [3].

- Pools hit 8 of 8 with 6 to 14 waiters. 96% of errors are `PoolTimeout`.

  Source: logs.txt, 10:14:02 to 10:14:12 [1].

  Source: traces.txt, error line [3].

- Theme: all three sources point to pool starvation. The logs, traces, and diff agree, and no source disagrees. Confidence is high, with three independent sources.

- Peak connections were 31 before and 8 after. Four pods at 8 connections each fit the "before" figure of 31 only if each request holds a connection for its whole life. At 405 requests per second and 48 ms, that is about 19 busy connections [inference]. This supports the cause: the pool cap of 8 per pod is below demand, and slow requests hold connections longer. The result is a feedback loop.

- The commit message cites a new DB connection limit per service (OPS-412). The diff does not state the value [2].

A3. A request holds its connection until it ends. Why not measured: the diff shows no session code. Validate by: read the session scope in the request handler. If wrong: Step 4 has no value, so drop it.

Alternative considered: revert the whole release. It loses because the ORM change is not the cause (query p95 +6 ms), and OPS-412 needs the lower pool limit [judgment].
