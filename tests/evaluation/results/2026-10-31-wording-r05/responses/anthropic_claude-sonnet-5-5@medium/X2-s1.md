## Headline

Limit each retrospective to one owned, dated change, and measure whether the team finishes it.

- The likely cause is weak follow-through, not a lack of ideas [judgment].
- A short review of the last three retrospectives confirms the cause before you change anything.
- One action with one owner is easier to finish than a long list.
- A new meeting format is a test, so cap its cost and measure it.

## Background

Your team attends retrospectives, but no change results. The request gives no data on format, team size, or action history. This answer assumes a software team that meets every two weeks.

## Relevance

Each empty retrospective costs one hour per person and teaches the team that the meeting has no effect. Attendance then drops, and the problem gets harder to fix.

## Information

**Evidence**

1. A standard retrospective has five stages: set the stage, gather data, generate insights, decide what to do, and close [1]. Teams that stop at insights produce talk and no change [inference].
2. Kerth treats the retrospective as a source of learning that the team must apply to later work [2].
3. The cause is "weak follow-through". This is likely (66 to 100%), basis: common pattern, no data from your team [judgment].

**Assumption log**

A2. The team has 5 to 10 members and meets every two weeks. Why not measured: the user has not answered. Validate by: check the calendar. If wrong: change the sample size in Step 3.

A3. The team lead can reserve sprint capacity for one action. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask the lead. If wrong: Step 2 needs a sponsor first.

**Alternative considered**

Replace the retrospective with a new format at once. It loses because it treats the symptom before the cause is known. Step 1 costs about 30 minutes [judgment].

## End

I recommend that we take these four actions, in this order:

1. **Diagnose the cause.** Evaluative. Cynefin: Confused, because the cause is not yet known.
   - Increment: in 30 minutes, list the actions from the last three retrospectives and mark each done or not done. Ask each member in writing: "What stops change here?"
   - Blocker removed: we do not know why nothing shifts.
   - Test (rubric, written first): criteria are completion rate (weight 40), named owner (20), action fits one sprint (20), and members' stated reasons (20). Score each against the list. Pass: one cause scores 60 or more of 100.
   - Step 2 MUST NOT start until Step 1 names a cause.

2. **Set a one-action rule.** Deterministic. Cynefin: Clear, because assigning an owner and date is a known practice.
   - Increment: at the next retrospective, pick one action, name one owner, set one date, and put it in the sprint backlog.
   - Blocker removed: no one owns the change.
   - Test:
     - Given a retrospective has ended
     - When the team reviews the sprint backlog
     - Then it holds exactly one retrospective action with an owner and a date

3. **Open each retrospective with a status check.** Deterministic. Cynefin: Clear, because the review of the prior action is a known practice.
   - Increment: spend the first 5 minutes on the prior action: done, not done, or dropped, with a reason.
   - Blocker removed: no one sees the result of past actions.
   - Test:
     - Given a retrospective starts
     - When the facilitator opens the agenda
     - Then the prior action has a recorded status before any new topic
   - Step 3 MUST NOT start until the test in Step 2 passes.

4. **Probe one format change.** Stochastic. Cynefin: Complex, because team response is visible only afterward. Leverage is a guess.
   - Increment: run one changed format (for example, a rotating facilitator) for four retrospectives.
   - Blocker removed: low engagement.
   - Test (written first): the metric is the share of actions done within one sprint. The baseline is the Step 1 rate. The threshold is the baseline plus 25 points. The sample size is four retrospectives, which is small, so treat a pass as weak evidence. The budget is 4 hours in total. The stop condition is two retrospectives in a row with no completed action. Run this probe in parallel with Steps 2 and 3.

If A1 is false, replace Step 2 with a fix for the cause that Step 1 finds.
