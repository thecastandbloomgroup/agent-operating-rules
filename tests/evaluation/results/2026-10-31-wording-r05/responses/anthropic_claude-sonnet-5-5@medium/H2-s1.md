## Headline

Hold all outside statements, name one decision owner, and verify the facts before the group picks a message.

- **Facts:** A two-hour check of the CEO's statement against records shows whether the story is true, false, or partly true.
- **Control:** One owner decides, so Legal, PR, and the board send input to one place.
- **Words:** A short holding line replaces silence until the facts are known.
- **Governance:** The board gets one brief and a fixed update time, not separate threads.

## Background

A journalist published that the CEO's statement from yesterday was false. Legal, PR (public relations), and the board each send you separate advice. No one owns the decision.

## Relevance

Each hour without a shared line raises the chance of two conflicting statements. Therefore a second story can start before the first one is checked [judgment].

## Information

Evidence:

1. Crisis communication research says to match the response to the responsibility the public assigns to the organization. Therefore the facts must come before the message [1].
2. A false claim repeated after the facts are known becomes a second error [judgment].
3. Three groups with three messages produce three public versions [judgment].

Confidence that "facts first, then message" is the right order: medium, based on one secondary source and the judgments above.

Assumption log:

A1. The disputed claim can be checked against internal records within hours. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: ask the records owner to find the source of the statement. If wrong: Step 1 takes longer and the holding line stays in force.

A2. No regulator or disclosure rule applies to the statement. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: Legal confirms in one message. If wrong: the Legal disclosure deadline overrides the timing in Step 3.

A3. The journalist's story is the only coverage so far. Why not measured: no tool can reach it. Validate by: search news and social media for the claim. If wrong: send the holding line sooner and to more channels.

Alternative considered: defend the CEO now. It loses because, if the story is true, a denial adds a second false statement [judgment]. Staying silent until the board agrees also loses, because the journalist's account then stands alone [judgment].

## End

I recommend that we take these four actions in order. Domain labels follow Cynefin [2].

1. **Check the facts (evaluative, Confused).** The domain is Confused because we do not yet know if the story is true.
   - Increment: within two hours, the records owner and Legal compare each disputed claim with the CEO's words and the source records.
   - Blocker removed: no one knows if the statement is false.
   - Test, written first. Criteria and weights: match to a primary record 40, written evidence in hand 30, independent confirmation 20, number of claims affected 10. Evidence needed: the record itself, a copy of the document, a second source, a claim count. Pass threshold: 70 or more marks a claim "confirmed", 30 or less marks it "refuted", and a score between marks it "open".

2. **Name one owner and freeze statements (deterministic, Clear).** The domain is Clear because standard practice exists: one owner and one document hold. Step 2 starts with Step 1 because it does not depend on it.
   - Increment: the board chair names an owner who is not the CEO, and Legal sends a document hold notice.
   - Blocker removed: no one has decision rights.
   - Test:
     - Given the board chair named the owner
     - When Legal, PR, or a board member wants to release a statement
     - Then the owner approves it first, and the hold notice reaches every person who handled the statement.

3. **Release a holding line (deterministic, Complicated).** The domain is Complicated because Legal and PR analysis picks the wording. Step 3 MUST NOT start until the test in Step 2 passes.
   - Increment: draft a note of 60 words or fewer that says we are reviewing the report and will update at a set time.
   - Blocker removed: silence and conflicting replies.
   - Test:
     - Given the fact check is not finished
     - When the owner reviews the draft
     - Then the draft states no fact about the disputed statement, names an update time, and Legal and PR approve it in writing.

4. **Decide the response and brief the board (evaluative, Complicated).** The domain is Complicated because the facts from Step 1 let analysis pick the practice. Step 4 MUST NOT start until Step 1 is complete.
   - Increment: score three options in a Pugh matrix, then send the board a one-page brief and hold one 30-minute call.
   - Options: correct and apologize, clarify with context, or stand by the statement.
   - Blocker removed: the board and the two teams disagree on the message.
   - Test, written first. Criteria and weights: accuracy to confirmed facts 40, Legal exposure 25, trust of staff and customers 20, speed 15. Evidence needed: Step 1 scores, a Legal opinion, a PR coverage review. Pass threshold: pick the highest weighted score, and reject any option that scores below the baseline on accuracy.

## Follow-up

Open questions: none.

Conditions that change the recommendation:

- If A2 is false, Legal sets the timing of Step 3.
- If A3 is false, release the holding line as soon as the owner approves it.
