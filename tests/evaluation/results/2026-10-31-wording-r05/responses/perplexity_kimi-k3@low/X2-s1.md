## Headline

Retrospectives fail when findings have no owner, no action, and no check, so fix the loop, not the meeting.

- Findings die because nobody owns them, so assign one owner per action [judgment].
- Actions vanish because they leave the room, so track them in the same place as work [judgment].
- Causes stay hidden because discussion stays at events, not systems, so use a root-cause step [3].
- Attendance without change signals low safety, so measure safety before you add process [4].

## Background

The team holds retrospectives, people attend, and no change follows. The request asks what to do about that pattern.

## Relevance

A retrospective that produces no change costs one to two hours per cycle per person. That pattern also teaches the team that speaking up has no effect.

## Information

Evidence. Theme from agile literature: actions without owners and outside the sprint backlog rarely complete; supported by two practice sources [1][2]. Baseline comparison: teams that review prior actions at the next retrospective complete a measurable share of them; teams that do not, complete near zero [judgment]. Assumption log and alternative follow.

Assumptions:

```text
A1. The team is a single agile team of 5 to 9 people. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: one message to the team lead. If wrong: scales change only the facilitator load, not the method.
A2. Psychological safety is moderate, not zero. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: a 5-question anonymous Edmondson survey, 15 minutes [4]. If wrong: if safety is low, Step 2 becomes the blocker and moves first.
A3. Prior actions, if any, are not tracked. Why not measured: the material does not state it. Validate by: check the last three retrospective notes for carried-over actions. If wrong: skip Step 1.
```

Alternative considered. Replace the retrospective with written async feedback: it lowers the meeting cost, but it removes the group discussion that surfaces system causes, so it loses on diagnosis quality [judgment].

## End

I recommend that we:

1. Log each action in the team backlog with one named owner and a due date. This removes the invisibility blocker. Test (deterministic): Given the retrospective ends, When the facilitator exports actions, Then every action has an owner and date in the tracker. Clear domain: a known best practice exists.
2. Run a 15-minute anonymous safety survey before the next retrospective [4]. This detects the silence blocker. Test (stochastic): metric is the Edmondson score; baseline is this first run; threshold is all responses collected, n = full team. Complex domain: results appear only after the probe, budget 15 minutes, stop if response rate is under 60%.
3. Open each retrospective with a review of prior actions for 10 minutes. This removes the no-follow-through blocker. Test (deterministic): Given the meeting starts, When the facilitator reads prior actions, Then each is marked done, open, or dropped with a reason. Clear domain.
4. Use a 5-Whys step on the top issue per retrospective [3]. This removes the surface-cause blocker. Test (evaluative): rubric weights root cause stated 50%, cause is a system not a person 30%, cause is testable 20%; pass at 70/100. Complicated domain: skilled analysis picks the method.
5. Cap actions at two per retrospective. This removes the overload blocker. Test (deterministic): Given actions are chosen, When more than two are proposed, Then the team votes and keeps two. Clear domain.

## Follow-up

Condition that changes this: if A2 is false and safety is low, run Step 2 first and use anonymous collection for two cycles.
