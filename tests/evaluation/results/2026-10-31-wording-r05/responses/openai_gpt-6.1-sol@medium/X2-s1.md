## Headline

Reset the next two retrospectives around one small change, a named owner, and a check of what actually changed.

- First, find where past actions stop: selection, ownership, execution, or proof of benefit.
- Then, reserve work time for one change instead of producing another action list.
- Finally, judge the reset by completed improvements, not attendance or discussion quality.

## Background

People attend your retrospectives, but you report no resulting changes. [1] The report does not establish whether actions stall or actions finish without helping.

## Relevance

The next useful step is to trace past actions before changing the meeting format. [judgment] Otherwise, a new format risks preserving the same failure. [judgment]

## Information

**Treat this as a broken improvement process, not automatically a meeting problem.** [judgment]

The Scrum Guide says teams should address their most useful improvements promptly. It also allows teams to include those improvements in their work plan. [2]

Use these checks to find the first broken link:

| Check | Evidence to seek | Response if absent |
|---|---|---|
| Did the team choose a specific change? | A concrete action, not “communicate better” | Define one observable change |
| Did someone accept ownership and reserve time? | A named owner and work allocation | Assign both before closing |
| Did the change happen? | A changed practice, document, tool, or decision | Remove the execution blocker |
| Did anyone check its effect? | A before-and-after result | Define and collect one measure |
| Did the result improve? | Evidence against the chosen measure | Revise or stop the change |

These checks provide a practical diagnosis, not proof of the underlying cause. [judgment]

## End

I recommend that we take these three steps:

### 1. Audit the last two retrospectives before the next meeting

**Test first, deterministic:** Given the last two retrospectives, when each action receives a status, then every action has evidence or an explicit evidence gap.

Spend **20 minutes** reviewing the records. If records do not exist, reconstruct the actions with attendees.

For each action, record its owner, completion status, result, and first broken link from the table. Count verified improvements across those two cycles.

This removes uncertainty about where progress stops. **Clear:** the task follows a fixed checklist.

### 2. Prepare one change that fits the team’s control

**Test first, deterministic:** Given one selected problem, when the team defines its change, then the record includes every field below.

After Step 1 passes, write one change card:

- **Problem:** What happens now?
- **Change:** What will the team do differently?
- **Owner:** Who accepts responsibility?
- **Capacity:** What work time will the team reserve?
- **Measure:** What observable result counts as improvement?
- **Review date:** When will the team inspect the result?

Keep the work within **one hour per cycle**. For example: replace “improve handovers” with one handover checklist tested on the next three transfers.

This removes vague commitments. **Clear:** the card has fixed completion criteria.

If the change needs outside authority, make the first action a named request with a response date.

### 3. Run a capped, two-cycle trial

**Acceptance test first, stochastic:**

- **Metric:** Number of completed changes with evidence of the intended benefit.
- **Baseline:** The count from the two cycles audited in Step 1.
- **Pass threshold:** At least one verified improvement across the next two cycles.
- **Sample:** Two retrospective cycles.

Start only after Step 2 passes. Begin each retrospective with a **five-minute review** of the previous change.

Cap improvement work at **one hour per cycle**. Stop and reassess if the first change exceeds that budget or needs unavailable authority.

This tests whether a smaller commitment produces an actual improvement. **Complex:** effects depend on team behavior, so this is a limited trial, not a proven fix.

Do not call the reset successful until the sample reaches two cycles and meets the threshold.
