## Background

## Relevance

A formatter alone does not settle who decides or how colleagues handle disagreement. [judgment] Therefore, change the team agreement alongside the tool. [judgment]

## Information

**Working diagnosis:** the team lacks an accepted way to settle low-stakes preferences. [judgment] Confidence is low because the evidence contains no review examples or team perspectives.

Separate three concerns:

| Concern | Decision method | Review treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Layout, such as indentation and keyword case | Agreed formatter configuration | Automated check |
| Meaning, such as unclear names or query structure | Explain the concrete readability concern | Human discussion |
| Correctness, such as joins, nulls, or results | Tests and technical evidence | Human review |

These boundaries are proposed team rules, not findings about your current practice. [judgment]

**Suggested team agreement**

> Different SQL styles are preferences, not evidence of competence.  
> The formatter decides layout.  
> Reviewers explain concerns about meaning or correctness.  
> Nobody blocks a review over personal layout preferences.  
> Anyone can propose a policy change outside an active review.

### 3. Run a bounded review trial

- **Baseline:** comments per review across the previous 10 SQL reviews.
- **Sample:** the next 10 SQL reviews.
- **Pass threshold:** at least 80% fewer layout-only comments, with no unresolved formatter-related correctness concern.
- **Budget:** two calendar weeks and two staff-hours for measurement.
- **Stop condition:** pause enforcement if the formatter changes SQL meaning.

The threshold is a proposed target, not an established benchmark. [judgment]

After Step 2 passes, apply the agreement during the trial. Redirect layout disputes to the formatter or the separate policy discussion.

Use this review response:

> “Does this concern change meaning or correctness? If not, the formatter decides; propose a policy change separately.”

At the end, record the result before accepting the policy. If fewer than 10 reviews occur, report insufficient evidence rather than success.

This tests whether the agreement reduces friction in practice. [judgment]

**Domain: Complex**, because people’s responses become visible only during the trial; the benefit estimate remains a guess.
