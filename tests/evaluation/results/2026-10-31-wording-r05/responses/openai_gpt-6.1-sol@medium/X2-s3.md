## Information

**Evidence.** The Scrum Guide describes retrospectives as a way to increase quality and effectiveness through useful changes. [2] Attendance alone does not meet that purpose. [inference]

**Working diagnosis.** Check each proposed change against these four states:

1. **Not agreed:** discussion ends without a specific action.
2. **Agreed but unfinished:** an action exists, but nobody completes it.
3. **Finished without improvement:** someone completes the action, but work does not improve.
4. **Finished with improvement:** someone completes the action, and evidence shows a useful result.

These states separate agreement, execution, and effect without treating them as the same problem. [judgment]

**Confidence:** Moderate confidence in this starting point, based on your report; the actual point of failure remains unverified. [judgment]

## End

I recommend that we take these three steps:

### 1. Audit the last three retrospectives

**Kind:** Deterministic. **Domain:** Complicated, because record analysis identifies where proposed changes stop.

**Test first:** Given the available notes, when the audit ends, then every proposed change has one state from the list above.

Review the notes in one 30-minute session. Record the action, owner, deadline, completion evidence, and result evidence.

Mark missing evidence as “not recorded,” rather than assuming failure. This removes the blocker of an unverified diagnosis.

### 2. Create one small change with a visible agreement

Start after Step 1 passes.

**Kind:** Deterministic. **Domain:** Clear, because the agreement has fixed, checkable fields.

**Test first:** Given one chosen problem, when the team agrees its next action, then the record contains:

- One owner with authority to act.
- One change requiring no more than two hours.
- One deadline before the next retrospective.
- One observable result and its current baseline.
- One date to review the evidence.

Choose a change that addresses the failure found in Step 1. This adds a clear path from discussion to delivery.

For example, replace “improve handovers” with “use a five-item checklist for the next five handovers.”

If people report fear of consequences, do not require public disclosure. Use a confidential route before choosing the change.

### 3. Run a capped trial across three retrospectives

Start after Step 2 passes.

**Kind:** Stochastic, because team behavior and work results vary.

**Domain:** Complex, because the effect becomes visible only after the trial. Any leverage estimate remains a guess. [judgment]

**Acceptance test first:**

- **Metric:** Share of agreed changes completed by their deadlines.
- **Baseline:** The share found in Step 1.
- **Sample:** Three change attempts, one per retrospective.
- **Pass threshold:** At least two finish on time, with at least one meeting its agreed result target.
- **Comparison:** Completion must improve against baseline, or remain at 100% if baseline already reaches 100%.

Use the first five minutes of each retrospective to review the previous action’s evidence. Then retain, adjust, or stop that change.

Cap each change at two hours and each review at five minutes. Stop any change that harms work or exposes people to risk.

This trial tests local usefulness; it does not establish a general causal effect. [judgment]

## Follow-up

If the audit shows an external approval block, address that block before testing another team-owned change.

If actions finish but produce no benefit, change the proposed solution rather than adding accountability measures. [judgment]
