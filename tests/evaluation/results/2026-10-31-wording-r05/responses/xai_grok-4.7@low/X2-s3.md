Background
A retrospective is a regular meeting where a team reviews recent work and chooses a change. Your team still meets, and people still come. However, the work after the meeting stays the same.

Relevance
Each cycle without a real change trains the team to ignore the meeting. Therefore the next retrospective should prove that one change can land.

End
I recommend that we take three steps.

1. Classify the failure before the next meeting. This step is Confused, because the cause is not clear enough to pick a practice. Increment: read the last three retrospective notes in one session. Blocker removed: the team stops guessing the cause. Write the rubric before the read. A rubric is a short score sheet. Criteria and weights: written decision 30, named owner 25, check date 25, sign that work changed 20. Pass threshold: a criterion fails when the note lacks that item. The mode is the criterion that fails in at least two of the three notes. Evidence is those three notes. Step 2 MUST NOT start until this score exists.

2. Close the next retrospective with one experiment. This step is Clear, because the known practice is to leave with one owned action. Increment: add a five-line close to the agenda and use it once. Blocker removed: the meeting can no longer end as talk only. Given the notes from Step 1 exist. When the meeting ends. Then the notes hold one experiment, one owner, one date, and one pass test. They hold no second experiment. Step 3 MUST NOT start until that close is in the notes.

3. Run the experiment until the next retrospective. This step is Complex, because the effect shows only after the cycle. It is a safe-to-fail probe with a budget of one cycle and one experiment. Stop if the check turns into blame, or if the owner cannot act. Metric: status of that experiment at the next meeting. Baseline: no completed change in the last three notes, if A2 is true. Pass threshold: the experiment is done, or a blocker is recorded. Sample size: one cycle. Leverage, that is the size of the block this step removes, is a guess.

If A2 is false, replace Step 2 with a quality test. The experiment must name the behavior that will differ and the evidence you will see. If the rubric shows silence on real issues, start with ten minutes of silent writing. Do not open debate before that writing ends.

Follow-up
If the manager blocks the experiment, pause Step 3 and use the approval path from A3.
